1. C2. Write C(T)=sum c_j Q_m(T)/(T-r_j). Since n>=m, equality after evaluation
also gives polynomial equality for these degrees. For C=prod_{i in S}(T-r_i),
c_j=0 on S, and for j in J=S^c the coefficient is 1/prod_{l in J-{j}}(r_j-r_l).
A rational number integral over Z is an integer. If |J|>=3, the smallest node
of J has a denominator of magnitude at least two. For |J|=2 both coefficients
are integral exactly at adjacent integer nodes. For |J|=1 the sole coefficient
is one. Multiplication by a base unit preserves integrality in both directions.
Thus at most 2m-1 of the proposed subset products have an orbit in the module.
Their valuation masks, considered in the ambient fields, sum the m seed masks.
The larger power-basis lattice used in S2 has determinant ratio (over k,
as an integral change of basis) of magnitude prod_{j<l}|r_j-r_l|=12 on
the four test nodes. Even its E=2 box omits T(T-1)(T-2)=T^3-3T^2+2T.

2. C1. Fix a Pisot root s of the stated degree. Write s^h in its reduced
power basis. As h tends to infinity its n-1 small conjugates stay bounded,
yet its coefficients cannot all remain bounded, since its large conjugate
tends to infinity. Thus the displayed coefficient bound using only n-1
values fails even over Z. A general full-support pointwise norm bound also
fails under a unimodular rebase. Start with paired powers 1,s,...,s^(m-1),
and subtract all preceding generators from the last one. Their all-ones
sum is the original paired power s^(m-1), unit on each side. This point is
ternary and has invertible evaluation when b is invertible.

3. C3 and C4. The corrected C3 generators on each side are a common scalar
times 1,T,...,T^(m-1). Their common ideal on that side is principal, generated
by the scalar, because 1 belongs to the power basis. Cancelling the common
ideal cancels the scalar's whole norm from every product, and its local
absolute values cancel identically from the normalized threshold product.
For C4b at a fixed nonzero polynomial C of degree j and the displayed constant
perturbation g=Phi_15+(T-b)(1+sqrt(2)), Res(C,g) is of degree j in b:
its leading coefficient is the leading coefficient of C to the power 8
times (-(1+sqrt(2)))^j. This gives the side exponent for that fixed family.
The CM supplement checks irreducibility only. Its b=600 modulus fails even
the base map, as certified in review_checks.txt:9. For C4a, if the b_i are
distinct primes, the reduced denominators of the rational leading slopes
a_i/b_i are those primes. Clearing denominators of any exact cancellation
forces b_i to divide each participating coefficient in O_k. Nonzero ternary
coefficients in an integral basis cannot meet this condition.

4. Tree-blindness. For Q(u,v)=13u^2+4uv+45v^2, N=581=7*83. The primes 3,5
are good and 5 is a simple slope root mod 15. With K=4,B=5,d_k=3 the
extension has d=15<=K^2. The only nonzero vectors in the symmetric box
are (0,+3),(0,-3). More generally, a slope r=t modulo 5t with t>K
coprime to 5 forces u=0 and 5|v; one can take 3-power t between K and
K^2/B for K sufficiently larger than B and impose the simple root by CRT.
At any fixed index d slope, ambient primitive density per unit area is
(1/zeta(2))/d times prod_{p|d}(1+1/p)^(-1), by prime-wise densities.
On p^a||d, primitivity in the slope lattice means p does not divide v.
