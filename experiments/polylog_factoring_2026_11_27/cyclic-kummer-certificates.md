# Public splitting constraints of arbitrary prime order

Given an odd semiprime $N=pq$ and an odd prime $\ell$ coprime to $N$, one can construct a cyclic degree-$\ell$ extension of $\mathbf Q$ in which **both** $p$ and $q$ split completely. The work is polynomial in $\ell$ and $\log N$. Randomizing the construction gives independent constraints which eliminate all other primes in a bounded interval with high probability. A candidate prime can be tested without factoring an auxiliary norm. Finding the accepted primes without scanning the interval remains open. The distinction is important because a single gcd already checks any proposed divisor. The additional structure here is that the constraints are genuine cyclic splitting characters. ## A field with a certified degree

Choose a prime $\lambda=1\bmod\ell$, $\lambda\nmid N$, and an element $w$ of order $\ell$ modulo $\lambda^2$ whose reduction modulo $\lambda$ has the same order. One may search for $\lambda$ in the stated progression: Linnik's theorem puts the first such prime at $\ell^{O(1)}$, and finding a prime divisor of $N$ during that search already gives a split on the semiprime promise. Raise a random unit modulo $\lambda$ to $(\lambda-1)/\ell$, retry if 1, and raise the result to $\lambda$ modulo $\lambda^2$ to lift it. A common factor with $\ell$ is likewise found by a gcd before construction. [Xylouris, least prime in a progression](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/83078)

Write $E=\mathbf Q(\zeta_\ell)$, $M=\ell^3N$. Take the centered residue $r=w+M^{-1}(1-\lambda)\bmod\lambda^2$ and put
$$
 \beta_0=1+M(\zeta_\ell-r),\qquad
 \alpha=\prod_{a=1}^{\ell-1}\sigma_a(\beta)^{[a^{-1}]_\ell},
 \qquad \sigma_a(\zeta_\ell)=\zeta_\ell^a ,
$$
where $\beta=\beta_0$ or any $\beta_0+M\lambda^2 z$ with $z\in\mathbf Z[\zeta_\ell]$. Exponents use the least positive residues. At the prime of $E$ corresponding to $\zeta_\ell\mapsto w\bmod\lambda^2$, $\beta=\lambda\bmod\lambda^2$. At the other primes over $\lambda$ it is a unit since the distinct conjugate roots have different residues. The valuation of $\alpha$ at the chosen prime is therefore 1. This certifies that it is not an $\ell$th power.

Let $L=E(\sqrt[\ell]{\alpha})$. Reindexing the product gives $\sigma_h(\alpha)=\alpha^h\bmod E^{\times\ell}$, so $L/\mathbf Q$ is Galois. The kernel $\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)=C_\ell$ is central: in the Kummer pairing the actions on the radical class and on $\mu_\ell$ both multiply by $h$. A group generated by a central kernel and a lift of its cyclic quotient is abelian. Here the two orders are coprime, giving $C_\ell\times C_{\ell-1}$. Let $K$ be its unique degree-$\ell$ subfield. For $s=p,q$, $\beta=1\bmod s$, hence $\alpha=1$ at every residue field above $s$. The unramified Frobenius at $s$ raised to $f=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(s)$ therefore acts trivially in $L/E$. Since $f$ is coprime to $\ell$, its projection to $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf Q)$ is trivial. The prescribed congruence modulo $\ell^3$ makes $\beta$ and $\alpha$ local $\ell$th powers at $\ell$ by Hensel's lemma. Thus $K$ is unramified at $\ell$. The formula specifies the field by a cyclotomic base and one radical; its degree and coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in $\ell,\log N,\log\lambda$ and the bit length of $z$. [Milne, *Class Field Theory*, Kummer theory](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf)

## A factored example and the conductor cost

For $\beta=\beta_0$, its norm is the explicit integer
$$
 V=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(Mr-1)^j M^{\ell-1-j}.
$$
No prime of $M$ divides $V$. For each prime $t\mid V$, the residue $w_t=r-M^{-1}\bmod t$ has exact order $\ell$. Indeed $\Phi_\ell(w_t)=0$, and $t\ne\ell$. Thus $t$ splits in $E$, with exactly one prime above it dividing the linear element $\beta_0$. Its valuation there is $e_t=v_t(V)$. The Kummer extension ramifies tamely over $t$ exactly when $\ell\nmid e_t$; a unit Kummer radicand at a prime away from $\ell$ only gives an unramified extension. Consequently the conductor of $K$ is $\prod_{t\mid V,\ \ell\nmid e_t}t$. Knowing $V$ and the construction is cheaper than supplying its prime factors. In the squarefree case the conductor equals $V\ge M^{\ell-1}/2$, by writing $V=M^{\ell-1}\Phi_\ell(r-1/M)$ and using $(y^\ell+1)/(y+1)\ge1/2$ for $y\ge0$.

When the factors are given, set $s^{(t-1)/\ell}=w_t^{d_t(s)}\bmod t$ for a unit $s$ at $V$. The character exponent is $\sum_t e_t d_t(s)\bmod\ell$. This is the $\ell$th power residue symbol $(s/\beta_0)_\ell$. Eisenstein reciprocity identifies it with $(\beta_0/s)_\ell$ for a rational prime $s$ coprime to $\ell V$ since $\beta_0=1\bmod\ell^3$. More explicitly, at any prime $\mathfrak p$ of $E$ over $s$, reindexing the conjugates in $\alpha$ gives $(\alpha/\mathfrak p)_\ell=(\beta_0/s)_\ell^{f}$: each prime over $s$ occurs $f$ times and the exponent $[a^{-1}]_\ell$ cancels $\sigma_a$ on roots of unity. Its kernel is the complete-splitting condition in $K$. [Lemmermeyer, *Reciprocity Laws*, Eisenstein reciprocity](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-12893-0)

For $N=91,\ell=5,\lambda=11,w=9$, the formula gives $M=11375,r=-1$ and conductor
$$
16{,}744{,}877{,}992{,}206{,}001=11\cdot61\cdot24{,}955{,}108{,}781{,}231 .
$$
The local exponent vectors at $7$ and $13$ are $(4,2,4)$ and $(2,0,3)$, each summing to zero modulo 5. Neither prime is a fifth power modulo the deliberately forced ramified prime 11. The extra components carry essential information. The full kernel allows $2{,}994{,}613{,}053{,}747{,}600$ unit residues. For 67 independent test primes the factored character agreed with direct Kummer powering in $\mathbf F_s[Z]/(\Phi_5(Z))$. This example supplies the norm factors by a checked test fixture.

## Random certificates and the actual candidate count

Let $X$ be a bound on candidate primes, and draw $z=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-2}u_i\zeta_\ell^i$ with independent uniform $0\le u_i<H$. For a fixed prime $s\le X$ outside $\ell\lambda N$, $\beta=\beta_0+M\lambda^2 z$ modulo $s$ is within total variation $(\ell-1)s/H$ of uniform on $\mathbf Z[\zeta_\ell]/s$. This follows from the discrepancy of reducing a uniform interval in each coefficient and the unit multiplier $M\lambda^2$. No prime-distribution assumption enters this estimate.

On this finite ring, reject nonunits. On the unit group, the map $\beta\mapsto(\beta/s)_\ell$ is a surjective character: the ring is a product of finite fields containing $\mu_\ell$, and one component can realize any residue symbol while the others equal 1. Therefore a false candidate passes one constraint with probability at most $1/\ell+(\ell-1)X/H$. Every actual prime factor passes every constraint. Membership at a candidate prime needs only arithmetic modulo that candidate: in $\mathbf F_s[Z]/(\Phi_\ell)$ check $\alpha^{(s^f-1)/\ell}=1$, with $f=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(s)$. This equality rejects nonunits too. For randomized $\beta$ a nonunit can occur even at a prime unramified in $K$; the test then uses the additional valid condition $s\nmid\operatorname{Norm}\beta$.

With $H\ge\ell(\ell-1)X$, one false prime survives $k$ independently drawn fields with probability at most $(2/\ell)^k$. A union bound over at most $X$ candidates gives failure probability at most $X(2/\ell)^k$ for the whole interval, with no independence needed between primes. The exceptional candidate primes $\ell,\lambda$ are settled by public gcds. Thus $O(\log X)$ certificates of polynomial bit length suffice at fixed $\ell$ to isolate the prime divisors within $[2,X]$ with high probability. Their common solutions form an implicitly described set. This bound supplies no fast method to list them.

With $N=91$, six seeded degree-five certificates and $H=10000$, the surviving prime counts through 100 were $23,8,3,2,2,2,2$, including the initial set after eliminating 5 and 11. The final set was $\{7,13\}$. Exhaustive local checks also gave equal counts for all character values at $(\ell,s)=(3,2),(3,7),(5,3)$, respectively 1, 12, and 16 units per value. The factorization of 91 enters only in checking the reported output. [Executable checks](check_cyclic_kummer_certificate.py)

A quadratic analogue takes independent $D_i=1+Nu_i$ and requires $(D_i/s)=1$ for a candidate odd prime $s$. Input primes always pass, while a uniform residue at another prime passes with probability at most $1/2$. For balanced semiprimes the direct Jacobi sieve extends to all nonsquare integer candidates. The cyclotomic construction gives the higher-order prime test, with arithmetic certification even at primes which are not $1\bmod\ell$. Joint inversion or small searchable conductors would connect these conditions to factor recovery.
