# How many bits of a second fixed multiplier suffice?

Let $P,Q$ be independent uniform odd residues modulo $2^k$. Observe $PQ\bmod 2^k$, the whole AND $A=P\mathbin{\&}Q$, and designated bits of $B=(hP)\mathbin{\&}(hQ)$. Read the periodic patterns below from low to high, taking a bit of $B$ at each 1. Either pattern gives a **tight terminal list** of compatible ordered endpoints. The maximum prefix list is polynomial in $k$ with probability tending to one. A fixed shift of the pattern or omission of any constant number of bottom positions preserves both conclusions.

| Multiplier $h$ | Repeated selection pattern | Fraction of second stream supplied | Necessary fraction for subexponential typical lists |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 3 | 011 | $2/3$ | at least $0.273632\ldots$ |
| 5 | 01011 | $3/5$ | at least $0.257466\ldots$ |

The last column applies to any *predetermined* positions at that multiplier. The interval between the sufficient and necessary densities is open. For distinct balanced primes sampled from $[R,2R]$, quarter-depth lists have the same high-probability upper bounds. Once the readings are supplied, the existing affine divisor method then completes each residue in polynomial bit time. The selected readings have explicit central-binomial indices. The cost of evaluating them from $N$ remains open. [Full second-stream theorem and binomial indices](two-fixed-and-streams.md) · [Exact certificate checker](check_sparse_fixed_multipliers.py)

## An integer certificate for each periodic selection

For four input digits and their four multiplication carries, let $T_0$ be the integer transition matrix that keeps a digit tuple when the unscaled pairwise ANDs agree. Let $T_1$ additionally require the two scaled ANDs to agree, as in the full-stream carry calculation. With period $p_0,\ldots,p_{r-1}$ put $W=T_{p_0}\cdots T_{p_{r-1}}$ in chronological order. The vector $v=W\mathbf1$ is strictly positive. Direct integer arithmetic gives the following maxima over all $h^4$ carry states.

| $h$ | Carry states | $R_h=\max_s(Wv)_s/v_s$ | $8^r$ | $\lambda_h=R_h^{1/r}$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 81 | $64591/127$ | 512 | $\approx7.982203$ |
| 5 | 625 | $286956018/9253$ | 32768 | $\approx7.912369$ |

The checker independently verifies *every* component of $v$ by grouping actual word pairs by their two observed ANDs. Since $W$ is nonnegative and $Wv\le R_hv$, it follows that $W^a\mathbf1\le R_h^a v/\min v$. Any interval of $b$ successive positions consists of whole periods and fewer than $2r$ edge bits. The row sums of either one-bit matrix are at most ten. Thus all compatible word quadruple counts, for any interval and any starting carry state, are at most $C_h\lambda_h^b$, where $\lambda_h<8$.

For completeness, consider an alternative endpoint that first differs at bit $d$ from the actual first endpoint. The product fixes its partner, which also first differs there. On the next $b=\min(d,k-d)$ positions their two difference words obey $\Delta v=c\Delta u\bmod 2^b$. The slope $c=-Q/P\bmod 2^b$ is uniform on odd residues and independent of the true upper blocks under the stated random-input law. The first differences are odd, so a particular quadruple specifies exactly one possible slope. Union over at most $h^2$ common incoming carry pairs to allow their dependence on the slope. The probability of any such alternative is at most $2h^2C_h(\lambda_h/8)^b$, since the true block pair has probability $4^{-b}$ and the prescribed slope has probability $2^{1-b}$. This bounds existence of a matching block and hence of a complete alternative.

Summing over $t\le d\le k-t$ gives $O_h((\lambda_h/8)^t)$. Each observation has at most $4^t$ endpoints without any alternative starting in these middle positions. Indeed for each low $t$-bit residue of the first endpoint all such good endpoints agree through the middle, and there are at most $2^t$ possible final suffixes. The input is uniform given its observation. In any fiber of size greater than $2\cdot4^t$, at least half the endpoints have a middle alternative. The probability of such a fiber is therefore $O_h((\lambda_h/8)^t)$. Choosing $t$ constant gives tightness. Choosing $t$ a sufficiently large multiple of $\log k$ and taking a union bound over all prefix lengths gives the high-probability polynomial bound. These rates are asymptotic and the certificates do not give a small practical polynomial degree. The proof averages over the product residue and takes all calculations modulo the stated power of two. ## A necessary density for the extra readings

Let $w=k-1$ and take a predetermined set $J$ of $s$ nonunit positions. Write $H=H_2(1/4)$ for binary entropy and $\gamma=1-H$. For any fixed odd $h\ge3$, put
$$
z=\left(\frac{h+1}{2h}\right)^2,\qquad H_c=\tfrac14H_2(z)+\tfrac34 H_2((1-z)/3).
$$
For any fixed $\eta>0$, with probability tending to one the compatible list has at least $2^{\gamma w-sH_c-\eta w}$ endpoints. In particular a fixed asymptotic density below $\gamma/H_c$ leaves exponentially many candidates on typical inputs. The binary-count argument using only that each new bit is Bernoulli $1/4$ gives the weaker universal threshold $\gamma/H\approx0.232623$. The refined calculation accounts for its dependence on the unscaled bit at the same position. [Product-and-AND entropy](shared-and-fiber-entropy.md)

At stationarity the multiplication carry for one factor is uniform on $\{0,\ldots,h-1\}$ and independent of the next input bit. Uniformity is invariant because the values $c+h x$, for these $h$ carries and $x=0,1$, cover $0,\ldots,2h-1$ exactly once. Both input factors use independent copies. Thus $A_j$ and $B_j$ separately have probability $1/4$ of being one. Given $A_j=1$, both scaled output bits are one exactly when both carries are even, which has probability $z$. Given $A_j=0$, the scaled AND is one with probability $(1-z)/3$. This gives the conditional entropy $H_c$. The joint probability of two ones is exactly $1/9$ at $h=3$ and $9/100$ at $h=5$.

The empirical four outcome frequencies on $J$ concentrate about this stationary law even for irregular predetermined sets of positive density. After $l$ fresh uniform digits, the carry from any initial $c$ is $\lfloor(c+hU)/2^l\rfloor$ for uniform $U\in[0,2^l)\cap\mathbf Z$. Counting integers in each of the $h$ intervals puts this law within $O_h(2^{-l})$ total variation of uniform. The same bound holds for the two independent carries from any initial pair. Consequently each outcome indicator at index $j$ has the stationary mean up to $O_h(2^{-j})$ after the forced odd bit, and two indicators a distance $l$ apart have covariance $O_h(2^{-l})$. This follows by conditioning on the carries and digits just after the earlier bit. Each of the four counts on $J$ therefore has variance $O_h(s)$, so its proportion converges in probability when $s\to\infty$. On the unselected positions the unscaled AND is iid Bernoulli $1/4$.

The number of typical AND transcripts is thus at most $2^{(w-s)H+s(H+H_c)+o(w)}$ by counting strings with specified empirical frequencies. Sets of $o(w)$ selected or unselected positions can be charged directly at $o(w)$ bits. There are at most $2^w$ choices of the odd product residue and $2^{2w}$ equiprobable input pairs. Multiplying the count of typical observations by the proposed small-list threshold and dividing by $2^{2w}$ proves the lower bound. The prime-sample comparison cited above transfers the resulting probability to dyadic periods at most $R^{1-\delta}$ for fixed $\delta>0$. The selected positions are fixed in advance. Adaptive choices can change the conditional bit frequencies.

The whole unscaled word is known, so the same count can also condition on its preceding bits. Fix a context length $l$ and put $H_l=H(B_j\mid A_{j-l+1},\ldots,A_j)$ in the stationary law. For any fixed $l$ the list bound improves to $2^{\gamma w-sH_l-\eta w}$ with probability tending to one. To see this, first choose a typical full unscaled word in at most $2^{wH+o(w)}$ ways. For this word the positions in $J$ are partitioned by their length-$l$ suffix. Within each class the number of scaled bit strings giving its observed one frequency is bounded by the usual binomial entropy bound. The joint frequencies of suffix and label converge to their stationary values by the same covariance argument. Overlapping contexts contribute only $O_l(s)$ extra to the variance, and the first $l$ positions cost $O(l)$ bits. Multiplying the conditional type counts over the $2^l$ fixed classes gives $2^{sH_l+o(w)}$ choices of the additional readings.

For $l=8$ the checker starts from every pair of carries and counts all $4^8$ pairs of eight fresh input bits. Each context word $a$ must occur exactly $h^2 3^{8-\operatorname{popcount}(a)}$ times. These integer counts define $H_8$ exactly via binary entropy. They give $H_8\approx0.68969044$ at $h=3$ and $H_8\approx0.73299466$ at $h=5$. The ratios $\gamma/H_8$ give the necessary densities in the opening table. Even the single-position conditioning already gives $0.246297\ldots$ and $0.236931\ldots$, respectively.
