# One cyclic class subgroup suffices for the fixed disk queries

For each odd modulus, all forms in the common-cutoff reduction belong to one explicit cyclic subgroup of the proper form class group. The generator and the exponent of each queried class can be computed in polynomial bit time. The subgroup has known power-of-two order. This removes arbitrary class selection from the oracle target. The unresolved step is evaluating a signed disk count modulo 4 at a supplied power. [Fixed-discriminant reduction](fixed-discriminant-split.md)

## Ideal coordinates

Take the same power of two $L$ and even integer $D>1$ as in the disk construction. Put $\omega=\sqrt{-D}$, $O=\mathbb Z[\omega]$ and $O_L=\mathbb Z+L O$, of discriminant $-4L^2D$. In $O/LO$, every $1+t\omega$ is a unit because its norm $1+Dt^2$ is odd. For $t\bmod L$ consider the fractional $O_L$-ideal
$$I_t=\mathbb Z(1+t\omega)+L O.$$
These lattices multiply by the rule
$$I_s I_t=I_{(s+t)/(1-Dst)\bmod L}.$$
Indeed, the product contains $L^2 O$ and $L(1+s\omega)O$. Together these contain $LO$ by invertibility modulo $L$. The residue line of the product is generated by $(1+s\omega)(1+t\omega)$. Its real coordinate $1-Dst$ is odd and can be normalized to 1. In particular $I_tI_{-t}=O_L$, establishing invertibility. The classes are distinct. Each $I_t$ extends to the unit ideal in $O$, so a scalar identifying two classes must be a unit of $O$. Those units are $\pm1$, both preserving every residue line.

The ideal $I_{j^{-1}}$ has oriented basis $L,j+\omega$ for every odd $j$, because the reduction line is generated by $j+\omega$ and the lattice has index $L$ in $O$. The norm form in that basis is $(Lx+jy)^2+D y^2$. No normalization factor is needed because the lattice and $O_L$ have the same covolume. This identifies the class of each queried form by the usual norm-form correspondence. Reversing the global correspondence convention inverts both the forms and the generator, leaving the exponent relation intact.

## Generator and exponent recovery

In the parameter law, doubling $t$ gives $2t/(1-Dt^2)$. Multiplication by the odd denominator inverse preserves the 2-adic valuation. Starting at $t=1$, repeated doubling therefore yields a parameter of valuation exactly $b$ for the $2^b$-th power until it becomes zero modulo $L$. Since the subgroup has $L$ elements, $I_1$ generates it. The public generator form is $(Lx+y)^2+D y^2$.

To recover an exponent of a target parameter, start at the identity and match the parameter modulo successive powers of two. At bit $b$, composing with the $2^b$-th power toggles precisely bit $b$ modulo $2^{b+1}$. This follows directly from the parameter law and the odd denominator, as the change is $u(1+Dt^2)/(1-Dtu)$ for a parameter $u$ of valuation $b$. Each queried $j$ is odd, so its exponent is odd. Computing all necessary powers and lifting the exponent takes $O(\log L)$ modular operations on $O(\log N)$-bit inputs.

Signed disk counts are invariant under proper change of basis. Consequently the previous reduction can ask for the count at a supplied power of a single public generator, using fixed discriminant and cutoff. The group law here gives the queried class and does not supply its integers of norm divisible by $N$ inside the disk.

## Checks

The check script verified 5,456 ideal lattice products by their exact generator determinants. On 6,478 disk queries it recovered the exponent and independently checked it by powering $1+\omega$ in the quadratic ring modulo $L$. Run `python cyclic_class_queries.py`.
