# Square-root size weighting over every integer modulus

Given positive integers $N$ and $d$, draw a subgroup $H$ of $(\mathbb Z/N)^d$ with probability proportional to $\sqrt{|H|}$. The result is a column Hermite basis for its inverse image in $\mathbb Z^d$. The general sampler has polynomial **expected** bit cost, using fast polynomial multiplication for interval products. Its rare branches can search a long interval for factors. The balanced-semiprime construction gives a different guarantee for the same distribution in odd dimension, with at most 96 polynomial-time Coppersmith windows. ## Selecting unknown prime divisors with probability of order $1/\sqrt p$

The earlier even-dimensional uniform sampler implements independent prime events of order $1/p$ with random residue gcds. Here set $h_p=\min(1,C/\lceil\sqrt p\rceil)$ for a positive integer $C$. We need a list of independently selected prime divisors with these probabilities, without supplying the factorization. [Earlier construction](sublattice-sampling.md) · [New prime selector](sqrt_prime_marks.py)

Partition the possible primes into dyadic bands $[P,2P)$, with $P=2^j$ and $B=\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor$. At most $\lfloor B/j\rfloor$ distinct prime divisors of $N$ lie in a band, since their product divides $N$. Open the band with public probability $\min(1,C\lfloor B/j\rfloor/\lceil\sqrt P\rceil)$. The union bound shows that this covers the probability of at least one mark. On a closed band, output nothing.

On an open band, find the prime divisors in it. Compute the products in consecutive blocks of length $O(\sqrt P)$ using the Pollard–Strassen product polynomial and fast multipoint evaluation modulo the remaining cofactor. Scanning the block products and resolving the first nonunit gives the least prime divisor up to the cutoff, since the first integer at least two with a nontrivial gcd is prime. Remove its full power and repeat. There are at most $B$ removals. This method also handles a block product divisible by the entire modulus and tests an integer endpoint when it might itself be prime. With fast multiplication the band cost is $\sqrt P\,\mathrm{poly}(B)$ bit operations. Weighting by its opening probability and summing over at most $B$ bands gives polynomial expected work. This estimate charges the factor searches in full. [Pollard–Strassen implementation](../../src/trackC/baselines.py) · [Costa and Harvey on the product-polynomial approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.2116)

The implementation uses FLINT or packed decimal multiplication at bounded sizes. Its general fallback uses a radix-two transform with dyadic fixed-point arithmetic. For padded convolution length $L$ and coefficient height $M$, it uses $O(\log L+\log M)$ bits of precision. An explicit error bound below one half makes the final integer rounding exact. The $O(L\log L)$ arithmetic operations then give the required bound even beyond native transform limits. The optional plain Karatsuba backend supplies an exact cross-check. [Precision bound and implementation](sqrt_prime_marks.py) · [Convolution and prime-search checks](check_sqrt_prime_marks.py)

Once the primes are known, let $a$ be the band opening probability. Give each nonempty subset its independent-product mass divided by $a$, and give the remainder to the empty set. To avoid enumerating subsets, assign each possible first mark its mass $h_i\prod_{j<i}(1-h_j)/a$ and sample subsequent marks independently at their usual rates. Averaging over the band coin recovers the full product law. Independent coins in separate bands preserve independence across all primes, including when several lie in the same band. [Prime-selection checks](check_sqrt_prime_marks.py)

## The local law and exact correction

At $p^e\parallel N$, represent the subgroup type by $d\ge r_1\ge\cdots\ge r_e\ge0$. The $j$th coordinate counts cyclic factors of order at least $p^j$, so the subgroup order is $p^{\sum r_j}$. Multiply the classical Birkhoff count for this type by $p^{\sum r_j/2}$. Write $r_0=d$, $r_{e+1}=0$ and $C_t=\prod_{i=1}^t(1-p^{-i})$. Its weight is
$$
V(\mathbf r)=p^{\sum_j [r_j(d-r_j)+r_j/2]}
\frac{C_d}{\prod_{j=0}^{e} C_{r_j-r_{j+1}}}.
$$
The CRT factorization of the subgroup order makes the target types independent. [Unweighted counts and references](sublattice-sampling.md)

Use the constant type $r_j=m=\lceil d/2\rceil$ as the default. Moving one coordinate one step toward $m$, with its position chosen to preserve monotonicity, raises the displayed exponent by at least $1/2$. If the shrinking adjacent gap was $g\ge1$ and the growing gap $u\ge0$, the ratio of the remaining factors is $(1-p^{-g})/(1-p^{-(u+1)})\ge1-1/p$. A type at distance $T=\sum_j|r_j-m|$ therefore has weight relative to the default at most $[\sqrt p(1-1/p)]^{-T}$. Its deviations form two signed partitions. There are at most $4^T$ choices for $T\ge1$, regardless of $d,e$. When $z=4/[\sqrt p(1-1/p)]<1$, the total ratio of exceptional weights to the default is at most $z/(1-z)$, and the exceptional probability is at most $z$.

For $C=8$ this probability is at most $h_p$ at every prime. If $\lceil\sqrt p\rceil\le8$ the mark is certain. For $p>64$, $4\lceil\sqrt p\rceil\le8\sqrt p(1-1/p)$ and $z<1$. The argument only uses the default as a reference weight at small primes, where other types can be more likely.

For a marked prime, extract $p^e$ by exact division and compute its true exceptional probability $\delta_p$ by the local type recursion. Use an exceptional type with probability $\delta_p/h_p$, drawing it with its conditional weights. All unmarked components use the default. The recursion uses $O(ed^2)$ transitions and tracks coefficients in the basis $1,\sqrt p$. Signs of two-term expressions can be tested by integer comparisons and squaring when the terms have opposite signs. Refining a uniform random binary fraction against the cumulative weights samples the branches exactly. The coefficients have polynomial bit length in $d,\log N$. A single uniform basis change acts transitively on subgroups of each type. Lifting to integer Hermite form completes the draw. [Implementation](weighted_sublattice_general.py)

This construction makes independent local rank deviations of order $1/\sqrt p$ accessible at every modulus. It does so by paying for the occasional factor search. At a large prime, the rarer occurrence and the larger cost compensate in expectation. In particular the expected cost per sample and the expected cost to obtain a divisor from the samples are different quantities.
