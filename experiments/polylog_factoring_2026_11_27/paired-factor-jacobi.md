# A common Jacobi test on a candidate and its floor quotient

For a balanced semiprime $N=pq$ with distinct odd primes $p<q<2p$, one random denominator can test **two independent quadratic signs** at a false candidate. On a fixed 32-bit input the true smaller factor was the only survivor after five rows. Testing the smaller coordinate alone on the same candidates and random rows required ten. Both explicit scans still visit an interval of order $\sqrt N$. ## The independence comes from the exact floor quotient

Visit odd $x$ in $[\lceil\sqrt{N/2}\rceil,\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor]$ and set $y=\lfloor N/x\rfloor$. Remove cases where $y$ is even, either coordinate is square, or $xy$ is square. For the last test it suffices to compare $xy$ to $\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor^2$. In fact $N-\sqrt N<xy\le N$ and no other square can be in that range. The true pair survives.

For any remaining false pair, $M=\operatorname{lcm}(x,y)$ is coprime to $N$. Both coordinates are below $2p$, so any nontrivial shared factor would make one coordinate $p$ or $q$. If $y=q$ then $x=p$, because $x<p$ gives $N/x\ge N/(p-1)>q+1$, while $x>p$ gives $N/x<q$. If $y=p$ then $x\le y=p$, which would give $y\ge q$. The other possibilities follow directly from the smaller-factor interval.

Let $D=1+4tN$ and require $(D/x)=(D/y)=+1$. On units modulo $M$ the individual characters and their product are nonprincipal, since $x,y,xy$ are all nonsquares. An odd prime occurring to odd order in any of the three denominators supplies a nontrivial local character. Thus the sign map onto $\{\pm1\}^2$ is surjective even when $x,y$ share factors. As $t$ varies over a full period modulo $M$, $D$ covers every residue. Exactly $\varphi(M)/4$ values pass both tests. Both true factors always pass.

Take $T$ independent offsets uniformly in $[0,N(T+1))$. A partial period adds at most one complete period's worth of hits, so the one-row probability is at most $(1+1/(T+1))/4$. A union bound gives failure probability at most $e\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor/4^T$. Consequently $T=\lceil(\operatorname{bitlength}(\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor)+f+2)/2\rceil$ rows suffice for error $2^{-f}$. These moduli have $O(\log N+\log(T+1))$ bits. Filtering $xy$ is essential for this uniform two-character estimate because equal squarefree kernels would otherwise leave only one independent sign.

The checker counted all four sign patterns on 132 complete false-pair periods, including 33 pairs with a nontrivial coordinate gcd. At $N=2{,}989{,}601{,}243$ the initial filters gave 4,040 pairs. The first five rows left 744, 165, 44, 8 and 1. The remaining pair was $(42{,}697,70{,}019)$.

## Exact limits of block products and histograms

For the same input choose $t=3,6,18$. Consider the six-integer blocks $[42{,}697,42{,}702]$ and $[39{,}308,39{,}313]$. Each has two eligible floor-quotient pairs. On **every** row and in **both** blocks the exact histogram is one sign pair $(+1,+1)$ and one $(+1,-1)$. The first block has one common survivor, the smaller factor itself. The second has none. Thus even joint signs of both coordinates counted exactly on every individual row lose the required alignment across rows. Products of all coordinate values followed by Jacobi evaluation give only their sign products, which are already determined by these histograms. A multirow correlation can distinguish the two blocks.

A direct reference method computes $\gcd(N,\prod_{x\in I,\,x\ {\rm odd}}x)$ for the smaller-factor block $I$. In this interval its value is exactly $p$ if $p\in I$ and 1 otherwise. Arithmetic-progression product and remainder trees take $\widetilde O(1+\sqrt{|I|})$ modular operations. On a block $I=[A,B]$ with few distinct $x+\lfloor N/x\rfloor$, testing every even sum between $B+\lfloor N/B\rfloor$ and $A+\lfloor N/A\rfloor$ by a square discriminant and an interval check can also be cheaper. This follows from monotonicity of $x+N/x$ below $\sqrt N$. The paired sieve has no demonstrated improvement on these block algorithms. ## A constructive obstruction to single-coordinate block rejection

Suppose $T$ arbitrary denominators $D_i=1\bmod4$ of magnitude $N^{O(1)}$ are prescribed, possibly for a particular block. Set $Q=2\prod D_i$. For each odd $Q$-unit $u$ record the $T$ signs $(u/D_i)$. If $u,v>1$ have the same signs and distinct squarefree kernels, then $uv$ is odd, nonsquare, composite, and passes every row. On the fixture the code finds $40{,}795=199\cdot205$ from the first three rows. This witness pertains to the original one-coordinate integer sieve. A primality requirement or a separate test on the floor quotient changes it.

More generally, take $A\asymp\sqrt N$, $V=\sqrt A$, and let $J$ denote a bound on gaps between consecutive $Q$-units. In the seed interval $[\lceil V\rceil,\lceil V\rceil+L]$, the number of integers with a squarefree kernel occurring more than once is $O(L^2/V)$. Indeed two values $du^2<dv^2$ with kernel $d$ differ by at least $2\sqrt{dV}$, so repetition requires $d\le L^2/(4V)$. Summing $1+L/(2\sqrt{dV})$ over these $d$ gives the bound. Take $L=4\cdot2^T J$ and suppose $2^T J^2=o(V)$. More than $2^T$ units then remain after excluding all repeated kernels. Two share their entire sign vector. Their product lies between $A$ and $A+O(2^T J\sqrt A)$.

Iwaniec's Jacobsthal bound gives $J\ll(1+\omega(Q))^2\log^2(2+\omega(Q))$. For $T=O(\log N)$ this is polynomial in $\log N$. Therefore for $2^T\le N^{1/4-\epsilon}$ every balanced block of length at least $2^T N^{1/4}\operatorname{polylog}N$ contains a composite nonsquare survivor of those one-coordinate rows. At fixed $T$ the witness search itself uses only polynomially many seed tests up to the guaranteed first match. The result imposes no comparable obstruction on arbitrary joint or prime-only block tests. [Iwaniec, *On the problem of Jacobsthal*](https://doi.org/10.1515/dema-1978-0121)
