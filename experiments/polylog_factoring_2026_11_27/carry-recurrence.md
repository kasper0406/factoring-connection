# Recovering a multiplication from recurrent carry sequences

Let $N=pq$ have odd factors $p\le q\le2p$ and let $n$ be its bit length. Suppose that at some unknown cut, the carry sequence follows a rational linear recurrence when read inward from the low end and another when read inward from the high end. If both orders are at most $k$, we can deterministically factor $N$ in $2^{O(k)}\operatorname{poly}(n)$ bit operations. Thus $k=O(\log n)$ suffices for polynomial time. Only $N$ and the order bound are given. The case of zero carries and the idea of correcting digit polynomials are older. The parameter here measures how many consecutive end values determine the rest of each segment. [Landau and Immerman](https://web.cs.umass.edu/publication/docs/1992/UM-CS-1992-066.pdf) · [Earlier repository carry search](https://github.com/kasper0406/factoring-connection/blob/c6de6b5/notes/codex_r2_generic_ring.md#L398-L425) · [Reader](carry_recurrence.py)

Write $U_z(X)=\sum_j z_jX^j$ for the binary digits of $z$, and $a_j=[X^j]U_pU_q$. Incoming carries satisfy $c_0=c_n=0$ and
$$
 a_j=N_j+2c_{j+1}-c_j,\qquad 0\le j<n .
$$
The word being split is $(c_1,\ldots,c_{n-1})$. A sequence has order at most $k$ if some monic linear recurrence over $\mathbb Q$ of order at most $k$ holds after its initial terms. Either segment can be empty. A recurrence can have singular initial data or a zero constant coefficient.

## How to obtain the starting values

1. Put $s=\min(2k,n-1)$. Enumerate odd $u\bmod2^s$. Its possible partner has low bits $v=Nu^{-1}\bmod2^s$. Schoolbook multiplication of these bits computes the first $s$ carries exactly for the true candidate.
2. Try the factor bit lengths allowed by balance and the top $s$ bits of each factor, or its whole word if shorter. These determine the highest $s$ raw columns, since the degree sum is known for each pair of lengths. Starting with $c_n=0$, compute $c_j=N_j+2c_{j+1}-a_j$ downwards. The implementation narrows the partner prefixes by integer division of $N$ by the lower and upper endpoints of each first-factor prefix.
3. Each seed has only one possible continuation beyond $2k$ under the order bound. If two recurrences of order at most $k$ fit, extend each sequence by its own recurrence. The product of their monic annihilating polynomials annihilates their difference. Its first $2k$ terms vanish, so the difference vanishes identically. Berlekamp–Massey over $\mathbb Q$, or fraction-free linear algebra on the seed, therefore suffices. Keep shorter prefixes even when a seed has no long admissible continuation. Stop extrapolating when a predicted carry ceases to be integral or lies outside $[0,n]$.
4. For every cut and every compatible pair of prefixes, recover the full coefficient vector using the displayed equation. Factor the resulting polynomial over $\mathbb Z$, evaluate its irreducible factors at two and test the gcds with $N$. For the true reconstruction $U_pU_q$, some irreducible factor of $U_p$ has absolute evaluation greater than one and dividing $p$, since their evaluations multiply to $p$. Thus the gcd is proper. The same argument works for composite operands. For prime operands Cohn's theorem also gives irreducibility of their digit polynomials. [Brillhart, Filaseta and Odlyzko, Corollary 2](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1981-080-0)

There are $2^{O(k)}$ choices of end bits, $n$ cuts, and polynomial work for each trial. A recurrence of order at most $k$ obtained from the seed can be chosen with coefficient bit lengths $O(k\log(nk))$ by a minor bound. The extrapolation stops at the physical carry bound; rational evaluations and polynomial coefficients have polynomial bit length. The bit-complexity theorem uses polynomial-time integer-polynomial factorization. The executable uses the shared FLINT or SymPy reader. [Integer polynomial factorization, Lenstra–Lenstra–Lovász](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01457454) · [Bounded changes in carries and slopes](carry-polynomial.md)

## Size and checks of the promise

The promise is rare for random balanced primes. For fixed $n,k$, all possible exact low seeds over all inputs come from two $2k$-bit strings. At the high end two such strings, the top $2k$ digits of $N$, and the choice of degree sum give the seed. Thus the full family of feasible recurrent words across the entire size band has at most $n2^{O(k)}$ members, including every cut. For random independent odd $b$-bit operands and any fixed word, condition on their lower digits at free column $j=1,\ldots,b-2$. The next carry is $\lfloor(A+p_j+q_j)/2\rfloor$ with $A$ already fixed. Each of its two possible values has conditional probability at most $3/4$. Consequently that word occurs with probability at most $(3/4)^{b-2}$. Distinct-prime conditioning costs $O(b^2)$. For $k=O(\log n)$ the proportion meeting the promise is at most $N^{-\log_2(4/3)/2+o(1)}$ on this distribution. A wrong reconstruction can also factor $N$, so this count bounds the promise. [Carry prediction bounds](carry-prediction-bounds.md)

The small exhaustive check tried 159 input-and-order combinations and recovered a factor in all 62 qualifying cases. On the selected example $6{,}872{,}061{,}269=78{,}643\cdot87{,}383$, the uncorrected digit polynomial gave no factor. With $k=5$ the reader reconstructed the genuine convolution after 144 integer-polynomial factorizations. The true carries had sum 72. The factor inputs of this experiment were selected by scanning short periodic digit patterns.

A control illustrates why a verified factor does not by itself verify a guessed carry word. For $m$ odd, take $a=(5\cdot2^{m-1}+1)/3$ and $b=2^m-1$. The lower carries through $c_{m-1}$ are $\lfloor j/2\rfloor$ for $1\le j<m$. The remaining ones, in reverse order, are $1+\lfloor j/2\rfloor$ for $1\le j\le m$. This follows by convolving the alternating bits of $a$ with the run of $m$ ones and using $ab=(a-1)2^m+(2^m-a)$ for the output digits. Both sequences obey $s_j=s_{j-1}+s_{j-2}-s_{j-3}$ while the number of slope changes grows with $m$. At $m=31$ both operands are prime, but the reader returned a factor from its first, zero-carry trial. Indeed $U_{ab}=(1+X+\cdots+X^{m-1})[1+(X-1)U_{a-1}]$ for any $1<a\le2^m$ in this construction. The code records whether its winning polynomial equals the recovered factors' actual binary convolution. [Reproducible checks](check_carry_recurrence.py)

The remaining question is how to obtain a useful description of carries on inputs outside these promises. A parity template can sometimes recover a factor even when it differs at many positions from the true word. The relevant condition is equality of remainders modulo the hidden factor's binary polynomial. [Parity decoder](carry-parity.md) · [Counting actual remainder matches](carry-syndrome-windows.md)
