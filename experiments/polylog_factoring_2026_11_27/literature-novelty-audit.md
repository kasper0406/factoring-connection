# Which factoring results survive a literature comparison?

**28 November 2026.** The strongest candidate for a standalone result is the change in complexity of the same clipped count as the modulus varies. The two fixed binomial streams are a second candidate. The basic scaled quarter-bit reduction already appears in 1982. This review compared primary sources with the input, output, and cost in each claim. Priority and independent mathematical review remain open for both candidates.

## The clipped count changes difficulty with the modulus

The comparison counts solutions on a modular hyperbola in prescribed windows modulo $N^2$. It asks one bit about a signed difference of counts. Values at most $N/4$ and at least $3N/4$ must be distinguished. Either answer is allowed between them.

- **Two distinct odd primes.** On $N=pq$, the branch gives a deterministic polynomial-time equivalence with factoring. With $c=1$, $O(\log N)$ comparisons locate a mixed square root of 1. In the reverse direction the factors suffice to answer every promised comparison. The proof bounds the combined contribution from the other rows uniformly over the queries. Rubinstein already used the distribution of points on modular hyperbolas to factor in $O(N^{1/3+\epsilon})$ time, using two auxiliary moduli and a second-moment estimate. Shparlinski's single-rectangle estimate at modulus $N^2$ has error $N^{1+o(1)}$. Applied separately, that bound does not resolve our order-$N$ difference. [Branch proof](clipped-gap-kloosterman.md) · [Rubinstein, 2013](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/n12/n12.pdf) · [Shparlinski, Theorem 13](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.2879)
- **Many prime factors.** The branch reduces Subset Sum to two nonadaptive calls to that same comparison on a constructed odd modulus. It supplies the complete prime-power factorization, and both calls satisfy the promise. Manders–Adleman established NP-completeness for bounded quadratic feasibility. Jansen–Klein–Lassota further restrict the factorization of the modulus in a hard quadratic-congruence family. The additional step here is to make the actual signed counts cross the prescribed thresholds despite contributions from nonstationary rows. The hard family uses a growing number of distinct primes. [Branch proof](clipped-gap-subset-hardness.md) · [Manders–Adleman](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(78)90044-2) · [Jansen–Klein–Lassota, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.12928)

I found no exact antecedent for this pair of statements. A worthwhile next step for a paper is an independent check of the uniform cancellation bound and the promise on both Subset Sum queries. A factoring algorithm needs only enough comparisons along the two-prime search path, evaluated using the unfactored $N$.

## Two fixed binomial streams

For $h=1,3$, let $t=\lfloor hN/2^{j+1}\rfloor$. At the specified levels the yes/no answer to $N\mid\binom{2t}{t}$ gives bit $j$ of the bitwise AND of $hp$ and $hq$. Given both streams, the branch recovers a factor in deterministic polynomial time on a density-one set of pairs of distinct primes drawn from the same dyadic interval. Kummer's carry criterion is classical. Ford–Konyagin study central-binomial divisibility statistically. Heninger–Shacham and Maitra–Sarkar–Sen Gupta reconstruct keys from partially supplied bits. I found no match for these particular joint observations and the proved typical running time. [Branch theorem and input distribution](fixed-binomial-streams.md) · [Granville on Kummer's criterion](https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/granville.pdf) · [Ford–Konyagin](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10252034) · [Heninger–Shacham](https://www.iacr.org/archive/crypto2009/56770001/56770001.pdf) · [Maitra–Sarkar–Sen Gupta](https://souravsengupta.com/publications/2010_africacrypt.pdf)

The useful next result would produce the readings from $N$ at the needed bit levels. The existing decoder even permits a stated local error budget. A new prediction method can be tested against that budget before doing more reconstruction work. [Noisy decoding guarantee](noisy-fixed-streams.md)

## The scaled quarter bit has a direct precedent

Goldwasser, Micali and Tong's Example 4 gives an oracle $O_4(z)$ that reports whether the two smaller square roots of a unit square $z$ modulo $N=pq$ have different parity. They already factor in $O(\log N)$ calls. For each lower-half root $a$ of $c^2$, folding its double to $\min(2a,N-2a)$ gives a lower-half root of $4c^2$. This is a bijection on the two lower-half representatives, and the folded value is even exactly when $a<N/4$. Therefore

$$
\#\{1\le a\le\lfloor N/4\rfloor:a^2\equiv c^2\pmod N\}\bmod 2
= O_4(4c^2).
$$

Removing the publicly known contribution from $\pm c$ gives the branch's mixed-root bit. The identity concerns the known scales $c$ used here. The weak-prediction version also uses an established technique, recovery of a hidden multiplier from noisy interval observations by significant Fourier coefficients. Direct enumeration confirmed the displayed identity on all 9,428 unit scales for the 45 products of two distinct odd primes below 32. [Goldwasser–Micali–Tong, §3.2, Example 4](https://fog.misty.com/perry/Bleichenbacher/Goldwasser-1982-FOCS.pdf#page=5) · [Akavia–Goldwasser–Safra, §§5 and 7](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~safra/PapersAndTalks/hardcore.pdf) · [Branch reduction](quarter-fourier.md)

### A narrower question in the generalized Lehmer literature

Keep the square fixed at 1 and the window fixed at the first quarter. For odd $M\ge5$, let $I$ contain the multiples of four in $[1,M)$ coprime to $M$, and put $f(x)=16x^{-1}\bmod M$ using least positive representatives. Then $|I\cap f(I)|$ has the same parity as the first-quarter root count. Indeed, $x=4a$ and $f(x)=4b$ correspond exactly to $ab\equiv1\bmod M$ in the quarter square. Swapping $a,b$ pairs off every off-diagonal solution. Subtracting the root 1 gives the parity for the nontrivial roots. Direct enumeration checked every odd $M$ from 5 through 401. This specializes the branch's earlier inverse-pair identity. [Earlier swap argument](single-quadratic-count.md#swap-symmetry-gives-an-exact-interval-parity-target)

Can one compute this intersection **modulo two for one supplied composite** in polynomial bit time? Published work on the generalized D. H. Lehmer problem estimates related counts in residue classes, including for composite moduli. Its error bounds leave the parity undetermined. An exact congruence that also handles the multiplier $16c^2$ would give the established scaled factoring oracle directly, since the same swap pairs solutions of $ab\equiv c^2\bmod M$. For the fixed multiplier 16, one proposed reduction would query only $M=N\ell$ for a chosen auxiliary prime $\ell$. Its factoring implication still needs a checked proof. I found no earlier evaluator or factoring reduction for that restricted oracle. [Shparlinski on generalized Lehmer counts](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607414) · [Cochrane and coauthors on power maps between classes](https://www.math.ksu.edu/~cochrane/research/CJGKPart2SubRev.pdf)

## What to set aside

- **Cubic witnesses.** Loxton, Khoo, Bird and Seberry already use cubic-root types and a second root to split selected Eisenstein moduli. Dixon collects smooth relations to obtain a nontrivial square root, and Pollard uses cubic integers for inputs of special form. The branch's uniform Shanks construction reaches the Dixon-style $L_N[1/2,\sqrt2+o(1)]$ bound on balanced inputs. Lenstra–Pomerance achieved constant 1 in this exponent. Reopening this route would require a lower cost per independent relation that distinguishes the hidden primes, including the cost of rejected seeds. [Branch cost analysis](shanks-smooth-batch.md) · [Loxton et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00193566) · [Dixon](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~cs812-1/dixon.pdf) · [Pollard, *Factoring with cubic integers*](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/TOPICS/factoring/1993_Book_TheDevelopmentOfTheNumberField.pdf) · [Lenstra–Pomerance](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/paper85.pdf)
- **Divisor-prefix counts.** The November 28 revision of ePrint 2026/3020 directly addresses factoring in Proposition 2.6. It reports deterministic $2^{O(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ time and polynomial space for complete factorization of an $n$-bit integer under its cited tensor-contraction theorem. Rudich–Rousseau previously gave the prefix strategy, and Shunia's October 30 preprint states the same quantitative bound. Computing prefixes in polynomial time remains open. [Proposition 2.6](https://opencryptology.org/2026/3020.pdf#page=10) · [Rudich–Rousseau](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00600v1) · [Shunia, Theorem 1](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00711v1#page=1)
- **The Lean binomial analogy.** The Gaussian-binomial identity in the shorter RH proof counts subspaces and collapses a weighted sum to a product. To use it here one would need a space constructible from $N$ whose kernel dimension determines a needed factor observation. We found no such construction. [Lean source at the verified revision](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/Lib/General/Combinatorics.lean#L89) · [Sutherland's verification record](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/shortgrh/)
