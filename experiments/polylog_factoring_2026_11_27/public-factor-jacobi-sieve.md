# A short public sieve that isolates both balanced factors

For $N=pq$ with distinct odd primes $p<q<2p$, a common list of $O(\log N+\log(1/\epsilon))$ public Jacobi tests leaves exactly $p,q$ among the balanced integer candidates with probability at least $1-\epsilon$. Each proposed candidate can be tested in polynomial bit time. The unresolved operation is finding the survivors without visiting the candidate interval. Directly testing $\gcd(x,N)$ already decides any one proposed $x$. [Self-checking implementation](public_factor_jacobi_sieve.py)

Take the odd integers $x\in[\lceil\sqrt{N/2}\rceil,\lfloor\sqrt{2N}\rfloor]$ and remove perfect squares by integer square root. For each test choose $t$ uniformly and independently in $[0,H)$, where $H=100\lceil\sqrt{2N}\rceil$, and set $D=1+4tN$. Retain $x$ precisely when its Jacobi symbol $(x/D)=+1$. Since $D\equiv1\pmod4$, reciprocity gives $(x/D)=(D/x)$, including when both symbols are zero. Both true primes pass every draw because $D=1\bmod p,q$.

The interval ends below $2p$. Thus every false candidate in it is coprime to $N$. Its odd nonsquare denominator $x$ defines a nonprincipal Jacobi character on the units modulo $x$. Indeed at least one prime power in $x$ has odd exponent, and CRT permits a quadratic nonresidue at that prime and $1$ at all other primes. Consequently exactly half the units have character $+1$ and all nonunits have character zero. The map $t\mapsto1+4Nt\bmod x$ permutes a complete period. The probability of survival in a complete period is $\varphi(x)/(2x)\le1/2$. The incomplete final period adds at most $x/H$, giving a uniform upper bound of $0.51$. If $U=\lfloor\sqrt{2N}\rfloor$, the probability that **any** false candidate survives $T$ independent common tests is at most $U(0.51)^T$. Independence across candidates is unnecessary. The supplied random moduli have $O(\log N)$ bits each and require no auxiliary factorization.

On the 32-bit fixture $N=2{,}989{,}601{,}243$, the initial interval had 19,332 odd candidates and 19,291 odd nonsquares. The seeded scan retained 6,923 after one draw, 517 after four, 26 after eight and five after twelve. After eighteen draws the two survivors were exactly 42,697 and 70,019. The same module checks 106 complete nonsquare character periods by direct enumeration. [Reproduce](public_factor_jacobi_sieve.py)

The earlier common Jacobi sieve applies to candidate **factor sums** and tests whether the discriminant could be a square. This construction tests the proposed factor directly and rejects zero as well as negative characters. The seeded cyclic-cubic construction can also place both true factors in a smaller set of classes, with an explicit one-third list when its conductor is a known prime. A short description of allowed integers becomes input to the residue-list factoring bound only once the surviving classes can be generated at the required cost. The scan above still inspects $\Theta(\sqrt N)$ integers.
