# Reading one signed coordinate from the packed ternary sphere

Let $N$ be the product of distinct odd primes. Choose $x\ge2$ coprime to $N$ with Jacobi symbol $(-(x^2+1)/N)=-1$. The factor can be recovered by centered CRT on **one signed linear coordinate**, using primes of product greater than $2N$. If the evaluator only distinguishes empty classes from singletons and may answer arbitrarily on larger classes, product greater than $4N^2$ suffices. The queries are nonadaptive. Their number and the public work are polynomial in $x$ and $\log N$. A fast way to decide the scalar congruence queries on ordinary inputs is still missing. [Sphere packing and its exact decision problem](packed-ternary-decision.md) · [Implementation](packed_ternary_linear.py)

## The signed coordinate

Put $A=x^2+1$. The integral frames satisfy $u^2=A, u\cdot v=-Nx, v^2=N^2$, where squares of vectors denote squared lengths. The 24 coordinate frames are signed permutations of $((0,1,x),(0,0,-N))$. The 48 other frames form a single free orbit of signed permutations. Their second vectors have content $d$, the prime factor at which $-A$ is a nonsquare. Consequently all their sorted, nonnegative first vectors have a single common representative. [Frame counts](mixed-ternary-embeddings.md) · [Orbit proof](ternary-frame-moments.md)

Enumerate those representatives $u$ of squared length $A$ and discard any imprimitive one. A common divisor of its entries would divide both $A$ and $Nx$, which are coprime. Every remaining $u$ has a nonzero entry $u_j$ occurring only once. Three equal entries are impossible modulo 3. The remaining possible obstruction is $(0,a,a)$, where primitivity forces $a=1$ and hence $x=1$. The subgroup of signed permutations fixing $u$ fixes the index and the sign at $j$. Two zero entries are impossible because $x^2<A<(x+1)^2$. Thus its order is at most two, from a single zero or a single repeated pair.

If $u$ has noncoordinate partners, this subgroup acts transitively on them. Their signed entries $t=v_j$ are therefore identical. In fact $-N<t<0$. The identity $\|Nu+xv\|=N$ gives $u_j+xv_j/N\le1$, so $v_j\le0$. Equality at zero forces $u_j=1$ and $Nu_i=-xv_i$ at every other index. Since $x$ is coprime to $N$, that would give $N\mid v$. Likewise $|v_j|=N$ would give content $N$. Thus $d\mid t$ while $N\nmid t$, implying $\gcd(N,t)=d$.

## Reading the residues

Use the shared packing $Q=7^h\ge8(x+1)(N+1)$, $M=(x+QN)^2+1$. For each eligible prime $ℓ\nmid210AMN$, ask the scalar sphere predicate on every $W\equiv u-Qz\pmod{Qℓ}$ with $u\cdot z\equiv-Nx, z^2\equiv N^2\pmod{ℓ}$. The packing identity makes a class correspond exactly to the integral partners $v\equiv z\pmod{ℓ}$. The full menu depends only on $N,x$.

For existence, collect the distinct signed residues $z_j$ of the yes answers for each $u,ℓ$. Except for $u=(0,1,x)$, a set is empty or a singleton. For that public $u$ there is a known axis partner $v_0=(0,0,-N)$. Use the unique value different from $(v_0)_j$ if present and use $(v_0)_j$ otherwise. This reads the true residue even at a prime where the two signed coordinates coincide. A representative with no partners contributes no residues. One with only the coordinate partner recovers a trivial gcd. Once the product exceeds $2N$, centered CRT recovers the actual signed integer. Exactly one representative gives a proper gcd.

For the weaker predicate, omit the entire public axis class and retain primes giving at least one yes on the other classes. For the productive $u$ there are either one or two noncoordinate partners, by the stabilizer calculation. In the one-partner case $u$ has trivial stabilizer, so there is no axis partner. In the two-partner case choose one nonzero component difference $\Delta$ between them. Its magnitude is at most $2N$. At every prime not dividing $\Delta$ these are two distinct singleton classes. Neither can coincide with the axis, since the stabilizer interchanges the two partners and fixes the axis. At any other prime a yes on a remaining class still gives the correct signed coordinate. The product of primes with no usable yes therefore divides $|\Delta|$. A menu of product greater than $4N^2$ leaves product greater than $2N$. A one-sided lift to $[-N,0]$ would also allow thresholds $N$ and $2N^2$, respectively.

## Public selection and checks

Scan $x=2,3,\ldots,L$, returning any proper gcd from $x$ or $x^2+1$, or the first coprime mixed $x$. For $L\ge7$ dropping 0 and 1 preserves the earlier coverage. Write $s_x=-(x^2+1)$. Then $s_7=25s_1$ and $s_1s_2s_3=-100=100s_0$. When $5\nmid N$, a negative sign at 1 repeats at 7, and a negative sign at 0 requires a negative sign at one of 1, 2, 3. A zero at one of those tests gives a factor unless its norm is a multiple of $N$, impossible for the small norms on an odd distinct semiprime. If $5\mid N$, $x=2$ gives a factor. A skipped $x$ divisible by $N$ has the same sign as $x=0$. The previous density and conditional short-parameter bounds apply. [Short-parameter bounds](short-jacobi-grams.md)

Seven scalar-sphere fixtures use **444 exact-existence queries** and **938 parity queries**. Each individual class count is independently checked by direct Gram-frame enumeration. At $N=395,x=11$ the genuine $t=-55$ agrees with the public axis coordinate modulo 11. At $N=395,x=6$, the two genuine partners coincide modulo 11, so parity discards that prime and recovers $t=-30$ from the others. Both reveal factor 5. Further cases cover unused and imprimitive representatives, as well as a public first vector admitting only an axis partner. The checks also compare 1,122 public selectors and exhaust the 143 possible signed lifts modulo $11\cdot13$. [Reproduce the checks](check_packed_ternary_linear.py)
