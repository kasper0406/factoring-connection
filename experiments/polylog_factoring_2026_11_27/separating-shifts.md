# Recovering a witness from a translated power batch

Let $N$ be composite, $H$ monic of positive degree $D$, and $m\ge1$ given in binary. There is a randomized algorithm with expected bit cost polynomial in $D,\log m,\log N$ that returns **either a proper divisor of $N$ or a shift $c$ and a polynomial $L$**. In the second case, on the squarefree support of $H$, writing $Y=(c-X)^m$ gives $L(Y)=X$. The caller can choose a scalar target $u$ after seeing $c$. Evaluating $L(u)$ then recovers the only possible scalar witness at every prime where the batch hits. This solves the choice of a shift for decoding. Finding a target that gives a proper divisor remains open. [Implementation](check_separating_shift.py) · [Preceding batch evaluator and exponent tests](translated-batches.md)

## An exact identity for arbitrary scalar targets

Work in $A=R[X]/H$, $R=\mathbb Z/N$, and take **any** element $Y=P(X)$ represented in degree below $D$. Form the $D$ columns $1,Y,\ldots,Y^{D-1}$ in the usual basis $1,X,\ldots,X^{D-1}$. Let $K$ be their determinant. A proper $\gcd(K,N)$ immediately splits $N$. If $K$ is a unit, solving the linear system gives $X=L(Y)$ in $A$. For every $u\bmod N$, even one that depends arbitrarily on the entire calculation, put $t=L(u)$. Then
$$\gcd\big(N,\operatorname{Res}_X(H(X),P(X)-u)\big)
=\gcd\big(N,H(t),P(t)-u\big).$$
This identity also holds for nonsquarefree $H$ and repeated prime factors of $N$ whenever $K$ is a unit. At the resulting divisor $b$, the element $t\bmod b$ is an actual simultaneous solution, and every such solution equals $t\bmod b$.

For the proof, the characteristic polynomial $C$ of multiplication by $Y$ is monic of degree $D$. Cayley–Hamilton and the basis condition give $A\simeq R[U]/C(U)$ by $U\mapsto Y$. In $R[U]$, the ideal generated by $H(L(U))$ and $P(L(U))-U$ equals $(C(U))$. Indeed the quotient by the two displayed relations is isomorphic to $A$ via $X\leftrightarrow L(U)$ and $U\leftrightarrow P(X)$, using $L(P(X))=X\bmod H$. Specializing $U=u$ gives equality of the generated ideals in $R$. Their positive gcds with $N$ therefore coincide. For uniqueness evaluate $L(P(X))=X\bmod H$ at any solution. The routine verified the gcd and witness on 3,830 targets, including prime powers and composite inputs with repeated prime factors. [Decoder and exhaustive checks](check_batch_witness.py)

A singular determinant can reveal information that **every scalar target misses at that prime**. Take $N=35$, $H=X^4+5X-2$, $Y=X^2$. The determinant is $10\bmod35$, so its gcd is 5. At 5, $Y^2=2$ has no scalar solution. The resultant for a scalar $u$ is $(u^2-2)^2\bmod5$, always nonzero. For example the ordinary target $u=1$ gives resultant 11 modulo 35 and gcd 1. The earlier quotient-algebra investigations already used ranks and coefficient gcds to distinguish primes. Here the unit determinant also gives a public inverse for every scalar target. ## Why one can find a separating shift or split the input

Handle even inputs and perfect powers directly. The polynomial Euclidean algorithm and squarefree decomposition over $R$ either encounter a proper nonunit pivot, giving a factor, or produce a common squarefree support of $H$ at all primes of $N$. Trial division up to the degree handles small characteristics. Replacing $H$ by this support preserves its local root set. With repeated factors it need not preserve the integer multiplicities in the original resultant. The decoder reports the polynomial to which its exact identity applies. Below $D$ denotes the support degree and $n$ the bit length of $N$. [Squarefree preprocessing](check_correlated_batch.py)

At a hidden prime $r$, the Vandermonde determinant on the distinct roots of $H$ is a unit. Evaluating the columns at the roots shows that $K$ vanishes precisely when two distinct roots $\alpha,\beta$ have equal images. For $P(X)=(c-X)^m$, a collision requires both differences nonzero and
$$\left((c-\alpha)/(c-\beta)\right)^m=1.$$
For uniform $c\bmod r$, two cases suffice.

* For roots in the same Frobenius orbit of degree $d$, write $\beta=\alpha^{r^h}$, $g=\gcd(h,d)<d$. The image of $v\mapsto v^{1-r^h}$ on $\mathbb F_{r^d}^\times$ has order $S=(r^d-1)/(r^g-1)$. Pulling back characters of this image along $v=c-\alpha$ gives collision probability at most $\gcd(m,S)/S+(d-1)/\sqrt r$.
* For roots from different irreducible factors of degrees $a,b$, the two field factors form a finite étale algebra, meaning a product of finite separable fields. The element $(\alpha,\beta)$ generates the product because the minimal polynomials are distinct. In the compositum the ratio map $(v,w)\mapsto v/w$ has image of order $M=\mathrm{lcm}(r^a-1,r^b-1)$. Since $r-1\mid M$, the collision probability is at most $\gcd(m,r-1)/(r-1)+(a+b-1)/\sqrt r$.

Both statements follow by expanding the indicator of the subgroup killed by $m$ in multiplicative characters. The principal contribution is at most the reciprocal index. Every other character pulls back to a nontrivial character on the field or product algebra. Katz bounds its sum on the translated line by $(\dim A-1)\sqrt r$, with characters extended by zero off the unit locus. The pole positions cannot collide. These are applications of the classical character estimate. [Katz, Theorems 1–2](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/estcharsums.pdf#page=2) · [Conjugate-ratio calculation](translated-batches.md#a-uniform-shift-between-conjugates-in-an-extension-field)

There are at most $n$ hidden primes and at most $D(D-1)/2$ pairs per prime. Set $B=4nD^2$ and $T=(4nD^3)^2$. If all hidden primes exceed $T$ and all the relevant indices $S/\gcd(m,S)$ and $(r-1)/\gcd(m,r-1)$ exceed $B$, a union bound makes the probability of any collision at any prime less than $1/2$. A unit determinant therefore appears in fewer than two trials on average.

Otherwise there is a prime at most $T$, or the public $E=m\,\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)$ covers a scalar order or a relative norm-one order at a hidden prime. The algorithm interleaves fresh shifts with trial division and independent exponent tests. For the relative order, sample a degree-$g$ base algebra and an upper algebra of degree $d/g$. At the covered prime both can be fields with probability $\Omega(1/d)$, and the power $y^E$ lies in the base algebra. At another prime where $s-1\nmid E$, a linear component in the base and a simple linear factor plus complement above it give a non-base coefficient with constant probability. If $s-1\mid E$, the scalar exponent test suffices. A gcd of the coefficients outside the base with $N$ detects this discrepancy, including when $N$ has repeated prime factors. Cycle through the polynomially many possible degrees. The implementation keeps each exponent short by choosing a fresh uniform $t\in[1,B]$ and trying $mt$ in place of the full lcm completion. It chooses the required missing cofactor with probability at least $1/B$ each round. The resulting expected trial count is still polynomial. Interleaving bounded trial division detects a small factor in polynomial time if one exists. [Tower proof and historical precedents](translated-batches.md#a-uniform-shift-between-conjugates-in-an-extension-field)

For the original mixed-root problem $H=X^2-1$ on odd $N$, the simple choice $c=1$ already works for **every** $m$. The two images are $0$ and $2^m$. Modulo $H$ the inverse is $L(U)=1-2^{1-m}U$, with the power of 2 invertible modulo $N$. A scalar target that chooses a different image at two hidden primes is itself a scaled nontrivial CRT idempotent. The missing step for factoring is producing that target from $N$.

## When the target uses the same random shift

The preceding inverse works even for arbitrary scalar targets. A separate bound concerns targets of the succinct form
$$u(c)=b\prod_i F_i(c)^{e_i}\pmod N,$$
where $b$ is a unit, the $F_i$ are monic and the signed exponents are binary integers of unrestricted magnitude. All are fixed before drawing $c$ and may depend on $N$. Regular shifts require all polynomial factors to be units. Take $N=pq$ with distinct odd primes and $p<q$. Let $W=\deg H+\sum_i\deg F_i$ and $D=\deg H$. If one shift yields a proper batch gcd with probability at least $\delta$, polynomial-time preprocessing gives a factor, a small-prime alternative, or an explicit polynomial-size list of exponents on which the scalar or absolute norm-one factoring tests have a guarantee. This statement uses the compact support degree $W$, even when the expanded rational function has exponential degree.

Use squarefree layers of $H,F_i$ and pairwise polynomial gcds to obtain common monic blocks with known multiplicities. If a leading coefficient fails inversion, its gcd already splits $N$. Write $w_j=\sum_i e_i\,v_j(F_i)$ for the integer weight of block $G_j$, where $v_j$ is its multiplicity. Distinct blocks are coprime and squarefree at both primes. Output $m$ for the norm-one test, and for the scalar test list $m$, the nonzero $|w_j|$, and the nonzero $|m-w_j|$ for blocks in $H$. The list is public and has size $O(W)$. [Classification implementation](check_correlated_batch.py)

At $r\mid N$ fix an irreducible $h\mid H$ with root $\alpha$ of degree $d$. Form the product algebra with one field for each distinct irreducible factor of $h\prod_i F_i$ and let $\theta$ be the tuple of its roots. For a regular shift, evaluate $c-\theta$ and apply the multiplicative map that takes the $h$ coordinate to its $m$th power and divides by the product of the field norms of all coordinates to their block weights. The resulting value must equal $b$ at a hit. The signs are fixed by $\mathrm{Norm}(c-\theta_j)=h_j(c)$ for monic $h_j$. Write $J$ for the size of the image of this map. If $b$ lies in that image, Katz’s product-algebra estimate gives hit probability at most $1/J+(W-1)/\sqrt r$. Outside the image the regular hit probability is zero. [Katz, Theorem 2](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/estcharsums.pdf#page=2)

For $d>1$, project the image onto $\mathbb F_{r^d}^\times/\mathbb F_r^\times$. All scalar norms disappear, so $J\ge S_d(r)/\gcd(m,S_d(r))$. In fact this restriction applies to **any** scalar target $u(c)$ at a nonlinear root, since a scalar hit first requires $(c-\alpha)^m$ to be scalar. For a linear $h$, its own coordinate contributes scalar exponent $m-w_j$, where the weight is zero if that factor is absent from the target. Any other irreducible in a block of nonzero weight $w_l$ contributes $-w_l$ by surjectivity of its norm. Each nonzero such exponent $v$ gives $J\ge(r-1)/\gcd(v,r-1)$.

The remaining case is an exact identity with no nonzero exponent contribution. Here the sole active block is globally linear, occurs in $H$, and has weight $m$. Indeed it can contain no other irreducible at the prime under consideration and is squarefree there, forcing its public degree to be one. If $b=1$ at just one prime, $\gcd(b-1,N)$ splits. If $b=1\bmod N$, the root exists at both primes for every regular shift and the resultant gcd there is always $N$. If $b-1$ is a unit the identity produces no hits. Other roots, if any, still obey the preceding bounds.

Irregular shifts cost at most $2W/p$, and summing the character errors at the two primes costs at most $2DW/\sqrt p$. For $p>[8W(D+1)/\delta]^2$, success $\delta$ forces one of the remaining image sizes to be at most $4D/\delta$. Completing the associated listed exponent by $\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,\lceil4D/\delta\rceil)$ supplies the corresponding classical splitting test. Smaller $p$ is found by bounded trial division. For instance $H=X^2-2$, $N=77$, $u(c)=c^2-2$ and $m=492$ produce the proper factor 11 at 55 of 77 shifts before any prefiltering. The quadratic is irreducible at 11 and $492\equiv12\bmod120$ acts as the field norm there. This high success rate comes with the predicted multiple of $11+1$. The exponent was chosen for this control.
