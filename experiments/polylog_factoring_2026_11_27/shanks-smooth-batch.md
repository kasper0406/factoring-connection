# Random smooth cubic relations give a native factoring algorithm

Let $N=pq$, with distinct primes $3<p<q<2p$. A uniform Shanks seed produces a short rational parameter together with a modular cube-root witness. At the random-squares smoothness scale, these actually suffice to factor. Write $L(c)=\exp(c\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})$ and $B=L(b)$ for fixed $b>0$. The probability per uniform seed of an accepted $B$-smooth row is

$$L\big(-1/(2b)+o(1)\big). \tag{1}$$

This holds for every sufficiently large balanced semiprime and needs no assumption about the distribution of cubic symbols at the factor-base primes. The reference algorithm has expected bit cost at most $L(2+o(1))$. Standard batch smoothness and sparse linear algebra give a theoretical upper bound of $L(\sqrt2+o(1))$. This matches Pomerance's rigorous constant for a refinement of Dixon's random-squares method. Lenstra and Pomerance later proved the smaller constant $1$ for a class-group algorithm on general integers. The cubic construction gives a $2/3$ split probability per independent nonzero dependency. At polylogarithmic $B$, the earlier private-prime obstruction still applies. ## Relations and mixed roots

Use the Eisenstein ring $E=\mathbf Z[\omega]$ and $\gamma(w)=w/\bar w$. From seed $x$ obtain a parameter $t\bmod N$ and take the shortest primitive lift $a/b_0=t\bmod N$ in the norm $Q=\operatorname{Norm}(a-3b_0\omega)\le3N$. The previously proved identity gives $u=\gamma(x-\omega)$ with $u^3=\gamma(a-3b_0\omega)\bmod N$. A proper gcd at any stage already factors the input. Retain $Q$ smooth over the primes $r\le B$ which are $1\bmod3$, excluding $3$. For each such $r$ choose one Eisenstein generator $\pi_r$ of norm $r$, normalized to $1\bmod3$. The six units give all six unit residues modulo $3$, so this normalization is always possible. The conjugate is also primary. [Shortest lift and primitive norm](shanks-conductor-relations.md#rational-lifts-reduce-the-uniform-norm-bound-from-quadratic-to-linear) · [Witness identity](shanks-witness-fusion.md)

Primitivity puts all of a given $r$ on one side, so record a signed valuation $v_r$ according to which generator divides $a-3b_0\omega$. Removing all the generators leaves a unit congruent to the rational $a\bmod3$, necessarily $\pm1$. Thus $u^3=\prod_r\gamma(\pi_r)^{v_r}\bmod N$ with no unit coordinate. Let $s$ be the number of base primes. From $m$ accepted rows of rank $k_0$ over $\mathbf F_3$, the dependency space has dimension $d=m-k_0\ge m-s$. Each reduction is sparse, with at most $O(\log N)$ nonzero entries per row. There is no assumption that the rank equals $s$: it is at most $s$ minus the rank of the map $e\mapsto\prod_r\gamma(\pi_r)^{e_r}$ to the local norm-one group modulo cubes. Every accepted row is in the kernel of this map.

For a dependency $e$, use entries in $\{0,1,2\}$ and compute

$$ z=\prod_i u_i^{e_i}\prod_r\gamma(\pi_r)^{-\sum_i e_i v_{ir}/3} \pmod N. $$

Then $z^3=1$. At either hidden prime its three possible values are $1,\omega,\omega^2$. If the two labels differ, comparing both coefficients with those of each global root by gcd gives a proper factor. Conditional on the accepted parameters, the witnesses have independent uniform pairs of local labels: each good parameter has exactly three root preimages at each prime, including when the same parameter appears twice in independent sampling. Fix any basis of dependencies using only the parameters. The vector of its label differences is uniform in $\mathbf F_3^d$ because the basis has row rank $d$ acting on $m$ independent uniform input label differences, up to a fixed translate. Testing all basis vectors therefore splits with probability exactly $1-3^{-d}$. A single nonzero dependency gives $2/3$. Restarting failed batches keeps the algorithm Las Vegas. ## Why smooth short lifts occur this often

For the upper bound count all smooth Eisenstein ideals of norm at most $3N$. Six generators per ideal suffice, and each admissible parameter has nine seed preimages. Rankin's bound divided by $N$ is at most a constant times

$$N^{\sigma-1}\prod_{r\le B}(1-r^{-\sigma})^{-2},\qquad 1-\sigma=\frac{\log\log N}{2\log B}.$$

Here and below $r$ denotes a rational prime. Partial summation using $\pi(t)\ll t/\log t$ gives $\sum_{r\le B}r^{-\sigma}\ll\log\log B+B^{1-\sigma}/((1-\sigma)\log B)$: in the integral for the sum, split at $\log t=1/(1-\sigma)$ and use $\int_1^v e^u/u\,du\ll e^v/v$ for large $v$. The Euler product thus contributes $L(o(1))$ and the leading term is (1).

For the lower bound fix a small constant $c>0$ and $X=(cN)^{1/3}$. First count ordinary integers $m\le X$ supported on split primes $r\le B$ with few prime factors counted with multiplicity. Fix $0<\eta<1/2$. Append primes from $[B^{1-\eta},B]$ to a product $P$, starting from $1$, until $X/P\le B$. All choices keep $P\le X$. The maximum number of steps is $M=\lceil\log X/((1-\eta)\log B)\rceil$. The reciprocal mass $Z=\sum_{r=1\bmod3,\ B^{1-\eta}\le r\le B}1/r$ tends to $-\tfrac12\log(1-\eta)>0$ by the prime number theorem in the fixed progression. At the final step choose a split prime between $X/(2P)$ and $X/P$, or use the unit when $X/P$ is bounded. There are $\gg (X/P)/\log B$ choices uniformly, by that same prime number theorem and the bounded case. The resulting integers lie between constant multiples of $X$.

Draw each appended prime with probability $1/(rZ)$. Summing $1/P$ over the terminal ordered paths gives $\mathbb E Z^{\mathrm{length}}\ge\min(1,Z)^M$. Every resulting integer has at most $M+1$ prime factors and at most $2(M+1)!$ representations, allowing the optional unit. By Stirling the number of distinct products is at least

$$X L\big(-1/(6b(1-\eta))+o(1)\big).$$

Map each such integer to its canonical generator $w=\prod\pi_r^{v_r(m)}$. The image of $\gamma(w)$ in the norm-one group modulo $N$, modulo cubes, has only nine possible classes. Indeed the two local cyclic norm-one groups have orders $p-(\!-3/p)$ and $q-(\!-3/q)$, each divisible by three. At least one class contains a ninth of the list. **Any three members of this class give a product $\beta=w_1w_2w_3$ whose $\gamma(\beta)$ is a modular cube.** The proof only counts this class; the algorithm never needs to identify it. The number of triples per resulting norm is at most $3^{3(M+1)}=L(o(1))$, by assigning the prime occurrences to the three inputs. We have thus constructed at least $N L(-1/(2b(1-\eta))+o(1))$ distinct norms.

Only one orientation above each prime is ever used, so $\beta$ is primitive and $\beta=1\bmod3$. It has the form $a-3b_0\omega$ with coprime $a,b_0$ and $b_0\ne0$ (a real primitive canonical product has norm one). Also $\operatorname{Norm}\beta\le cN$. Choose $c$ small enough that $|b_0|<p$. Then $b_0$ and the norm are units modulo $N$, the latter because $B<p$. The local cube condition at $p,q$ gives nine good seeds by inverting the norm-one parametrization. In particular $\gamma(\beta)\ne1$ locally, as $b_0$ is a unit. The lattice $a=tb_0\bmod N$ in the complex embedding has area $3\sqrt3 N/2$, greater than $cN$. Any independent vector as short as $\beta$ would span area at most $cN$, so this primitive vector is the unique shortest up to sign. Distinct constructed norms therefore give distinct attainable parameters. Divide the count by $N$, then let the fixed $\eta$ approach zero. This proves (1).

## Cost and checks

Collect $s+O(1)$ rows. By (1) this takes expected $L(b+1/(2b)+o(1))$ seed draws, counting incidental gcds as early termination. The reference code trial-divides each row and uses dense elimination, for total exponent $\max(2b+1/(2b),3b)$. Taking $b=1/2$ gives 2. The one-time search for primary generators and all dependency extractions fit within this bound. [Reference implementation](shanks_smooth_batch.py)

For the theoretical bound, screen batches of $\lceil B\rceil$ norms using a product and remainder tree and the product of the base primes. Raising its remainder modulo each norm to a power of two exceeding $\log_2(3N)$ and taking a gcd recognizes smoothness. A batch costs $B(\log N)^{O(1)}$ bit operations with fast multiplication. Only accepted rows require individual prime exponents, costing at most $B^2(\log N)^{O(1)}$ across the matrix. Finding one certified nonzero dependency with sparse Wiedemann/Kaltofen–Saunders elimination likewise takes expected $B^2(\log N)^{O(1)}$. For the small base field, pad the transposed matrix to square and solve $Ay=Ax$ for independent uniform $x\in\mathbf F_3^m$ over an extension of sufficiently large polynomial cardinality in $m$. Give the solver only $A$, the right-hand side, and independent coins. Fix an extension basis containing $1$. Conditional on $Ax$ and the solver output, $x$ is uniform in a base-field solution coset, and the coordinate of $y$ along $1$ is another solution in that coset. The coordinate of $x-y$ along $1$ is therefore uniform in the base-field kernel. Verify it and retry on zero, which costs only a constant factor since $d\ge1$. All these choices avoid the witnesses and preserve the mixed-label law. The total exponent is $\max(b+1/(2b),2b)$, minimized at $b=1/\sqrt2$. This is the same smoothness and linear algebra tradeoff as random squares. ## What can correlated generation change?

**Collection rate.** Suppose $T$ possibly dependent seeds each have a uniform marginal distribution modulo $N$, or assign each residue probability at most $L(o(1))/N$. By linearity of expectation, the expected number of accepted rows is at most $T L(-1/(2b)+o(1))$, irrespective of the correlations. Collecting $\Theta(s)=L(b+o(1))$ such rows with probability bounded below therefore needs at least $L(b+1/(2b)+o(1))$ candidate seeds, by Markov's inequality. The exponent is at least $\sqrt2$ for every $b$. This argument counts candidate seeds in this collection strategy. It allows an earlier incidental gcd, useful dependencies among fewer rows, or a different sampling law.

The same per-seed exponent persists if a row may additionally contain up to $J$ distinct primes in $(B,B^A]$, of any multiplicity, for fixed $A>1$ and fixed $J$. In the Rankin upper bound each exceptional rational prime contributes at most $(1-r^{-\sigma})^{-2}-1=O(r^{-\sigma})$ including both Eisenstein orientations and all powers. Since $(B^A)^{1-\sigma}=(\log N)^{A/2}$, their sum is at most $(\log N)^{O_A(1)}$. Summing the products for up to $J$ choices introduces only a multiplicative factor $L(o(1))$, even when $J=o(\sqrt{\log N/\log\log N})$. Fully smooth rows supply the matching lower bound. In particular the usual one- and two-large-prime allowances at these cutoffs can change the finite yield, while retaining the leading per-seed smoothness exponent. A proof exploiting partial relations earlier than the $\Theta(s)$-row threshold would be a separate argument.

**Dependency rank.** Write $V$ for the original integer valuation matrix, with one row per witness and one column per generator $g_r=\gamma(\pi_r)$. Suppose newly formed rows have $V'=CV+3D$, and the corresponding witnesses are exactly $u'_j=\prod_i u_i^{C_{ji}}\prod_r g_r^{D_{jr}}$, allowing a global cube-root-of-unity multiplier on each witness. Every $e\in\ker(V'^T\bmod3)$ gives $C^Te\in\ker(V^T\bmod3)$. Substituting into the extraction formula shows that the new mixed-root test returns the original comparison for $C^Te$, up to a global cube root of unity. In particular adding a multiple of three to the integer representatives of $C^Te$ does not affect its comparison, by the original row identities. For transformations fixed by the original parameters alone and original independent uniform seed labels, the number of independent label differences is exactly the rank of the map $e\mapsto C^Te$ on the new dependency space, over $\mathbf F_3$. Testing its full basis splits with probability $1-3^{-\operatorname{rank}(C^T|_{\ker V'^T})}$. The formal nullity of a larger synthesized matrix can be much higher. Choosing a new global lift after modular reduction can change the valuation data, so that the exact recombination identity would need to be verified for that construction. [Earlier correlated-witness analysis](shanks-witness-fusion.md#which-correlated-root-transformations-only-reproduce-the-original-field)

For comparison, a genuinely new generator at norm scale $N^\theta$ with smoothness probability $L(-\theta/(2b)+o(1))$, candidates costing $L(o(1))$ amortized per trial, and sufficient independent label randomness would change the same accounting to $\max(b+\theta/(2b),2b)$. Optimizing gives $\sqrt{2\theta}$. Reaching $\theta=1/2$ under those hypotheses would match the published class-group constant $1$. These properties have only been proved here at $\theta=1$ for the uniform generator.
