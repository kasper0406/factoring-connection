# Reading a dyadic bit query on balanced semiprimes

For distinct odd primes $p<q<2p$ with $N=pq$, a selected dyadic product is divisible by $N$ exactly when both $p$ and $q$ have a one among the selected positions. The two methods below compute that Boolean from $N$ and the positions. They exploit different shapes of the set of odd integers with zeros at those positions. [Prime-bit identity and earlier enumeration](cofactor-zero-queries.md) · [Evaluators](submask_batch.py)

Both primes lie in $[L,H]=[\lceil\sqrt{N/2}\rceil,\lfloor\sqrt{2N}\rfloor]$. Its only nonunits modulo $N$ are $p,q$, since $H<2p$. Write $b=\operatorname{bitlength}(N)$ and let $k$ count free positions from 1 through $\lfloor\log_2 H\rfloor$. The units bit is fixed to one.

## Batch the submask candidates

Scattering increasing binary ranks into the free positions gives all avoiding odd integers in order. Two binary searches locate the contiguous rank interval of length $M$ inside $[L,H]$. Choose a power-of-two block size $d$ within a constant factor of $\sqrt M$. For the $d$ assignments to the lowest $\log_2 d$ free positions, form the monic polynomial $P(X)=\prod_u(X+u)\bmod N$. Shared multipoint evaluation at the $O(1+M/d)$ full-block starts finds every nonunit block. Direct gcds handle the two partial boundary blocks. If a block gcd is $N$, scanning at most $d$ offsets recovers a proper divisor. Any recovered factor determines both primes and the original Boolean.

The hitting candidates can instead be partitioned by their first selected one. Let $s$ be the number of selected active positions, and $M_0,M_1$ the avoiding and hitting counts. The evaluator computes the rank intervals for both sides and chooses the smaller sum of rounded square roots. By Cauchy–Schwarz it costs $(1+\min(\sqrt{M_0},\sqrt{sM_1}))\operatorname{poly}(b)$ bit operations, at most $2^{k/2}\operatorname{poly}(b)$. This is the classical product-polynomial technique for finding congruent elements in two lists, applied to scattered bit positions with rank clipping. [Hittmeir, section 3, Algorithm 3.2](https://publications.sba-research.org/publications/042021_Hittmeir_A%20time-space%20tradeoff%20for%20Lehman%27s%20deterministic%20integer%20factorization%20method.pdf) · [Earlier conic product batching](pooled-conic-batch.md)

## Group free positions inside an affine window

Let $R=\max(2,\lfloor N^{1/4}/8\rfloor)$. Choose an interval of bit positions containing the largest number $r$ of free positions for which their summed weights $W$ satisfy $W/2^h\le2R$, where $h$ is their lowest position. Enumerate assignments to the other $k-r$ free positions. Given their value plus one, $a$, every assigned candidate lies in $a+2^h z$, $0\le z\le W/2^h$. Intersect with $[L,H]$, then round the midpoint and enlarge the shorter side by at most one. Multiplication by the inverse of $2^h$ modulo $N$ gives a monic affine problem of radius at most $R$. The existing Coppersmith implementation certifies every qualifying offset using an exact reduced-row norm bound. Sparse inner bits or midpoint padding can introduce incidental divisors. We recompute the original Boolean on both recovered primes. Exhausting all assignments without a proper divisor proves both primes hit. The cost is $2^{k-r}\operatorname{poly}(b)$. [Affine certificate and dimension bound](coupled-rank-windows.md) · [Howgrave-Graham on approximate common divisors](https://cr.yp.to/bib/2001/howgrave-graham.pdf)

At $N=(2^{127}-1)(162\cdot2^{120}+1)$ take free positions 40–79, 121, 125 and 127. There are $4\cdot2^{40}$ avoiding candidates in range. The product method would need a degree-$2^{21}$ polynomial and $2^{21}$ block values. The affine method made four dimension-three lattice calls and found the larger prime. Moving position 125 to 124 preserved those counts and certified divisibility. These masks were constructed using the fixture factors. A separate 19-free-bit pair exercised the product evaluator on 262,144 candidates per mask with degree 512 and 512 block values. Both evaluators also agreed with literal factorial-ratio products on all 956 small queries. [Reproduce the checks](check_submask_batch.py)

A singleton or pair can leave roughly $b/2$ positions free. Batching gives a general $N^{1/4}\operatorname{poly}(b)$ upper bound on this balanced domain. The affine method becomes polynomial when $O(\log b)$ free positions lie outside a certified window. Neither bound supplies a polynomial evaluator for arbitrary singleton or pair queries. Factoring first already gives a deterministic $N^{1/5+o(1)}$ bound for those queries. [Harvey and Hittmeir](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3708)
