# What a large residue reveals about the hidden binary norm

For a balanced odd distinct semiprime $N=pq$ and $A=x^2+1$ with $(-A/N)=-1$, fix $\|u\|^2=A$. Given a candidate $z\bmod\ell$ at a prime $\ell\nmid 2AN$, one can find **every** integral partner $v$ with $v^2=N^2$, $u\cdot v=-Nx$, $v=z\bmod\ell$. For short $x$ the new expected bit bound is
$$
(1+N^{1/4}/\sqrt{\ell})\operatorname{poly}(x+1,\log N,\log\ell).
$$
In particular a square-root-scale marking prime makes one specified decision polynomial in the input length. The relevant precision is the *extra* factor $\ell$ in the packed modulus $Q\ell$. The large coarse factor $Q$ specifies the first vector $u$. ## Recovering the slope from the mark

Write $u_0=(x,1,0)$. As in the earlier reduction, a signed Householder transformation $G$ gives $Gu=Du_0$, $G^2=D^2 I$, $1\le D\le2A$. For $u=\pm u_0$ just take $D=1$ and the appropriate sign. Otherwise choose $\sigma=\pm1$, put $D=A-\sigma u\cdot u_0$, $w=u-\sigma u_0$, and $G=\sigma(DI-ww^\top)$. At a prime $\ell\nmid2A$, at least one of the two choices has $\ell\nmid D$ since the two denominators sum to $2A$. Set $N'=DN$. The transformed partner looks like
$$
Gv=(-N'+t,-xt,y),\qquad y^2=t(2N'-At).
$$
Write the rational slope $y/t=k/j$ in lowest terms and include $[1:0]$ for $t=0$. For $F=k^2+Aj^2$, the exact integer identities are
$$
(2N'-At,y,t)=C(k^2,kj,j^2),\qquad C=2N'/F .
$$
For finite slopes, $C$ is integral because $t=Cj^2$ is integral and $\gcd(F,j)=1$. The inert prime for $-A$ cannot divide a primitive $F$, so $F\mid2Dr$ for the other prime $r$. Balance ($\max(p,q)\le2\min(p,q)$) gives $F\le H=2D\lfloor\sqrt{2N}\rfloor$. Conversely each candidate with $F\mid2DN$ yields a unique rational partner via these formulas and $G/D^2$. Checking integrality and the supplied mark is exact. [Derivation before introducing a marking modulus](short-jacobi-grams.md)

Compute the triple on the left modulo $\ell$ using $Gz$. Since $\ell\nmid2DN$, the scalar $C$ is a unit. If $t$ is a unit the slope is $[y/t:1]$. Otherwise $2N'-At$ is a unit and the slope is $[1:y/(2N'-At)]$. Thus a single mark specifies a projective line, whose preimage in $\mathbf Z^2$ is a lattice of determinant $\ell$. For a factored composite marking modulus $s$ coprime to $2AN$, a single sign choice can leave $\ell\mid D$ at some prime powers. At $\ell^a\parallel s$ divide the triple by its common $\ell$-part, capped at $\ell^a$. A genuine solution loses at most $v_\ell(D)$ digits because $C\mid2DN$ and $k,j$ are coprime. Excess loss rejects the mark. The two projective charts then glue by CRT to a line of determinant $S\ge s/\gcd(s,D)$. For a prime mark the sign choice gives $S=\ell$.

## Exact search and bit cost

The norm $k^2+Aj^2$ on the line lattice has a Gauss-reduced form $[a,b,c]$ with $|b|\le a\le c$ and $4ac-b^2=4AS^2$. If $a>H$ the ellipse is empty. If $a\le H<c$, only multiples of the shortest direction fit: for second coordinate $\pm1$ the minimum is $c$, and higher absolute values cost at least $3c$. Only the shortest vector and its negative can be primitive on that line. If $c\le H$, the search lies in a rectangle with side lengths $K=O(\sqrt{H/a})$, $L=O(\sqrt{H/c})$, $L\le O(K)$, $KL=O(H/(\sqrt A S))$. Direct enumeration of reduced slopes and the inverse map gives a deterministic bound polynomial in bit length times $1+H/(\sqrt A S)$. The implementation enumerates in a disk after scaling $j$ by $\max(1,x)$, which changes these bounds by at most a constant. In particular, when $\ell\sqrt A>H$, two independent weighted slopes in the ellipse would have parallelogram area at most $H$ and at least $\ell\sqrt A$, a contradiction. Uniqueness here is ordinary two-dimensional rational reconstruction. The contribution of the mark is the computed projective slope and its bound on $F$. [Böhm, Decker, Fieker and Pfister on lattice rational reconstruction](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/2951)

For a faster decision, pool products of the reduced form values modulo $N$ across the dyadic row intervals in the ellipse. The product and multipoint trees cost $\widetilde O(L+\sqrt{KL})$ modular operations, hence $\widetilde O(1+\sqrt H/(A^{1/4}\sqrt S))$ when $H<N$. The form-reduction and gcd costs are polynomial in the bit lengths. For $H\ge N$, trial division costs only $O(D)$ divisions on this branch. A proper $\gcd(D,N)$ suffices immediately. If a pooled gcd gives any proper factor, reconstruct the finite partner orbits with a modular square root at the now-known prime, the fixed-rank Gram embedding, signed permutations and an exact mark test. A randomized known-prime square root has expected polynomial bit cost. An incidental gcd can occur even at an empty mark, which is why this final test is necessary. If no gcd occurs and $\gcd(D,N)=1$, each admissible $F$ is coprime to $N$ and $N\mid C$. Then $N$ divides $Gv$, hence $v$, so it suffices to check the six signed coordinate axes. These cases establish completeness. [Pooled polynomial algorithm](pooled-conic-batch.md) · [Frame reconstruction](packed-quaternion-collapse.md)

For a native scalar query $(M,m,c)$ with one supplied prime conductor $T$, the earlier Gaussian decomposition of a known prime (randomized Cornacchia) gives the two ordered possibilities for $(B,Nt)$. Only the original orientation can divide $m$ as required: if the swapped real coordinate $Nt$ worked, $2ANt$ would divide $2ABt\ell$, forcing $N\mid B\ell$ despite $\gcd(N,B\ell)=1$. In the original orientation $t=\gcd(Nt,m/(2AB))$. Thus the reader obtains $N,x,u,z,\ell$ from the stated scalar data in expected polynomial bit time and can apply the marked search. This bound treats the stated conductor and marking prime as promised or verified by polynomial-time primality testing. The Python reader also performs SymPy primality checks. ## How often does a large mark help?

Fix the input and transport denominator $D$, in the nontrivial range $H<N$, $\gcd(N,D)=1$. Every pair in the ellipse producing any proper gcd, including incidental hits, has $k^2+Aj^2=er$ for $r\in\{p,q\}$ and $e\le4D$. Embed $k+j\sqrt{-A}$ into the maximal imaginary quadratic order. There are at most $\tau(er)$ integral ideals of norm $er$, by prime ideal factorization, and at most six generators per principal ideal. The embedding is injective. Since $\tau(er)\le2\tau(e)$, summing over $e$ and both primes gives $O(D\log(2D))$ productive pairs in the entire ellipse. For a prime $\ell\nmid2AN$ with $\ell^2>4D$, none vanish in both coordinates modulo $\ell$. Each productive pair lies on precisely one of $\ell+1$ projective lines. A uniformly random line therefore hits with probability at most $O(D\log(2D)/\ell)$. The locally admissible marks remove at most two isotropic lines when $\ell\nmid D$, giving the same bound. This covers random lines on each fixed input. Choosing a nonuniform productive line from public data is a separate question. The earlier predictor work already asked for information of that kind. [Earlier predictor ledger](README.md#working-ledger-for-the-quarter-parity-evaluator)

On the 255-bit check, a 130-bit planted positive mark yielded the split prime as the shortest norm after reduction. A locally admissible negative mark at the same modulus was empty. One shortest-direction gcd sufficed for each. Both supplied residues had gcd one with $N$. A 107-bit positive mark on the same input used 8,704 block evaluations and 130 scalar gcds. The factors are used to *construct the test labels* only. The evaluator receives $N,x,u,z,\ell$. Across 1,596 small admissible marks, both implementations agreed with exhaustive frames (504 occupied). An additional 729 prime-square controls and 1,152 slope lattice generators exercised the composite, axial and empty cases. At $N=251\cdot257$, enlarging $\ell$ from 3 to 521 changed the local menu from 4 marks to 520; the pooled full-menu operation counters went from 230 to 822. This is a per-mark speedup. An end-to-end factorer still needs a way to choose the mark or process a whole menu efficiently.
