# Both factorizations for a specified three-square query

Given an odd distinct semiprime $N$ and a public $x=\operatorname{poly}(\log N)$ with Jacobi symbol $(-(x^2+1)/N)=-1$, we can supply a **single prime** $T$, the factorization of the squared radius $M=(x^2+1)T^2$, and the factorization of every query modulus $m$. All three come from $N,x$. With a polynomial-time primality test, the construction takes expected polynomial time in $\log N$ on every such input using the October 2026 zero-gap preprint. Older published results suffice outside an $O(\log R/R)$ fraction of products of two distinct primes in $[R,2R]$. A polynomial-bit algorithm for the individual three-square decision remains open. The change here is to draw both freely chosen coordinates with their factorizations and require only their quadratic form value to be prime. ## A sampler that retains the factors

Put $A=x^2+1$. Choose an integer $H$, write $X=H^2$, and draw $B$ and $t$ independently and uniformly with complete factorizations in the rectangle

$$
1\le B\le H,\qquad 1\le t\le\lfloor H/N\rfloor.
$$

Keep a sample if $B>N$, $t\ge2$, and $T=B^2+N^2t^2\le X$ is a prime coprime to $2AN$. Kalai's sampler starts with a uniform integer in $[1,H]$ and repeatedly draws a uniform integer between 1 and the current value, inclusive, until it reaches 1. Form the product $v$ of the prime entries with repetitions. Restart if $v>H$, otherwise retain with probability $v/H$. The retained output is exactly uniform, with its factorization already recorded. The expected number of primality tests is $O((1+\log H)^2)$. Use the same procedure with the smaller bound for $t$. [Kalai, *Generating Random Factored Numbers, Easily*](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/user/akalai/genfactor/web/factor/factor.html)

Set $C=Nt$, $P=B^2-C^2-2xBC$ and $Q=2ABt$. A prime norm gives $\gcd(B,C)=1$ and opposite coordinate parities. Thus $P$ is a unit at every prime of $2Bt$. At each prime of $A$, $P\equiv(B-xC)^2$, and a zero there would imply $T\equiv0$, excluded by construction. Hence $\gcd(P,Q)=1$. Both $AB^2>N^2$ and $At^2>1$ hold, so the packing identity isolates precisely the required frames in the coarse class. For $m=Q\ell$, supply the prime lists for $2,A,B,t,\ell$, adding multiplicities. For $M$ supply those for $A,T^2$. Trial division of $A$ is polynomial in $x+1$. [Packing identity and reconstruction](fixed-field-packing.md)

## Density in the coordinate rectangle

Take $K=\mathbf Q(i)$ and let $L$ be the ring class field of $\mathbf Z+N\mathbf Z[i]$. For rational primes away from $2N$, complete splitting in $L$ is equivalent to representation by the principal form $B^2+N^2t^2$. Each such prime up to $X$ provides a positive pair in the rectangle. An axis point cannot give an unramified prime. Distinct primes give distinct pairs. The ring class number and maximum Hecke conductor norm satisfy

$$
h=\frac N2\prod_{s\mid N}\left(1-\frac{\chi_{-4}(s)}s\right)\asymp N,
\qquad \max_\chi \operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbf Q}\mathfrak f_\chi \le N^2.
$$

The index two in the unit groups supplies the factor $1/2$. Every ring class character factors through the ray class group of modulus $N\mathcal O_K$, giving the conductor inequality. [Cox, *Primes of the Form* $x^2+ny^2$, chapters 7 and 9](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/~ila/Cox-Primes_of_the_form_x2%2Bny2.pdf)

Apply Thorner and Zaman's Theorem 1.4 to $L/\mathbf Q$, its abelian subgroup $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$, and the identity conjugacy class. Here $D_K=4$, the base degree is two, and $[L:\mathbf Q]=2h$. For $X\ge N^{C_0}$ with a sufficiently large absolute $C_0$, their relative error is less than $1/2$. The main term is $\operatorname{Li}(X)/(2h)$, with $\operatorname{Li}(X^\beta)/(2h)$ subtracted if their exceptional real zero exists. A bound $1-\beta\gg1/\log(8N)$ makes the difference $\gg X/(N\log X)$ on enlarging $C_0$. The range uses the conductor estimate above. [Thorner and Zaman, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.02823#page=3)

A nontrivial real character of the ring class group cuts out a quadratic extension over $K$. Complex conjugation acts by inversion and has order two, so the quotient extension over $\mathbf Q$ has group $C_2\times C_2$. Artin formalism factors the character's Hecke $L$-function into the two rational quadratic Dirichlet $L$-functions for the subfields other than $K$. Both primitive conductors have odd support in the primes dividing $N$ and are at most $8N$, by the fundamental discriminant formula. Dropping ramified Euler factors introduces no zeros near 1. The trivial character gives $\zeta_K(s)=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{-4})$, which has no real zero in $(0,1)$ by the alternating series. Banks's theorem gives $(1-\beta)\log q\ge0.003$ for the primitive real Dirichlet factors. This all-input application uses an October 2026 **preprint**, specifically its real-zero gap. [Banks, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15483v1#S1.SS2) · [Kerr's treatment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21099)

Discarding $B\le N$ or $t=1$ removes at most $2H$ pairs. Discarding primes of $2AN$ removes at most $O(\log(2AN))$ prime values. These losses are negligible for short $x$ on taking $C_0>2$. The rectangle contains at most $X/N$ pairs, so a trial succeeds with probability $\gg1/\log X$. One may use a sufficiently large fixed power of $N$ for $X$. To avoid choosing that exponent in the experiment, the code starts at $H=N^2$, uses $\operatorname{bitlength}(H)^2$ fresh pairs, then squares $H$ on exhaustion. After reaching the threshold, the probability of another widening is at most $\exp(-c\log H)$. The bit length doubles on widening, so the sum of expected costs remains polynomial in $\log N$. The default Python backend certifies large primes by factoring the auxiliary integer one less than the candidate and applying Lucas's criterion. The complexity statement uses a polynomial-time primality predicate such as AKS. [Agrawal, Kayal and Saxena](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2004.160.781)

## What follows using the older published estimates

Fix the uniform distribution on products of two distinct primes in $[R,2R]$. Landau–Page allows at most one primitive real character of conductor up to $32R^2$ with a real zero greater than $1-c/\log(32R^2)$. For sufficiently large $R$, any such exceptional conductor has an odd prime divisor, since the pure 2-power quadratic conductors form a fixed finite set. Pick one such divisor. Every affected input contains it. Counting possible other primes excludes at most $O(R/\log R)$ products among $\asymp R^2/\log^2 R$. The remaining inputs enjoy the same rectangle density. The sampler needs no knowledge of the exceptional divisor. [Ford, Green, Konyagin, Maynard and Tao, Lemma 7.1](https://www.ams.org/journals/jams/2018-31-01/S0894-0347-2017-00876-2/S0894-0347-2017-00876-2.pdf)

There is an average-time factoring reduction including every input in this distribution. Scan for a mixed $x$ to $(\log N)^D$, with fixed $D>2$, returning any proper gcd encountered. If the scan fails, use trial division. The earlier short-parameter estimate leaves at most $R^{2/D+o(1)}$ products needing this fallback. For the sampler take $H=2^{\operatorname{bitlength}(N)^2}$ and cap at $\operatorname{bitlength}(N)^4$ independent coordinate-pair trials. On good inputs the chance of exhausting this budget is $\exp(-\Omega((\log N)^2))$. On exhaustion use trial division, costing $O(R)$ divisions on this family. Even charging this cost in full to each exceptional input contributes $O(\log R)$ divisions to the mean. A polynomial-time exact specified-class decision would therefore give polynomial expected factoring time averaged over balanced semiprimes and coins, using the older published analytic inputs. The worst-case question of finding short $x$ is separate. [Short Jacobi scan and its quantifiers](short-jacobi-grams.md)

## Reproducible distinction

For $N=77,x=1$, the sampler produced $B=85=5\cdot17$, $t=16=2^4$, and the prime $T=1{,}525{,}049$. Each query carries $M=2T^2$ and $Q=2^6\cdot5\cdot17$ with the additional marking prime $\ell$. At the same modulus $13Q=70{,}720$, the admissible residue $(53{,}401,37{,}081,16{,}320)$ yields the partner $(-14,-63,-42)$, while $(58{,}841,31{,}641,65{,}280)$ has no integral point. Across the full reduction direct enumeration found 48 occupied and 190 empty queries and recovered 7. The sampler checks cover 11 inputs through a 160-bit squared radius. An exact rational calculation verifies equal one-trial acceptance probabilities for all outputs at each sampler bound through 18. Reopen this direction for a method that lowers the cost of deciding one residue class given these factorizations, or for a native factoring step.
