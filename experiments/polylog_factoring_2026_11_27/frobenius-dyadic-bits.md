# What can polynomial splitting patterns say about the next factor bit?

Fix a power of two $m=2^k$, $k\ge4$, and an odd prime class $a\bmod m$. Its competing lift from precision $m/2$ is $a'=a+m/2\pmod m$. For all but four classes, **every fixed finite list of rational polynomials has the same limiting joint distribution of factorization degrees at primes in the two classes**. Increasing the number or degrees of the polynomials does not remove this symmetry. Observing a *named Frobenius element* carries more information, but to distinguish the two lifts that way some defining polynomial in the list must have degree at least $2^{k-2}$. These statements fix the list before sampling primes. [Finite checks](check_frobenius_dyadic_bits.py) · [Two binomial bit streams](two-fixed-and-streams.md)

## Factorization degrees and coprime powers

Put $K=\mathbf Q(\zeta_m)$ and form the Galois compositum $L$ of $K$ with the splitting fields of all polynomials in the list. Repeated factors can be removed over $\mathbf Q$ and the finitely many bad primes omitted. Write $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbf Q)$. Conditional on the prime class $a\bmod m$, Chebotarev gives the distribution on conjugacy-invariant observations by counting elements of $G$ projecting to $a$. This holds for primes in $[X,2X]$ as $X\to\infty$ at fixed fields. The degrees of the irreducible factors are exactly the cycle lengths on the polynomial's roots.

Whenever $a,a'$ generate the same cyclic subgroup, choose a positive $u$ with $a^u=a'$ and $\gcd(u,\exp G)=1$. An odd exponent works in the dyadic group, and CRT lets us set it equal to 1 modulo every odd prime dividing $|G|$. The bijection $g\mapsto g^u$ sends the whole fiber over $a$ to the fiber over $a'$. It keeps the cycle lengths of every permutation action, since $u$ is coprime to every cycle length. Hence all the observed factorization patterns have identical joint laws. This also covers fixed-point counts of all powers of Frobenius on any fixed finite étale scheme, including finite étale Weil restrictions. [Chebotarev and the Frobenius splitting rule](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1996d/cheb.pdf)

To see exactly when the subgroup agrees, write $a=(-1)^s5^j$ in $C_2\times C_{2^{k-2}}$. Multiplication by $\epsilon=1+m/2=5^{2^{k-3}}\bmod m$ adds $m/2$ to each odd residue. Unless $j=0$ or $2^{k-3}$ modulo $2^{k-2}$, the powers $j$ and $j+2^{k-3}$ have the same 2-adic valuation. An odd power therefore takes $a$ to $a\epsilon$ and preserves its sign coordinate. At the four exceptions $a\in\{1,-1,\epsilon,-\epsilon\}$ the two cyclic subgroups differ. For example two distinct order-two elements generate different subgroups.

For two independently sampled primes one can apply this equality separately in both coordinates. Compare the state with both residues $a$ to the state with both residues $a'$. Both give the same product modulo $m$. For each odd multiplier $h$, bit $k-1$ of $(hp)\mathbin{\&}(hq)$ flips between the two states. If $a$ is outside the exceptions, fixed splitting patterns at the individual primes have identical limiting distributions even if both lists are supplied already. This comparison gives the observer the product *residue*. The full integer products are different.

The same symmetry applies to traces of fixed **finite-image** representations with rational character values. The eigenvalues of $g$ are roots of unity, and raising them to $u$ Galois-conjugates the trace; a rational trace is fixed. It also applies to traces valued in $\mathbf Q(\zeta_M)$ for any odd $M$, even if the complex values are labelled, since one may require $u\equiv1\bmod M$ at the same time. More generally if $j\ne0,2^{k-3}$ and $w=v_2(j)$, the necessary exponents $u$ have $v_2(u-1)=k-3-w$. Any fixed common value-field conductor with $v_2(M)\le k-3-w$ can still be fixed pointwise. To see the trace identity, choose $u$ coprime to the exponent of $G$ and to $M$ and use the power automorphism of the single cyclotomic field containing all eigenvalues and $\zeta_M$. In particular using oriented cube or fifth roots of unity as the value field does not evade the generic-lift equality. The binomial multipliers $3,5$ themselves are ordinary integers and carry no such finite-image assumption.

## What a labelled Frobenius would cost in degree

Let $E$ be the compositum of the polynomial splitting fields, without adjoining $K$. If every polynomial degree is less than $2^{k-2}$, the 2-part of $\exp\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbf Q)$ is at most $2^{k-3}$. Indeed the group embeds in a product of symmetric groups, and the 2-part of a permutation order cannot exceed its largest permitted power-of-two cycle length bound. The quotient $\operatorname{Gal}(E\cap K/\mathbf Q)$ inherits this exponent bound. Thus $\epsilon=5^{2^{k-3}}$ acts trivially on $E\cap K$. The fiber-product description of $\operatorname{Gal}(EK/\mathbf Q)$ now shows that exactly the same Frobenius elements on $E$ are compatible with $a$ and $a'$, with the same multiplicities. This argument applies to all four exceptional classes as well.

The degree is sharp for named Frobenius classes. The real cyclotomic field $\mathbf Q(\zeta_m+\zeta_m^{-1})$ has degree $2^{k-2}$, and its abelian Galois group distinguishes $a$ from $a'$ since $\epsilon\notin\{1,-1\}$. A short recurrence can describe the field's high-degree defining polynomial. Explicit basis arithmetic still has the stated degree, and finding the named local Frobenius at a hidden prime is a separate operation. A factorization pattern records only its permutation cycle lengths.

For example $8777=67\cdot131$ and $37769=179\cdot211$ both equal 9 modulo 32. Both factors of the first input are 3 modulo 32 and both of the second are 19. At the top bit all three multipliers $1,3,5$ give zero on the first input and one on the second. The defining polynomial of the real 32nd cyclotomic field, $X^8-8X^6+20X^4-16X^2+2$, is irreducible at **all four** primes. At the exceptional classes represented by 97 and 113, it has eight linear factors and four quadratic factors respectively.

## Scope for the native calculation

The publicly known cyclotomic substitution $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^N$ composes the two named actions: $\sigma_N=\sigma_p\sigma_q$. Both lifts have the same product action. The ordinary power $(1+\zeta)^N$ does something different: at $p$ its value is $(1+\zeta^p)^q$, retaining the unknown outer exponent. The preceding theorems do not assert that its full coefficients in integer representatives are distributed equally. Earlier work shows that a componentwise Frobenius operator supplied on a separating algebra already yields a proper gcd, for example from the sign of $\sqrt D$ at the two primes when $(D/N)=-1$. [Earlier operator reduction, §5.2](../../notes/nonsmooth_results.md) · [Power-root and trace certificates](power-and-trace-classes.md)

There is a precise complementary statement for a chosen complex embedding. Given the correct unordered state modulo $m/2$ and $N\bmod m$, calculate its two candidate lifts $\{a,b\}$ and $\{a+m/2,b+m/2\}$. Their numerical values of $S=\zeta_m^p+\zeta_m^q$ are $z$ and $-z$, where $z=\zeta_m^a+\zeta_m^b$. If $z=0$ the two lifts are the same unordered pair. Otherwise $|z|=2|\cos(\pi(a-b)/m)|\ge 2\sin(\pi/m)$. Thus an absolute error below $1/m$ suffices to choose the true lift with $O(k)$ bits of precision. The required input-only operation would be to approximate the *hidden* symmetric sum. Equivalently on a distinct semiprime one could approximate $\sum_{d\mid N}\zeta_m^d$ and subtract the known terms at $1,N$. The numerical precision is affordable; the earlier splitting data do not provide this complex value.

The comparison settles a fixed splitting-pattern proposal. It does not give a uniform estimate for fields chosen from the actual product, a bound on positive-dimensional Frobenius traces or an impossibility result for implicit high-degree arithmetic. An input-dependent computation on integer representatives needs its own readout and cost analysis.
