# Finding small conductors in a batch of Shanks cubics

Given public Shanks root certificates, integer gcds, cube tests and linear algebra over $\mathbf F_3$ recover **every combination unramified outside a chosen small-prime set**. The computation requires no factorization of the large discriminants. For $N=1247$ two fields of conductors 37 and 259 give a field of conductor 7. For independent uniform public seeds, we prove that even a polynomial number in $\log N$ almost surely produces no such combination at a polylogarithmic prime bound. This identifies the missing input as a supply of correlated certificates. [Root construction](shanks-split-seeds.md) · [Implementation and tests](shanks_conductor_relations.py)

## One field and repeated parameter values

Write $\Delta_t=t^2+3t+9$. For every prime $r\ne3$, the conductor of the Shanks field $K_t$ contains $r$ exactly when $v_r(\Delta_t)\not\equiv0\pmod3$, in which case its exponent is 1. Its 3-part is 9 precisely when $3\mid t$ and $t\not\equiv12\pmod{27}$. To decide whether the conductor is $\le B$ for $B\ge3$, strip from $\Delta_t$ every prime $\le B$ and form the predicted small part by this formula. Return it if it is $\le B$ and the residual integer is a perfect cube. This test costs $\operatorname{poly}(B,\log(2+|t|))$. At $t=1259$ it recognizes conductor 7 while leaving $61^3$ unfactored. [Gil-Muñoz and Tinková, Proposition 2.7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-024-01005-2)

Normalize an integral parameter by the involution $t\mapsto-t-3$ to $t\ge-1$. Okazaki's theorem, with a second proof by Hoshi, gives the only fields with several normalized parameters. The lists are $\{-1,5,12,1259\}$, $\{0,3,54\}$, $\{1,66\}$ and $\{2,2389\}$. Thus a large parameter cannot usually be replaced by a different small integral Shanks parameter defining the same field. This statement concerns integral Shanks parameters. Rational parameters can have infinite fibers. [Hoshi, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.3374)

There are $O(C)$ cyclic cubic fields of conductor $\le C$. In fact the conductor is $m$ or $9m$ for squarefree $m$ supported on primes $1\bmod3$. The local choices of primitive order-three characters on $m$ number $2^{\omega(m)}$, with two more choices at 9 if present. The same count $2^{\omega(m)}$ enumerates ideals of norm $m$ in $\mathbf Z[\omega]$, where $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$. This Euclidean ring has $O(C)$ ideals of norm $\le C$ by counting lattice vectors $a+b\omega$ with $a^2-ab+b^2\le C$. Each cyclic field accounts for at most eight integral Shanks parameters by the classification above. Each good centered parameter for $N=pq$, $p,q>3$ distinct, has nine preimages among seeds modulo $N$. Hence the probability that a uniform seed gives **any** field of conductor $\le C$ is $O(C/N)$, including all possible discriminant-index cancellations. [Kronecker–Weber and conductors](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf) · [Nine-seed count](shanks-split-seeds.md#why-targeting-small-coefficients-costs-work)

## All simultaneous cancellations

For $\beta_t=t-3\omega$ put $\gamma_t=\beta_t/\overline{\beta_t}$. The Shanks equation gives $((x-\omega)/(x-\bar\omega))^3=\gamma_t$, and the substitution is invertible. Thus adjoining $\sqrt[3]{\gamma_t}$ to $\mathbf Q(\omega)$ gives $K_t\mathbf Q(\omega)$. A vector $e\in\mathbf F_3^m$ specifies the product $\prod_i\gamma_{t_i}^{e_i}$ in the Kummer group and a cubic character up to the common choice of orientation, possibly trivial. Both true primes split under this product whenever they split in all the supplied fields. At $r\mid\Delta_t$, $r\ne3$, the two roots of $\Delta_T$ modulo $r$ are distinct and sum to $-3$. The choice of root $t\bmod r$ determines which of the two Eisenstein primes divides $\beta_t$. Consequently its Kummer valuations at the two primes are $\pm v_r(\Delta_t)$. Tame ramification disappears exactly when that valuation is zero modulo 3.

The following algorithm computes the full space of vectors unramified at every prime $>B$, where $B\ge3$.

1. Strip all primes $\le B$ from each $\Delta_{t_i}$, leaving tails $A_i$. Form a coprime basis for all $A_i$ together with every nonzero $|t_i-t_j|$. To compute it, take the distinct input integers greater than 1. On finding two distinct members $a,b$ with $g=\gcd(a,b)>1$, replace them by the distinct nonunits among $g,a/g,b/g$. Every old member is still a product of new ones. The sum of the logarithms of the distinct basis members drops by at least $\log2$ at each refinement. The resulting pairwise coprime integers need not be prime.
2. For a basis member $d$ appearing in a tail, write $a_i$ for its exponent in $A_i$. Choose a reference $t_j$ with $a_j>0$. Wherever $a_i>0$, put $s_i=1$ if $t_i=t_j\bmod d$ and $s_i=-1$ otherwise. The sign works simultaneously at every prime dividing $d$. If a nonzero difference contains one such prime, its representation in the basis forces it to contain $d$. The alternative root throughout is then $-3-t_j$.
3. A rational prime $r\mid d$ has signed local valuation $v_r(d)\sum_i e_i s_i a_i$ up to an overall sign. If $d$ is a perfect cube every such valuation vanishes modulo 3. Otherwise some $v_r(d)$ is nonzero modulo 3, so impose the single equation $\sum_i e_i s_i a_i=0$ over $\mathbf F_3$. The kernel of all these equations is exactly the desired space. Every step takes polynomial time in $m,B$ and the input bit lengths.

For the small tame primes repeat step 2 at each known prime itself. A nonzero sum includes the prime in the conductor. At 3 let $\pi=1-\omega$. Divide numerator and denominator of $\gamma_t$ by the common power of $\pi$ so both are units. Modulo $\pi^3$ their quotient has form $\pm\omega^{h_t}$. For $t\bmod27$ the exponent is 1 at $3,6,15,24$, 2 at $0,9,18,21$ and zero elsewhere. This finite calculation uses $\pi^2=-3\omega$ and $v_\pi(\beta_t)\le3$, so $t\bmod27$ suffices. The wild conductor contributes 9 exactly when $\sum_i e_i h_{t_i}\ne0\bmod3$. To see the criterion, in an unramified cubic extension a cube root of a base-field unit reduces to $\pm1$ because the cube map is a residue-field automorphism. Its cube is then $\pm1\bmod\pi^3$. Conversely, for $u=1+\pi^3 c$ the substitution $X=1+\pi v$ into $X^3=u$ yields a monic equation reducing to $v^3-v-c$. This is separable, so its roots lift over an unramified extension. A sign is itself a cube. A resulting conductor of 1 gives the trivial character by Kronecker–Weber. This computes each supported vector's exact conductor, while minimizing its numerical value over a large kernel is a separate search.

For $N=1247$, the seeds 63 and 66 produce $t=-7,41$ with $\Delta_t=37,1813=7^2\cdot37$. Their tame conductors are 37 and 259. The roots at 37 are opposite orientations since $-7+41+3=37$. The vector $(1,1)$ cancels 37 and retains 7, so both prime factors belong to $\{1,6\}\bmod7$. At $N=2813$, seeds 78 and 111 give $t=-44,-7$, now with equal orientation at 37. Their difference gives the same conductor 7. The factors 29 and 97 occupy opposite allowed classes. These fixtures illustrate strict compression of the two particular input fields. The implementation also compares 3,240 candidate combinations with fully factored tame valuation vectors. [Executable checks](shanks_conductor_relations.py)

## Why independent seeds almost surely have no usable cancellation

Fix $a,d,k>0$. For $N=pq$ as above draw at most $(\log N)^d$ independent uniform seeds and retain successful parameters. Except on an event of probability $O_{a,d,k}((\log N)^{-k})$, **each retained row has a private ramified prime greater than $(\log N)^a$**. Hence every nonzero combination ramifies at a prime above that bound. The independence hypothesis leaves structured sampling open.

Choose $A>\max(1,a,2d+k+1)$ and $Y=(\log N)^A$. One row has conductor $\le\sqrt N$ with probability $O(N^{-1/2})$ by the field count above. If a larger conductor is $Y$-smooth, its squarefree tame part exceeds $\sqrt N/9$. For large $N$ it thus has a squarefree divisor $D\in[N^{1/4},N^{1/4}Y]$ supported at primes $\le Y$, obtained by multiplying prime factors until the lower threshold is reached. Each $D$ divides $\Delta_t$. For fixed such $D$, the CRT gives at most $2^{\omega(D)}$ parameter residues, and the seed mass of a single centered $t$ is at most $9/N$. As $D<N$, the event costs at most $18\,2^{\omega(D)}/D$.

Take $\epsilon=1/(2A)$. Summing that estimate with Rankin's elementary bound gives
$$
 \sum_{\substack{D\ge N^{1/4}\text{ squarefree}\\p\mid D\Rightarrow p\le Y}}
 \frac{2^{\omega(D)}}D
 \le N^{-\epsilon/4}\prod_{p\le Y}(1+2p^{-1+\epsilon})
 \le N^{-1/(8A)+o(1)}.
$$
The product bound follows even by summing $u^{-1+\epsilon}$ over all integers $u\le Y$, since $Y^\epsilon=\sqrt{\log N}$. A union bound over the rows retains this polynomial decay.

Finally, if two valid parameters share a prime $r>Y$ in their norms then $r\mid(t_i-t_j)(t_i+t_j+3)$. The two exact equalities $t_i=t_j$ and $t_i=-3-t_j$ together have probability at most $18/N$ for an independent pair. Otherwise $r\le N+3$ by centeredness. For fixed $r$, the per-row probability that $r\mid\Delta_t$ is at most $18(1/r+1/N)$, which squares under independence. Summing over $Y<r\le N+3$, even over all integers as an upper bound, costs $O(Y^{-1}+N^{-1})$. A union bound over pairs and the choice of $A$ finish the proof. The challenge left by this argument is to construct correlated public roots that reliably share the necessary large-prime valuations while leaving a small, nontrivial character.

## Rational lifts reduce the uniform norm bound from quadratic to linear

The seed supplies $t\bmod N$. Instead of lifting it to an integer, take a shortest nonzero vector $(a,b)$ of the lattice $a=tb\bmod N$ in the positive quadratic form
$$
 Q(a,b)=a^2+3ab+9b^2=\operatorname{Norm}(a-3b\omega).
$$
Its complex embedding has covolume $(3\sqrt3/2)N$. Gauss reduction in dimension two gives $Q(a,b)\le(2/\sqrt3)(3\sqrt3/2)N=3N$ in polynomial bit time. The bound follows directly because two reduced basis vectors have angle between $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$, and the second is at least as long as the first. A vector with $b=0$ costs at least $N^2$, so choose its sign to make $b>0$. A nonunit $b$ gives a proper gcd since $Q\ge27b^2/4$ bounds $b<N$. Otherwise make $(a,b)$ primitive. Its rational value reduces to $t$ modulo $N$ and the same modular root certifies that each input prime splits in the rational Shanks specialization, or the field is trivial.

For a primitive pair, each prime $r\ne3$ dividing $Q$ divides only one of $a-3b\omega$ and its conjugate. Otherwise $r\mid b$ and $r\mid a$. Its Kummer valuation gives the same tame-conductor rule as above. At 3 the wild coordinate is zero if $3\mid b$ and otherwise is $h_{a/b\bmod27}$. When it is nonzero, $3\mid a$ and $9\mid Q$. Thus **the full conductor is at most $3N$**, compared with $O(N^2)$ for centered integral parameters. A cube test after removing powers of 3, together with the wild coordinate, detects a trivial field without factoring $Q$. On all good seeds at $N=713,1247,2813$ the checker found 144 proper gcds, 135 trivial specializations and 3,852 nontrivial fields within the bound.

The linear scale arises from a count of small norms. Only $O(T)$ pairs have $Q(a,b)\le T$, so the event that *any* primitive admissible lift achieves that bound has probability $O(T/N)$. Allowing hidden cube factors, suppose $Q\le3N$ and the conductor is $\le C$. Write $Q=3^j g h w^3$ where $g$ is the squarefree tame conductor and $h\mid g$. Primitivity gives $j\in\{0,2,3\}$, using $Q/9=(a/3)^2+(a/3)b+b^2$ when $3\mid a$. For each $g\le C$ there are at most $\tau(g)(3N/g)^{1/3}$ choices for $(h,w)$. Each resulting norm has at most $6\tau(Q)=N^{o(1)}$ Eisenstein vectors. Summing over $g$ and using the nine-seed bound gives
$$
 \Pr[\exists\text{ an admissible rational lift with }Q\le3N,\ \operatorname{cond}\le C]
 \le N^{o(1)}(C/N)^{2/3},\qquad 1\le C\le3N.
$$
This includes trivial rational specializations by taking $C=1$.

The private-prime theorem extends to any rule selecting one such short rational lift separately for each independent seed. For a squarefree tame $D$ supported on split primes, a primitive vector with $D\mid Q$ belongs to one of at most $2^{\omega(D)}$ Eisenstein ideals of norm $D$. Each ideal is a rotated and scaled Eisenstein lattice, with $O(N/D)$ vectors in the norm disk $Q\le3N$ whenever $D\le3N$. Hence the fixed-$D$ probability remains $O(2^{\omega(D)}/D)$. For two independent rows the shared-prime probability above $Y$ is directly $O(\sum_{r>Y}r^{-2})=O(1/Y)$. Replace the single-row $O(N^{-1/2})$ small-conductor estimate by $N^{-1/3+o(1)}$ using $C=\sqrt N$ above. The previous Rankin and union bounds then apply without change. The rational representation improves the guaranteed conductor size, while reaching a polylogarithmic conductor by mixing still requires a source of dependence between the rows.
