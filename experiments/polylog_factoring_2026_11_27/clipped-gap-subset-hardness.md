# Two prescribed one-sided gap queries decide Subset Sum

There is a deterministic polynomial-time reduction from positive-weight Subset Sum to **two nonadaptive comparisons in the original clipped modular-hyperbola reduction**. It supplies the complete factorization of the odd modulus, uses dyadic lower widths and uses the original fixed upper half and thresholds. Both queries satisfy the promise. Their Boolean OR is the Subset Sum answer. Thus a deterministic polynomial-bit evaluator correct on every prescribed query implies $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$. A randomized polynomial-time evaluator with bounded error on each promised query would imply $\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{BPP}$, by amplifying the two answers before taking their OR. The construction uses a growing number of distinct prime factors and specially chosen public query parameters. It gives no hardness conclusion for two-prime inputs or for the reduction's distribution of random trials. The proof uses the archive's signed CRT encoding of subset weights. Two further properties are needed. Every nonstationary row of the **original** clipped count must have small total effect, and a positive instance with any number of solutions must cross the specified threshold against the canonical reference. Earlier bounded quadratic-congruence hardness and exact window-count results provide the starting encoding. ## The original query

Let $N$ be odd, $c$ a unit in $[0,N)$, $H=(N-1)/2$, and $j$ the public upper digit. The lower window $W$ selects a consecutive cyclic interval of residues $a\bmod N$. For each unit $a$, let $C_j(a)$ count the $N$ lifts $x=a+Ny\bmod N^2$ whose phase $x+(c+Nj)^2/x\bmod N^2$ has upper digit in $[0,H]$. Form $D(j)$ by summing $C_j(a)-C_0(a)$ over $W$ with the public affine sign, excluding the public stationary residues $\pm c$. For polarity $\sigma=\pm1$ the one-sided answer is zero for $\sigma D(j)\le N/4$, one for $\sigma D(j)\ge3N/4$, and unrestricted in between. The known additive constants in the packed comparison are computed from the public parameters.

Put $g(a)=\gcd(a^2-c^2,N)$. The lift expansion makes the upper digits one residue class modulo $g(a)$, each entry repeated $g(a)$ times. Thus $|C_j(a)-C_0(a)|\le g(a)$, and the difference vanishes when $g(a)=1$. If $g(a)=N$, write $a=cu\bmod N$, $u^2=1$, and let $U^2=1\bmod N^2$ be its unique lift with the same CRT signs. The phase is constant over the block and equals $2(c+Nj)U\bmod N^2$. This follows by evaluating at the stationary lift $x=(c+Nj)U$. ## Place all solutions in one narrow window

Given positive weights with sum $S$ and target $0<T<S$, append a marker of weight $2S+1$ and add this weight to the target. Write $S',T'$ for the resulting sum and target, $k$ for the new number of weights, and $s_*=2T'-S'$. Every solution uses the marker. Neither the empty nor the full subset solves the instance. Boundary and out-of-range targets are decided directly.

Choose a large integer scale $L$, distinct odd primes $p_i$, and powers $q_i=p_i^{e_i}>1000kL$. A deterministic choice and its bit cost are given below. Put $N=\prod_i q_i$. For weight $w_i$, choose the nearest integer $v_i$ to $q_iw_i/L$ coprime to $p_i$. Its rounding error is at most $3/2$. Set

$$\theta_i=(N/q_i)v_i,\qquad c=\sum_i\theta_i\pmod N.$$

The roots of $a^2=c^2\bmod N$ are the $2^k$ distinct signed sums $a_\epsilon=\sum_i\epsilon_i\theta_i\bmod N$. On the unit circle $a_\epsilon/N$ is within $1/(500L)$ of $\sum_i\epsilon_iw_i/L$. Distinct signed totals differ by at least two. Take $w$ to be the largest power of two at most $N/(4L)$ and center a consecutive interval of $w$ residues at $Ns_*/L$, rounding its position to the nearest integer. Its fractional width is between $1/(8L)$ and $1/(4L)$. It contains exactly the stationary residues encoding solutions. Endpoint rounding costs at most $1/N$ and is smaller than the margins here. The public roots $\pm c$ are outside it. Every selected sign vector has positive marker sign. Use affine multiplier $1$ and sign offset equal to the isolation offset, so every residue in the lower window has positive sign.

## Bound the genuine nonstationary rows

For $d\mid N$, the congruence $a^2=c^2\bmod d$ defines $2^{\omega(d)}$ residue classes modulo $d$. Each extends to a stationary residue modulo $N$ by retaining its signs at the prime powers dividing $d$ and choosing any missing signs. All stationary residues divided by $N$ lie within $(S'+1)/L$ of zero on the circle. The lower window also lies in that arc with the same bound. For $h=N/d$, a point congruent to a stationary residue modulo $d$ either coincides with it or has circle distance at least $1/h$ after division by $N$. Consequently a nonstationary point in the window with $g(a)=d$ must satisfy $d/N\le 8(S'+1)/L$.

A width-$w$ interval meets a single progression modulo $d$ in at most $w/d+1$ places. Summing the preceding absolute block bound, uniformly for every upper digit, bounds the entire nonstationary contribution $E_j$ by

$$\frac{|E_j|}{N}\le
\sum_{\substack{d\mid N,\ 1<d<N\\ d/N\le 8(S'+1)/L}}
2^{\omega(d)}\left(\frac wN+\frac dN\right)
\le \prod_i(1+2e_i)\frac{1/4+8(S'+1)}{L}<\frac1{10}.$$

This also deals with divisors that stop partway through a prime power. The last inequality follows from the choices below.

## Put all solution phases on the same side

For each of two queries prescribe a common target fraction for the marker component, respectively $+1/4$ and $-1/4$, and target zero at every other component. Let $B_i=N^2/q_i^2$. Pick an integer $h_i$ in the class $h_i=2cB_i^{-1}\bmod q_i$ nearest to $q_i^2$ times its prescribed fraction. Then $h_i/q_i^2$ is within $1/(2q_i)$ of that fraction. Compute $A=\sum_i B_i h_i\bmod N^2$ and $c'=A/2\bmod N^2$, using modular division. Since $A=2c\bmod q_i$ for all $i$, the canonical $c'$ equals $c+Nj$ for a legitimate public upper digit $j$.

At a stationary root with signs $\epsilon_i$, the normalized phase $2c'U/N^2$ is $\sum_i\epsilon_i h_i/q_i^2\bmod1$. Every selected root has marker sign $+1$. All selected phases are therefore close to $1/4$ in the first query and $3/4$ in the second, within $\sum_i1/(2q_i)$. The first query puts every selected stationary block inside the upper gate and the second puts every one outside.

Let $K$ be the number of solutions and $E$ the number whose stationary phase falls in the upper gate at the fixed reference $j=0$. Take positive polarity on the first query and negative on the second. Their normalized stationary contributions are the integers $K-E$ and $E$. Each is perturbed by less than $1/10$. Each query is consequently on a promised side of the threshold. If $K=0$ both answers are zero. If $K>0$ at least one answer is one. As a separate consequence, numerical estimates of both unnormalized comparisons to absolute error $N/8$ give the exact count $K$ by summing the signed contrasts, dividing by $N$ and rounding. The total error is less than $2(1/10+1/8)<1/2$. This counting consequence requires numerical answers.

## Size of the deterministic construction

For example let $t=k+\lceil\log_2(S'+1)\rceil+10$, $\ell=128t^2$ and $L=2^\ell$. Choose $k$ primes exceeding $P_0=2^{50}(t+1)^{10}$ and at most $P_0^2$. An ordinary sieve of that interval is polynomial in the source input length since $P_0$ itself is polynomial in that length. Elementary prime-counting bounds give enough primes in the interval. Choose the least powers $q_i>1000kL$, so $q_i\le1000kLP_0^2$ and $e_i\le1+\log_2(1000kL)$. In particular $\log_2\prod_i(1+2e_i)\le k\log_2(3+2\log_2(1000kL))<\ell/2$. Also $1/4+8(S'+1)<2^{\ell/2}/10$. These bounds ensure the claimed error. The modulus has $O(k(\ell+\log P_0))$ bits. The chosen primes exceed even the initial trial-division ceiling $2^{31}(1+\operatorname{bitlength}(N))$ of the original reduction. The large constants simplify the proof. The small computational fixtures check the arithmetic without using this preprocessing ceiling.

There is also a squarefree version with randomized expected-polynomial preprocessing. Choose $L\ge\max(128(S'+1),1024\cdot3^k)$ and draw distinct primes $p_i>1000kL$, with polynomial bit length, using a primality test. They may also be required to exceed the preprocessing ceiling. Here every proper $d\mid N$ satisfies $d/N\le1/p_{\min}$. Summing over the proper divisors gives $|E_j|/N\le 3^k(w/N+1/p_{\min})<1/10$. The same two upper digits work.

### A fixed lower scale and an aligned binary interval

The squarefree construction can also fix $c=1$. Take the original $r$ weights of sum $S$ and $0<T<S$. Use the archive's aligned-interval construction with a power of two $L\ge\max(16(S+1),1024\cdot3^{r+1})$. Its $r$ core primes and one auxiliary prime $P$ give a squarefree $N$. A power of two $H_0<N$ sets $D=2H_0/L$. The interval $[TD,(T+1)D)$ contains exactly one root of unity per subset of weight $T$. Every such root has local sign $-1$ at $P$, and the interval excludes $\pm1$. Since $D/N<2/L$ and all prime factors exceed $32rL$, the preceding squarefree bound gives $|E_j|/N\le 3^{r+1}(2/L+1/(32rL))<1/10$. Use $P$ as the marker in the two upper-digit queries, prescribing $-1/4$ there for the inside query and $+1/4$ for the outside query. Both decision and numerical counting conclusions follow with this fixed lower scale and aligned interval. [Signed-root locations in the aligned interval](fixed-one-window-hardness.md#programming-the-crt-coefficients)

The archive samples $P$ in a specified short interval and residue class. Its expected-time proof uses Drappeau and Fiorilli's prime-distribution estimate with a possible exceptional character and two coprime core products, so that at least one core avoids the exceptional conductor. To enforce the original trial-division cutoff at every stage, use $B=2^{100(r+\log_2L+1)}$ as the base for the two sets of core primes in the disjoint intervals $[2^iB,2^{i+1}B)$, $1\le i\le2r$. For the larger core product $R$, set $h=\lceil\log_2(2R)\rceil^3$ and $H_0=2^h$. Then $\operatorname{bitlength}(N)\le h+1$ and every core prime, as well as $P>H_0/R$, exceeds $2^{31}(h+2)$. After $h^2$ unsuccessful auxiliary-prime trials, replace $B$ by $B^2$ and draw fresh cores. The cited estimate gives failure probability $\exp(-\Omega(h))$ per sufficiently large stage. Squaring $B$ doubles its bit length, so this rule preserves expected polynomial bit time. ## Checks and scope

On the instance with five unit weights and target two, the two normalized stationary contributions were $6$ and $4$. Their sum recovered all ten subsets. An unsatisfiable instance returned two zeros. Seven squarefree or repeated-power controls used exact counts of each gcd stratum to certify the off-root error without enumerating the large lower window. The default proof-sized construction for weights $(1,2)$ and target $2$ produced a 111,121-bit modulus. Its smallest prime exceeded the original preprocessing cutoff, and its normalized off-root error bound was below $2^{-36953}$. The code uses a smaller sufficient polynomial prime floor and verifies that cutoff directly. Two literal packed counts and 35 direct contrasts at small moduli checked compatibility with the original query.

The hard width is much smaller than the width calibrated to the actual number of prime factors, and the window and upper digits are chosen to encode the instance. The principal deterministic construction also chooses the lower scale. The forward randomized reduction needs sufficiently accurate upper-digit responses on enough trials that isolate a mixed root. Accuracy on mostly empty windows leaves that condition open. Under $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ the deterministic all-query target is closed. The distribution of useful trials and the semiprime search path remain candidates for a native algorithm.
