Consultant C — linked modules over a fixed small field

Result. There is a height obstruction for the relative-rank-m case that survives vanishing minors and an infinite unit group. At the proposed L[1/4] balance its subtractive error is O_k(m log n), which is o(x^{3/4}). This kills the cheap-generator construction for the reduced type-D pair. Sparse selection inside a high-height module remains open. Sparse common polynomials supplied fresh smooth ideal rows at toy sizes. All three searches yielded zero fresh rows at the aggregate threshold on every 84- and 96-bit input.

1. The open case and its ledger

Let x=log N and write L[β]=exp(Θ(x^β(log x)^{1−β})). Fix a number field k of degree e with positive unit rank. Take K,K′ of degree n over k, d=en and D=2d. Two integral O_k-orders have unital evaluations to Z/N agreeing on O_k. There are m≥3 k-linearly independent generator pairs with equal evaluations; r=em is the absolute rank. In the proposed escape m grows. After the repeated-subfield and coincident-side reductions, the ratios of the generators on each side generate that field, and the two projective tuples are distinct. The generators may have arbitrary algebraic dependencies. Common base-field units act on both sides and preserve ideal rows. See notes/rankdegen_results.md:126,164 and notes/grdeg_results.md:55.

G_r combines growing-degree point counting with an N^{−(r−1)} linking probability for a common projective evaluation. With total height N^h the predicted upper count is N^{(d+r−1)h−(r−1)}. At h≈1/d it predicts scarcity. A constructive height escape needs correlations strong enough to change the power of r in the height cost from 1 to c<1. Grouping coordinates over a fixed k leaves m−1 relative projective matches, still linear in r. The other escape is through counts inside a single module. In general position the volume term is C_r N^{−r(r−1)/(D(D−1))}Y^{r/D}. H5_r requires r(D+1)(2E+1)^{r−1}≤B/4 for the box error. In the yield-relevant ternary box that condition fails. An escape needs enough accessible, independent smooth rows among the exceptional small values after removing lower-rank and multiplicative contributions. The two claims concern different populations. See notes/resbarrier_results.md:338, notes/rank3_results.md:197 and notes/rankdegen_results.md:506.

For coefficients in {−1,0,1}, C=r log 3 is the log candidate count. Up to log factors, log X has terms r^c x/d and (D/r)C. With B=L[β], u=log X/log B, the usual yield budget is C≳log B+u log u. Enumeration and sparse linear algebra cost exp(C)+B^{2+o(1)}. Balancing with r=x^{α+o(1)}, β=α and d=x^{δ+o(1)} gives δ=(1+cα)/2 and α=1/(4−c). At c=0 the refined typical-size term d log r gives d≈√(x/log x), r≈x^{1/4}(log x)^{3/4}, log X≈√(x log x), and u≈(x/log x)^{1/4}, up to constant factors. The projected cost is L[1/4]. At c=1 it is L[1/3]. The arithmetic is exact at exponent level. Both algorithmic endpoints use the project's smoothness and relation-rank model. See notes/grdeg_results.md:84, notes/midk_results.md:161 and notes/suffmap_results.md:157.

2. A determinant obstruction [proved]

Write w_j=(u_j,u′_j) for integral generators. At each tagged absolute embedding i put M_i=max_j |i(u_j)| on its side, and H=Σ_i log M_i. Let s be the larger of the two projection dimensions over k, so m/2≤s≤m. Let C_k≥1 bound the absolute values of all conjugates of a fixed integral basis of k. For the reduced pair above,

         T=2 ceil(2n/s)+1,           H ≥ [x−D log(C_k D)]/T.

When both projections have dimension m, one can sharpen the numerator to x−en log(2n). No unramifiedness, squarefreeness or general-position hypothesis enters.

Proof. Choose a vector w in the k-span nonzero on both sides. Divide that span coordinatewise by w to obtain V containing 1. The reduced-pair hypothesis says k[V]=K×K′. Indeed a subalgebra surjecting onto two fields is either their product or the graph of an isomorphism. This division is only for computing dimensions. All index and height estimates below use original integral monomials.

On the larger projection let U be the image of V, with dim U=s and k[U]=K after relabelling. Powers denote spans of products. If W=U^{j+1} is proper, its stabilizer J={a:aW⊆W} is a proper subfield. Write h=[J:k]. Apply linear Kneser to U^j and JU. Their product span is W, so dim W−dim U^j≥dim JU−h. Since 1∈U and U generates K, dim JU≥max(s,2h). The gain is at least s/2. Thus U^t=K by t=ceil(2n/s). The field inequality used here is Hou–Leung–Xiang, also proved by Bachoc–Serra–Zémor, Theorem 2 and its stabilizer formulation. https://upcommons.upc.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/f813e05f-971a-41dc-9ade-72ffa6f1b0ba/content

Now V^t has dimension ≥n. If it has dimension n it must grow at the next step, since equality would give a V-stable space containing 1 and hence the whole product. Therefore V^{t+1} contains a nonzero (z,0). Multiplying by V^t supplies K×0 in V^T. Undo the normalization. The k-span S of the original degree-T monomials still contains K×0. For full projections it is the whole product.

Put Λ=S∩(O×O′). The difference of the evaluations maps Λ onto Z/N because (1,0)∈Λ. Choose a k-basis of S from the monomials. Its O_k-span Γ lies in the kernel, so [Λ:Γ] is a multiple of N.

For the volume estimate put H_i=Σ log M over each entire side i. Each H_i≥0 by the nonzero integral norm of a generator on that side. Rescale each embedding coordinate by (exp(H_i/d)/M)^T. The product of the scale factors on each side is one. Every nonzero vector of the rescaled Λ has Euclidean length ≥1 by the norm on a nonzero side, counting both complex embeddings. In its q-dimensional real span (q≤D), disjoint balls of radius 1/2 give covolume ≥D^{−q/2} by inscribing a cube. The rescaled Γ has a basis of integral-basis multiples of the chosen monomials. Divide its rows on side i by exp(TH_i/d); each basis column now has length ≤C_k√D. Restoring these row factors increases any q-volume by at most exp(TH), since all factors are ≥1. Hadamard gives N D^{−q/2}≤covol(Γ)≤exp(TH)(C_k√D)^q. This proves the bound. For full projections, use a 2n-square embedding matrix at each embedding of k. The product of its absolute determinants is ≥N by the same index and the relative discriminant formula. Row-wise Hadamard gives the sharper numerator.

Consequences. The main term is Ω(mx/n). Its error is O_k(m log n). The aggregate target H=x^{1/2+o(1)} is excluded when the main term is x^{3/4+o(1)}. The single additional assumption for the height-to-yield step is that the yield-producing box points have the typical norm size charged by this envelope and the degree term [heuristic]. For a precise surviving exception, group points of |F|≤Y whose log absolute values at each embedding lie in a common interval of width w_i, after any chosen common-k-unit rescalings of each point. If this group contains m independent points, their envelope is ≤log Y+Σ w_i. Applying the theorem to them forces that quantity above the proved floor. Each narrower group therefore lies in a proper k-subspace. Bounding how many such groups supply fresh rows is still open.

3. Construction and measurements

Sixteen balanced fixtures have N=A²−2, giving √2↦A from N alone. Eight further split semiprimes grant one root A by CRT in the harness. The latter require advice; some general N admit no map for this fixed k. The selectors see no factors. Over k=Q(√2), base-b expansion produces monic P with P(b)=A. Take Q=P+j(T−b)(T²+1)(T−1), testing that P²−2 and Q²−2 are irreducible. Both sides evaluate T↦b. The divided differences use the polynomial quotients of P and Q by T²+1 and T−1; congruent remainders give identical evaluations by polynomial identity, even at a noninvertible denominator. A second selector uses exact LLL on the full index-N linking lattice, weighted by rounded conjugate coordinates. A third minimizes the envelope over common sparse polynomials, including lacunary powers. Every non-power test passed dim_k U²>2m−1 on both sides, which excludes even a scalar multiple of a consecutive power basis.

For an explicit 96-bit example take N=75412582492814395416229545223, A=274613514767235, b=63 and j=1. The coefficients of P from constant to leading term are (0,−6,−30,26,23,10,−18,7,1). At m=3, all 80 points under the aggregate cap were already in the first two coordinates. The eight-dimensional relative fields had quadratic product-space dimensions 6 on each side.

All three share the ledger in §1 and its speculative favourable-correlation assumption. Their absolute embedding rows split into two complementary blocks over the embeddings of k; the absolute frame bound does not apply. At each bit length l the finite normalizations were B=2^ceil((l ceil(log₂ l)^3)^{1/4}), X₀=3^D 2^ceil(l/d), X₁=3^D 2^ceil((r−1)l/(D−1)). X₁ is a G_r-scale comparator. G_r predicts no exact finite relation rank.

Including eight binary controls, 128 boxes on 24 moduli used 437,920 nonzero coefficient evaluations. For η=log(1+incremental rank)/(r log 3), the sparse capped range was 0–0.167. All three candidates had η=0 at 84 and 96 bits under X₀. The held-out (n,m) schedule (7,3),(8,3),(9,4),(10,4) gave the same failure at the two largest sizes. These finite exponents measure independent row yield; the experiments cannot determine an asymptotic factoring exponent.

4. Ranking

No algorithm survived the preregistered screens. I put the residual type-D sparse-selection possibility below 0.5% for a general-N heuristic sub-third algorithm. No separate mechanism clears that threshold in my assessment. In particular, even free construction of one smooth integer per short interval leaves a residual smoothness target with log X=x^{1−β+o(1)}, B=L[β], and L[max(β,1−2β)] candidate cost in the single-interval model. Its optimum is 1/3. See notes/suffmap_results.md:318,337. Theorem J's adaptive gap at notes/sihard_results.md:425 leaves the construction-cost question open.
