Second experiment, registered after the divided-difference results and before running any lattice reduction or measuring this design.

Hypothesis. The first family wastes height on its prescribed denominators. Reducing the full integral congruence lattice in an archimedean metric might recover genuinely non-power tuples with fresh smooth rows. This is a rescue test on the same eight fixtures, not an independent sample. If possible also use eight separately seeded balanced split semiprimes with a square root of 2 supplied by the diagnostic fixture generator through CRT. On those cases report the root advice explicitly. Use the same n, ternary boxes, B, X0, X1, projective deduplication, rank quotient and finite exponent as the first registration. Use matched power controls on each field pair.

Algorithm. Use both absolute order bases {T^j,sqrt(2)T^j}, 0<=j<n. One coordinate has image 1, hence provides a triangular integral basis for the lattice of linked pairs of index N. Embed a vector by its conjugate values, rounding real and imaginary parts to a fixed common dyadic grid, and append the original coefficient vector to the lattice metric. Use exact integer LLL (PARI or FLINT); certified balls for the initial grid. Start the tuple at (1,1) and (T,T). Try reduced rows in increasing embedded Euclidean length, then sums and differences of the first sixteen rows if needed. Pick the first passing pair for each new coordinate. Require full projection rank and dim of the quadratic product space >2m-1 on *each* side. To avoid a projectively genuine tuple made solely from unrelated basis shifts in a common power parameter, also require a new coordinate that disagrees between the two rational polynomials in the absolute primitive element, in at least one of the new coordinates. Never inspect candidate smoothness when choosing generators. Cap the rescue search at the declared pool. Report unreached ranks. Exact full-product degree/index checks as before, plus exact linked generator residues.

Screen. Apply the original threshold (>=m new rows under X0, mixed points k-span m, >=2*max(1, matched control's new rank)) to both 84-bit and both 96-bit fixtures at one common m. Report outcomes separately by fixture class. If that test fails, any sporadic positive rank is a finite-size effect until supported by a scaling law. A general-N algorithm would additionally need the subfield evaluation at its stated cost.
