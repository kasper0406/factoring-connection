Round 2, exploratory M2 pilot, before any quadratic-form decoder trials.
Test whether selecting projective split-prime branches by the shortest positive rational representatives recovers more distinct primitive smooth quadratic values than direct sampling at an equal number of norm tests. The scan is a positive control for whether witnesses exist, not the proposed decoder. This probes a concrete cheap decoder, not a lower bound on one-witness decoding.

Use four deterministic squarefree balanced semiprimes at each of 48,64,80,96 bits. Obtain one known factor-base class per modulus by composing small split-prime forms; retain a reduced form with sqrt(N)/4 <= A <= 2 sqrt(N) to test the advertised size regime. Factorization of N is not an input. Use n=bit length, B=2^ceil((n (ceil(log2 n))^3)^(1/4)) and K=2^ceil((n^3 ceil(log2 n))^(1/4)*3/16), toy constants. For each form perform a complete primitive [1,K]^2 sieve as ground truth if below 2 million points; otherwise use a fixed first 1,024,000-point control and label it a lower bound. For the search sample consecutive distinct split primes from the base in randomly shuffled batches, combine the two roots of Q(z,1) by CRT, and rank intermediate branches by a Gauss-reduced lattice vector's sup norm. Retain four branches. At modulus >2K^2 the short primitive slope, when present, is unique. Cap at 512 batches per form, also report branch/lattice reductions (these cost more than single norm tests). Sample an equal number of distinct primitive pairs uniformly, up to exhaustion. Report unique primitive hits, plus counts before conditioning and controls. A pass requires strictly more hits at both 80 and 96 bits versus equal norm-test counts AND fewer lattice reductions per hit than direct norm tests per hit. A pass would require held-out replication, full sieve cost accounting and hidden-square-root extraction.
