# Factoring through constructive and discrete-log targets

**Finding a smooth ideal in a specified imaginary-quadratic class ranks first.** Public multipliers improved toy yields 6.8× and 3.4×. No general sub-⅓ algorithm emerged.

F denotes factoring, DLP discrete logarithm. Arrows run from solver to consequence. L[a]=L_N[a,O(1)] includes base size K and retries. Odds are subjective. Several reductions cover only N=pq.

## Ranked candidates

**1. A smooth representative of a specified class. Speculative P(a<⅓) = 0.5%.** Take fundamental D=−4kN with small factored k and d=|D|. **Proved under GRH.** Sample a nearly uniform class x and request a root z of x². Then z/x is nearly uniform in Cl(D)[2]. A reduced-form coefficient gcd splits N with probability at least ½−o(1). Given the factors, Gauss duplication computes a root in randomized polynomial time. Expected O(1) calls preserve a. Native class-group structure and DL algorithms use relations at L_d[½]. Known smooth exponents permit Pohlig–Hellman. Random class-number smoothness costs L_d[1−a] trials at bound L_d[a] (heuristic). **A reachable easy subgroup.** Put conductor N above D. In the ring-class kernel, units modulo scalar units have coordinates 1+bu with u²=0 modulo N. Multiplication adds b modulo N. The global units are ±1. Even granting free DL on arbitrary kernel encodings, the kernel has odd order N. Projection therefore identifies the two groups’ 2-torsion. The kernel projects to the principal class. NICE’s lattice shortcut needs a hidden squared conductor and a kernel hint. [genusleak:575](/Volumes/git/factoring-connection/notes/genusleak_results.md:575) [genusleak:438](/Volumes/git/factoring-connection/notes/genusleak_results.md:438) [Ramified-conductor DL](https://csrc.nist.gov/CSRC/media/Presentations/lhe-from-class-groups-of-quadratic-fields/images-media/20210616-castagnos-slides---LHE-from-class-groups-of-quadratic-fields.pdf)

**Missing lemma.** Given a reduced target form Q, find a primitive pair with B-smooth Q(x,y), B=L_d[a], in L_d[a] work across a box of side L_d[1−a]. Collect poly(B) usable relations. Factoring needs a dependency yielding hidden 2-torsion. The stronger L_d[a] class-group DLP claim needs a sufficient relation lattice and descent for arbitrary targets. One cyclic-subgroup log is insufficient. [codex/subthird_2_gemini.out:32](/Volumes/git/factoring-connection/codex/subthird_2_gemini.out:32) The cited M2 trial spent 554 lattice reductions per hit against 474 direct norm tests. [subthird:75](/Volumes/git/factoring-connection/notes/subthird_results.md:75) [pilist:106](/Volumes/git/factoring-connection/notes/pilist_results.md:106)

**Why a small box fails (proved under GRH).** For X=√d·L_d[a], the fraction of uniform classes containing a B-smooth integral ideal of norm ≤X is at most
$$
 \frac{X^s}{h(D)}\prod_{\ell\le B}(1-\ell^{-s})^{-2}.
$$
Each ideal covers one class. Each rational prime has at most two prime ideals above it. Put x=log d and s=1−c log(x)/log(B), with 0<c<1−a. The log Euler product is O(x^c log log B), while h(D)≫√d/log log d under GRH. For 0<a<½ the fraction is ≤1/L_d[1−a,Ω(1)], uniformly in fundamental D. Balanced forms on a side-L_d[a] box meet this norm cap. M2’s wider box escapes. [Class-number bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3595)

**[Pre-registered diagnostic](prereg.txt).** Ranking prime multipliers 101–199 by small split primes gave 476 versus 70 smooth norms at 16-bit primes and 17 versus 5 at 24 bits, among 62,112 per arm per size. Both cleared the 2× threshold before selection cost. Leading coefficients were prime. Target coverage, relation rank and asymptotic savings remain untested. [Counts](toy-results.txt)

**2. Isogeny neighbours modulo N. Speculative P = 0.2%.** The notes plant edges at genus-zero prime levels. **Sharper level-two promise (proved).** Choose a private uniform Legendre parameter and reveal only j. Away from collisions and exceptional j, the modular cubic splits completely at both primes. The six parameter orderings give each of three hidden neighbours twice. Conditional on j and any returned root, the local matches are independent with probability ⅓ each. A gcd therefore splits with probability exactly 4/9. Given p,q, polynomial root-finding and CRT invert the reduction. [conn_cmp.md:48](/Volumes/git/factoring-connection/notes/conn_cmp.md:48),[L121](/Volumes/git/factoring-connection/notes/conn_cmp.md:121) [Altuğ–Chen](https://iacr.org/archive/asiacrypt2019/119210106/119210106.pdf)

**Steering ledger.** Coprime-degree auxiliary neighbours with a unique common completion make a polynomial gcd linear. SIDH gives a different easy input through auxiliary torsion images. The missing construction produces such data from j alone in L[a]. Publishing the labels makes branches computable by globally consistent rational formulas. The pre-registered labelled test gave 0/4,000 splits. A separately pre-registered follow-up with private labels and a factor-assisted validation oracle gave exactly 62,752/141,192 = 4/9 over three exhaustive prime pairs. **3. An anomalous elliptic component. Speculative P = 0.1%.** A single projective count M for any fixed good curve gives F with expected polynomial overhead for every good pq. A quintic auxiliary algebra closes the earlier exception. Conversely, factor then count over the two prime fields with Schoof. On trace-one curves DL is polynomial over the known field. Cheng’s N-only specialization already splits by multiplying a point by N when 4p−1=Ds² for suitable small CM discriminant −D. **Steering ledger.** For p around P, D≤Y covers O(√(PY)) candidate integers by summing over s for each D. At Y=L[a] prime coverage is at most P^(−½+o(1)). Twisting changes trace sign. Multiplying N by a public cofactor leaves p fixed. General factoring needs an N-only sampler hitting trace one at one hidden prime with inverse-L[a] probability in L[a] work. At Y=1023 the pre-registered census covered 87/2897, 18/2354 and 2/1982 primes near 2¹⁶, 2²⁰ and 2²⁴. The last rate was 0.10%.
