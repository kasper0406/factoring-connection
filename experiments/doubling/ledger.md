# Yield ledger for the central-binomial zero test

**Outcome — measured and proved where indicated below.** No polynomial-time single-input evaluator emerged. A public hypergeometric sum computes T0 in Õ(N^(1/4)) time with a factor returned on sufficiently unbalanced inputs. The special index also gives one formula for both Lucas cross terms and a counterexample to the normalized Eisenstein test. Throughout, p<q are distinct odd primes, N=pq≡1 mod 4, and m=(N−1)/4.

## Calibration

**Proved.** For n<p, the first nonunit central binomial occurs at n=(p+1)/2. Query powers of two until the first nonunit. That power is <p, so bisection within the bracket recovers p, even if the first observed gcd is N. A Boolean oracle for N dividing the binomial has a different threshold. For equal b-bit factors, n=2^(b−1) is below p and above q/2. Bisection up to that n finds (q+1)/2. The first argument alone gives neither an unrestricted full-zero reduction nor factorial residues.

**Proved.** In class 1,1 put t_s=(s−1)/4. Since m=p t_q+t_p and 2t_p<p, Lucas gives B≡C(2t_p,t_p) C(2t_q,t_q) mod p, including when t_q has several base-p digits. The larger prime always sees a unit. In class 3,3 the low digit of m modulo either s is (3s−1)/4 and forces a carry. Thus T0 holds without balance. For N=65, B=40 has proper gcd 5 in class 1,1.

## D1. Special-index evaluations

**Built and proved — a shorter, factor-free CM sum.** Handle 3 by gcd first. Put
\[
 K=\lfloor\sqrt N/2\rfloor+1,\qquad
 T_K=\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\binom{4j}{2j}\binom{2j}{j}72^{-j}\pmod N.
\]
This is the truncation of ₂F₁(1/4,3/4;1;8/9). The term ratio is (4j−3)(4j−1)/(18j²). Accumulate its numerator, the partial sum, and the common denominator using triangular 2-by-2 matrices. If the denominator has a nonunit gcd, it gives exactly p because p<K≤q and the denominator is a product of 18j². Otherwise K≤p and q/4<K.

For s=p,q let h=(s−1)/2. In the translated curve polynomial x(x²+3x+2), the coefficient of x^(s−1) in its h-th power equals 3^h T_K modulo s. Indeed, after extracting x^h the remaining multinomial coefficient at index j is h!/(j!²(h−2j)!), times 3^(h−2j)2^j. This matches the hypergeometric term after division by 3^h. The series terminates locally at floor(s/4) since one numerator parameter is −floor(s/4) modulo s. Translation preserves this coefficient, by Lucas applied to exponents between s and 3h. On x³−x it is zero exactly when s≡3 mod 4. Thus the joint residue is zero or a unit. On q<4p the length-N CM truncation also decides T0: its base-s digit factorization at z=8/9 gives T_N≡T_s T_(N/s) mod s, and T_p≡T_q mod the larger prime q since p>q/4. The digit factorization follows from multinomial Lucas on (4j choose 2j,j,j) and Fermat on 72.

**Measured / obstruction.** The monic block product costs Õ(√K)=Õ(N^(1/4)) and uses N alone. At public equal-bit cutoff L=2^(b−1), all 48 local comparisons on 24 inputs passed. Median 64-bit time was 0.50 seconds. The uniform cutoff passed 147 small and six large inputs, returning 114 proper factors on the small set and none on the large set. The block polynomial has degree proportional to √K. All 33 direct full-length CM sums with 5≤p<q<4p and N<1500 passed, with a proper gcd of 13 at N=377. The cost bound applies to the block implementation. At z=1 the analogous full truncation is 1 mod 21, although B(21)=0. [Runnable evaluator](hypergeom.py) · [CM measurements](hypergeom.json) · [Uniform cutoff and split list](uniform.json)

**Built and proved.** For 1,1 with q<2p put d=(q−p)/4 and
\[
 A_d=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{4j-3}{4j-1}.
\]
All factors are units modulo N. With U_n=C(2n,n)/4^n and s either prime,
\[
 U_m\equiv 4a_s^2 A_d\pmod s,\qquad
 \left(\frac{B}{N}\right)=\left(\frac{(q-p-1)!!}{N}\right).
\]
Here a_s is the signed odd coordinate of s=a_s²+b_s² with a_s≡1 mod 4. Lucas and Fermat give U_m≡U_{t_p}U_{t_q} in either field. Starting at t_p≡−1/4 mod p, the forward ratio U_{t_p+j}/U_{t_p+j−1} is (4j−3)/(4j−1). Starting at t_q≡−1/4 mod q, the backward ratio U_{t_q−j}/U_{t_q−j+1} is the same. Gauss gives U_{t_s}²≡4a_s². A quadratic character agrees on a unit and its inverse, so the product contributes every odd integer below q−p. **Measured.** All 768 local identities and 384 Jacobi predictions passed. **Obstruction, proved.** Knowing d factors N because (p+q)/2=√(N+4d²). The identity locates the remaining unknown in one product. [Gauss congruence and its prime-power refinement](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~revans/ChowlaDwork.pdf)

**Built, measured, then refuted.** Write F* for specialization at (E4,E6)=(1,0), so E_k* is the pure E4 coefficient and equals E_k(i)/E4(i)^(k/4). All 35 semiprimes below 400 passed the zero test. Nevertheless, at **N=1,093,973,597=397·2,755,601**, E_(N−1)* is defined and zero modulo N, while B=900,609,246 is a unit. An independent prime sieve reproduced B.

**Proof of the counterexample.** Write G_k=c_k E_k with c_k=−Bernoulli(k)/(2k). For h≡k mod s−1, 4≤h<s−1, the weight congruence gives G_k≡E_(s−1)^((k−h)/(s−1)) G_h mod s and c_k≡c_h. At the two primes the reduced weights h are 232 and 396. Exact low-weight arithmetic gives G_h*=0 in both fields and c_h=382 and 2,166,789 respectively, both units. Hence E_k*=0 in both. The certificate computes the small forms by triangular Fourier expansion in E4 and Δ. Separately, E_27168* has a pole at 101 for N=101·269. There G_27168*=22 mod 101 and c_27168=5151 mod 101². The targeted scan also found eight one-sided numerator zeros with q≤5000 and p≤401, for example a proper gcd of 61 at N=2257. The two-prime counterexample rules out classification by the reduced numerator. [Weight congruence, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02427) · [Numerical certificate](certificate.json)

**Proved / known.** The Hasse coefficient [x^(N−1)](x³−x)^(2m) equals (−1)^m B. Vandermonde gives the exact terminating hypergeometric value ₂F₁(−m,−m;1;1)=B. Dwork's digit congruences use prime Frobenius. Replacing its exponent by N fails already on (1+x)^N, whose coefficient at x^p is q mod p. None of these replacements provided fast coefficient extraction. Factoring first and applying prime-field block factorials costs only Õ(N^(1/4)) on balanced inputs using Strassen-type factoring. The generic length-N recurrence estimate is an upper-bound comparison. [Dwork congruences](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.5811) · [Block factorial algorithms](https://specfun.inria.fr/bostan/publications/BoGaSc07.pdf)

## D2. Doubling the zero image

**Built and proved.** Let G(X) be the Gaussian binomial [2m choose m] over the integers. Form the monic gcd with (X^N−1)/(X−1). The answer is 1 in class 1,1 and Φ_p Φ_q in class 3,3. Indeed the cyclotomic exponent at e is floor(2m/e)−2floor(m/e), and among nontrivial divisors of N only p and q can occur. Evaluating the gcd at 1 gives exactly 1 or N for every factor ratio. **Obstruction, proved.** Its degree in class 3,3 gives p+q−2, hence the factors. Explicit polynomial gcd pays for that degree. A fast evaluator of its value at 1 alone remains open. A small public root order e exposes the digit at e and leaves an ordinary binomial at the quotient index by q-Lucas. **Measured.** Direct q-Pascal polynomial gcds gave the predicted degrees on all 35 small pairs. [Root-of-unity q-Lucas theorem](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/bsagan/Papers/Old/cpq-pub.pdf)

**Proved obstruction for algebraic doubling of the Boolean support.** Write H(x)=Σ 1_{s∤C(2n,n)} x^n over F_s. Carry-free digits give H(x)^(s−1)=1/L(x), where L=1+x+⋯+x^((s−1)/2). Take s≡1 mod 4, D=2^j, and a primitive Dth root ζ. The roots of the D polynomials L(ζ^i t) are disjoint and simple because (s+1)/2 is odd. Valuation at a root unique to each polynomial gives D independent Kummer classes modulo (s−1)st powers. Over the algebraic closure of the constants adjoined to t, each radical H(ζ^i t) can therefore be multiplied independently by any (s−1)st root of unity. The binary section selecting n≡r mod D is, after x=t^D, their sum weighted by ζ^(−ir)/(Dt^r). The summands belong to distinct characters, so its orbit has (s−1)^D elements. Even for s=5 a bounded-degree radical tower representing that whole section needs Ω(2^j) gates. For s≡3 mod 4, (s−1)/2 is odd. At t=ζ^(−i) the parity matrix of valuations of the L factors consists of blocks J−I of even size gcd(D,(s+1)/2) for D≥2. These blocks are invertible over F₂, so the square classes are independent. Adjoining odd-degree radicals preserves that independence, giving independent sign changes and degree at least 2^D also in this class. This bound concerns an exact generating-function state. A single promised midpoint could admit a smaller state.

**Proved.** Reflection of the recurrence on [0,2m] loses the midpoint. Modulo s each multiple of s frees a new coordinate, and after the halfway digit its coefficient vanishes until the next reset. At 2m the low digit is (s−1)/2 and at least one reset has occurred, so the two outside coordinates are independent in both classes. At m the image is zero exactly in 3,3. The full outside relation therefore cannot recover it. For the tested Lucas sequences, away from discriminant primes a zero means the eigenvalue ratio has order dividing the index. Changing the base in the digit test reads carries at the chosen base.

## D3. Amortisation

**Built and proved.** For equal b-bit factors take L=2^(b−1). The product P of j≡3 mod 4 in [L,2L) has gcd N or 1 according to T0. This interval contains both primes and no higher multiple of either. A single modular product by monic polynomial blocks of width Θ(√L) costs Õ(N^(1/4)). The batch construction forms P once and reduces it down a product tree of K moduli. Total bit cost is Õ(L+K), giving polylogarithmic cost per member at K=L. The explicit P alone has Θ(L log L) bits. Padding a single target pays this total. Multipliers k≡1 mod 4 preserve the low digits since (kN−1)/4−m is a multiple of N, and they lengthen the prefix. For shifts N+kM the shared modulus is exactly gcd(N,kM). **Measured.** Single-modulus blocks passed 24/24. A 32-bit tree gave 32,768/32,768 correct gcds, zero splits, from one 127,446-bit product. [Harvey–Sutherland remainder trees](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3246)

## D4. Registered measurements

**Measured.** At each of 32, 48 and 64 bits, 128 inputs per class gave 768 exact residues. The main evaluator took N alone, factored with FLINT, used Lucas and modular factorials, then CRT. It matched integer binomials on 147 small pairs. A factor-blind segmented sieve independently matched all four designated 32-bit residues. All 48 pre-B predicates failed exactness and the replicated 1% family-wise Fisher threshold. After supplying B, 29/52 passed association with T0. Zero, nonunit and Jacobi zero were exact on these balanced samples. Proper feature gcds numbered zero in all three strata. The 147 small pairs, which include unbalanced inputs, yielded 31 proper binomial gcds. Baseline factorizations are separate.

**Measured.** Within 1,1 the positive Jacobi counts were 69, 63 and 62 out of 128. None of 240 comparisons of Jacobi or small residue buckets with the signs, sign product or relative magnitudes of the two Gaussian coordinates passed. Among comparisons with consistent direction, the best worst-size p-value was 0.315. A follow-up using ranks of the signed coordinates and normalized absolute coordinates gave within-size family-wise permutation p-values of 0.981, 0.268 and 0.142. These results cover the tested finite bank. A polylogarithmic scalar zero test and its algorithmic lift to T1 remain open.
