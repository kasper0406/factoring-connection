1. Shared bases and union of exceptional sets.
Write X=x/T and L_m=(em/2)log m+O_k(m). For any fixed invertible ternary B put
E_B=sum_i log(M_{B,i}/t_i), nonnegative tag by tag. The k-span of B w is the
original k-span. Apply KH to the integral monomials of B w using the original
ideal lattice and the original unit-evaluation witness in the common k-span.
Then Q >= (x-D log(C_k D))/T - E_B, irrespective of whether the ideals
generated by B w are coprime to N. If B is within exp(O_k(m)) of the threshold
on D-o(D) tags, E_B=O_k(Dm)+o(D)L_m=o(X). Even a positive proportion of exceptions
works if their total excess (including the good-tag overhead) is <=(1-eta)X.
A few groups of size comparable to D with different optimal bases need not satisfy
this condition. Distinct choices of optimal bases do not rule out near-optimal sharing.

2. A stronger transfer to twists, at the normalization in comp_common.md.
Suppose invertible ternary B applied to a new linked tuple w contains, among its
rows, a fixed nonzero sidewise scalar multiple of a rank-r standard integer-split
tuple v on the SAME fields, with integer remainders and total pole degree O(r).
Write r/m -> theta>0. By split_poles.txt lines 34 and 50, before subtracting
the Cramer loss, (1+O(r/n)) H(v) >= (r-6)x/n + 2n(r-6)log(r-6) - O(Dr).
In units of mx/n this is at least 3 theta/2-o(1). The product of the absolute
scalar norms times J_v is >= J_w by ideal inclusion. Hence
  sum_i log M_{B,i}(w) - log J_w >= H(v).
The same argument allows a boosted 1-coordinate when its house at every tag is
>=1 and the other core columns generate the unit ideals on both sides.
If B is within exp(O(m)) on all but fD tags, where 0<=f<=1, its total excess
over threshold is at most fD L_m + O(Dm) = (f+o(1))mx/n for k=Q(sqrt(2)).
Consequently Q(w) >= (3 theta/2-f-o(1))mx/n. This includes f=1 and theta=1,
without any sharing hypothesis. In M3 and M4 the recovered tuple is respectively
a rational integer scalar or base-unit scalar times the boosted split tuple,
and each pole coordinate is a unit. Thus the Q branch holds for growing families
of the displayed form whenever the linked fields and degrees meet the assumptions.
This does not prove the asserted distribution of losses or existence of irreducible
specializations at every size.

3. Cancellation test for the divided difference.
Fix n>=4, R=T(T-1), and P=T^n+K T with K an even positive integer tending to infinity.
P-sqrt(2) is Eisenstein at sqrt(2). For s=sqrt(2), a real root alpha~s/K tends to 0.
The other n-1 roots have moduli asymptotic to K^(1/(n-1)), by rescaling the equation.
The Lagrange summands at 0 and 1 are each of order K in absolute value: at 0 divide
P(0)-s by -alpha and R'(0), and at 1 divide P(1)-s by 1-alpha and R'(1).
Their sum is (P div R)(alpha)=1+alpha+...+alpha^(n-2), tending to 1. This refutes
the local dominance inference following the exact identity even for a split pole.
