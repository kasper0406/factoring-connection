Compensation under a condition on numerator roots or on simultaneous rank

1. Divided differences. A norm polynomial for each row [proved]
Write s=sqrt(2), f=P-s, E=Q-P and f_z=f+zE. The sides are monic of the same
degree n, so deg E<n. For a ternary row a, keep just its active pole indices I,
write U=prod_{j in I} R_j and S_j=P mod R_j. Its fixed reduced-denominator numerator is
  G=(a_1+a_2 T)U + sum_{j in I} a_{2+j}(s-S_j)U/R_j = V+sW.
Both integer coordinate polynomials have degree at most p+1 for the archived poles.
More generally G has integral k-coefficients. Put d=deg G and exclude rows zero on a side.
With X_z=P+zE, the remainder of X_z modulo each pole R_j is S_j, and X_z div R_j
is its integral quotient representative. At a root of f_z the row is G/U. In particular
  C_a(z)=N_{k/Q}( Res_T(f_z,G) / Res_T(f_z,U) ) is in Z[z].
Here the norm multiplies the polynomials over all base embeddings and fixes z. The
denominator is constant since E is divisible by U. Integrality follows by taking the
resultant of the monic f_z with the row in its integral quotient representatives over
O_k[z]. The product over the roots of G shows deg C_a <= e d even with shared roots
of G and E. Its endpoints give exactly |C_a(0)C_a(1)|=|F(a)|. Both ideals are trivial.

When Res(E,G) is nonzero, the leading coefficient has an arithmetic lower bound.
Writing g=lc G and l=deg E, the relative leading coefficient is, up to sign,
  g^(n-l) Res(E,G) / Res(f,U).
Indeed each root beta of G contributes the factor f(beta)+z E(beta). Factor
E=(T-b)U E_0. Res(T-b,G)=G(b). Modulo R_j the only surviving term of G is
a_{2+j}(s-S_j) U/R_j, including for multiple roots of R_j. Hence Res(U,G) equals,
up to sign, Res(f,U) times prod a_{2+j}^deg(R_j) times prod_{j!=k in I} Res(R_j,R_k).
After cancellation every additional factor, including Res(E_0,G), is a nonzero
algebraic integer. Consequently |lc C_a| >= |N_{k/Q} G(b)|. If Res(E,G)=0 one
must use the actual degree and leading coefficient, not the vanished nominal one.
For integer b large and nonconstant G of degree d, one concrete sufficient height test is
sum_{j<d}|sigma(G_j)| <= |sigma(g)b|/2 at each base embedding. It gives
log|lc C_a| >= ed log|b|-e log 2 in the nonvanishing case. This quantity can reach
the KH floor for d proportional to m. Small support can leave d small or even zero.

2. A bound using roots near both endpoints [proved]
For any integer polynomial C with nonzero endpoints write C=c prod h^v over primitive
irreducible integer factors. For h, let b_j(h)=sum_{h(zeta)=0} log^+(1/(2|zeta-j|))
for j=0,1, counting complex roots separately. Put Gamma=sum v min(b_0(h),b_1(h)).
Also put A_far=sum_{all roots of C} log^+(2 min(|zeta|,|zeta-1|)), with multiplicity.
Then, with D_C=deg C,
  log|C(0)C(1)| >= log|c lc C| - D_C log 2 + A_far - Gamma.                    (1)
For each factor use the endpoint with smaller b_j. Expand log|h(j)| root by root.
Its positive terms after extracting 2^(-deg h) dominate that factor's A_far.
The value of h at the other endpoint is a nonzero integer of modulus at least one.
Multiplication proves (1), including constant polynomials. The fixture test dropped
A_far, which is nonnegative. Thus large leading height carries through provided the
loss at both endpoints within individual irreducible factors stays within budget.
The integral polynomial Kz(z-1)+1 for K>=5 is irreducible (K(K-4) lies strictly
between consecutive squares). Its leading height tends to infinity while both
endpoint values are 1. This is a counterexample to inference from integrality alone.

At X=x/T the explicit sufficient norm hypothesis is log|c lc C|+A_far-Gamma
>= (1-o(1))X for each eligible nonscalar row when the threshold branch fails.
The degree cost is O(m)=o(X) for DD. A simpler test is log|lc C|>=(1-o(1))X
and Gamma=o(X). All roots and inputs concern the deformation between the linked
sides. A large loss in (1) requires the same rational irreducible factor to have
roots near BOTH endpoints if the leading height by itself should have forced a floor.
The conditioned local bases do not supply any bound of this strength for Gamma.

3. Compensating pole places [proved]
For a root alpha_i of f and a monic pole R of degree v, the quotient generator at
alpha_i is the coefficient of T^(v-1) in the remainder of f(T)/(T-alpha_i) modulo R.
To see this write f(T)/(T-alpha_i)=R A+B with deg B<v. Multiplying back, division
by R has quotient (T-alpha_i)A+lc_{v-1}(B), which gives the identity on evaluation.
For squarefree R this coefficient equals
  sum_{R(r)=0} prod_{l!=i}(r-alpha_l)/R'(r)
by Lagrange interpolation. The remainder formula includes repeated roots. The sum
can cancel, whereas a single linear pole has one term. A dominant generator of size
M_i still forces t_i >= c_min M_i/2 whenever all the others are <=c_min M_i/(2Cm).

For the annular Gemini construction, at the pole-adjacent root the omitted-root
product is >=(0.7 rho)^(n-m+2) 0.9^(m-3). Its generator dominates there. The other
places can contribute negatively to Q. Since M_i>=1, the general local Cramer bound
charges at most O_k(Dm log m) for those negative terms. At n/m -> infinity and
log rho >= (1/2-o(1))m log m, the pole tags alone therefore give
  Q >= 4(m-2)(n-m+2) log(0.7 rho) - O_k(Dm log m).
This is Omega((1-m/n) Dm^2 log m), so H>=Q lies outside H=O(X) at balance. This
uses the archived root packet and dominance estimates, not a generic separation claim.

For the half-embedding construction, the deformation between sides is E=2(T-1)U_all.
Its independent large parameter B adds B T^(n/2) E to f. At a root beta of a fixed,
nonconstant active numerator, the parameter root of the norm polynomial is
 -B beta^(n/2) - (P_0(beta)-s)/E(beta).
The exclusions in the construction make beta E(beta) nonzero. Thus the roots go
to infinity, Gamma vanishes and the A_far term of (1) diverges. Single-pole constants
and the affine directions at 0 and 1 are handled by their explicit norms in the
archived proof. Only the six scalar units and possibly +/-sqrt(2) survive the cap.
Here B can also send H outside the low-height range.

For Gemini C1b each R=T-j has P(j)=Q(j)=1. The generator (s-1)/(alpha-j)
has one-side absolute norm |N_k(s-1)|^(n-1)=1 by the denominator resultant.
It is nonscalar, and its linked evaluation is a unit. Thus even one such direction
prevents an inference from the reported full-support floor to every nonscalar row.
The 112-bit negative Q is a finite observation. No asymptotic low-H counterexample
follows. The norm polynomial for this direction is constant of absolute value 1.

4. Common rows give a threshold certificate [proved for general reduced modules]
For nonnegative budgets b_i, take the common set of ternary rows with
  |L_i(a)| <= t_i exp(b_i) for every i.
Let integral witnesses from this set span a subspace W of paired elements. Suppose W
contains an integral point w of unit common evaluation, and W/w generates the product
of its two projected fields over k. Here w need not itself satisfy the archimedean
budget if it is an integral combination or a point of the original ideal lattice in W.
Require w in the original generator ideals. Write s_W for the larger projection
dimension, n_* for the larger generated relative field degree (at most n), and put
T_W=2 ceil(2 n_*/s_W)+1. Then
  Q >= (x-D log(C_k D))/T_W - sum_i b_i.                                     (2)
For the growth step let V=W/w, label the larger projection 1, and set
l=ceil(2 n_*/s_W). By the field part of KH, l powers fill the first projected field.
If projection 2 on V has a kernel, a nonzero (z,0) in V multiplied by V^l supplies
the full first side. Otherwise its dimension is dim W, so both projections have the
same dimension s_W=dim W and both are filled by V^l. Write n_1 for the first field
degree. If V^l lacks a one-sided vector, projection 1 is injective there, and
dim V^l=n_1. It grows at the next power since a stable space containing 1 would
already be the product. Thus V^(l+1) has a one-sided vector. In each case multiplying
a one-sided vector by V^l fills its side by degree at most 2l+1. This also handles
unequal field degrees, as in codex/subthird_2_gemini.out, lines 5 and 7.

Use the integral homogeneous products of witnesses in W in the KH index argument.
The corresponding component of w^T_W is an integral unit-evaluation witness after
undoing normalization. KH's rescaled covolume proof in the original ideals gives (2)
using the envelope t_i exp(b_i). Each envelope side-product is at least its ideal norm
by the norm of any witness nonzero on that side. For fixed W and specified witnesses,
the least nonnegative b_i is log^+(max_witness |L_i|/t_i). Only their total enters.
A reduced common rank r>=delta m gives s_W>=r/2, T_W=O_delta(T). A total budget
o(X) suffices, in particular b_i=O(m) at the stated balance. In the DD test both
projections are injective. If the simultaneous span contains 1 and T, it generates
the product since the side minimal polynomials are distinct. Full rank suffices too.
This is one structural extra hypothesis for the first branch. Its budget is minimal
for the specified witnesses and nonnegative dilations. No global necessity is claimed.

5. A general multiplication determinant [proved]
Work with linked integral orders and generator ideals coprime to N. At each side use
a full domain lattice S and a range lattice I=S intersect c so that [S:I]=N(c)
and both evaluations are onto Z/N. These lattices are available even for nonmaximal
linked orders O. Enlarge to S=O+N^k O_K for k sufficiently large that S_p=O_p at
p|N. At all other primes S_p is maximal. Thus S/NS agrees with O/NO, the evaluation
extends, and [S:I]=N(c) by localization. Coprimality makes the restricted evaluation
onto. Multiplication by the corresponding row element maps S to I. Its integer
matrix has determinant of absolute value |N(a)|/N(c).

Choose bases on both sides so that the domain evaluations and the range evaluations
each have the same row (1,0,...,0) modulo N. This is possible for any surjective
row of length at least 2. Elementary determinant-one operations over Z/N take a
unimodular row to e_1 and lift to integer elementary operations. Equivalently use
CRT over prime powers. If needed pad the smaller side by an identity block with
zero evaluation on the extra coordinates. Call the two integer matrices A,B of size d.
Equality of the linked images forces the first row of B-A to vanish modulo N.

The integer polynomial C(z)=det(A+z(B-A)) has endpoint product |F|/J. If
det(B-A) is nonzero it is divisible by N and is lc C. There is a common choice
of range bases for which this holds simultaneously for every eligible row in
the finite box. Multiply all second-side matrices on the left by one unimodular
V congruent to the identity mod N. For each pair of nonsingular A,B the locus
det(VB-A)=0 is proper on SL_d. Take V=A Z B^(-1) with det Z=det B/det A and
choose diagonal Z with no diagonal entry 1. The principal congruence subgroup is
Zariski dense since its closure contains every one-parameter elementary unipotent
subgroup (it contains the parameter values N times all integers). These generate SL_d.
A finite union of such proper closed subsets can be avoided simultaneously.

For a capped row |F|/J<=Y, (1) now implies Gamma >= x-log Y-d log 2. Some single
irreducible factor has b_j(h)/deg h >= (x-log Y-d log 2)/d at BOTH endpoints.
In particular it has a root within (1/2)exp(-(x-log Y-d log 2)/d) of each endpoint
when the exponent is positive. This is an unconditional necessary condition.
The compatible bases can themselves be large and introduce these pairs even for
scalars. The height of the original generators does not bound the size of this
coordinate change. A useful general argument must control the root distances in a
specified compatible choice or find common rows for (2). A bound on the number of
proper covering spaces alone does not control the independent parity inside them.
