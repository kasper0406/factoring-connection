Divided differences, small values and a family with large conditioning loss

1. Proved. The product bound and the quantifiers.
Write s = sqrt(2), k = Q(s), U = product R_j, f = P-s, n = deg f, d = p+1.
Take the monic irreducible side first. Clearing U gives the polynomials
G_1 = U, G_2 = TU, G_{j+2} = (s - (P mod R_j)) U/R_j, where the remainder is taken before subtraction.
They lie in O_k[T], with degree at most d. For a box row a write G_a = sum a_j G_j.
At each absolute embedding i above sigma of k, let B_i = max_j |G_j^sigma(alpha_i)|.
Then |L_i(a)|/M_i = |G_a^sigma(alpha_i)|/B_i. In particular t_i/M_i is the minimum,
over all k-bases of box rows, of the largest of these normalized evaluations in the basis.
The two integer coordinate polynomials in G_a = V_a + s W_a each have degree at most d.
Replacing s by P produces one integer polynomial but can raise the degree to n+d.

For each fixed row nonzero on this side, the product of |G_a^sigma(alpha_i)| over the 2n
absolute embeddings equals |N_{k/Q} Res_T(P-s,G_a)|, a positive integer. Equivalently
take the absolute resultant of P^2-2 with V_a+P W_a. For an irreducible factor use the
corresponding monic minimal polynomial and its coefficient embeddings. Let H_a be the maximum
coefficient l1-norm of the two conjugates of G_a. Bounding each other factor by
H_a max(1,|alpha_l|)^d gives the explicit uniform small-value estimate
  |G_a^sigma(alpha_i)| >= max(1,|alpha_i|)^d / [H_a^(2n-1) Mahler(P^2-2)^d].
Division by B_i gives the local normalized estimate. It still charges the coefficient and root heights.

There is also a denominator-aware counting bound. Divide the resultant by the matching norm
of U. This gives |N L(a)|, with the factor N(c) recovered if c is the integral generator ideal.
On the two sides together let J=N(c)N(c'), H=sum_i log M_i-log J and C=1+sqrt(2).
For a fixed row nonzero on both sides, sum_i log(M_i/|L_i(a)|) <= H, and each summand
is at least -log(Cm). If the row has loss >= lambda at u places, then
  u (lambda + log(Cm)) <= H + D log(Cm).
The basis attaining t_i may depend on i. Even with the same rows at many i, the resultant
permits payment at the other conjugates and through H. At the proposed balance H can be
of order Dm log m even in the height range of interest. This estimate allows u comparable to D.

2. Proved. Ill-conditioning at half the embeddings with p in the requested range.
For m>=4 put q=m-2, choose distinct integers r_j>=7, R_j=T^2-r_j^2, U=product R_j, p=2q.
Fix any odd A giving a supplied split semiprime N=A^2-2. Set b=1. Choose any even
n>=4q+4, h=n/2. Let C_0 be an invertible q by q zero-one matrix and v=C_0^{-1}e_l
with log ||v||_infinity >= (q/2) log q - O(q) (Alon and Vu, Main Theorem, part 2,
https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/av1.pdf, PDF p. 4).
Take a large positive integer S. Choose integer c_j with |c_j/r_j^2-S v_j| < S/(8q).
We will choose them in the following full-rank coset, with further finite exclusions below.

Set P_0=T^n+A-1+2L(T^2) with L in Z[X], deg L<=q+1 and L(1)=0. The residues
c_j=P_0 mod R_j and the constant P_0(0) vary independently in a rectangular lattice
translate by interpolation at 0,1,r_1^2,...,r_q^2 followed by clearing a fixed denominator.
Explicitly, multiples of the Lagrange cardinal polynomials with a common denominator
cleared give independent integer steps at all nodes other than 1. Each target interval for
c_j grows linearly with S. Thus it admits arbitrarily many choices in this lattice.
Define E=T^h(T-1)U and
  P_B=P_0+2B E,     Q_B=P_B+2(T-1)U,      B a large positive integer.
Both polynomials are monic of degree n, have value A at 1, and have the same c_j.
Subtract s from either side. Eisenstein at (s) applies since all the lower coefficients
before subtraction are even and the constant after subtraction has valuation exactly one.
The absolute minimal polynomials have degree 2n since s belongs to the field of a root.
They are distinct. The two maps alpha -> 1, s -> A modulo N link all the generators
because their integral quotient representatives differ by 2(T-1)U/R_j. Each side is generated
by T. The distinct relative minimal polynomials give the full product algebra by CRT.
The generators are k-independent on each side. A relation after clearing U has degree
<=p+1<n, so vanishes identically. Evaluation at r_j kills every term except the
j-th pole term, which has nonzero residue. This leaves only an affine linear relation.
The ideals are both the unit ideal and s_l<=l(p+1)+1 for each product order l.

At zero the rational generator functions have values (1,0,(c_j-sigma(s))/r_j^2).
For either sign these last q coordinates are within S/(4q) of S v_j for large S.
Thus the independent ternary rows diag(I_2,C_0) all have absolute values below 2S
throughout a small disk about zero, and the largest coordinate exceeds S||v||_infinity/2
there for large q. Choose its radius less than 1 and use the same disk on both sides.
On its boundary 2B E eventually dominates the remaining terms of either equation
P_B-sigma(s)=0, Q_B-sigma(s)=0. Rouche's theorem puts exactly h zeros of each equation
in the disk. With both signs and sides this gives D/2 absolute embeddings with
  log(M_i/t_i) >= log ||v||_infinity - log 4 >= (m/2) log m - O(m).
The general Cramer estimate supplies the upper bound O(m log m). No selection of
different local bases or conjecture on the infinitude of these semiprimes is required.

3. Proved. At a prescribed cap 1<=Y<N^2 this family can have only scalar candidates.
Here parameters are chosen for each finite m,n,A,Y. For a box row with active pole
indices H (nonzero coefficients d_j), put U_H=product_{H} R_j. The reduced numerator is
  F=(a_1+a_2 T)U_H + sum_{j in H} d_j(s-c_j) U_H/R_j.
When H is nonempty, choose the c_j so that F(xi) is nonzero at every zero xi of E.
At active poles this holds automatically. At every other such xi it excludes a proper
affine hyperplane in the c_j, since the coefficient of each active c_j is nonzero.
If a_1=a_2=0 and |H|>=2, also require sum d_j(s-c_j) nonzero, making deg F=2|H|-2.
For fixed m there are finitely many exclusions. A union of finitely many proper
hyperplanes cannot cover products of lattice intervals whose minimum cardinality tends
to infinity (fix all but one coordinate for each hyperplane with a nonzero coefficient).
We may simultaneously make all |c_j| large enough for the single-pole case below.

For nonconstant F its resultant with P_B-s has degree exactly deg F in B. In the product
over the roots of F, every leading factor is a nonzero multiple of E at that root.
The resultant with U_H is constant and nonzero in B by the fixed pole residues.
Their ratio is the relative norm of the box element. Both signs and sides have the same
growth conclusion, so its two-sided absolute norm eventually exceeds Y. The remaining
case with an active pole is F constant, a single pole times a scalar d. Its one-sided
absolute norm is |N_k(d)|^n |c_j^2-2|^(n-2), which also exceeds Y by choice of c_j.

For affine rows with a_2 nonzero the same resultant argument applies unless -a_1/a_2
is a zero of E. Its absolute value is <=(1+sqrt(2))/(sqrt(2)-1)<7, excluding the poles.
Root 1 gives two-sided norm |N_k(a_2)|^(2n) N^2. Root 0 gives the analogous product
using |P_0(0)^2-2| and |Q_0(0)^2-2|, both arbitrarily large by the independent choice
of P_0(0). Take B sufficiently large for every box row and the disk argument together.
Exactly the six scalar unit letters, and the two letters +/-sqrt(2) if 2^(2n)<=Y,
are then nonzero capped candidates. Their common evaluations are units modulo N.
All lie on one proper k-line, so incremental higher-rank parity is zero.

4. Partial. Ledger and next step.
For fixed m,n,N, the coefficient height tends to infinity with B. Some roots are
unbounded by Vieta, M_i>=max(1,|alpha_i|), and J=1. The general Cramer inequality
then also sends sum log t_i to infinity. The construction refutes a height-uniform
exclusion of the positive-fraction loss. The ideal-relative threshold inequality
at the factoring balance remains open. There x/T and Dm log m both have size
x^(3/4+o(1)), whereas the proposed L[1/4] norm budget is x^(1/2+o(1)).
KH gives the generator-height exponent c=1 (model alpha>=1/3); the fresh yield
needed for c=0, alpha=1/4 remains unestablished. Restrict the next resultant
argument to H=O(x/T), necessary for failure by chase/ch_L2c/proof.txt lines 42-51,
and charge the conjugates compensating the local cancellations to sum log t_i.
