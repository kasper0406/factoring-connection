# HATCH 1 preregistration, before generating instances or measuring

Use 40, 64, 80, 96 bits, six independent fixtures per size, deterministic RNG seeded with 617030 + 1000*bits + replicate. No planted positives, retries on empty lists, or tuning from outcomes. Compare pooled elapsed search seconds per distinct smooth point (infinity if no hits), coverage, and operation counts. Single process, one timed run, no claims about constant-factor optimality. Exclude generating shared public fixtures and the prime list, charge method-specific setup and exact candidate verification. Record raw rows and full list counts. Report any departures.

* Interval. Uniform bit-length start X with room for 32768 consecutive values. Smoothness bounds 256, 2048, 16384, 65536 respectively. Compare exact division on sieve progressions with nested Taylor-coefficient lattice interpolation, centered in tiles of 64. At primes 2,3,5,7 respectively use integer layer increments [4,4,4,2,2,2,2], [4,4,2,2], [2,2], [2]. Use six scaled coefficients, LLL, the first nonzero short row and all of its integer roots in the tile, then exact smoothness tests. Record guaranteed vs extra roots using each row's actual height bound. List all exact log-smooth-part fractions at 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1 and at 1/4, 1/2, 1 times the full-window prefix Johnson threshold. Compute using integer comparisons where thresholds coincide with equality.
* M2. Reuse the subthird public balanced reduced-form generator, with a new seed and K=64. Use bounds 256, 1024, 2048, 4096. Work in the positive primitive box. For every integer d in (2K²,4K²] supported entirely on odd split primes <=B coprime to the leading coefficient and discriminant, Hensel-lift both projective roots and combine by CRT. For every resulting slope use rational reconstruction to recover a small fraction. Compare with row-by-row enumeration of the identical forced lattices and an unrestricted prime-line lattice sieve of the whole box. All three verify full B-smoothness. Record distinct points, full truth and duplicates, modulus and branch counts. Cross-check matched candidate sets and whole-box truth with direct arithmetic. For the list curve use good-prime mass only, separately record total smooth points including exceptional primes. Use the two-choice projective Johnson benchmark. Check explicitly that both conjugate components never vanish on a primitive point at a good split prime.
* Prediction. The interval lattice can find some smooth values with heavy small-prime factors but will lose coverage. The two forced M2 methods should return identical witnesses. The full folded zero tuple should give no good-prime agreement on primitive inputs. A positive search result requires lower pooled time per fresh witness than the appropriate unrestricted sieve at the two largest sizes, together with a mechanism that improves the exponent. Counts and time describe these implementations only.
