# HATCH 1 · Prime-power multiplicities and Galois folding

**Verdict.** A partial dent in HATCH 1. On integer intervals, even an ideal Johnson decoder with a separately chosen, fixed weight at every valuation depth has at most Dickman-rate smooth yield per call at leading exponential order. On primitive quadratic norms, a split prime supplies one of two conjugate zeros. Requiring a whole folded zero symbol gives zero agreement at every good split prime. Recovering the surviving branch was faster than lattice sieving at toy sizes, but enumerating its possible divisors costs the box-size exponent. General adaptive selection remains open. ## Multiplicity

**Proved.** In an interval of $V$ consecutive integers starting at $X$, encode the offset modulo each $q^{e_q}$, $q\le B$, with caps large enough for the maximum integer. Against the received word $-X$, matching the first $j$ digits earns $j\log q$. The score of $z$ is $A=\log\gcd(z,\prod q^{e_q})$. Smoothness is exactly $A=\log z$. Write $W=\sum e_q\log q, t=\log V, x\sim\log X$. Rate is $t/W$, required agreement is $x/W$, and two distinct offsets agree in mass at most $t$. Thus agreement exceeds rate by $x/t$ in the short-window regime. The leading Johnson benchmark is $J=\sqrt{tW}$. With full caps, $W\sim\pi(B)x$. For $B=L[\beta,\kappa]$ and $1\ll\log V\le x^{O(1)}$ in this short-interval regime, $A/J=L[\beta,-\kappa/2]$ at a witness. Keeping just first digits reduces $J$ by about $\sqrt{x/\log B}$ while losing higher valuations. The mean list in the Prop SI regime is $L[2/3,\nu-2/(3\kappa)]$ for $V=L[2/3,\nu]$. At exponent cancellation its lower-order terms decide whether the list is small. **Proved extension of Theorem J.** Give each prefix level an arbitrary nonnegative weight $h_{qk}$, fixed independently of the window location. Define
$$
 S(z)=\sum_q\log q\sum_{k\le v_q(z)} h_{qk},\qquad E=\sum_{q,k}h_{qk}^2\log q .
$$
Grant every candidate with $S\ge\sqrt{Et}$ at unit cost, $E>0$. For a uniform integer start $X_0\le X<2X_0$, put $x=\log X_0, F_a=x^a(\log x)^{1-a}$. Fix $0<\beta<1, \kappa>0, B=L[\beta,\kappa]$, and $2\le V\le\exp(O(F_{1-\beta}))$. Uniformly in the weights, the expected number of **smooth, certified** integers per call is at most
$$
 \exp\!\left[-\left((1-\beta)/\kappa-o(1)\right)F_{1-\beta}\right].
$$
Thus the leading gain over Dickman-rate testing is zero. The bound is sharp at this scale by forcing a power of two between $V$ and $2V$ and averaging the smooth cofactor count. An individual witness can cross the unweighted threshold under reweighting. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $S^2\le EA$, with equality for weights indicating that witness’s occupied levels. Finding such weights cheaply from the public target is precisely the missing selection step. Published GSS scores equality at coprime moduli. The repository's Theorem J takes one weight per prime. [GSS, Theorem 4](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/2000/gss-conf.pdf) · [sihard_results.md:404](/Volumes/git/factoring-connection/notes/sihard_results.md:404)

**Proof.** Rescale weights to $E=t$. Write $u=x/\log B, \theta=1-(\log u-2\log\log x)/\log B$, so $\theta\to1$. Each integer occurs in at most $V$ windows. Rankin's inequality on $z\le3X_0$ and exponential Markov on $S\ge t$ bound the log mean by
$$
 -(1-\theta)x+(1-2\theta)t+\sum_{q\le B}\log M_q+O(1),\quad
 M_q=\sum_{j\ge0}q^{\theta(2\sum_{k\le j}h_{qk}-j)} .
$$
Extend weights by zeros. Put $D_q=\sum_k h_{qk}^2, Z=\log x, F=F_{1-\beta}$. Completing the square gives $2\sum_{k\le j}h_{qk}-j\le D_q$. For $j\ge16D_q$ it is at most $-j/2$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. Hence $M_q\ll(1+D_q)q^{\theta D_q}$. Use this bound at $q\le Z$ or $D_q\ge1/16$. Their summed log overhead is $O(Z\log(2+t)+t/\log Z)=o(F)$, since $\sum D_q\log q=t$.

For the remaining primes set $v=\sqrt{D_q}<1/4$. Each partial sum is at most $jv$, so $\log M_q\le-\log(1-q^{-\theta(1-2v)})$. Taylor-expand this upper bound at zero. The summed constants are $O(B^{1-\theta}\log\log B)=o(F)$. The linear terms are $O(\sqrt t)$ by Cauchy–Schwarz and $\sum_q (\log q)q^{-2\theta}=O(1)$. The remainders are $O(t\sup_{q>Z}(\log q)q^{-0.4})=o(F)$. Thus $\sum\log M_q\le\theta t+o(F)$. The surviving $+(1-\theta)t=o(F)$, and $(1-\theta)x\sim u\log u\sim(1-\beta)F/\kappa$. This also bounds the union of any fixed repertoire of $\exp(o(F))$ profiles at the same leading order.

**Construction.** Every additive map from $\mathbb Z$ to itself multiplies by its value at 1, so an additive integer derivative supplies no independent digit. For nonincreasing integer layer increments, use the Taylor basis $d_i(T+C)^i$, where $C$ is the tile center and $v_q(d_i)=\sum_k(h_{qk}-i)_+$. At $v_q(T+C)=j$, every term supplies at least $\sum_{k\le j}h_{qk}$ powers. For dimension $d$ exceeding every increment, the diagonal moduli contribute $\tfrac12\sum(h^2+h)\log q$ to log determinant. Scaling the columns for radius $H$ adds $d(d-1)\log H/2$. Balancing these terms gives the usual square-root scale. We extracted all in-tile integer roots of the first LLL row, including roots outside its divisibility guarantee. The theorem above bounds only its specified ideal certificate class. ## Folding and quadratic norms

**Proved.** Encode an algebraic integer at a prime ideal by the tuple of reductions of $a,\sigma a,\ldots,\sigma^{m-1}a$. Rational integer messages are fixed by every $\sigma$, so folding repeats their residue. If $\sigma$ stabilizes the ideal, it preserves the valuation. Residue Frobenius gives a power of the same symbol. If it moves the ideal, the tuple reads adjacent primes in its orbit. At a completely split rational prime, residue Frobenius is the identity. A full zero orbit covering all primes says $a\in q\mathcal O_K$. Norm divisibility asks for *one* zero. CRT permits the local pattern $(0,1,\ldots,1)$. For example, at an unramified inert $p$ with Frobenius $\sigma$, the Parvaresh–Vardy relation $\sigma(a)\equiv a^p\bmod\mathfrak p$ holds. Using it for root finding requires an interpolation equation from the split primes. [Guruswami–Rudra](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0511072) · [Function-field folding](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4139) · [Polynomial ideal codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07930) · [Number-field decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.2321)

For M2 write $Q=au^2+buv+cv^2$, discriminant $-4N$, with $x=\log N$. The scalar residue word $(Q\bmod q^{e_q})_q$ against zero gives precisely the same valuation score as in the interval. For the all-prime modulus $P>\max Q$, its rate is $\log|\{Q(u,v)\}|/\log P$. Different points can give the same scalar word, and scalar collisions measure differences of norms. Projective coordinates retain the primitive pair when the modulus exceeds $K^2$. At good split $q\nmid2aN$, Hensel lifting gives $Q=a(u-rv)(u-sv)$ modulo every $q^e$, with $r-s$ a unit. These factors are conjugate components up to units. Their simultaneous vanishing forces $q\mid u,v$, excluded by primitivity. Partial agreement at depth $k$ means exactly one matching projective slope, $r$ or $s\bmod q^k$. Its log-weight sum is the good-prime smooth part in logarithms. Full smoothness includes the separately tested exceptional primes. Unramified inert primes away from $2a$ cannot divide a primitive value.

**Local obstruction.** On the primitive norm-zero locus the folded symbols are the two punctured axes in $\mathbb F_q^2$. A polynomial of individual degrees $<q-1$ vanishing on that locus is divisible by $UV$ because its restriction to each axis has $q-1$ roots. Hence a linear auxiliary polynomial vanishing on all admissible symbols reduces identically to zero. A quadratic can recover the known norm factor. Across several such primes, every coefficient of a pure power and the constant belongs to the product of their prime ideals. If their individual absolute field norms are below that ideal norm, these coefficients vanish in the number field, so the global interpolant also contains $UV$. This closes the direct whole-symbol interpolation step, leaving room for target-dependent equations on a smaller set of residues.

With $W=\sum_{q\text{ good split}}e_q\log q$, a primitive $K\times K$ box has message entropy $b_0=\log\#\text{primitive points}\sim2\log K$. The raw two-component rate is $b_0/(2W)$ and its whole-zero agreement is zero. The projective rate is $b_0/(W+\sum\log(1+1/q))$. Distinct positive primitive slopes agree to mass at most $t_0=\log K^2$ because the matched prime powers divide their nonzero determinant. The two-choice Johnson benchmark $J_2=\sqrt{2Wt_0}$ follows by applying Cauchy–Schwarz to occupancies of the two labels per level, whose total label weight is $2W$, with pair overlap at most $t_0$. In detail, $\ell$ messages of score at least $A$ satisfy $\ell^2 A^2\le2W(\ell W+\ell(\ell-1)t_0)$. On good-only smooth witnesses near the M2 scale $s=\log Q\sim x/2$, agreement is $s/W$. With $n=B^{1-o(1)}$ good split primes, $W\sim ns$ and $s/J_2=L[\beta,-\kappa/2]$. ## Exponent ledger

**Proved for this traversal.** The toy lifts both roots at every chosen prime power, combines their orientations by CRT, then recovers the small fraction $u/v$ by Euclid. Reconstruction is unique on each branch of a forced modulus $d>2K^2$. The implementation visits every product of $m=\lfloor\log(2K^2)/\log B\rfloor$ distinct good primes before it finishes. With M2's $K=L[1-\beta,\nu]$, $\beta<1/3$, and $n=B^{1-o(1)}$, we have $\log\binom nm\sim2\log K$. The traversal alone costs at least $L[1-\beta,2\nu]$ per completed box. Faster recovery per branch cannot change that exponent. [experiment.py:226](/Volumes/git/factoring-connection/codex/scratch/hatch_h1c/experiment.py:226)

**Heuristic / counterfactual.** M2 density at $s\sim x/2$ is $L[1-\beta,-(1-\beta)/(2\kappa)]$. Choose $2\nu>(1-\beta)/(2\kappa)$ for positive leading expected supply. A one-witness $n^a$-work decoder with constant success on suitable forms, useful independent relations and standard class-group extraction would give $L_N[\beta,\max((1+a)\kappa,2\kappa)]$, including $n$ calls and quadratic sparse algebra. At $\beta=1/4$ this genuinely crosses $1/3$. At positive slack the heuristic mean list is $L[1-\beta,2\nu-(1-\beta)/(2\kappa)]$, so enumerating every witness would exceed the one-witness budget on average. The interval Prop SI target at $\beta=1/3$ instead improves the MNFS constant for $a<1.14156$.
