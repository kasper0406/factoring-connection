HATCH 2 — Where must the small norm come from?

Verdict. Partial obstruction proved. In the proposed L[1/4] regime, concentrating cancellation at a few individual embeddings forces every sufficiently small candidate into a controlled list of proper k-subspaces. At bounded coefficient height one can also quantify how many conjugates must cancel outside that list. The published subspace counts do not settle diffuse cancellation or bound the parity rank contributed inside the list. Both multiplicative searches returned zero incremental rank.

Target. Write x=log N, e=[k:Q] fixed, n=[K:k]=[K′:k], D=2en, r=em. The target is enough independent two-sided rows below Y to meet N_ind ρ(log Y/log B) ≥ π(B) with L[α] work, α<1/3. The quotient needs a specified family of proper spaces with a valid lower-rank charge. If every line ka could be declared exceptional after seeing a, the quotient would vanish identically. The known norms 1 and 8 already preclude a pointwise floor. See notes/subthird_results.md:83, notes/rankdegen_results.md:506, codex/subthird_2_codex.out:9.

Proved — A subspace bound with a coefficient-height cutoff

Fix the linked generator basis. At each tagged absolute embedding i let L_i be its linear form, M_i its largest coefficient in modulus and σ=i|k. Count complex conjugates separately. For integral coefficient vectors a put A_σ=max_j|σa_j|, I=(a_1,…,a_m), and h(a)=(Σ_σ log A_σ−log Norm I)/e. This is projective logarithmic height. Define the cancellation at i by
    d_i(a)=log(M_i A_σ / |L_i(a)|).
If a side vanishes, discard the candidate. Let H̃=Σ log M_i−log J, with J the product of the absolute norms of the two fixed generator ideals. Put F₀=|F(a)|/[J (Norm I)^(2n)]. Removing coefficient content only strengthens a lower bound for the charged norm. Direct multiplication gives
    Σ_i d_i = H̃ + D h(a) − log F₀.

For any u≥0 there are at most D proper k-subspaces such that every point of height h(a)≤u outside their union satisfies
    d_i(a) ≤ τ(u) = r(u + ½ log m) for every i.
For each i take the k-span of the height-bounded points violating this inequality. If m such rows were independent, their determinant Δ would be nonzero in the product of their content ideals. Divide each conjugate determinant in modulus by the product of the row maxima there. The product of these normalized determinants over σ is ≥ exp(−em u), by the ideal norm inequality. At each σ, Hadamard bounds the normalized determinant by m^(m/2). At σ=i|k replace a column with maximal |coefficient of L_i| by the L_i-column divided by that coefficient. The determinant is unchanged, and all normalized entries in the replacement column have modulus < exp(−τ). The product is then < m^(em/2)exp(−τ)=exp(−em u), a contradiction. These spans depend only on the forms and cutoff, and apply to all unit representatives. A span of dimension t>0 has logarithmic Plücker height at most t u+(t/2)log t. Choose t generating rows and apply Hadamard to their t-by-t minors at each embedding. Each minor lies in the product of their content ideals.

Two consequences follow. For h≤u and G=H̃−log Y>0, a point with F₀≤Y outside these D spaces must have d_i>G/(2D) at ≥G/(2τ(u)) embeddings, since their sum is ≥G. If G>Dτ(u), all such points are covered. At bounded height and log Y=o(mx/n), KH gives Ω(x/(n log m)) required embeddings in the proposed balance. For D≈√x this is Ω(√x/log x). For n≈√(x/log x) and m=x^(1/4+o(1)), it is a positive fraction of D, or the cover already contains every point. The exact cutoff retains the constants. See notes/subthird_results.md:126.

Even large coefficient heights allow a restriction. Partition h+½log m into dyadic intervals from ½log m to h_max+½log m. Applying the determinant bound with u equal to each upper endpoint minus ½log m costs at most D[1+floor(log₂(1+2h_max/log m))] proper spaces. Outside them d_i≤2r(h+½log m). If at most q≤D/(2r) embeddings have d_i>γ, for γ≥0, then
    log F₀ ≥ H̃ + (D−2qr)h − qr log m − Dγ ≥ H̃ − D(γ+½log m).
Thus a search aimed at a few roots can escape only through the proper spaces or through actual cancellation at additional conjugates. Choosing a few weights does not imply that only a few conjugates cancel.

Exponent ledger (conditional model). With polynomial γ in log x and explicitly represented coefficients of log-height ≤exp(poly(x)), the cover costs B^o(1) in multiplicity. KH supplies H̃≥[x−D log(C_kD)]/[2 ceil(2n/s)+1] with s≥m/2. In the usual ledger r=x^(α+o(1)), D=x^(δ+o(1)), log B=x^(α+o(1)), the degree/entropy term requires δ≤2α. The smoothness budget requires log Y≤x^(2α+o(1)). For α<1/3, the KH term x^(1+α−δ+o(1)) dominates both this budget and D(γ+½log m). This rules out the few-embedding route outside the displayed spaces. If their induced families satisfy the lower-rank ledgers, this subclass forces α≥1/3. A general proof must still charge them. At α=1/4 the competing powers are 3/4 and 1/2. See notes/grdeg_results.md:34 and notes/lowerbound_results.md:30.

Published bounds — where uniformity is lost

• Evertse’s Theorem 1, for one nondegenerate field norm of degree d=D/2 in r rational variables, gives (16d)^((r+11)^3/3) Y^((r+Σ_{j=2}^{r−1}1/j)/d) (1+log Y)^(r(r−1)/2). Theorem 2 allows a weaker hypothesis but still excludes V^k=V for this totally real base. Its cover bound, for P≥1 and invariant determinant height H*, is
    (16d)^((r+10)^2) max(1,(Y^(r/d)P/H*)^(1/(r−1))) [1+log(exp(1)YH*)/log(exp(1)P)]^(r−1).
Even after optimizing P, the logarithms of the prefactors alone have sizes Θ(r³log D) and Θ(r²log D), or x^(3/4+o(1)) and x^(1/2+o(1)). Neither the two-sided product nor KH’s ideal-relative height is the field norm or invariant height in those theorems. Common units give infinitely many integral representatives at one nonzero norm. Product-form results assuming finite archimedean volume also lose that hypothesis here. The unit action preserves volume and the norm region, and sufficiently spaced unit powers move a small open box into disjoint images. See notes/rankdegen_results.md:492, Evertse, Theorems 1–2 (https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/592/download/10_5486_PMD_2000_2325.pdf) and Stewart, Theorem 1 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01097).

• Evertse–Győry do cover products of fields and unit cosets, for a fixed right-hand-side ideal up to S-units. Even for the unit right-hand side the coset bound has factor (2^33 d_A²)^(s₀ b(m)) over k, where d_A=2n, s₀ is the number of allowed places and b(m)=m(m+1)(2m+1)/3−2. An inequality ranges over many ideals. Over Q, simply summing fixed-integer bounds costs up to floor(Y) terms. See Evertse–Győry, Theorem 1, https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~evertsejh/96-families.pdf

• Evertse–Ferretti, Theorem 3.1, improves Evertse–Schlickewei for one specified weighted system of independent forms. In dimension l, with R distinct forms, individual coefficient-field degrees ≤d₀ and inhomogeneous form heights ≤H*, the large-point threshold is max((H*)^(1/(3Rd₀)),l^(l/ε)). The cover bound is 10^9·4^l l^14 ε^(−3) log(3Rd₀/ε) log(log(3Rd₀)/ε). Taking l=m over k would help. At fixed ε, R,d₀ polynomial in D, its log is O(m+log log D). An exp(cD) loss swamps every subthird quota here. An exp(O(m)) loss can be affordable when m=o(log B), but does not give the required subpower loss when m≈log B. The ternary box has bounded projective height over fixed k. For its determinant-normalized product inequality, Corollary 3.2 also pays a factor (9l²/ε)^(l·number of places). The absolute norm cap does not supply the required determinant-normalized inequalities with surplus ε. A naive frame choice alone offers up to binom(2n,m)^e systems. For small parameters the separate twisted-height Theorem 2.2 contains (90l)^(l[K:Q]) and log log(3 H_L^(1/R)), with H_L the product of local maximal minors and K the field defining that system. Even one proper k-line through (1,1) can supply independent rows via distinct unramified rational primes when Y permits. A bound on spaces still needs a lower-rank yield estimate. https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v177-n2-p04-p.pdf

Proved — Converting a unit-invariant rational cover can be cheap in this cubic. Let u=z², z=2cos(2π/7). Its three positive conjugates are distinct. Any e=3 distinct powers are Q-independent. A polynomial relation would vanish at all e conjugates, whereas Descartes’ rule allows at most e−1 positive roots for a nonzero polynomial with ≤e monomials. If t proper rational spaces cover u^j a for 0≤j≤(e−1)t, some one contains ka. Its k-core {v:kv is contained in that space} is proper. The same t cores therefore suffice given that orbit coverage. The maximum absolute embedding of any coefficient grows by a factor of at most house(u)^((e−1)t), where house(u)=max_σ|σu|. A cover confined to the original box lacks this hypothesis.
