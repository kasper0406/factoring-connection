\title[Deterministic factoring of balanced semiprimes on average]{Deterministic factoring of
balanced semiprimes\\ in average time $N^{1/6}(\log N)^{O(1)}$}

\author{}
\address{}
\email{}

\date{DRAFT --- not for distribution. Version of 18 October 2026.}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We give a deterministic algorithm that receives only an integer $N$ and outputs its complete
prime factorisation. On every input its cost is that of the Harvey--Hittmeir algorithm,
$O(N^{1/5}\log^{16/5}N/(\log\log N)^{3/5})$ bit operations, up to lower-order terms. Averaged
over the uniform distribution on the $n$-bit semiprimes $N=pq$ with primes $p<q\le2p$, its
cost is $N^{1/6}(\log N)^{O(1)}$. We know of no previously proved average-case or almost-all
deterministic exponent below $1/5$ for this population.

The engine is a deterministic weighted collision search, which is a Lehman-type method. It
takes the McKee--Pinch divisibility event $\ell\mid Bp-Aq$ over all primes $\ell\equiv3\pmod
4$ in four dyadic bands near $N^{1/6}$ and all small coprime weights $(A,B)$. It adds the
Harvey--Hittmeir modulus-squared lock, and one baby-step list per band, shared by all primes
of the band through cofactor-powered handles. One run with weight height $H$ costs
$N^{1/6}H^{5/3}(\log N)^{O(1)}$ on every input and never outputs a wrong answer. Its coverage
step misses the true factor on at most an $O((\log N)/H^2)$ and an $O((\log N)^4/H^4)$ fraction
of the pairs; the second bound comes from a two-variable fourth-moment estimate. It gives, for every
$\varepsilon\le1/40$, cost $N^{1/6+5\varepsilon/12+o(1)}$ with exceptional fraction
$O(N^{-\varepsilon})$. The complete algorithm reruns the search with heights
$H\leftarrow H^{6/5}$ behind a baby-step giant-step detector for primes with small
$\ord_q(2)$. This works because the proved failure fraction decays faster in $H$ than the cost
grows.

We prove two further results. First, the worst case reduces to coverage alone: a modified
search factors every balanced $N$ whose true pair is caught by one band. So a pair-covering
hypothesis implies worst-case cost $N^{1/6+o(1)}$, and any covering theorem with weight
height at most $Y^{1/2}J^{-c}$, for some $c>3/8$, would beat $1/5$ in the worst case. Second, in a sumset
collision-search cost model with residue-local, degree-one targets, reaching the true factor
of a constant fraction of the pairs requires cost $N^{1/6}/(\log N)^{O(1)}$. Our search attains this up to polylogarithmic factors.
(Here costs are per input, or averaged over all $n$-bit integers; budgets averaged only over the
semiprimes are not covered.) The result says nothing about other kinds of
algorithm, and our cost proxy at 40--80 bits favours Harvey--Hittmeir.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Background}
Few exponents are known for deterministic integer factoring with rigorous bounds.
\begin{itemize}
\item Trial division costs $N^{1/2}$.
\item Lehman's method \cite{lehman1974} gives $N^{1/3+o(1)}$.
\item The Pollard--Strassen method \cite{pollard1974,strassen1976} gives $N^{1/4+o(1)}$. It was
  refined by Bostan--Gaudry--Schost \cite{bgs2007} and Costa--Harvey \cite{costaharvey}.
\item Hittmeir saved a subexponential factor at exponent $1/4$ \cite{hittmeir2018}. He then
  proved exponent $2/9$ with a time--space trade-off for Lehman's method \cite{hittmeir2021}.
\item Harvey \cite{harvey2021} proved exponent $1/5$. Harvey and Hittmeir \cite{hh2022}
  improved the logarithmic factor, and Gao, Feng, Hu and Pan \cite{gao2025} improved it again
  for balanced semiprimes, at the same exponent.
\end{itemize}
Below $1/5$ the known routes are conditional or partial.
\begin{itemize}
\item Umans and Wang \cite{umanswang2025} showed that a divisor-cover conjecture would give
  $1/6$. He and Sahai \cite{hesahai2026} refuted that conjecture's one-dimensional
  arithmetic-progression form.
\item Pollard's $p-1$ method succeeds on a positive proportion of the nearly balanced semiprimes
  $pq$ with $x^{1-\varepsilon}<p,q\le x$ at
  exponents approaching $1/(4\sqrt e)\approx0.1516$. This is Ward--Maestro
  \cite[Prop.~35.3]{wardmaestro2026}, deduced from Friedlander's theorem on smooth shifted primes
  \cite{friedlander1989} and Fouvry's on shifted primes with a large prime factor. They note
  that it gives no average-time bound for a complete algorithm: the failure set has positive
  density.
\item Carella \cite{carella} claims a deterministic $O(N^{1/6+\varepsilon})$ algorithm. Its
  displayed proof has a normalisation defect (Section~\ref{sec:prior}).
\end{itemize}

This paper is about the \emph{average} cost over the balanced population, the semiprimes with
two factors of comparable size. For unrestricted semiprimes almost all have a factor below
$N^{\delta}$ \cite{crisanerban}, so the typical input is easy, although an average over a
complete algorithm can still be dominated by a sparse expensive tail. Balanced semiprimes are
the opposite. Every method above is analysed in the worst case or on a positive proportion,
and we are not aware of a proved average-case or almost-all deterministic exponent below $1/5$
for them.

\subsection{Population and cost}
Let $n$ be the bit length, $P=2^{n/2}$, and
\[
  \Sn=\{(p,q): p<q\le 2p,\ p,q\ \text{prime},\ 2^{n-1}\le pq<2^n\}.
\]
We count $\Sn$ by pairs. By unique factorisation this is the same as counting the semiprimes
$N=pq$, and ``uniform on $\Sn$'' means uniform on these semiprimes. For $(p,q)\in\Sn$ we have
$p\in[\sqrt{N/2},\sqrt N]\subset[P/2,P]$ and $q\in(P/\sqrt2,\sqrt2P)$. The prime number theorem
gives $c_0P^2/n^2\le|\Sn|$ for an absolute $c_0>0$ and all large $n$. Costs are bit operations
on a multitape Turing machine, with integer multiplication in time $M(b)=O(b\log b)$
\cite{hvdh2021}; $\tilde O(\cdot)$ hides factors polynomial in $n$, and a \emph{word operation}
is an operation on $O(n)$-bit integers. $\HH(N)=O(N^{1/5}\log^{16/5}N/(\log\log N)^{3/5})$ denotes the Harvey--Hittmeir
bound \cite{hh2022}.

\subsection{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[average-case complete factoring; proved]\label{thm:AV}
There is a deterministic algorithm $\LAD$ and an absolute constant $c$ with the following
properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Completeness.} On every input $N\ge2$, $\LAD$ outputs the complete prime
  factorisation of $N$.
\item \emph{Worst case.} On every $n$-bit input its cost is at most
  \[\HH(N)+O(n^{c+4}2^{7n/36})+O(n^{O(1)}2^{3n/20})=O(\HH(N)).\]
\item \emph{Average over $\Sn$.} For all large $n$,
  \[
    \frac1{|\Sn|}\sum_{(p,q)\in\Sn}\mathrm{cost}(pq)=O\bigl(2^{n/6}n^{c+1}\log n\bigr),
  \]
\item \emph{Other distributions.} The bound in (iii), multiplied by $K$, holds for every
  distribution on $\Sn$ with point masses at most $K/|\Sn|$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

that is, $N^{1/6}(\log N)^{c+1}\log\log N$ on average. The constant $c$ is the polylogarithmic exponent in the cost of one run of the search
(Proposition~\ref{prop:onerun}(ii)). Item (iv) covers, for instance, the product of two
independent uniform $n/2$-bit primes conditioned to be distinct, for even $n$: its support lies
in $\mathcal S_n\cup\mathcal S_{n-1}$ and its mass ratio is bounded (apply (iii) to each shell).
It says nothing about uniform $n$-bit integers, or about key-generation rules that restrict $p$
and $q$ further.

\begin{theorem}[almost-all factoring at fixed cost; proved]\label{thm:D}
There are deterministic algorithms $\DCL_{n,H}$ (Section~\ref{sec:alg}) with the following
property. Each sees only $N$. Each outputs either a proper divisor of $N$ or \texttt{FAIL}, and
never anything else.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item For every fixed $\varepsilon\in(0,1/40]$ and $n\ge n_0(\varepsilon)$, the choice
  $H=\lceil n2^{\varepsilon n/4}\rceil$ gives cost $N^{1/6+5\varepsilon/12}(\log N)^{O(1)}$ on
  every $n$-bit input. It outputs \texttt{FAIL} on $O(2^{-\varepsilon n})|\Sn|$ pairs of $\Sn$,
  i.e.\ on a fraction $O(N^{-\varepsilon})$.
\item For every fixed $a>0$, the choice $H=\lceil n^{1+a}\rceil$ gives cost
  $N^{1/6}(\log N)^{O(1+a)}$. It outputs \texttt{FAIL} on
  $O\bigl(n^{-\max(4a,\,1+2a)}\bigr)|\Sn|$ pairs.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

An earlier version of (a), proved with the arithmetic large sieve alone, gave cost
$N^{1/6+5\varepsilon/6+o(1)}$ for $\varepsilon\le1/60$ (Theorem~D$'$ of the project notes;
Remark~\ref{rem:Dprime}). The improvement comes from the two-variable fourth moment of
Section~\ref{sec:fourth}.

\begin{theorem}[worst case from coverage; proved]\label{thm:R}
There is a deterministic algorithm $\DCLwc$ (Section~\ref{sec:worst}) that takes as public
parameters a weight set and blocks of primes. Suppose $N=pq\in\Sn$ and some block contains a
prime $\ell$ with $\ell\mid Bp-Aq$ for some weight $(A,B)$. Then $\DCLwc$ outputs a proper divisor
of $N$. Its cost is $N^{1/6+o(1)}$ plus the list cost of the shared collision search over the blocks,
including target construction and rejected (weight, prime) tests
(Theorem~\ref{thm:Rprecise}(iii)). No hypothesis on any multiplicative order is needed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[proved]\label{cor:conditional}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item If Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:DCHpair} ($\mathrm{DCH}_{\mathrm{pair}}(\kappa)$) holds for some
  $\kappa$, then every $N\in\Sn$ is factored deterministically in $N^{1/6+o(1)}$ bit operations.
\item Fix $c\in(3/8,1/2]$. Suppose that for all large $n$ there is an $n$-only band scale
  $Y=N^{1/(7-2c)+o(1)}$ such that every pair of $\Sn$ has a prime $\ell\equiv3\pmod4$ in
  $(Y,2Y]$ dividing some $Bp-Aq$, with a primitive weight of height $H\le Y^{1/2}J^{-c}$, where
  $J\asymp Y/\log Y$ is the number of such primes. Then every $N\in\Sn$ is factored
  deterministically in $N^{(1-c)/(7/2-c)+o(1)}$ bit operations, and $(1-c)/(7/2-c)<1/5$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[true-factor floor for local designs; proved in the model]\label{thm:T1}
Consider a sumset collision search in which labels are charged, at most $X$ targets are issued
and the windows have total size at most $X^2$. Suppose its targets come from a fixed family of residue-local degree-one generators
(Section~\ref{sec:lower}), and that the two budgets hold per input or in expectation over all
$n$-bit integers. Then the prescribed coverage event (a target certifies the true factor) holds
for at most $Cn^2X^{3/2}2^{-n/4}|\Sn|$ pairs. Coverage of a constant fraction $\sigma$ of $\Sn$ therefore needs
$X\ge c\,\sigma^{2/3}2^{n/6}n^{-4/3}$. The theorem does not bound successes through
accidental collisions outside the coverage event.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:D}(b) attains the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:T1} up to polylogarithmic factors:
the algorithm $\DCL_{n,H}$ is dominated, at constant-factor cost, by a design of this kind
(Proposition~\ref{prop:Dinmodel}). This replaces a claim made in an earlier draft that the
algorithm is ``optimal in its model''. That claim was based on a lower bound for covering
\emph{every} integer candidate (Appendix~\ref{app:full}), which does not bound an algorithm that
only needs to reach the true factor.

\begin{remark}[scope]\label{rem:scope}
Each item below is easy to over-read, so we state them plainly.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Theorems~\ref{thm:AV} and~\ref{thm:D} concern inputs drawn from $\Sn$, with exceptions
  counted by pairs. Neither gives a worst-case bound below $1/5$. Theorem~\ref{thm:AV} is an
  \emph{expected-time} statement for a complete algorithm. By Markov's inequality it implies
  tail bounds on the cost; Theorem~\ref{thm:D} supplies sharper explicit tails in its parameter
  range.
\item Theorems~\ref{thm:AV} and~\ref{thm:D} treat only ratios $q/p\le2$.
  Section~\ref{sec:unbalanced} extends the almost-all statement to $q/p\le N^{o(1)}$, and shows
  a ratio gap with peak $N^{1/5}$ at $q/p\approx N^{1/5}$.
\item ``Never wrong'' means that every output is validated.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:T1} is a theorem inside a stated cost model. It is not a lower bound for
  factoring. Pollard's $p-1$ method, which lies outside the model, already reaches a positive
  proportion of a nearly balanced family below $N^{1/6}$ (\cite[Prop.~35.3]{wardmaestro2026},
  \cite{friedlander1989}). The cost budget in Theorem~\ref{thm:T1} is per input or averaged over
  all $n$-bit integers; budgets averaged only over $\Sn$ are not covered.
\item All statements are asymptotic. At 40--80 bits the search is $2.3$--$4.9$ times more
  expensive than Harvey--Hittmeir in our cost proxy (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\subsection{How it works}
Fix a prime $\ell$ and coprime weights $(A,B)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $\ell\mid Bp-Aq$, then $p\bmod\ell$ is one of the two square roots of $AN/B$ modulo
  $\ell$, and these roots are computable from $N$.
\item On that residue class the weighted sum $Bp+Aq$ is determined modulo $\ell^2$ (the
  Harvey--Hittmeir lock). So $g=(Bp+Aq-c)/\ell^2$ is an integer in a window of length about
  $H\sqrt N/\ell^2$.
\item Exponentiating, $2^{(L/\ell^2)(B+AN-c)}\equiv\gamma^{g}\pmod p$, with $\gamma=2^{L}$ and
  $L=\prod\ell^2$ over the band. So every target, for every prime of the band, is one giant step
  against the band's single baby list $\gamma^0,\dots,\gamma^{m-1}$.
\item Primes near $N^{1/6}$ balance the baby list ($\approx H\sqrt N/\ell^2$) against the number
  of targets ($\approx H^2\ell$).
\item Coverage of the true $p$ is a sieve problem: the large sieve gives the failure fraction
  $O(n/H^2)$, and a fourth moment over both variables gives $O(n^4/H^4)$.
\item The collision modulo $p$ is an exact identity, so no order lemma is needed there. It
  remains to rule out a simultaneous collision modulo $q$. Four disjoint bands make one band
  coprime to $q-1$, and on that band a two-sided collision forces $\ord_q(2)$ to divide a
  specific small expression, which is rare.
\end{itemize}
The failure fraction falls like $H^{-2}$ while the cost grows like $H^{5/3}$. So rerunning on
failure with $H\leftarrow H^{6/5}$ costs $N^{1/6}\polylog$ per rung on average, and after
$O(\log\log N)$ rungs only a fraction $N^{-1/30}$ of pairs remains for the $N^{1/5}$ fallback.
The order condition is handled up front by a baby-step giant-step search for the least $k$ with
$\gcd(2^k-1,N)>1$, up to $N^{3/10}$.

\subsection{What is new, and what is not}
We claim novelty only in the form ``we are not aware of''. Each ingredient is known
(Section~\ref{sec:prior}).
\begin{itemize}
\item The divisibility event $\lambda p\equiv\pm\mu q\pmod k$ with small multipliers, the
  $N$-determined residues and a modulus-squared reduction are due to McKee and Pinch
  \cite[\S5]{mckeepinch1996}. So is a \emph{heuristic} balanced method over primes
  $k\approx N^{1/4}$.
\item The modulus-squared lock in the form we use is Harvey--Hittmeir
  \cite[Prop.~3.3]{hh2022}.
\item Shared baby-step/giant-step collision search with multipoint evaluation over $\Z/N\Z$ is
  Hittmeir's \cite{hittmeir2018}, specialised to powers and to the simultaneous Lehman search
  by Harvey \cite[Alg.~4.1]{harvey2021}. Exact-match handling is \cite[Alg.~4.2]{harvey2021}.
\item The order-selection tools behind Theorem~\ref{thm:R} adapt Harvey--Hittmeir 2026
  \cite{hh2026}.
\item The large sieve is Montgomery's \cite{montgomery1968,mv1973}.
\end{itemize}
We are not aware of a prior statement of any of the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a band of primes at $N^{1/6}H^{-1/3}$, made possible by one baby list per band shared
  through cofactor-powered handles;
\item a proof that the event catches the true factor for all but $O(n/H^2)$, or
  $O(n^4/H^4)$, of the pairs;
\item for the average case, an extraction argument with no order hypothesis: the collision
  modulo $p$ is an unconditional identity, and a clean-band argument bounds the two-sided
  collisions on average; for the worst case, a separate extraction algorithm
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:R});
\item the (routine, once the tail bound is available) observation that the proved tail beats the
  cost growth, which turns the almost-all algorithm into a complete one with average exponent
  $1/6$ by standard restarts and a baby-step giant-step order search. The substantive input to
  Theorem~\ref{thm:AV} is the one-run analysis (Proposition~\ref{prop:onerun});
\item the reduction of the worst case to coverage (Theorem~\ref{thm:R});
\item a true-factor lower bound for local designs (Theorem~\ref{thm:T1}).
\end{enumerate}
The full-candidate covering floor $N^{1/6}$ of Appendix~\ref{app:full} is \emph{not} new. The
same exponent, by a closely related argument, is in Ward--Maestro \cite[Thm.~3.6]{wardmaestro2026}
(2 September 2026) for Lehman forms. Their model allows a common modulus: thinning by residue
classes (Lemma~2.3, at the free-giant level) and Harvey--Hittmeir-type single-class locks
(Theorem~2.8, with materialised giants). Our appendix version allows per-target moduli at the
free-giant level. We derived it independently and later.

\section{Prior art}\label{sec:prior}
%======================================================================

We read McKee--Pinch \S5, Ward--Maestro and Carella \S5 ourselves. The other descriptions
come from GPT-6.1-sol literature sweeps (see the Acknowledgements), which checked statements
against the sources. They are marked \emph{[sweep]}. Where even the sweep did not check a
source, we say UNVERIFIED.

\paragraph{Lehman and weighted Fermat searches.}
Lehman \cite{lehman1974} searches for $4kN=x^2-y^2$ over small multipliers $k$ and proves a
deterministic $N^{1/3+o(1)}$ bound [sweep]. Weighted Fermat searches go back to Lawrence
\cite{lawrence1895} (UNVERIFIED). The weighted sums $Bp+Aq$ used here are Lehman's mechanism.

\paragraph{McKee--Pinch 1996, \S5: the direct precursor.}
We read \S5 (pp.~221--223) of \cite{mckeepinch1996} from the public scan indexed at
\url{https://cr.yp.to/bib/1996/mckee-deterministic.html}. It contains five things.
\begin{itemize}
\item Lehmer's speed-up: if $p\equiv q\equiv1\pmod k$, Fermat's method runs in $O(q/k^2)$.
\item An extension: for $p\equiv\pm q\pmod k$, Fermat runs in $O(q/k^{2-\epsilon})$, with $u$
  or $v$ in $n=u^2-v^2$ determined modulo $k^2$ from square roots modulo $k$. This is a
  modulus-squared lock.
\item Multipliers: if $\lambda p\equiv\pm\mu q\pmod k$, the same method splits $\lambda\mu n$.
\item A deterministic $O(n^{1/3+\epsilon})$ algorithm, built on a pigeonhole lemma.
\item A heuristic balanced method, described by the authors as ``not however an algorithm''.
  It takes primes $k\in(n^{1/4},2n^{1/4})$ and small $\lambda,\mu$, and hopes that
  $\lambda p-\mu q\equiv0\pmod k$ for some of them, at expected cost $O(n^{1/4+\epsilon})$.
\end{itemize}
So the core event of this paper is theirs: a band prime divides a small-weight combination,
with $N$-determined residues and a modulus-squared reduction. We trade a larger window for
fewer, smaller primes through one baby list per band. We also prove the coverage statement, the
extraction, the average-time theorem and the worst-case reduction.

\paragraph{Hittmeir 2018, 2021.}
Hittmeir \cite{hittmeir2018} combines baby-step/giant-step with sieving of $p+q$ modulo many
small primes and proves $N^{1/4}\exp(-\Omega(\log N/\log\log N))$ [sweep].
Hittmeir~\cite{hittmeir2021} proves $N^{2/9+o(1)}$ through a time--space trade-off for Lehman's
method [sweep]. Hyperbola sieving over many small primes is therefore old. What differs here is
that each prime is used \emph{selectively}: only the at most two classes reached by a short weight
vector are used, rather than all classes being covered.

\paragraph{Harvey 2021; Harvey--Hittmeir 2022.}
Harvey \cite{harvey2021} proves the deterministic bound
\[O(N^{1/5}\log^{16/5}N),\]
using a single shared baby-step giant-step search over Lehman's family. He uses the congruences
\[\alpha^{aq+bp}\equiv\alpha^{aN+b}\pmod p\]
and multipoint evaluation over $\Z/N\Z$
(Alg.~4.1, which Harvey describes as almost identical to Hittmeir's Algorithm~3.2
\cite{hittmeir2018}), with exact matches handled in Alg.~4.2 [sweep]. Harvey--Hittmeir \cite{hh2022} save a
factor $(\log\log N)^{3/5}$. Their Proposition~3.3 contains the congruence $aq-bp\equiv0\pmod m$
and the modulus-squared lock we use as Lemma~\ref{lem:lock}, with a single modulus and
lattice-chosen weights [sweep]. Harvey remarks that exponent $1/6$ would need a ``fully
square-root speedup'' of Lehman's search [sweep].

\paragraph{Harvey--Hittmeir 2026; Oznovich--Volk 2025.}
These works give deterministic algorithms that find an element of large multiplicative order
modulo $N$, or a factor \cite{hh2026,oznovichvolk2025} [sweep]. Theorem~\ref{thm:R} adapts
Harvey--Hittmeir's smooth-number subgroup argument and their order dichotomy
(\cite[Lemmas~2.1, 2.3, 2.4 and \S3.1]{hh2026} [sweep]) to a one-sided reading: the first $r$
with $\gcd(b^r-1,N)>1$ gives a factor unless the two orders coincide. Large order modulo $N$ does
not give large order modulo a specific prime. Our average-case theorems therefore never need an
element of large order: $\ord_q(2)$ enters only in counting the exceptional sets ($E_a$, $E_c$,
$C(T)$) and in the cheap detector of Section~\ref{sec:AV}.

\paragraph{Ward--Maestro 2026.}
We read the report \cite{wardmaestro2026} (dated 2 September 2026, 110 pages, six parts). Three
parts of it bear on this paper.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The covering floors.} The report defines an ``oblivious Lehman-cell BSGS''
  model (Definition~2.1): forms $aq+bp$ with $a,b\ge1$ and tested windows that must cover every
  real $p$ in the range. In that model it proves floors of $N^{1/3}$ candidates
  (Corollary~3.3), $N^{1/5}$ with materialised giants (Theorem~3.4) and $N^{1/6}$ with free
  giants (Theorem~3.6). It also treats unbalanced ranges, forms of bounded degree
  (Theorem~9.4), thinning by residue classes modulo $q\le N^{1/20}$ (Theorem~2.8), and
  randomised covering (Remark~3.5).
\item \emph{Partial information.} It gives deterministic searches given partial information,
  including $N^{1/5+o(1)}M^{-2/5}$ given $p\bmod M$ with $M\le N^{1/7}$ (Proposition~14.2).
\item \emph{The $p-1$ result.} It proves the positive-proportion $p-1$ result quoted in the
  introduction (Proposition~35.3).
\end{itemize}
Its $N^{1/6}$ floor and the main idea of its proof (the curvature bound on the covering sum,
Lemma~3.2) pre-date and overlap our Appendix~\ref{app:full}. Our version allows per-target lock
moduli and charges window counts per modulus, but the exponent and its proof idea are theirs.
\begin{itemize}
\item The report contains no algorithm with an almost-all or average-case guarantee below
  $N^{1/5}$.
\item It does not use many-prime selective covering, a prime band near $N^{1/6}$, or a baby list
  shared across moduli.
\item It considers no true-factor counting under residue-local keys of the kind in
  Theorem~\ref{thm:T1}.
\end{itemize}
Proposition~14.2 there is related in spirit to our targets, which compute $p\bmod\ell$ from the
event $\ell\mid Bp-Aq$ rather than receive it. Ward--Maestro cite Carella's claim as unverified.

\paragraph{St\u{a}nic\u{a}--Mulder--Hittmeir 2026.}
\cite{smh2026} studies unions of weighted modular-hyperbola sieve sets, their CRT behaviour and
elliptic-trace obstructions [sweep]. Its averages are over finite-field parameters. The sweep
found no global coverage theorem over prime pairs in it.

\paragraph{Gao--Feng--Hu--Pan 2025.}
For balanced semiprimes, \cite{gao2025} claims a deterministic algorithm of cost
\[O(N^{1/5}\log^{13/5}N/(\log\log N)^{3/5})\]
via rank-3 lattices [sweep]. The exponent
remains $1/5$.

\paragraph{Umans--Wang 2025; He--Sahai 2026.}
Umans and Wang \cite{umanswang2025} show that a Strong Prefactored Divisor Conjecture would
give deterministic exponent $1/6$ (their Thms.~5.2 and~5.5). He and Sahai \cite{hesahai2026}
refute the one-dimensional arithmetic-progression version at the proposed parameters; higher
ranks remain open [sweep]. Our results are unconditional but average-case. The covering
statement that would make our method worst-case (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:DCHpair}) is a different
object. We know of no implication between it and the Umans--Wang conjecture in either
direction, and we know of no transfer of the He--Sahai argument to it.

\paragraph{Carella, arXiv:1308.2891.}
Carella \cite{carella} states (Theorem~1.1) a deterministic algorithm that factors a balanced
$N=pq$ in $O(N^{1/6+\varepsilon})$ operations. The algorithm guesses the least significant
$(\log N)/6$ bits of $p$ and recovers the rest by a bivariate small-root method. We read its \S5.
The argument there runs as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item Put $B=\lfloor N^{1/6}\rfloor$. Write $p=x_1B^3+Bx+x_0$ and $q=y_1B^3+By+y_0$ with
  $|x|,|y|\le N^{1/3}$, where $y_0\equiv Nx_0^{-1}\pmod B$.
\item Equation~(17) considers $f(x,y)=pq-N$ and lists its coefficients.
\item The listed coefficients of $x$ and $y$ are $y_1B^3+y_0$ and $x_1B^3+x_0$, and the
  coefficient of $xy$ is $B^2$.
\item From this list $f$ is declared primitive. With $X=Y=N^{1/3}$ its height is $W=N$, and the
  small-root condition $XY<W^{2/3}$ is invoked.
\end{itemize}
Expanding the product directly gives linear coefficients $B(y_1B^3+y_0)$ and $B(x_1B^3+x_0)$.
The constant term $(x_1B^3+x_0)(y_1B^3+y_0)-N$ is also divisible by $B$, because
$x_0y_0\equiv N\pmod B$. So $f$ has content divisible by $B$. The small-root theorem applies to
the primitive polynomial $f/B$, whose height is about $N/B=N^{5/6}$. For it, $XY=N^{2/3}$ exceeds
$(N^{5/6})^{2/3}=N^{5/9}$, so the condition fails. (Even before this correction the condition
holds only with equality.) This matches the familiar fact that small-root methods need about
$(\log N)/4$ known bits of a factor, not $(\log N)/6$. We conclude only that the displayed
argument does not establish the claimed bound. The same defect was found independently in three
of our AI reviews.

\paragraph{Lower bounds in restricted models.}
Lower bounds for factoring exist only in restricted models. Brown \cite{brown2010} bounds the
length of straight-line factoring programs, a class that includes a variant of the elliptic
curve method. Ward--Maestro prove floors in covering models. Theorem~\ref{thm:T1} is of the same
kind, a statement inside a stated cost model, and we make no claim about factoring lower bounds in
general.

\paragraph{The large sieve; moments.}
We use the arithmetic large sieve in Montgomery's form for squarefree moduli
\cite{montgomery1968}, with the Montgomery--Vaughan constant \cite{mv1973}. The barrier remark
in Section~\ref{sec:fourth} uses Peled--Yadin--Yehudayoff \cite{pyy2011}.

\paragraph{Summary.}
Our own searches, and GPT-6.1-sol sweeps checking about twenty items, found no unconditional,
oracle-free deterministic result below exponent $1/5$ that factors almost all, or on average,
balanced prime pairs. The only exception is Carella's claim above. This is the outcome of a
search, not a proof of priority.

\section{The search \texorpdfstring{$\DCL_{n,H}$}{DCL}}\label{sec:alg}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Parameters}
All parameters depend on $n$ and on an integer weight height $H$, never on $N$. We assume
throughout
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hrange}
  H_0\le H\le 2^{n/50},\qquad n\ge n_0,
\end{equation}
for absolute constants $H_0,n_0$. The constant $n_0$ is an eventual, very large guard: for
example, the top rung of Section~\ref{sec:AV} needs $n^2 2^{\lceil n/60\rceil}\le2^{n/50}$, which
holds only for $n$ in the thousands. The experiments of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} run far
below it.
\begin{itemize}
\item The weights are $V=\{(A,B)\in[1,H]^2:\gcd(A,B)=1,\ A\ne B\}$. For $H\ge H_0$ we have
  $H^2/2\le|V|\le H^2$.
\item The band scale is $Y=\lceil 2^{n/6}H^{-1/3}\rceil$.
\item There are four bands, $k=0,1,2,3$:
  \[\Pk=\{\ell\ \text{prime}:\ \ell\equiv3\pmod 4,\ 2^kY<\ell\le2^{k+1}Y\},\qquad
  L_k=\prod_{\ell\in\Pk}\ell^2.\]
\item The window bound is $\spanbar=\lfloor\tfrac52H(2^{n/2}+1)\rfloor+2H+3$, and
  $\mbar=\lfloor\spanbar/(2^kY)^2\rfloor+2$.
\item The base is $\beta=2$.
\end{itemize}
Under \eqref{eq:Hrange} the following scale separations hold:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scales}
  Y\ge 16H^2,\qquad 16Y<P/2,\qquad Y^4>\sqrt2\,P,\qquad H\lceil\sqrt2P\rceil<Y^4.
\end{equation}
Indeed $H^{7/3}\le2^{7n/150}<2^{n/6}/32$, and $Y^4\ge2^{(2/3-4/150)n}>2^{n/2+1}H$.

\subsection{Steps}
Input: $N$ with $n$ bits.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item If $N<4$ output \texttt{FAIL}. If $N$ is even output $2$. Put
  $a_0=\lceil\sqrt{N/2}\rceil$ and $b_0=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$. Every $(p,q)\in\Sn$ has
  $p\in[a_0,b_0]$.
\item For $v=(A,B)\in V$ let
  \[F_v(x)=Bx+AN/x,\qquad \mathrm{Lo}_v=\lfloor2\sqrt{ABN}\rfloor,\qquad
  \mathrm{Hi}_v=\max_{x\in\{a_0,b_0\}}\lceil F_v(x)\rceil.\]
  \begin{itemize}
  \item By AM--GM, $\mathrm{Lo}_v\le F_v(x)$ for all $x>0$.
  \item By convexity, $F_v\le\mathrm{Hi}_v$ on $[a_0,b_0]$.
  \item $\mathrm{Hi}_v-\mathrm{Lo}_v\le(B+\sqrt2A)\sqrt N+2H+2\le\spanbar$.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
Then, for each block $k=0,1,2,3$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,resume]
\item \emph{Exponent tree.} Build the balanced integer product tree of
  $\{\ell^2:\ell\in\Pk\}$, and push $2$ down the tree: a node holding $2^{L_k/\Pi(\text{node})}$
  passes $2^{L_k/\Pi(\text{node})\cdot\Pi(\text{sibling})}$ to each child, where $\Pi$ denotes
  the product of the leaves below a node. This yields $u_\ell=2^{L_k/\ell^2}\bmod N$ for all
  $\ell\in\Pk$. Put $\gamma=u_{\ell_0}^{\ell_0^2}=2^{L_k}\bmod N$. If some $\ell\in\Pk$ divides
  $N$ and $1<\ell<N$, output $\ell$.
\item \emph{Targets.} For $\ell\in\Pk$ and $v\in V$, let $a=ANB^{-1}\bmod\ell$. If
  $a^{(\ell-1)/2}\not\equiv1\pmod\ell$, skip. Otherwise put $r_0=a^{(\ell+1)/4}\bmod\ell$; the
  roots of $Br^2\equiv AN\pmod\ell$ are $r_0$ and $\ell-r_0$. For each root $r$ put
  \[
    s=Nr^{-1}\bmod\ell^2,\quad c=(Br+As)\bmod\ell^2,\quad
    g_{\mathrm{lo}}=\Bigl\lceil\frac{\mathrm{Lo}_v-c}{\ell^2}\Bigr\rceil,\quad
    g_{\mathrm{hi}}=\Bigl\lfloor\frac{\mathrm{Hi}_v-c}{\ell^2}\Bigr\rfloor,
  \]
  and $W_\tau=g_{\mathrm{hi}}-g_{\mathrm{lo}}+1\le\mbar$. The triple $\tau=(v,\ell,r)$ is a
  \emph{target}. Let $T_k\le2|V||\Pk|$ be the number of targets. If $T_k=0$, skip the block.
\item \emph{Baby length.} Choose $m$ either as $m=\mbar$, or by the \emph{endpoint-competitive}
  rule: minimise $m+\sum_\tau\lceil W_\tau/m\rceil$ over an integer geometric grid whose last
  point is $\max_\tau W_\tau$. Under either rule $m\le\mbar$ and $m+T'\le\mbar+T_k$, where
  $T'=\sum_\tau\lceil W_\tau/m\rceil$.
\item \emph{Giants.} Precompute $w_\ell=u_\ell^N$. For each target and
  $0\le j<\lceil W_\tau/m\rceil$,
  \[
    G_{\tau,j}=w_\ell^{A}\,u_\ell^{\,B-c+\ell^2}\,\gamma^{-(g_{\mathrm{lo}}+1)}\,\gamma^{-jm}
    \equiv u_\ell^{\,B+AN-c}\,\gamma^{-(g_{\mathrm{lo}}+jm)}\pmod N.
  \]
\item \emph{Collision detection.} Build $f(x)=\prod_{0\le i<m}(x-\gamma^i)\in(\Z/N\Z)[x]$ by a
  product tree, and evaluate $f$ at all giants by a remainder tree. Only monic divisors occur, so
  this is valid over $\Z/N\Z$. For each giant compute $\gcd(f(G),N)$, and output it if it lies
  strictly between $1$ and $N$.
\end{enumerate}
If no block outputs a divisor, output \texttt{FAIL}.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:alg}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item No multiplicative order is computed, assumed or certified, and no randomness is used.
\item The weight $A=B$ is excluded. By Lemma~\ref{lem:modq} its true giant always collides
  modulo $q$ too.
\item One of the two rules in step~5 is mandatory for the cost bound. The bound $m\le\mbar$ alone
  would allow $m=1$, making $T'$ too large.
\item An implementation (Python, gmpy2, python-flint) is \texttt{src/dcltheorem/algorithm.py}.
  Every output $x$ is checked for $1<x<N$ and $x\mid N$. An exhaustive regression covers all
  $2\le N\le10^4$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{Analysis of one run}\label{sec:onerun}
%======================================================================

Throughout this section fix $(p,q)\in\Sn$, $N=pq$ and a block $k$. Write $L=L_k$, $u=u_\ell$ and
$d=\ord_q(2)$. By \eqref{eq:scales} every band prime is smaller than both factors and exceeds $H$.

\subsection{Lock, coverage and windows}

\begin{lemma}[modulus-squared lock; {\cite[Prop.~3.3]{hh2022}}]\label{lem:lock}
Let $\ell\in\Pk$, $(A,B)\in V$, and let $r$ be a root of $Br^2\equiv AN\pmod\ell$. If
$p\equiv r\pmod\ell$, then $g:=(Bp+Aq-c)/\ell^2\in\Z$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\ell\nmid ABN$. Write $p=r+\ell x$. Modulo $\ell^2$, $p^{-1}\equiv r^{-1}(1-\ell xr^{-1})$, so
$q\equiv s-\ell x\,sr^{-1}$ and $Bp+Aq\equiv c+\ell x(B-Asr^{-1})$. Since $sr^{-1}\equiv Nr^{-2}$
and $Br^2\equiv AN$ modulo $\ell$, the last term vanishes modulo $\ell^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[coverage criterion]\label{lem:coverage}
$p\bmod\ell$ is one of the computed roots if and only if $\ell\mid Bp-Aq$. In that case the
target $\tau=(v,\ell,p\bmod\ell)$ exists; we say that $\tau$ \emph{covers} $p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$Bp^2-AN=p(Bp-Aq)$ and $\ell\nmid p$. If $\ell\mid Bp-Aq$ then $a\equiv p^2$ is a nonzero
square, and for $\ell\equiv3\pmod4$ its roots are $\pm a^{(\ell+1)/4}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[window]\label{lem:window}
If $\tau$ covers $p$, then $g_{\mathrm{lo}}\le g\le g_{\mathrm{hi}}$. Put
$i_{\mathrm{full}}=g-g_{\mathrm{lo}}$, $j^*=\lfloor i_{\mathrm{full}}/m\rfloor$ and
$i^*=i_{\mathrm{full}}-j^*m$. Then $G_{\tau,j^*}$ is computed; we call it the \emph{true
giant}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{Lo}_v\le F_v(p)=Bp+Aq\le\mathrm{Hi}_v$ because $p\in[a_0,b_0]$, and
$i_{\mathrm{full}}\le W_\tau-1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact collision modulo \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p}; the clean band}

\begin{lemma}[mod $p$]\label{lem:modp}
If $\tau$ covers $p$, then $G_{\tau,j^*}\equiv\gamma^{i^*}\pmod p$. No hypothesis on any
multiplicative order is needed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$B+AN-(Bp+Aq)=(p-1)(Aq-B)$, so $B+AN-c=\ell^2g+(p-1)(Aq-B)$. Since $2^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p$,
$u^{B+AN-c}\equiv2^{L g}=\gamma^g\pmod p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[mod $q$]\label{lem:modq}
If $\tau$ covers $p$, then $G_{\tau,j^*}\equiv\gamma^{i^*}u^{(A-B)(p-1)}\pmod q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Modulo $q-1$ we have $N\equiv p$. So $B+AN-c\equiv B+Ap-c$, while $\ell^2g\equiv Bp+A-c$, and
the difference is $(A-B)(p-1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
The true giant $G$ is \emph{FULL} if $G\equiv\gamma^{i'}\pmod q$ for some $i'\in[0,m)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[one-sided extraction]\label{lem:extract}
If $\tau$ covers $p$ and its true giant $G$ is not FULL, then $\gcd(f(G),N)=p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The factor $i=i^*$ of $f(G)=\prod_{i<m}(G-\gamma^i)$ vanishes modulo $p$. Modulo $q$ no factor
vanishes, and $\Z/q\Z$ is a field.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[clean band]\label{lem:clean}
Call block $k$ \emph{clean for $q$} if no $\ell\in\Pk$ divides $q-1$. At least one of the four
blocks is clean for $q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Otherwise $q-1$ has four distinct prime factors exceeding $Y$, so $q-1>Y^4>\sqrt2P>q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[FULL on a clean block]\label{lem:full}
If block $k$ is clean for $q$, then the true giant is FULL if and only if
$d\mid(A-B)(p-1)-\ell^2\delta$ for some $\delta\in[-i^*,m-1-i^*]\subset(-m,m)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
FULL holds iff $2^{(L/\ell^2)[(A-B)(p-1)-\ell^2\delta]}\equiv1\pmod q$ for some
$\delta=i'-i^*$. Since $d\mid q-1$ and $\gcd(L,q-1)=1$, the factor $L/\ell^2$ cancels.
\end{proof}
Only the enlarged range $|\delta|<m\le\mbar$ is used for counting below.

\subsection{Counting lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[small orders are rare]\label{lem:smallorder}
For $Q_0\ge2$ and $T\ge1$,
$\#\{q>Q_0\ \text{prime}:\ \ord_q(2)\le T\}\le T(T+1)/(2\log_2Q_0)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Such a $q$ divides $2^j-1$ for some $j\le T$, and $2^j-1$ has fewer than $j/\log_2Q_0$ prime
factors exceeding $Q_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[coverage by the large sieve]\label{lem:sieve}
Fix a prime $q>16Y$ and a block $k$. Let $U_k(q)$ be the set of primes $p\in(P/2,P]$ with
$\ell\nmid Bp-Aq$ for all $(v,\ell)\in V\times\Pk$. Then $|U_k(q)|\le CP/H^2$ for an absolute $C$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The forbidden classes.} For $\ell\in\Pk$ we have $\ell\nmid qB$, so $\ell\mid Bp-Aq$ iff
  $p\equiv AqB^{-1}\pmod\ell$. These $|V|$ classes are distinct, because $0<|AB'-A'B|<H^2<\ell$. So $U=U_k(q)$
  avoids $|V|<\ell$ classes modulo every $\ell\in\Pk$. Let $Q=\lfloor P^{1/2}\rfloor$ and
  $Z=\lfloor Q/2^{k+1}Y\rfloor$. Then $1\le Z<Y$ and $\ln Z\ge c''n$ by \eqref{eq:Hrange}. $U$
  also avoids the class $0$ modulo every prime $r\le Z$, since $p>P/2>Z$.
\item \emph{The large sieve.} Montgomery's arithmetic large sieve \cite{montgomery1968}, with the
  constant of \cite{mv1973} (for a set avoiding $\omega(r)$ classes modulo each prime $r\le Q$),
  gives $|U|\le(P/2+1+Q^2)/\mathcal J$, where
  $\mathcal J=\sum_{d\le Q}\mu^2(d)\prod_{r\mid d}\omega(r)/(r-\omega(r))$.
\item \emph{Bounding $\mathcal J$.} Keep only the terms $d=\ell a$ with $\ell\in\Pk$ and $a\le Z$
  squarefree. These are squarefree and at most $Q$, because the primes of $a$ are below $Y<\ell$.
  Each contributes $\frac{|V|}{\ell-|V|}\cdot\frac1{\varphi(a)}$. Using
  $\sum_{a\le Z}\mu^2(a)/\varphi(a)=\sum_{\mathrm{rad}(m)\le Z}1/m\ge\sum_{m\le Z}1/m\ge\ln Z$
  (expand $1/\varphi(a)=\prod_{r\mid a}(1/r+1/r^2+\cdots)$), we get
  $\mathcal J\ge\lambda_k\ln Z$, where $\lambda_k:=|V|\sum_{\ell\in\Pk}1/\ell$.
\item \emph{Conclusion.} By the prime number theorem in the progression $3\bmod4$,
  $\sum_{\ell\in\Pk}1/\ell\ge c'/n$, so $\lambda_k\ge c'H^2/(2n)$. Hence
  $|U|\le 2P/(\lambda_k c''n)\le CP/H^2$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

Using only the prime moduli $\ell$, as in the first version of this work, gives $CnP/H^2$
instead. The refinement by the presieve over $r\le Z$ was pointed out by a GPT-6.1-sol referee.

\begin{lemma}[FULL is rare]\label{lem:crt}
Fix $q$ with $d=\ord_q(2)>T$ and a block $k$ clean for $q$. Let $F_k(q)$ be the set of integers
$p\in[P/2,P]$ for which some $(v,\ell)\in V\times\Pk$ has $\ell\mid Bp-Aq$ and
$d\mid(A-B)(p-1)-\ell^2\delta$ for some $|\delta|<\mbar$. Then
\[
  |F_k(q)|\le2|V||\Pk|\,\mbar\Bigl(\frac{PH}{2^{k+1}YT}+1\Bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $(v,\ell,\delta)$ and let $g'=\gcd(A-B,d)\le H$. The congruence on $p$ is unsolvable or holds
on one class modulo $d/g'$. Cleanliness gives $\ell\nmid d$. With $p\equiv AqB^{-1}\pmod\ell$, the
CRT gives one class modulo $\ell d/g'\ge\ell T/H$. There are at most $|V||\Pk|(2\mbar-1)$ triples.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One run}
For $T\ge1$ (an analysis-only parameter) and $(p,q)\in\Sn$, let $k(q)$ be the least block clean
for $q$. Define
\[
  E_a(T)=\{d\le T\},\quad E_b(H)=\{d>T,\ p\in U_{k(q)}(q)\},\quad
  E_c(H,T)=\{d>T,\ p\in F_{k(q)}(q)\}.
\]
(The set $E_b$ also depends on $T$; its bound does not, so we suppress $T$.)

\begin{proposition}[one run; proved]\label{prop:onerun}
There are absolute constants $C,c$ such that for $H$ in the range \eqref{eq:Hrange} and
$1\le T\le P/2$ the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\DCL_{n,H}$ outputs a proper divisor of $N$ or \texttt{FAIL} on every input.
\item It costs at most $Cn^cH^{5/3}2^{n/6}$ bit operations on every $n$-bit input.
\item Every \texttt{FAIL} pair of $\Sn$ lies in $E_a(T)\cup E_b(H)\cup E_c(H,T)$, and
  \[
  \frac{|E_a|}{|\Sn|}\le\frac{CnT^2}{P},\quad \frac{|E_b|}{|\Sn|}\le\frac{Cn}{H^2},\quad
  \frac{|E_c|}{|\Sn|}\le Cn\Bigl(\frac{H^{14/3}P^{1/3}}{T}+\frac{H^{10/3}}{P^{1/3}}\Bigr).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Success certificate.} Suppose $d>T$, $p\notin U_{k(q)}(q)$ and
  $p\notin F_{k(q)}(q)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:coverage} some target of block $k(q)$ covers $p$, and
  by Lemma~\ref{lem:window} its true giant is computed. By Lemma~\ref{lem:full} it is not FULL,
  since the exact $\delta$-range lies in $|\delta|<\mbar$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:extract} its gcd is
  $p$. So some block outputs a proper divisor.
\item \emph{Normalisation.} There are at most $3P/n$ primes $q\in(P/\sqrt2,\sqrt2P)$, and we
  divide by $|\Sn|\ge c_0P^2/n^2$. This needs no prime counts in short intervals.
\item \emph{$E_a$.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:smallorder} with $Q_0=2^{(n-1)/2}$, at most
  $T(T+1)/(n-1)$ primes $q$ qualify, each with at most $P/2+1$ partners.
\item \emph{$E_b$.} This follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:sieve}.
\item \emph{$E_c$.} Use $|V|\le H^2$, $|\Pk|\le2^kY$ and $\mbar\le3HP/(4^kY^2)$ in
  Lemma~\ref{lem:crt}. This gives $|F_k(q)|\le6H^4P^2/(Y^2T)+6H^3P/Y$. Then sum over $q$ and use
  $Y\ge P^{1/3}H^{-1/3}$.
\item \emph{(i).} Only validated gcds and band primes are output.
\item \emph{(ii).} See \S\ref{sec:cost}.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Theorem D$'$]\label{rem:Dprime}
Take $H=\lceil n2^{\varepsilon n/2}\rceil$ and $T=\lfloor2^{(1/4-\varepsilon)n}\rfloor$. Then
Proposition~\ref{prop:onerun} gives cost $N^{1/6+5\varepsilon/6+o(1)}$ and exceptions
$O(N^{-\varepsilon})$ for every fixed $\varepsilon<1/52$. The binding term is the first $E_c$
term, which needs $-1/12+13\varepsilon/3<0$. This is Theorem~D$'$ of the project notes, stated
there for $\varepsilon\le1/60$.

A dyadic recount of $E_c$ over the size of $\ord_q(2)$ removes $E_a$ and certifies the unchanged
algorithm for every $\varepsilon<1/40$. Combining D$'$ at $\varepsilon=1/55$ with a single
Harvey--Hittmeir fallback gives average cost $N^{2/11+o(1)}$ on $\Sn$. This was observed by a
GPT-6.1-sol referee and is superseded by Theorem~\ref{thm:AV}. (The dyadic recount and the
$2/11$ claim are proved in \texttt{notes/avgtime\_results.md}, Lemma~D and \S4. Neither is used
below.)
\end{remark}

\section{A fourth-moment coverage bound}\label{sec:fourth}
%======================================================================

The large sieve bounds the uncovered fraction by $O(1/\lambda)$, where $\lambda\asymp H^2/n$ is
the expected number of covering events. Here we average over both $p$ and $q$ and control the
joint statistics of up to four band primes. This gives $O(\lambda^{-2})$.

\subsection{Setting}
\begin{itemize}
\item Let $\Xb\ge1$ be an integer and $\mathfrak B_0=(0,\Xb]^2\cap\Z^2$.
\item Let $\mathcal P$ be a nonempty set of primes in $(Y,2Y]$, and $V$ a nonempty set of
  coprime pairs $(A,B)\in[1,H]^2$.
\item Put $s=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P}1/\ell$ and $\lambda=|V|s$.
\item For $x=(a,b)$ and $v=(A,B)$ put $F_v(x)=Ba-Ab$, and
  $z_\ell(x)=\#\{v\in V:\ell\mid F_v(x)\}$, $\mathcal W(x)=\#\{\ell\in\mathcal P:z_\ell(x)>0\}$.
  The point $x$ is \emph{uncovered} if $\mathcal W(x)=0$.
\item Let $\mathcal L=\bigcup_{v\in V}\R v$ and $\mathfrak B=\mathfrak B_0\setminus\mathcal L$.
  A prime pair $p\ne q$ with $p,q>H$ never lies on $\mathcal L$.
\item A \emph{configuration} of order $j$ is $\mathbf c=((\ell_1,v_1),\dots,(\ell_j,v_j))$ with
  distinct $\ell_i\in\mathcal P$ and $v_i\in V$. Its lattice is
  $\Lambda_{\mathbf c}=\{x:\ell_i\mid F_{v_i}(x)\ \forall i\}$, and
  $d_{\mathbf c}=\prod\ell_i$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[two-variable fourth moment; proved]\label{thm:fourth}
Assume $H\ge2$, $Y\ge16H^2$, $H\Xb<Y^4$ and $\lambda\ge1$. Then
\[
  \#\{x\in\mathfrak B:\mathcal W(x)=0\}\le \Xb^2\Bigl(\frac3{\lambda^2}+\frac1{\lambda^3}\Bigr)
  +2H\Xb+\lambda^{-4}\sum_{j=1}^4\kappa_j\mathrm{Err}_j,
\]
with $\kappa_1=15\lambda^3$, $\kappa_2=25\lambda^2$, $\kappa_3=10\lambda$, $\kappa_4=1$ and
$\mathrm{Err}_j=A_j+B_j+C_j+D_j+E_j+F_j$, where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $A_j=(|\mathcal P||V|)^j$;
\item $B_j=28.3\,\Xb(2Y)^{j/2}(2|V|)^j$;
\item $C_j=2H\Xb\sum_{r=\lceil j/2\rceil}^{j}\binom jr\lambda^r|\mathcal P|^{j-r}$;
\item $D_j=\Xb^2|V|s^4$ if $j=4$, and $D_j=0$ otherwise;
\item $E_j=6H\Xb\sum_{r\in I_j}\binom jr\lambda^r|\mathcal P|^{j-r}
  +2H\Xb|V|\sum_{r\in I_j}\binom jrs^{j-r}(|\mathcal P||V|)^r$, with
  $I_j=[\max(0,j-3),\lfloor j/2\rfloor]$;
\item $F_j=(3|V|)^j\Xb^2s/Y$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\subsubsection{Elementary facts}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(F\arabic*)]
\item If $\ell\in\mathcal P$ does not divide both coordinates of $x$, then $z_\ell(x)\le1$.
  Two events give $\ell\mid(AB'-A'B)a$ and $\ell\mid(AB'-A'B)b$, with $0<|AB'-A'B|<H^2<\ell$.
\item For $x\in\mathfrak B$, every $F_v(x)$ is a nonzero integer with $|F_v(x)|<H\Xb<Y^4$. So it
  has at most $3$ prime factors in $(Y,2Y]$, and $\sum_\ell z_\ell(x)\le3|V|$.
\item $\Lambda_{\mathbf c}$ has index $d_{\mathbf c}$, by the CRT, since $x\mapsto F_{v_i}(x)$
  maps onto $\Z/\ell_i$.
\item For $v\in V$ let $r_v(\mathbf c)=\#\{i:v_i\ne v\}$ and $t_v(\mathbf c)=\prod_{v_i\ne
  v}\ell_i$. Then $\Lambda_{\mathbf c}\cap\R v=\Z t_vv$, since $0<|B_iA-A_iB|<\ell_i$ when
  $v_i\ne v$.
\item The shortest vector $u_{\mathbf c}$ of $\Lambda_{\mathbf c}$ has
  $|u_{\mathbf c}|\le1.0746\sqrt{d_{\mathbf c}}$ (Hermite's constant in dimension two).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}[lattice points in a box]\label{lem:L}
For a lattice $\Lambda\subset\R^2$ of covolume $d$ with shortest vector length $\rho$, and a
convex $K$ of diameter $D$,
$|\#(\Lambda\cap K)-\mathrm{area}(K)/d|\le1+(4/\sqrt3)D/\rho$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take a Lagrange--Gauss reduced basis $b_1,b_2$, so that $d\ge(\sqrt3/2)|b_1||b_2|$. In these
coordinates $K$ becomes a convex $K'$ of area $\mathrm{area}(K)/d$. Its widths in the two
coordinates are at most $(2/\sqrt3)D/\rho$. Count $\Z^2\cap K'$ column by column. Each column
errs by at most $1$, and the column lengths are unimodal, so the column sum errs by at most the
other width.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[one configuration]\label{lem:conf}
Let $\mathbf c$ have order $j\le4$, $d=d_{\mathbf c}$ and $u=u_{\mathbf c}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $u\notin\mathcal L$, then
  $|\#(\Lambda_{\mathbf c}\cap\mathfrak B)-\Xb^2/d|\le1+3.266\Xb/|u|+\sum_{v'}\Xb/(t_{v'}\max(A',B'))$,
  where the sum runs over $v'$ with $r_{v'}(\mathbf c)\ge\lceil j/2\rceil$.
\item Otherwise $u=\pm t_v v$ for one $v\in V$. Put $r=r_v(\mathbf c)$ and $g=d/t_v$; then
  $r\le\lfloor j/2\rfloor$. If $j-r\ge4$, then $\Lambda_{\mathbf c}\cap\mathfrak B=\emptyset$. If
  $j-r\le3$, then
  \[
  |\#(\Lambda_{\mathbf c}\cap\mathfrak B)-\Xb^2/d|\le\frac{2\sqrt2\Xb}{t_v|v|}+\frac{2H\Xb}{g}
  +\sum_{v'\ne v}\frac{\Xb}{t_{v'}\max(A',B')},
  \]
  where again only $v'$ with $r_{v'}(\mathbf c)\ge\lceil j/2\rceil$ occur.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Directions $v'$ not parallel to $u$.} The vector $t_{v'}v'\in\Lambda_{\mathbf c}$ is
  independent of $u$, so $|t_{v'}v'|\ge d/|u|\ge0.93Y^{j/2}$. On the other hand
  $|t_{v'}v'|\le(2Y)^{r_{v'}}\sqrt2H$. If $r_{v'}\le\lceil j/2\rceil-1$, this contradicts
  $Y\ge16H^2$ for $j\le4$. (The tightest case is $j=3$, $r=1$, which would need
  $\sqrt Y\le3.3H$.) The points of $\Lambda_{\mathbf c}\cap\mathfrak B_0$ on $\R v'$ are
  $\sigma t_{v'}v'$ with $1\le\sigma\le\Xb/(t_{v'}\max(A',B'))$.
\item \emph{Case (i).} Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:L} to $\mathfrak B_0$ (diameter $\sqrt2\Xb$), and
  subtract the points on $\mathcal L$ counted in (a).
\item \emph{Case (ii), $r\le\lfloor j/2\rfloor$.} We have $|u|=t_v|v|\ge Y^r$ and
  $|u|\le1.0746(2Y)^{j/2}$. If $r>j/2$, then $Y^{1/2}\le4.3$, a contradiction.
\item \emph{Case (ii), line structure.} For $x\in\Lambda_{\mathbf c}$, $\det(v,x)\in g\Z$.
  Each line $\{\det(v,\cdot)=mg\}$ meets $\Lambda_{\mathbf c}$ in a coset of $\Z u$. On
  $\mathfrak B_0$, $|\det(v,x)|<H\Xb$.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item If $j-r\ge4$, then $g>Y^4>H\Xb$, so only $m=0$ occurs. That line is $\R v\subset\mathcal
    L$, which is removed.
  \item If $j-r\le3$, let $h(\sigma)$ be the length of the chord $\{y\in[0,\Xb]^2:\det(v,y)=\sigma\}$.
    It is concave, $\max h\le\sqrt2\Xb$, and $\int h=|v|\Xb^2$. Summing $h(mg)/|u|\pm1$ over the
    at most $2H\Xb/g$ nonzero $m$, and using $g|u|=d|v|$, gives the bound. Then subtract the
    other lines of $\mathcal L$ as in (a).
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Summation and assembly}
Summing Lemma~\ref{lem:conf} over all configurations of order $j$ gives
\[
  \sum_{\mathbf c}\bigl|\#(\Lambda_{\mathbf c}\cap\mathfrak B)-\Xb^2/d_{\mathbf c}\bigr|\le
  A_j+B_j+C_j+D_j+E_j.
\]
The rows arise as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{$A_j$.} This is the ``$+1$'' term, summed over at most $(|\mathcal P||V|)^j$
  configurations.
\item \emph{$B_j$.} In case (i) a configuration is counted at its own shortest vector $u$, which
  has $|u|\le R_j:=1.0746(2Y)^{j/2}$. Such a $u$ has $|F_v(u)|\le6.1HY^2<Y^3$, so it satisfies at
  most $2|V|$ events and lies in at most $(2|V|)^j$ lattices. Then use
  $\sum_{0<|u|\le R}1/|u|\le8R$.
\item \emph{$C_j$ and $E_j$.} Swap the sums over configurations and directions. For fixed $v'$
  and $r$, $\sum_{\mathbf c:r_{v'}=r}1/t_{v'}\le\binom jr\lambda^r|\mathcal P|^{j-r}$. Also
  $\sum_{v'}1/\max(A',B')<2H$ and $\sum_v2\sqrt2/|v|\le6H$. The $2H\Xb/g$ terms contribute
  $\sum_{\mathbf c:r_v=r}1/g\le\binom jrs^{j-r}(|\mathcal P||V|)^r$.
\item \emph{$D_j$.} Case (ii) with $j-r\ge4$ occurs only for $j=4$, $r=0$, with error
  $\Xb^2/d$ summing to $\Xb^2|V|s^4$.
\end{itemize}

Put $F_j(x):=\sum_{\mathbf c}1[x\in\Lambda_{\mathbf c}]$. By (F1),
$F_j(x)=(\mathcal W(x))_j$, the falling factorial, unless $x$ lies in
$\mathcal D=\{x:\exists\ell\in\mathcal P,\ \ell\mid a,\ \ell\mid b\}$. On $\mathfrak B$, both
quantities lie in $[0,(3|V|)^j]$ by (F2), and $\#\mathcal D\cap\mathfrak B_0\le\Xb^2s/Y$. Hence
\[
  \Bigl|\sum_{\mathfrak B}(\mathcal W)_j-\Xb^2\,\mathbb E(\mathcal Z)_j\Bigr|\le\mathrm{Err}_j.
\]
Here $\mathcal Z=\sum_\ell\mathcal Z_\ell$ with independent
$\mathcal Z_\ell\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(|V|/\ell)$, so that
$\mathbb E(\mathcal Z)_j=\sum_{\mathbf c}1/d_{\mathbf c}$.

Now expand
\[
(w-\lambda)^4=(w)_4+(6-4\lambda)(w)_3+(7-12\lambda+6\lambda^2)(w)_2+(1-4\lambda+6\lambda^2-4\lambda^3)(w)_1+\lambda^4 .
\]
For $\lambda\ge1$ the coefficients are bounded by the $\kappa_j$. The same identity holds for
$\mathcal Z$, and $\mathbb E(\mathcal Z-\lambda)^4\le\lambda+3\lambda^2$. Finally
$\#\mathfrak B\le\Xb^2$, and each uncovered point contributes $\lambda^4$. So
\[
  \lambda^4\#\{\mathcal W=0\}\le\Xb^2(3\lambda^2+\lambda)+2H\Xb\lambda^4+\sum_j\kappa_j\mathrm{Err}_j,
\]
which is Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth}. Every displayed inequality was also checked exhaustively on
small boxes by code (\texttt{tests/test\_sieve2d\_bound.py}). \qed

\subsection{Consequences}
The explicit constants in Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth} are poor. At the scale of
Theorem~\ref{thm:Eb4} the error terms (dominated by $B_4$ and $F_4$) fall below the main term
only for $n$ of order $200$--$300$. The bound is an asymptotic improvement over
Lemma~\ref{lem:sieve}, not a statement about moderate sizes.

\begin{theorem}[coverage exceptional set; proved]\label{thm:Eb4}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:onerun}, and with $C$ absolute,
\[
  \frac{|E_b(H)|}{|\Sn|}\le C\Bigl(\frac{n^4}{H^4}+n^6H^{1/3}N^{-1/6}\Bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$E_b$ is contained in the union over the blocks $k$ of the pairs uncovered by block $k$. Prime
pairs of $\Sn$ lie in $\mathfrak B$ with $\Xb=\lceil\sqrt2P\rceil$. The hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth} hold for each block with $Y_k=2^kY$, by \eqref{eq:scales}. We have
$\lambda_k\ge c_1H^2/n$. If $\lambda_k<1$ then $H\ll\sqrt n$ and the claimed bound exceeds $1$,
so it is trivial; assume $\lambda_k\ge1$. With $|V|\asymp H^2$, $s\asymp1/n$, $Y^3H\asymp P$ and
$H^2\ll Y$, the whole weighted error sum of Theorem~\ref{thm:fourth} satisfies
\[
  \frac{\lambda_k^{-4}\sum_j\kappa_j\mathrm{Err}_j}{\Xb^2}\ll H^{-6}+\frac{n^4H^{-2/3}}{P^{1/3}}
  +\frac{n^3H^{1/3}}{P^{1/3}}.
\]
The three terms come from rows $E$ (first part, $j=4$), $B$ and $F$; every other row is absorbed
by these using the same relations (\texttt{src/sieve2d/bound.py} evaluates each row). So the
uncovered integer points of block $k$ number at most
$\Xb^2[3\lambda_k^{-2}+\lambda_k^{-3}+O(H^{-6}+n^4H^{1/3}P^{-1/3})]$. Multiplying by
$n^2/(c_0P^2)$, the population loss from integer to prime pairs, gives the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:D}]
\begin{itemize}
\item Correctness and cost come from Proposition~\ref{prop:onerun}(i)--(ii).
\item \emph{(a).} Take $H=\lceil n2^{\varepsilon n/4}\rceil$ and
  $T=\lfloor2^{(1/4-\varepsilon/2)n}/n\rfloor$. Then $|E_a|/|\Sn|=O(nT^2/P)=O(2^{-\varepsilon n}/n)$.
  Theorem~\ref{thm:Eb4} gives $|E_b|/|\Sn|=O(2^{-\varepsilon n})$. The first $E_c$ term has
  exponent $-1/12+5\varepsilon/3$, which is at most $-\varepsilon-1/60$ for $\varepsilon\le1/40$.
  The second has exponent $-1/6+5\varepsilon/6$. The cost is $n^{O(1)}H^{5/3}2^{n/6}$.
\item \emph{(b).} Take $H=\lceil n^{1+a}\rceil$ and $T=\lfloor2^{n/5}\rfloor$. Then
  $E_a=O(n2^{-n/10})$ and $E_c=n^{O(1)}2^{-n/30}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:sieve} and
  Theorem~\ref{thm:Eb4}, $E_b=O(\min(n^{-1-2a},n^{-4a}))$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Where this method stops}
The following remark explains why the method stops at $\lambda^{-2}$.

\begin{remark}[a moment barrier; proved for its stated scope]\label{rem:PYY}
Fix $j_0\ge2$, and let $\pi_\ell\in(0,1/2]$ with $\max\pi_\ell\le2\min\pi_\ell$ and
$\sum\pi_\ell=\lambda\ge\lambda_{j_0}$. Peled, Yadin and Yehudayoff \cite[Cor.~1.2(1),
1.3(1)]{pyy2011} construct $j_0$-wise independent bits with a \emph{common} marginal $\pi^*=\max
\pi_\ell$ that are all $0$ with probability $\ge c_{j_0}\lambda^{-\lfloor j_0/2\rfloor}$ (for odd $j_0$
via their reduction (1.1)). Independent thinning by $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\pi_\ell/\pi^*)$ gives the
marginals $\pi_\ell$ and keeps $j_0$-wise independence and the bound. Attach to each event a uniform label in $V$. Then all labelled
event statistics of order at most $j_0$ agree exactly with the independent model, while the
uncovered mass is $\ge c_{j_0}\lambda^{-\lfloor j_0/2\rfloor}$. So no argument whose only input is the
joint distribution of at most $j_0$ band-prime events can prove $o(\lambda^{-\lfloor j_0/2\rfloor})$.

Our error analysis controls $j_0\le4$; $j_0=5$ is sketched in the notes, and by the above it cannot
improve on $\lambda^{-2}$. At $j_0=6$ the per-lattice error bounds add up to about $\Xb^2/H^2$,
far above the target $\Xb^2\lambda^{-3}$. This is a limit of this proof technique, not of the
truth. Exploratory sixth moments agree with the independent model to within $4\%$
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). A Poisson-quality bound $e^{-c\lambda}$ would need genuinely
new arithmetic input. Any such proof must also exclude the integer dilates $t(H,H+1)$ with
$\gcd(t,\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P}\ell)=1$, which are uncovered integer points. The barrier
concerns limited-information arguments only; it is not an arithmetic impossibility theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{A complete algorithm with average cost \texorpdfstring{$N^{1/6}$}{N\^{}(1/6)}}\label{sec:AV}
%======================================================================

\subsection{The small-order detector}

\begin{lemma}[detector; elementary]\label{lem:detector}
Let $N=pq$ be odd, $T\ge1$, $m=\lceil\sqrt T\rceil$, $J'=\lceil T/m\rceil$, and $d_r=\ord_r(2)$.
Form $f(X)=\prod_{0\le i<m}(X-2^i)$ over $\Z/N\Z$, and compute $G_j=\gcd(f(2^{jm}),N)$ for
$1\le j\le J'$ by a product tree and a remainder tree. At the first $j$ with $G_j>1$, compute
$\gcd(2^k-1,N)$ for every $k\in((j-1)m,jm]$, and output the first proper value; if there is none, the detector reports
\texttt{FAIL}. If $\min(d_p,d_q)\le T$ and $d_p\ne d_q$, this outputs the prime with the smaller order. The cost
is $\tilde O(\sqrt T\,n)$ bit operations.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $2^{jm}-2^i=2^i(2^{jm-i}-1)$, $G_j>1$ iff the block $((j-1)m,jm]$ contains a positive
multiple of $d_p$ or $d_q$. The first such block contains $d_{\min}$. At $k=d_{\min}$, the gcd is
divisible by the prime of order $d_{\min}$ and not by the other, whose order does not divide
$d_{\min}$. For smaller $k$ in the block the gcd is $1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[coincidences]\label{lem:coinc}
Let $C(T)=\{(p,q)\in\Sn:\ord_p2=\ord_q2\le T\}$. Then $|C(T)|\le C(T^3/P^2)|\Sn|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each $k\le T$, $2^k-1$ has fewer than $2k/(n-2)$ prime factors exceeding $P/2$, so at most
$(2k/(n-2))^2$ pairs have both orders equal to $k$. Summing over $k$ gives at most
$4T^3/(n-2)^2$ pairs.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The algorithm \texorpdfstring{$\LAD$}{LAD}}
All parameters are $n$-only:
\begin{itemize}
\item $T=2^{\lfloor3n/10\rfloor}$;
\item $H_1=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$ and $H_{i+1}=\min(\lceil H_i^{6/5}\rceil,H_{\max})$, with
  $H_{\max}=n^2\,2^{\lceil n/60\rceil}$, stopping at $H_k=H_{\max}$.
\end{itemize}
Then $k=O(\log n)$ and $H_{i+1}^{5/3}\le2^{5/3}H_i^2$. The constant $n_0$ is chosen so that
\eqref{eq:Hrange} holds for every rung.

On input $N$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,start=0]
\item If $n<n_0$, run Harvey--Hittmeir and stop. If $N<4$ the output is trivial. If $N$ is
  even, output $2$ together with Harvey--Hittmeir's factorisation of $N/2$.
\item Run the detector (Lemma~\ref{lem:detector}) with $T$.
\item For $i=1,\dots,k$, run $\DCL_{n,H_i}$.
\item If no step produced a divisor, run Harvey--Hittmeir \cite{hh2022}, which outputs the
  complete factorisation.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Finish.} As soon as a step outputs a proper divisor $d$, test $d$ and $N/d$ for primality
with AKS \cite{aks2004}. If both are prime, output $\{d,N/d\}$; otherwise run Harvey--Hittmeir on
$N$. On $\Sn$ the finish never calls Harvey--Hittmeir.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:AV}}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{(i) Completeness.} Every step outputs only validated divisors, and otherwise
  Harvey--Hittmeir factors $N$ completely.
\item \emph{(ii) Worst case.} Each rung costs at most $Cn^cH_i^{5/3}2^{n/6}\le
  Cn^{c+10/3}2^{7n/36}$, and there are $O(\log n)$ rungs. The detector costs
  $\tilde O(2^{3n/20})$, AKS is polylogarithmic, and Harvey--Hittmeir runs at most once.
\item \emph{(iii) Average: reach sets.} Let $R_i$ be the pairs of $\Sn$ that reach rung $i$, and
  $R_{\HH}$ those that reach the fallback.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item By Lemma~\ref{lem:detector}, a pair passes the detector only if $d_q>T$ or it lies in
    $C(T)$.
  \item By Proposition~\ref{prop:onerun}(iii), a pair with $d_q>T$ that fails rung $i-1$ lies in
    $E_b(H_{i-1})\cup E_c(H_{i-1},T)$.
  \item Hence $R_i\subseteq C(T)\cup E_c(H_{i-1},T)\cup E_b(H_{i-1})$ for $i\ge2$, and similarly
    for $R_{\HH}$ with $H_k$. Let $W\le Cn^{16/5}2^{n/5}$ be the worst-case total cost.
  \end{itemize}
\item \emph{(iii) Average: the five contributions.}
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item The detector and rung~1, paid by every pair, cost $O(n^{c+5/6}2^{n/6})$.
  \item Rung $i\ge2$ on $E_b(H_{i-1})$ contributes
    $Cn^cH_i^{5/3}2^{n/6}\cdot\min(1,Cn/H_{i-1}^2)\le C'n^{c+1}2^{n/6}$. Over the $O(\log n)$
    rungs this totals $O(n^{c+1}\log n\,2^{n/6})$.
  \item Harvey--Hittmeir on $E_b(H_k)$ contributes $Cn^{16/5}2^{n/5}\cdot n/H_{\max}^2=O(n^{1/5}2^{n/6})$.
  \item Pairs in $C(T)\cup\bigcup_iE_c(H_i,T)$ pay at most $W$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:coinc},
    $|C(T)|/|\Sn|=O(2^{-n/10})$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:onerun}(iii) with $H\le H_{\max}$,
    the $E_c$ terms have exponents $7/90+1/6-3/10=-1/18$ and $1/18-1/6=-1/9$. So these pairs
    contribute at most $Wn^{O(1)}2^{-n/18}=n^{O(1)}2^{13n/90}=o(2^{n/6})$.
  \item The primality tests are polylogarithmic.
  \end{enumerate}
\item \emph{(iv)} is immediate, since the cost is a nonnegative function of $N$.
\end{itemize}
\qed

\begin{remark}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item The ratio $6/5$ is not special. What matters is that the proved tail exponent $2$ in $H$
  exceeds the cost exponent $5/3$. With a tail $(n/H^2)^j$, any $j>5/6$ suffices after adjusting
  the schedule. A better tail, such as Theorem~\ref{thm:Eb4}, therefore changes only
  polylogarithmic factors of the average. (Within these bounds the first rung that fails on
  fewer than half the pairs already needs $H\gtrsim\sqrt n$, so a factor $n^{c+5/6}$ remains.)
\item The detector is needed within the proved bounds. Without it the $E_c$ count is limited by
  primes with $\ord_q(2)\approx\sqrt P$, and the best ladder exponent we can certify is
  $17/100$ (\texttt{notes/avgtime\_results.md}, \S5.2).
\item The finish's primality tests and the Harvey--Hittmeir fallback are standard. Only rungs,
  detector and fallback contribute to the exponent.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{The worst case reduces to coverage}\label{sec:worst}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Setting}
\begin{itemize}
\item A \emph{weight} is a primitive pair $(A,B)$ with $1\le|A|,B\le H$. \emph{Signed} weights
  allow $A<0$, and the diagonal $(1,1)$ is allowed.
\item A \emph{block} is a set of primes $\ell\equiv3\pmod4$ with $H<\ell<\sqrt{N/2}$.
\item The pair $(p,q)$ is \emph{covered} by (block, weights) if $\ell\mid Bp-Aq$ for some weight
  and some prime $\ell$ of the block.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The algorithm \texorpdfstring{$\DCLwc$}{DCL-wc}}
The algorithm sees only $N$ and the public parameters $H$, $V$, the blocks $\mathcal P_1,\dots,
\mathcal P_K$, $T$ and $B_0$. For block $k$ let $\ell_k^-$ be its least prime and
$\bar m_k=\lfloor 3HP/(\ell_k^-)^2\rfloor+2$; every window below has $W_\tau\le\bar m_k$,
because $\mathrm{Hi}-\mathrm{Lo}\le(B+\sqrt2|A|)\sqrt N+O(H)\le3HP$. The baby length $m_k\le\bar
m_k$ is chosen by the endpoint-competitive rule of Section~\ref{sec:alg}, and a block with no
targets is skipped.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,start=0]
\item If $N<4$, output \texttt{FAIL}. If $N$ is even, output $2$. If $N=s^2$, output $s$.
\item \emph{Base selection.} For $b=2,3,\dots,B_0$:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item check $\gcd(b,N)$;
  \item by baby-step giant-step polynomial evaluation, find the least $r\le T_{\mathrm{scan}}=
    T+O(\sqrt T)$ with $\gcd(b^r-1,N)>1$;
  \item if that gcd is proper, output it; if no such $r$ exists, \emph{select} $b$ and go to
    step~2; otherwise record $r_b$.
  \end{itemize}
  If every $b\le B_0$ was recorded, put $\Lambda=\mathrm{lcm}_b\,r_b$ and test
  $\gcd(1+j\Lambda,N)$ for $1\le j\le(\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor-1)/\Lambda$.
\item For each block, with $L=\prod\ell^2$, $u_\ell=b^{L/\ell^2}$ (by the exponent tree) and
  $\gamma=b^L$:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item build the targets of Section~\ref{sec:alg} for all weights in $V$. For $A<0$ use
    $\mathrm{Lo}=Ba_0+\lfloor AN/a_0\rfloor$ and $\mathrm{Hi}=Bb_0+\lceil AN/b_0\rceil$, since
    $x\mapsto Bx+AN/x$ is then increasing. Choose $m$ as in step~5 there.
  \item \emph{$\gamma$ pre-step.} Find the least $r\in[1,m)$ with $\gcd(\gamma^r-1,N)>1$, by a
    product tree and binary descent. If the gcd is proper, output it. If it equals $N$, run the
    \emph{equal-order split}: $E=Lr$ is a multiple of $\ord_N(b)$ with known factorisation. For
    each prime power $\varrho^e\Vert E$ compute $y=b^{E/\varrho^e}$ and $\gcd(y^{\varrho^i}-1,N)$
    for $0\le i\le e$, and output any proper value. Otherwise the common order $D$ is now known;
    test $\gcd(1+jD,N)$ for $1\le j\le(\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor-1)/D$.
  \item \emph{Giants.} Evaluate $f=\prod_{i<m}(X-\gamma^i)$ at all giants as in
    Section~\ref{sec:alg} (for $A<0$ this uses $w_\ell^{-1}$, one inversion per prime). Output any proper gcd, and collect the giants with
    $\gcd(f(G),N)=N$.
  \item \emph{Exploration.} For each such giant, descend the subproduct tree of $f$. At a node
    whose value has gcd $N$, evaluate both children. Output any proper gcd, and descend into the
    children whose gcd is $N$.
  \item \emph{Exact leaf.} If leaf $i$ is reached with $G\equiv\gamma^i\pmod N$, put
    $S=c+\ell^2(g_{\mathrm{lo}}+jm+i)$. If $S^2-4ABN=D_0^2$ is a perfect square, output a proper
    $\gcd((S\pm D_0)/2,N)$.
  \end{itemize}
\item Output \texttt{FAIL}.
\end{enumerate}
All outputs are validated.

\subsection{Theorem R}
\begin{theorem}[precise form of Theorem~\ref{thm:R}; proved]\label{thm:Rprecise}
Let $N=pq\in\Sn$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\DCLwc$ outputs a proper divisor of $N$ or \texttt{FAIL};
\item if $(p,q)$ is covered by $(\mathcal P_k,V)$ for some $k$, it outputs a proper divisor;
\item its cost is
\[
  \tilde O\Bigl(\sum_k\bigl(m_k+T'_k+|V||\mathcal P_k|+\textstyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal P_k}\log\ell\bigr)+B_0T^{1/2}+\sqrt N/T
  +\sqrt N/\Psi(\sqrt{N/2}-1,B_0)\Bigr)
\]
ring operations modulo $N$, plus $\tilde O(|V||\mathcal P_k|)$ word operations. Here $\Psi$
counts smooth numbers. With $T=\lceil N^{1/3}\rceil$ and $B_0=\lceil n^4\rceil$, the last three
terms are $N^{1/6}n^{O(1)}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[one-sided order dichotomy; after {\cite{hh2026}}]\label{lem:O}
For $b$ coprime to $N$, write $o_p=\ord_p(b)$ and $o_q=\ord_q(b)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item If $\min(o_p,o_q)\le T_{\mathrm{scan}}$, the scan returns $r_b=\min(o_p,o_q)$, and the gcd
  there is proper iff $o_p\ne o_q$.
\item If every $b\le B_0$ is recorded, the $\Lambda$-enumeration finds $p$ at cost at most
  $\sqrt N/\Psi(p-1,B_0)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item The first $r$ with $\gcd(b^r-1,N)>1$ is $\min(o_p,o_q)$, and at $r=o_q<o_p$ the gcd is $q$.
\item If $o_p=o_q=r_b$ for all $b\le B_0$, the subgroup $\langle2,\dots,B_0\rangle\le\mathbb
  F_p^\times$ is cyclic of order $\Lambda$ dividing $p-1$. It contains every $B_0$-smooth integer
  below $p$, so $\Lambda\ge\Psi(p-1,B_0)$. Then $p=1+j\Lambda$ with $j\le\sqrt N/\Lambda$.
  For $B_0\ge(\ln p)^4$, the elementary bound $\Psi(x,(\log x)^4)\ge x^{3/4}/(\log x)^3$ gives
  $\sqrt N/\Lambda\le P^{1/4}n^3$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$\gamma$ pre-step]\label{lem:G}
One of the following holds:
\begin{itemize}
\item a proper divisor is output;
\item $\ord_p(\gamma)=\ord_q(\gamma)<m$, and the equal-order split outputs a divisor, either
  directly or through the $D$-enumeration at cost at most $\sqrt N/T$;
\item $\ord_p(\gamma)\ge m$ and $\ord_q(\gamma)\ge m$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $\gamma^o\equiv1\pmod N$. Then $o_p,o_q\mid E=Lo$; the factorisation of $E$ is known
from the band primes and from trial division of $o<m$, at cost $\tilde O(\sqrt m)$. For each prime $\varrho\mid E$,
$y=b^{E/\varrho^e}$ has order $\varrho^{v_\varrho(o_p)}$ modulo $p$ and $\varrho^{v_\varrho(o_q)}$
modulo $q$. If these valuations differ for some $\varrho$, the corresponding gcd is $p$ or $q$.
Otherwise $o_p=o_q=D$, and $D>T$ because $b$ was selected; since $D\mid p-1$, the enumeration
finds $p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[exploration]\label{lem:X}
Assume $\ord_p(\gamma),\ord_q(\gamma)\ge m$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Each giant has at most one $i<m$ with $G\equiv\gamma^i\pmod p$. So at most one node per
  tree level has gcd $N$, and the exploration of a giant costs $O(\log m)$ node evaluations.
\item If the true giant $G^*$ has $\gcd(f(G^*),N)=N$, its exploration outputs a proper divisor.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) The $\gamma^i$, $i<m$, are distinct modulo $p$.

(b) Every node on the path to leaf $i^*$ has value divisible by $p$, so its gcd is $p$ (which is
output) or $N$. If leaf $i^*$ is reached with $G^*\equiv\gamma^{i^*}\pmod N$, then
$S=c+\ell^2g=Bp+Aq$. Hence $S^2-4ABN=(Bp-Aq)^2$ and $\{(S\pm D_0)/2\}=\{Bp,Aq\}$. Finally
$\gcd(Bp,N)=p$ because $1\le B<q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:Rprecise}]
(i) holds because all outputs are validated.

(ii) Steps 0--1 output a divisor or select $b$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:O}). In a covering block,
Lemma~\ref{lem:G} either outputs a divisor or gives orders $\ge m$. The true target exists and its
true giant satisfies $G^*\equiv\gamma^{i^*}\pmod p$, by Lemmas~\ref{lem:coverage}--\ref{lem:modp}
(the sign of $A$ plays no role). Then either $\gcd(f(G^*),N)=p$, or Lemma~\ref{lem:X}(b) applies.
The fallback enumerations in earlier blocks are complete, so they cannot abort.

(iii) We account for each step in turn.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Base selection.} Each base costs $\tilde O(\sqrt T)$ ring operations by baby-step
  giant-step polynomial evaluation. The $\Lambda$-enumeration runs at most once, at the cost of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:O}(b).
\item \emph{Exponent trees.} Per block, $u_\ell=b^{L/\ell^2}$ for all $\ell$ comes from the
  all-but-one exponent tree of \S\ref{sec:alg}, at $\tilde O(\sum_\ell\log\ell)$ ring
  operations.
\item \emph{$\gamma$ pre-step.} This is a product tree of the $\gamma^r-1$ and a binary descent,
  $\tilde O(m)$.
\item \emph{Equal-order split.} The powers $b^{E/\varrho^e}$ for all prime powers
  $\varrho^e\Vert E$ come from one all-but-one exponent tree over these prime powers. The
  valuations $v_\varrho$ are read off as the least $i$ with $y^{\varrho^i}\equiv1\pmod N$, and
  $D=\prod\varrho^{v_\varrho}$. The $D$-enumeration runs at most once, because its branch ends
  with a factor.
\item \emph{Giants and remainder tree.} These cost $\tilde O(m+T')$, as in \S\ref{sec:cost}.
\item \emph{Exploration.} This is batched depth by depth. At each depth the node polynomials
  have total degree at most $m$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:X}(a) at most two nodes per explored giant
  are evaluated, so at most $2T'$ evaluation points are assigned. Multipoint evaluation of each
  node at its assigned points therefore costs $\tilde O(m+T')$ per depth. There are
  $\lceil\log_2m\rceil$ depths.
\item \emph{Target construction.} The term $|V||\mathcal P_k|$ charges the rejected (weight,
  prime) tests, at $\tilde O(1)$ word operations each.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

The reference implementation (\texttt{src/dchworst/wcalg.py}) is a correctness prototype. Two
steps there are unbatched (the $\gamma$ pre-step scan and the exploration), so it does not meet
the cost bound (iii). Its outputs are unaffected.

\subsection{Consequences}

\begin{hypothesis}[$\mathrm{DCH}_{\mathrm{pair}}(\kappa)$]\label{hyp:DCHpair}
For all large $n$ and every $(p,q)\in\Sn$, some block $\Pk$ ($k=0,\dots,3$) of the design of
$\DCL_{n,H}$ with $H=\lceil n^\kappa\rceil$ contains a prime $\ell$ with $\ell\mid Bp-Aq$, for some
primitive $(A,B)$ with $1\le|A|,B\le H$. The diagonal $(1,1)$ is allowed.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:conditional}]
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{(a).} Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:Rprecise} with the design of $\DCL_{n,H}$ and signed
  weights. The list cost is $n^{O(1)}H^{5/3}2^{n/6}=N^{1/6+o(1)}$.
\item \emph{(b).} Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:Rprecise} with one block, the $J$ primes, and all
  signed weights of height $H$. With $J=Y^{j}$ and $H=Y^{1/2}J^{-c}$ the list cost is
  $HP/Y^2+JH^2$, which is balanced at $Y=N^{1/(5+2j(1-c))}$ with exponent
  $E(j,c)=(1+j(1-2c))/(5+2j(1-c))$. Since $\partial_jE$ has the sign of $3-8c$, for $c>3/8$ the
  best choice is $j=1$, which gives $Y=N^{1/(7-2c)}$ and $(1-c)/(7/2-c)$. The extraction overhead
  of Theorem~\ref{thm:Rprecise} is $N^{1/6+o(1)}$, which does not exceed this.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Minkowski.} With one prime $\ell\asymp P^{2/5}$, signed weights of height
  $\sqrt{2\ell}$ and $(1,1)$, pigeonhole covers every pair. So $\DCLwc$ recovers Harvey's
  unconditional worst-case exponent $1/5$. This is the case $c=0$, and it is not new.
\item \emph{Theorem~D$'$ sharpened.} With the design of $\DCL_{n,H}$, $\DCLwc$ fails only on
  pairs uncovered by every block. That set has fraction $O(n/H^2)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:sieve}
  applied to block $0$. The sets $E_a$ and $E_c$ disappear.
\item \emph{Squares.} An earlier draft stated a divisor-cover conjecture for every integer
  candidate $p\in[\sqrt{N/2},\sqrt N]$ of every $N$ coprime to $6$, with $A\ne B$. That form is
  false. For $N=t^2$ with $t$ prime and $p=t$, the quantity $Bp^2-AN=(B-A)t^2$ has no band
  divisor, since $0<|B-A|<H<\ell<t$. Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:DCHpair} quantifies over prime pairs
  only, and that is all Theorem~\ref{thm:Rprecise} needs.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[$\mathrm{DCH}_{\mathrm{pair}}$ needs $\kappa\ge1/2$; proved]\label{prop:N}
Let $\mathcal L$ be any set of primes in $(Y,16Y]$ with $16Y\le P^\theta$, $\theta<1/2$, and let
$V$ be any weight set with $|V||\mathcal L|=o(P)$. Put $\lambda=|V|\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal
L}1/(\ell-1)$. Then at most $(2/(1-\theta)+o(1))\lambda|\Sn|+o(|\Sn|)$ pairs are covered. For
the four blocks of $\DCL_{n,H}$, a positive proportion of $\Sn$ is uncovered by every block when
$H\le0.2\sqrt n$ (positive weights) or $H\le0.14\sqrt n$ (signed weights).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a prime $p$ let $I_p$ be the interval of $q$ with $(p,q)\in\Sn$. Fix a condition
$(A,B,\ell)$. The partners $q\in I_p$ with $\ell\mid Bp-Aq$ are the primes of one class modulo
$\ell$. Let $M_0=|V||\mathcal L|$ be the number of conditions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Interior $p$.} If $|I_p|\ge P/n^2$, the Brun--Titchmarsh inequality in the
  Montgomery--Vaughan form \cite{mv1973} bounds the partners by
  $2|I_p|/(\varphi(\ell)\log(|I_p|/\ell))$. Here $\log(|I_p|/\ell)\ge(1-\theta-o(1))\log P$.
\item \emph{Endpoint $p$.} The $p$ with $|I_p|<P/n^2$ lie within $O(P/n^2)$ of the ends of the
  $p$-range. For one condition they contribute $O\bigl((P/n^2)(P/(n^2\ell)+1)\bigr)$ by the
  trivial count. Summed over all conditions this is $O(\lambda P^2/n^4+M_0P/n^2)=o(\lambda|\Sn|)
  +o(|\Sn|)$.
\item \emph{Summing.} Sum over interior $p$ and over the conditions. The prime number theorem
  with de la Vall\'ee Poussin's error term gives
  $\pi(x+y)-\pi(x)=(1+o(1))y/\log x$ uniformly for $y\ge x/(\log x)^{3}$. So
  $|\Sn|=(1+o(1))\sum_p|I_p|/\log P$, summed over interior primes $p$, and the interior
  contribution is at most $(2/(1-\theta)+o(1))\lambda|\Sn|$.
\item \emph{The constants.} For the four blocks, $\lambda\approx(6/\pi^2)H^2(\ln16/2)/\ln
  Y\approx7.3H^2/n$ for positive weights, and twice that for signed weights. With $\theta\approx
  1/3$, so that the constant is $3$, the covered fraction is below $1$ when $H\le0.2\sqrt n$,
  respectively $H\le0.14\sqrt n$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[barriers to proving coverage]
\begin{itemize}
\item We have no design that provably covers every pair with any $c>0$.
\item Counting arguments with error budget $\gtrsim\sqrt J$ give only $c\le1/4$; this is a
  calculation, and it is heuristic as a barrier.
\item Single-modulus pigeonhole gives $c=0$.
\item A proof that also covers coprime non-prime pairs would cover, for $x\asymp Y^{25/8}$, a
  structured set of about $Y^{1/4}$ integers $bx-a$ by band primes. We know of no method for sets
  this sparse.
\item Exhaustive worst cases over all of $\mathcal S_{32}$ and $\mathcal S_{36}$ follow the
  Poisson extreme (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\section{A true-factor floor for local designs}\label{sec:lower}
%======================================================================

This section is a theorem inside a cost model. It explains why $N^{1/6}$ is the natural limit of
the method. It is not a lower bound for factoring.

\subsection{The model}
\paragraph{Cost.} For an input $N$, a design issues \emph{targets} (labels) $\tau$, each with a
finite window $S_\tau\subset\Z$ of exponent values. A shared baby list, with any sumset
structure, tests them. The design has cost $X$ if $\#\{\tau\}\le X$ and $\sum_\tau|S_\tau|\le
X^2$, either per input or in expectation over uniform $n$-bit $N$. A baby-step/giant-step search
with list length $m\le X$ and at most $X$ giants satisfies this, since every window costs
$\lceil|S_\tau|/m\rceil\ge1$ giants.

The model charges labels, not stored group elements: it assumes that one giant certifies at most
one label. For the targets of this paper two labels share a giant only in negligible cases, but
we have no general bound.

\paragraph{Generators.} A \emph{residue-local degree-one generator} $\iota$ consists of the
following data:
\begin{itemize}
\item fixed integers $A,B$, not both $0$;
\item integers $M\ge1$, $K$ with $M\mid K$, and $D\ge1$;
\item a side, $p$ or $q$;
\item arbitrary maps $\kappa\mapsto R(\kappa)\subseteq\Z/M$ and $\kappa\mapsto S(\kappa)\subset\Z$
  (finite sets), defined on keys $\kappa\in\Z/K$.
\end{itemize}
Write $x$ for the class variable ($x=p$ on side $p$) and $y$ for the partner. On input $N$, with
$\kappa=N\bmod K$, the generator issues $|R(\kappa)|$ targets. They cover $(p,q)$ iff
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gen}
  x\bmod M\in R(\kappa)\quad\text{and}\quad\lfloor(Bx+Ay)/D\rfloor\in S(\kappa).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Locality hypothesis (L1).}
\begin{itemize}
\item $D/|A|\le C_1M^2$ if $A\ne0$, and $D/|B|\le C_1M^2$ if $A=0$;
\item $K\le C_2M^2$;
\item $M,K\le P/2$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why this shape is general for degree one.}
In canonical normalisation, every identity-valid degree-one target has exponent $(Bx+Ay-c)/D$,
with $D=C_dM^2$ and $c\in[0,D)$ fixed by the class and by $N\bmod D$ (the denominator lemma and
its Lemma~K$'$ in the multimod notes, \S3.1(i)). For true pairs this exponent equals
$\lfloor(Bx+Ay)/D\rfloor$, so any window placement is some finite set $S$. Moving a global offset
into the window is harmless only when the offset is itself determined by the key; this is part of
hypothesis (L1), not automatic. The count below never uses validity: it holds for every rule of
shape \eqref{eq:gen}. The generator family and its maps are fixed for each $n$; only the key
varies with the input.

\subsection{The bound}

\begin{lemma}[fixed-key partner count]\label{lem:partner}
Fix $x$ with $\gcd(x,K)=1$ and a unit key $\kappa$, with $A\ne0$. The partners $y\equiv\kappa x^{-1}\pmod K$ of $x$
satisfying \eqref{eq:gen} number at most
$\min\bigl(|S(\kappa)|(\lfloor D/(|A|K)\rfloor+1),\ \lfloor\ell_y/K\rfloor+1\bigr)$, where
$\ell_y\le x$ is the length of the partner range.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The partners form an arithmetic progression of step $K$. Each value $s\in S$ confines $y$ to an
interval of length $D/|A|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[T1; precise form of Theorem~\ref{thm:T1}; proved]\label{thm:T1precise}
Assume (L1), and let a design be a union of generators. Suppose
$\mathbb E_N\#\{\tau\}\le X$ and $\mathbb E_N\sum_\tau|S_\tau|\le X^2$, where $\mathbb E_N$
averages over uniform $n$-bit $N$. Then
\[
  \Pr_{(p,q)\in\Sn}[\text{the true pair is covered}]\le C\,n^2X^{3/2}N^{-1/4},
\]
with $C$ depending only on $C_1,C_2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \emph{Sum by keys.} Both primes exceed $K$, so they are units modulo $K$, and each $x$ has
  one partner class $\kappa x^{-1}$ per unit key $\kappa$. Let $\varphi(\kappa)$ denote the bound
  of Lemma~\ref{lem:partner}. Then
  \[
  \mathrm{Count}(\iota)\le\sum_\kappa\varphi(\kappa)\,\#\{x:x\bmod M\in R(\kappa)\},
  \]
  and the last factor is at most $c_s|R(\kappa)|P/M$, with $c_s=1$ on side $p$ and $5/4$ on side
  $q$.
\item \emph{Bound $\varphi$.} Using $\min(u,v)\le\sqrt{uv}$ and $K\le P/2$,
  $\varphi(\kappa)\le\sqrt{2P|S(\kappa)|(D/|A|+K)}/K$.
\item \emph{Pass to $\mathbb E_N$.} Every residue class modulo $K$ contains at least
  $2^{n-2}/K$ integers of $[2^{n-1},2^n)$. So $\sum_\kappa f(\kappa)\le2K\,\mathbb E_Nf(N\bmod
  K)$ for $f\ge0$. With (L1), $\sqrt{D/|A|+K}\le M\sqrt{C_1+C_2}$, which gives
  \[
  \mathrm{Count}(\iota)\le c_1P^{3/2}\,\mathbb E_N\bigl[|R(N\bmod K)|\,|S(N\bmod K)|^{1/2}\bigr].
  \]
\item \emph{$A=0$.} Swap sides: put $R'(\kappa)=\{\kappa r^{-1}\bmod M:r\in
  R(\kappa)\cap(\Z/M)^\times\}$ and $(A',B')=(B,0)$. Discarding nonunit labels loses no true
  pairs, since $p,q>M$. The coverage of true pairs is identical, $|R'|\le|R|$, and $A'\ne0$.
\item \emph{Sum over the design and apply the budget.} Summing over generators gives
  $\mathbb E_N\sum_\tau|S_\tau|^{1/2}$, which is at most
  $\sqrt{\mathbb E\#\tau\cdot\mathbb E\sum|S_\tau|}\le X^{3/2}$ by Cauchy--Schwarz. Finally
  divide by $|\Sn|\ge c_0P^2/n^2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

The proof counts integer pairs and needs no flatness, height or prime equidistribution.
Arbitrary finite windows are allowed: a sparse window counts by its cardinality, not its hull.
This is what also covers searches over several rational bases with known multiplicative
relations, after a reduction to prime-valuation coordinates (\texttt{notes/below16\_results.md},
Prop.~MB).

\begin{proposition}[the search lies in the model; proved]\label{prop:Dinmodel}
$\DCL_{n,H}$ run with the baby-length rule $m=\mbar$ is dominated, at constant-factor cost, by a
design satisfying (L1) with $M=K=\ell$ and $D=\ell^2$. (This rule suffices for
Theorem~\ref{thm:D}.) By Theorem~\ref{thm:T1precise}, any design in the model that succeeds on a
constant fraction of $\Sn$ costs at least $N^{1/6}/(\log N)^{O(1)}$. So
Theorem~\ref{thm:D}(b) is within a polylogarithmic factor of the best such design.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The targets are $(A,B,\ell,\text{root})$. The class set is the set of roots of $Br^2\equiv AN$,
whose key is $N\bmod\ell$. Replace each window by the $n$-only superset
$[\lfloor F_{\min}/\ell^2\rfloor-1,\lfloor F_{\max}/\ell^2\rfloor+1]$ over all $n$-bit $N$. Its
span is at most $2.12HP<\spanbar$, so each width is at most $\mbar+1$. Then
$\sum|S|\le\sum_kT_k(\mbar+1)\le(\sum_k(\mbar+T_k))^2$. With $m=\mbar$ the list cost is
$\sum_k(\mbar+T'_k)\ge\sum_k(\mbar+T_k)$, so the design has budget $X=O(\text{list cost})$.
The coverage event of the design contains the success certificate of
Proposition~\ref{prop:onerun}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what the model does not cover]\label{rem:T1scope}
The model leaves out several things.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Partial variants.} A second theorem T2 allows about $n/2$ bits of size information
  about $N$ with interval windows and gives the same bound (\texttt{notes/below16\_results.md}
  \S2.2). Targets of degree $\ge2$ give $N^{1/5}$ on average, conditionally on a real-fibre
  hypothesis (ibid., Cor.~F).
\item \emph{Excluded designs.} Not covered are keys finer than $C_2M^2$, sparse windows combined
  with size information, several labels per giant, and global selectors that localise $p$
  cheaply.
\item \emph{Smooth-order methods.} These lie outside the model. Pollard's $p-1$ method factors a
  positive proportion of the nearly balanced family $x^{1-\varepsilon}<p,q\le x$ (a family much
  larger than $\Sn$) at cost $N^{0.152}$
  \cite[Prop.~35.3]{wardmaestro2026}, below the floor. By the same report (remarks after
  Prop.~35.3) it fails on a positive proportion for every smoothness bound up to $N^{0.33}$, so it
  gives no almost-all statement.
\item \emph{Relation methods.} A deterministic quadratic sieve would be heuristically
  subexponential on $\Sn$. Proving this needs smooth-value concentration and a nontrivial Jacobi
  character on the relation kernel, neither of which is known.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\section{Unbalanced ratios}\label{sec:unbalanced}
%======================================================================

This section summarises \texttt{notes/unbalanced\_results.md}. There the proofs are written out
in full, and they were reviewed by an independent Opus critic and two GPT-6.1-sol reports (one
blind, one adversarial; both accept Theorems~\ref{thm:U} and~\ref{thm:Utheta}). Here we only
sketch them.

\subsection{Ratio bands}
For $j\ge0$ let
\[
  \mathcal S_{n,j}=\{(p,q)\ \text{primes}:\ 2^jp<q\le2^{j+1}p,\ 2^{n-1}\le pq<2^n\},
\]
and write $R=2^j$. Then $p\asymp\sqrt{N/R}$ and $q\asymp\sqrt{NR}$. The prime number theorem
gives $|\mathcal S_{n,j}|\ge c_02^n/n^2$ uniformly for $j\le n/3$. Exceptions are counted by pairs
in each band. A bound $O(2^{-\varepsilon n})|\mathcal S_{n,j}|$ in every band $j\le\theta n$
therefore gives an $O(N^{-\varepsilon})$ fraction of the union $\mathcal S_n^\theta$, the pairs
with $q/p\lesssim N^\theta$.

\begin{theorem}[ratios up to $N^{\varepsilon}$; proved]\label{thm:U}
Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1/60]$, and let $H=\lceil n2^{\varepsilon n/2}\rceil$ and $Y$, $\Pk$ be as
in Section~\ref{sec:alg}. For $2^j\le H^2/16$ replace $V$ by the rectangle
\[
  V_j=\{(A,B):1\le A\le H2^{-j/2},\ 1\le B\le H2^{j/2},\ \gcd(A,B)=1,\ A\ne B\},
\]
with windows computed over the $p$-range of band $j$, and keep one baby list per block for all
bands. For $n\ge n_0(\varepsilon)$ the resulting algorithm has three properties:
\begin{itemize}
\item it outputs only proper divisors or \texttt{FAIL};
\item it costs $N^{1/6+5\varepsilon/6}(\log N)^{O(1)}$ on every input;
\item it fails on $O(2^{-\varepsilon n})|\mathcal S_{n,j}|$ pairs of every band with
  $2^j\le H^2/16$, hence on an $O(N^{-\varepsilon})$ fraction of the pairs with
  $q/p\le N^{\varepsilon}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Window span.} On band $j$, $Bx+AN/x\le H2^{j/2}\sqrt{N/R}+H2^{-j/2}\sqrt{2RN}\le
  (1+\sqrt2)H\sqrt N$. So the window bound $\spanbar$, the baby length $\mbar$ and the band
  primes are those of $\DCL_{n,H}$. Only the number of targets grows, by the number of bands,
  $O(n)$.
\item \emph{Weights.} $|AB'-A'B|\le H^2<\ell$ and $|V_j|\gg H^2$, so the lock and the coverage
  criterion hold verbatim. So do the extraction lemmas, the clean band ($q\le H2^{n/2}<Y^4$) and
  the large sieve, applied to the $p$-interval of length $\asymp q/R$.
\item \emph{FULL count.} Lemma~\ref{lem:crt} holds with $\gcd(A-B,d)\le H^2$ in place of $H$.
  This costs one factor $H$, and the first $E_c$ exponent becomes $-1/12+23\varepsilon/6$, which
  is still below $-\varepsilon$ for $\varepsilon\le1/60$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[polynomial ratios; proved]\label{thm:Utheta}
Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1/60]$ and $\theta\in(0,1/5-3\varepsilon]$, and put $h=H^2$. For each band
with $H^2/16<2^j\le2^{\theta n}$, use the following data:
\begin{itemize}
\item the weights $(1,B)$ with $2\le B\le h$;
\item the band scale $Y_j=\lceil(2^{(n+j)/2}/h)^{1/3}\rceil$;
\item four blocks of primes $\ell\equiv3\pmod4$ in $(2^kY_j,2^{k+1}Y_j]$, each with its own $L$,
  $\gamma$ and baby list.
\end{itemize}
Run this after the algorithm of Theorem~\ref{thm:U}. For $n\ge n_0(\varepsilon,\theta)$ the
combined algorithm outputs only proper divisors or \texttt{FAIL}, and costs
\[
  N^{1/6+\max(5\varepsilon/6,\ \theta/6+2\varepsilon/3)}(\log N)^{O(1)}
\]
on every input. It fails on $O(2^{-\varepsilon n})|\mathcal S_{n,j}|$ pairs of every band
$j\le\theta n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Cost.} The window span on band $j$ is at most $18Q_j$ with $Q_j=2^{(n+j+1)/2}$. So a
  block's list costs $O(Q_j/Y_j^2+hY_j)=O(h^{2/3}Q_j^{1/3})$, and the geometric sum over bands gives
  $h^{2/3}2^{(1+\theta)n/6}n^{O(1)}$.
\item \emph{$E_a$ and $E_b$.} With $T_j\asymp Q_j^{1/2}N^{-\varepsilon}$, the set $E_a$ is
  $O(n2^{-2\varepsilon n})$. The large sieve with the $h-1$ distinct classes $qB^{-1}$ gives $E_b=O(n^2/h)$,
  using $Y_j^2\le\sqrt{N/R}$, which holds for $\theta\le1/5$.
\item \emph{$E_c$.} Here $\gcd(B-1,d)\le h$, and the FULL count gives
  \[
  O\bigl(n(h^{8/3}Q_j^{-1/6}2^{\varepsilon n}+h^{4/3}R^{5/6}N^{-1/6}+h^{2/3}N^{(2\theta-1)/3})\bigr).
  \]
  The second term is $O(2^{-\varepsilon n})$ exactly when $R\le2^{(1/5-14\varepsilon/5)n-O(\log
  n)}$. This is where $\theta<1/5$ enters.
\end{itemize}
A fine-band variant, with weights $(1,B)$ for $B\approx R$, has the same exponent; its FULL count
fixes $(p,B,\ell,\delta)$ and counts $q$ instead.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The envelope}
Pollard--Strassen finds every prime factor up to $z$ deterministically in
$z^{1/2}(\log N)^{O(1)}$ \cite{pollard1974,strassen1976,bgs2007}. At ratio $q/p=N^\theta$ we have
$p\le N^{(1-\theta)/2}$, so it costs $N^{(1-\theta)/4}$ in the worst case. Combining it with
Theorem~\ref{thm:Utheta}, every band of ratio $N^\theta$ is factored, except for $O(N^{-\varepsilon})$
of its pairs, at cost $N^{e(\theta)+O(\varepsilon)}$, where
\[
  e(\theta)=\min\Bigl(\frac{1+\theta}6,\ \frac{1-\theta}4\Bigr),\qquad0\le\theta\le1/3.
\]
The first branch is valid for $\theta\le1/5-3\varepsilon$, and the second for all $\theta$. The
envelope $e(\theta)$ exceeds $1/6$ for every $0<\theta<1/3$. It peaks at $e(1/5)=1/5$, at
$p\approx N^{2/5}$ and $q\approx N^{3/5}$. A whole range of ratios $q/p\le N^{\theta_0}$ with
$\theta_0\ge1/5$ therefore costs $N^{1/5+O(\varepsilon)}$, which is no better than Harvey's
worst-case bound.

\subsection{A full-coverage barrier}
The peak at $\theta=1/5$ is not an artefact of our designs, at least for full coverage in the
following model.

\paragraph{Model $\mathfrak L$ (one-dimensional Lehman-linear collision searches).}
Fix $N$ and a ratio band $R_0$. The candidates are the integers $p\in[x_1,x_2]$ with
$R_0\le N/p^2\le2R_0$. A search consists of:
\begin{itemize}
\item baby lists $\{\gamma_i^e:e\in S_i\}$, where $S_i\subset\Z$ is an \emph{arbitrary} set of
  size $m_i$;
\item giants $G$, each compared against one list $i(G)$ and carrying a target
  $(\alpha,\beta,c_0,M,r,g_0)$ with integers $\alpha,\beta,c_0,g_0$ and $M\ge1$, where
  \[
  (\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0).
  \]
  A target with $\alpha=\beta=0$ does not depend on $p$; its collision is two-sided and can never
  be extracted.
\end{itemize}
The giant covers a candidate $p\equiv r\pmod M$ if
$\lfloor(\alpha p+\beta N/p+c_0)/M^2\rfloor\in g_0+S_{i(G)}$. For other candidates this is a
relaxation, which only enlarges coverage. Rational normalisations by $D\mid\beta M^2$ are allowed.
The cost is $X=\sum_im_i+\#\{\text{giants}\}$. Nothing is assumed about the group, the base, the
moduli (prime, composite or CRT products) or the side that is extracted.

\begin{proposition}[Proposition O$'$; proved in $\mathfrak L$; full coverage]\label{prop:Oprime}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item If $\beta\ne0$, a giant whose list has size $m$ covers at most $2m+8\sqrt{mx_2/R_0}$
  candidates.
\item If $\beta=0$, it covers at most $m$.
\item Hence a search in $\mathfrak L$ that covers a fraction $f$ of the candidates has
  \[
  X\ge\min\bigl(\sqrt{fx_2}/4,\ (f/64)^{2/3}(x_2R_0)^{1/3}\bigr)\asymp\min\bigl(\sqrt{fp},\
  f^{2/3}q^{1/3}\bigr).
  \]
  At $R_0=N^{1/5}$ this is $\gg f^{2/3}N^{1/5}$, with any number of moduli.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{(a).} Write $p=r+Mx$. Then $g(x)$ has $g''=2\beta N/p^3$; the factor $M^2$ cancels.
  So $g$ is convex with curvature $\kappa\ge2|\beta|N/x_2^3$. On each side of the minimum,
  $g'\ge\sqrt{\kappa(g-\min g)}$. Hence the preimage of the $i$-th window value has length
  $O(1/\sqrt{\kappa i})$. Summing over the $m$ values gives $2m+O(\sqrt{m/\kappa})$.
\item \emph{(b).} The map is linear with a nonzero integer slope on the refined class.
\item \emph{(c).} The total coverage is at most $2X^2+8\sqrt{x_2/R_0}\,X^{3/2}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[scope of Proposition~\ref{prop:Oprime}]
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Full coverage only.} This is a \emph{full-coverage} statement: it bounds how many
  candidates one search covers for a fixed $N$. It is not an average-case bound. An almost-all
  algorithm only needs the true factor, and it could beat the bound only if its covered sets
  favoured the true factor beyond their density.
\item \emph{Sharpness.} Explicit searches attain the bound inside $\mathfrak L$, with relaxed,
  non-lock coverage. Real algorithms reach the envelope $e(\theta)$ only in the almost-all sense:
  Theorem~\ref{thm:Utheta} on one branch and Pollard--Strassen on the other.
\item \emph{Outside the model.} Targets nonlinear in $(p,q)$, multi-dimensional searches, lattice
  methods and polynomial evaluation are not covered.
\item \emph{Prior art.} Ward--Maestro \cite[Prop.~3.7]{wardmaestro2026} treat unbalanced
  factors $p\approx N^{\beta}$ in their oblivious covering model, which has no per-target
  locks. With materialised giants they obtain $T\gg N^{\max(1/5,(1-\beta)/3)}$. Since
  $\beta\approx(1-\theta)/2$, their branch $(1-\beta)/3$ is our $q^{1/3}$ branch $(1+\theta)/6$,
  and it binds for $\theta\ge1/5$. Model~$\mathfrak L$ allows arbitrary lock moduli and
  arbitrary baby sets. That is why its bound falls to $(1+\theta)/6$ below $\theta=1/5$, where
  Theorem~\ref{thm:Utheta} attains it for almost all pairs. The curvature argument is the same
  as theirs.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Which populations the results serve}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Bounded and $N^{o(1)}$ ratios.} For $\Sn$ and for every band with
  $q/p\le N^{o(1)}$, the exponent is $1/6+o(1)$ with power-saving exceptions
  (Theorems~\ref{thm:D} and~\ref{thm:U}).
\item \emph{Both primes at least $N^{1/3}$, counted by pairs.} Then $\theta$ has a continuous law
  on $[0,1/3]$, with $\Pr[\theta\le t]=\log_2((1+t)/(1-t))$ asymptotically. At cost
  $N^{1/6+\eta}$ our methods handle only a fraction of about $30\eta$ of these pairs. Exponent
  $1/5-\eta'$ fails only on a fraction of about $30\eta'$, but no exponent below $1/5$ holds for
  $1-o(1)$ of them. We do not claim that no method can.
\item \emph{All semiprimes up to $X$.} Almost all have a factor below $X^\delta$, so the
  question is trivial with $o(1)$ exceptions. With power-saving exceptions nothing below $1/5$
  follows from our methods, since a fraction $\asymp\eta/\log\log X$ of these semiprimes has
  $\theta$ within $\eta$ of $1/5$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations and open problems}\label{sec:open}
%======================================================================

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Worst case.} By Theorem~\ref{thm:R}, what is missing is coverage alone. Prove
  Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:DCHpair} for some $\kappa$, or any covering theorem with $c>3/8$. The
  first test case is a covering statement for the union of short intervals
  $\{bx-a\}$ with $x\asymp Y^{25/8}$ and height $Y^{1/8}$.
\item \textbf{Exceptional rate.} The proved tails are $n/H^2$ and $n^4/H^4$. The data support
  $e^{-cH^2/n}$. Remark~\ref{rem:PYY} shows that this needs information beyond five-prime
  statistics.
\item \textbf{Below $1/6$.} Theorem~\ref{thm:T1} rules this out for residue-local degree-one
  collision designs. Remark~\ref{rem:T1scope} lists the escapes that remain open: a global
  selector, keys finer than $M^2$, label multiplicity, and relation methods. Smooth-order
  methods already beat $1/6$ on a positive proportion, but not almost everywhere.
\item \textbf{Ratios.} Is there a deterministic algorithm that factors almost all pairs with
  $p\approx N^{2/5}$, $q\approx N^{3/5}$, that is $q/p\approx N^{1/5}$, in time $N^{1/5-\delta}$?
  Proposition~\ref{prop:Oprime} shows that this needs either a non-uniform covering that favours
  the true factor, or a mechanism outside one-dimensional Lehman-linear searches.
\item \textbf{Practice.} At 40--80 bits the search is $2.3$--$4.9$ times slower than
  Harvey--Hittmeir in our proxy. The heuristic subexponential methods (ECM, the quadratic and
  number field sieves) are far faster still. This work concerns rigorous deterministic bounds
  only.
\end{itemize}

\section{Full-candidate covering bounds (context; statements only)}\label{app:full}
%======================================================================

This appendix is context only, and nothing in the main text depends on it. It concerns families
that cover \emph{every} integer candidate for a \emph{fixed} $N$. It does not bound algorithms that
only reach the true factor, and it is not a lower bound for factoring.

\paragraph{Priority.} The exponent $1/6$, and the curvature argument behind it, are due to
Ward--Maestro \cite[Lemma~3.2, Cor.~3.3, Thm.~3.6]{wardmaestro2026}, for Lehman forms with a
common modulus. The same report gives the $N^{1/5}$ floor with materialised giants
\cite[Thm.~3.4]{wardmaestro2026} and bounded-degree forms \cite[Thm.~9.4]{wardmaestro2026}. The
variant below allows a separate lock modulus for each target. We obtained it independently and
later. Its proofs are in the project notes (\texttt{notes/multimod\_results.md} \S3 and
\texttt{notes/cycloharvey\_results.md} \S3), where they were checked by one AI critic and one AI
referee. We do not reproduce them here.

\paragraph{Model.} Fix $d_0\ge1$ and $\theta>1$, and write $R=\sqrt N$. The candidates are the
integers $p\in[R/\sqrt\theta,R]$. A target consists of:
\begin{itemize}
\item a modulus $M$ with $\gcd(M,N)=1$ and a unit class $r\bmod M$;
\item an exponent map $h(x)=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_ix^{i}-c$, read with $x^{-j}:=(N/x)^j$, where
  $I\subseteq[-d_0,d_0]\setminus\{0\}$, $\alpha_i\in\Z[1/M]\setminus\{0\}$, and $h$ is
  $\ell$-integral on the whole class for every $\ell\mid M$;
\item an integer window of width $W$.
\end{itemize}
A target covers $p$ if $p\equiv r\pmod M$ and $h(p)$ lies in its window. The cost is $X$ if there
are at most $X$ distinct triples $(M,r,\alpha)$, at most $X$ targets, and $\sum W\le X^2$. The
targets of $\DCL_{n,H}$ fit this model with $M=\ell$ and $h=(Bp+Aq-c)/\ell^2$.

\begin{theorem}[full-candidate floors; proved in the project notes, not reproduced]\label{thm:A}
In this model, constants depending on $d_0$ and $\theta$ only:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item every covering family has $X\ge cN^{1/6}$;
\item if degree-one targets cover fewer than half of the candidates, then $X\ge cN^{1/5}$;
\item if the degree-one two-term targets use $J$ distinct moduli, then
  $X\ge cN^{1/5}(JN^{o(1)})^{-1/5}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The integrality hypothesis bounds the denominators by $C_{d_0}M^{K}$. A degree-one two-term
target then covers at most $C(RW/\eta)^{1/2}+C$ candidates, where $\eta$ is its weight height, and
$M$ cancels. Part (a) follows by Cauchy--Schwarz. Part (c) also counts the admissible (class,
weight) pairs per modulus. Lock lattices are multiplicative under the CRT, so covering \emph{all}
classes of a composite modulus gains nothing. What escapes the $N^{1/5}$ bound is
\emph{selective} covering, which is what $\DCL_{n,H}$ does. Whether it covers almost every
candidate for a fixed $N$ is not proved.

\section{Reproducibility}
Code, raw data and notes are in the project repository ([URL and archival revision withheld in
this draft]). The main entry points are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{src/dcltheorem/} (the search);
\item \texttt{src/avgtime/} (detector, ladder, exact exponent audit);
\item \texttt{src/sieve2d/} (coverage bounds and experiments);
