## 1. Proof audit

The recurrence lemmas survive. Several claimed algorithmic obstructions do not: they replace an exact linear-readout hypothesis with an unrestricted selection or factoring claim.

I checked the principal counterexamples below by exact computation. I have not independently reproduced the agents’ full experimental datasets.

The useful corrected index bound is
\[
D\ge \min(M,i+1)
\]
when the observable isolates a class and is nonzero on \(X^i\). For \(i<M\), the first \(i\) positions are zeros; for \(i\ge M\), use the preceding \(M-1\) positions.

**Support size: CORRECT in the balanced semiprime setting.** Put \(\delta=q-p\). Lucas gives
\[
\operatorname{supp}(F_N\bmod p)
=p\bigl([1,\delta]\cup[p,q-1]\bigr),
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}(F_N\bmod q)
=q[1,p-1].
\]
The sets cannot intersect at an interior index: an intersection would be divisible by \(pq\). Thus
\[
|\operatorname{supp}F_N|=2\delta+p-1=2q-p-1.
\]

For repaired C4, the \(p\)-side parameter values \(j=j_0+tM\) lie inside \([1,q-1]\). Their **span** satisfies
\[
t_{\max}-t_{\min}\le
\left\lfloor\frac{q-2}{M}\right\rfloor\le p-2

\]
Thus the exponents are distinct modulo \(p-1\). This replaces the incorrect printed inequality involving \((q-1)/M+1\). The \(q\)-side argument is similar.

The broader assertion that scalar-function traps occur only for Carmichael \(N\) at \(M=1\) is **WRONG**, even for odd squarefree \(N\). For example,
\[
S_{0,4}(F_{51})
=34Y^3+34Y^6+17Y^9+17Y^{12}

is nonzero but vanishes at every scalar modulo 51. Modulo 17 it is zero; modulo 3 it reduces as a function to
\[
Y+Y^2+2Y+2Y^2=0.
\]

**“\(F_N\) vanishes on all of \(R_N\) iff \(N\) is prime or Carmichael”: CORRECT for \(N>1\).** Define \(g(a)=a^N\). The hypothesis says
\[
g(a+1)-g(a)=1,
\]
hence \(g(a)=a\) on all residues by induction. Conversely, \(a^N=a\) implies the stated identity. For composite \(N\), this is exactly the Carmichael property.

Three further corrections matter:

- **Mask bounds:** the preceding-zero-run bound is correct. A field prefix bound also needs a sufficiently long **observed trailing zero block**. Over \(\mathbb F_5\), \(u_t=1-2^t\) has a two-dimensional realization and values \(0,4,2,3,0\) on \(0,\ldots,4\). It isolates the visible prefix \([0,4)\), is nonzero at 3, and has dimension below 4.
- **Circuit growth:** the E/O identities are correct; the claimed universal growth proof is not. A product coordinate depends on all input coordinates for generic independent inputs, but fixed circuit values can have cancellations and sparse dependencies. Degree \(\ge M\) alone does not force all \(M\) coordinates to survive. The reported DAG counts remain measurements of the implementation.
- **Dimension versus cost:** \(D\ge M\) is not by itself an \(\Omega(M)\) bit-operation theorem. Charging for \(D\) explicitly stored coordinates is an additional representation assumption.

## 2. A unified theorem—and its actual boundary

The strongest common statement is a finite-state recurrence theorem, not a factoring theorem.

**Model.** Let \(R\) be a commutative unital ring, \(V\) an \(R\)-module generated by \(D\) elements, \(T\in\operatorname{End}_R(V)\), \(v\in V\), and \(\lambda:V\to W\) an \(R\)-linear map into another \(R\)-module. Define
\[
u_t=\lambda(T^tv),\qquad
L(f)=\lambda(f(T)v).
\]

An **observable** means exactly this linear map \(L\). Arithmetic circuits, repeated squaring, towers and algebra products may compute the state. Their intermediate arithmetic need not be linear. But \(V,T,v,\lambda\) must remain fixed as the index \(t\), or the input coefficients of \(f\), vary.

**Unified gap theorem.**

1. \(u_t\) satisfies a monic constant-coefficient recurrence of order at most \(D\).
2. A block of \(D\) consecutive zeros forces every subsequent value to be zero.
3. The same holds along arithmetic progressions, replacing \(T\) by \(T^b\).
4. Over a field, if the nilpotent part has dimension \(e\), such a block forces \(u_t=0\) for every \(t\ge e\).
5. For scalar outputs over a field, the sharp parameter is the minimal realization dimension—the rank of the infinite Hankel matrix—not the dimension of an unnecessarily large representation.

**Proof.** The determinant trick gives a monic \(\chi\) of degree \(D\) with \(\chi(T)V=0\). Applying \(\lambda T^t\) yields the recurrence. Forward propagation proves item 2. Fitting decomposition proves item 4.

This covers A1, O1, fixed-quotient projected coefficient sequences, and fixed-matrix Krylov/Hankel sequences. A polynomial readout of degree \(d\) can be linearized on at most
\[
\binom{D+d}{d}
\]
monomial coordinates. The theorem then bounds that enlarged dimension.

It does **not** convert arbitrary gcds, pivots, adaptive decoders or nonlinear predicates into faithful linear observables. Nor does it prove the B/D conjectured root-frequency estimates.

The algorithm boundary is:

| Algorithm | Relation to the model |
|---|---|
| Pollard \(p-1\) | Its scalar power is inside; \(a^t-1\) has a dimension-two homogeneous realization. Its zeros are periodic order conditions, not an exact prefix/class mask. Therefore the gap lower bound does not apply. |
| ECM | Curve arithmetic is nonlinear in its coordinates; successful point annihilation and gcd extraction do not supply the stipulated faithful linear zero pattern. |
| BGS / Pollard–Strassen | The factorial recurrence has index-dependent transition \(u_{K+1}=(K+1)u_K\). Product construction, shifts and multipoint evaluation are outside the fixed-transition hypothesis. |
| Harvey | Uses collision searches and growing collections of powers, together with integer arithmetic. Individual power sequences can be inside; the complete decoder is outside the faithful-observable task. |
| QS/NFS | Smoothness testing, integer or number-field relations, and parity dependencies do not constitute one fixed linear-power observable with the required zero pattern. |
| Coppersmith | Uses integer lattices and size bounds to recover small roots. Modular evaluation subroutines do not capture that extraction operation. |

These distinctions match the actual constructions in [Lenstra’s paper, including its description of Pollard \(p-1\)](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~cs812-1/Lenstra1987.pdf), [BGS](https://mathexp.eu/bostan/publications/BoGaSc07.pdf), [Harvey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05450), [Pomerance’s sieve account](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/paper109.pdf), and [Coppersmith](https://research.ibm.com/publications/small-solutions-to-polynomial-equations-and-low-exponent-rsa-vulnerabilities).

For a boundary check, Pollard finds
\[
\gcd(2^6-1,77)=7
\]
using one scalar power. Any proposed unified theorem that requires dimension at least 7 for this computation has silently added a false faithful-mask assumption.

## 3. Novelty calibration

The following are the nearest statements, and the specific distinctions that survive.

- **Unified gap theorem, A1 and O1:** standard linear-recurrence/LFSR theory plus the determinant trick. Massey’s synthesis theorem concerns the shortest recurrence generating a sequence; the “\(D\) zeros kill a state” argument is elementary recurrence propagation. Arithmetic-progression restriction and freezing multilinear arguments introduce no new lower-bound mechanism. The useful contribution is applying this language carefully to coefficient access. [Massey, 1969](https://crypto.stanford.edu/~mironov/cs359/massey.pdf)

- **B1, support blocks and C1–C4:** Frobenius, Lucas and linear independence of distinct finite-field multiplicative characters. These provide exact instance structure, not a new factoring primitive. The repaired C4 observation is useful because it separates evaluation access from formal identity testing.

- **Six-orbit structure:** classical Cauchy–Mirimanoff symmetry. Boyd–Martin–Thom give the same six-element action and exceptional orbits in Lemma 5.2, and explicitly trace the history to Mirimanoff and Helou. Their polynomial differs from the cross-prime polynomial here, but the group action is the same. The new census is experimental data, not a new symmetry theorem. [Boyd–Martin–Thom, §5](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/papers/downloads/SVTD.pdf)

- **O2/O2′:** O2 is an elementary interval-versus-multiplicative-fibre argument using the first two moments. I did not find its exact formulation in the primary sources checked. It may be useful packaging, but no general SLP lower bound follows. O2′ is simply the composition bound for fibre cardinalities.

- **Sparse/lacunary root bounds:** these are related but different. Canetti–Friedlander–Konyagin–Larsen–Lieman–Shparlinski bound roots using exponent-difference gcds. Kelley’s Theorem 2.3 gives, for a \(t\)-nomial,
  \[
  R(f)\le 2(p-1)^{1-1/(t-1)}C(f)^{1/(t-1)},
  \]
  where \(C(f)\) is the largest multiplicative coset contained in the zero set. Coset dependence cannot be discarded: \(X^{(p-1)/2}-1\) already has \((p-1)/2\) roots. These bounds concern sparse **expanded** polynomials; \(F_q\) is not a three-term sparse univariate polynomial after expanding \((1+X)^q\). Neither theorem establishes the B/D \(O(1/p)\) sporadic rate. [Kelley, Theorems 1.1 and 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.00208)

- **O4:** the elementary \(\sqrt p\) argument is much weaker than known results on small generators. Martin–Parvardi prove that subset products of integers up to \(O_\varepsilon(p^{1/(4\sqrt e)+\varepsilon})\) cover the multiplicative group, which also precludes a proper subgroup containing that entire prefix. Thus O4 is not a new asymptotic obstruction. [Martin–Parvardi, Corollary 3](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/papers/downloads/SSRC.pdf)

- **Shub–Smale, Lipton and Bürgisser:** Shub–Smale’s Theorem 2 is precisely about a polylogarithmic BSS procedure deciding \(z\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), implying that factorials are *ultimately easy*: some nonzero multiples have short constant-free programs. Bürgisser’s Theorem 1.1 connects factorial hardness to constant-free permanent lower bounds. Lipton’s published abstract connects short programs with many rational roots to easy factoring; its full quantitative theorem was not accessible here, so I do not endorse a sharper formulation. [Shub–Smale](https://janos.cs.technion.ac.il/COURSES/238900-13/Papers-1/ShubSmale-pap97.pdf), [Bürgisser](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2006/113/download/), [Lipton](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-58691-1_45)

  These do **not** prove equivalence with the stated finite-range mod-\(p\) question. A missing lifting/height argument is essential: adding the product of the primes in a finite range preserves all those modular functions while destroying the corresponding integer roots.

- **Generic-ring literature:** Aggarwal–Maurer prove a reduction from generic RSA inversion to factoring, not an unconditional factoring lower bound. Brown’s integral-generic model directly studies ring-operation programs producing gcds and includes substantially more than fixed linear-state readouts. Its bounds are not the claimed \(\Omega(p)\) obstruction. [Aggarwal–Maurer](https://crypto.ethz.ch/publications/files/AggMau09.pdf), [Brown](https://eprint.iacr.org/2010/047)

- **Cheng and truncation hardness:** Cheng conditionally constructs short programs for **nonzero multiples** of factorials using division polynomials. Extra prime factors prevent those multiples from automatically implementing an exact unit predicate. Dutta–Jindal–Pandey–Sinhababu prove hardness implications for specified characteristic-zero truncation families; applying them to modular \(F_N\) sections needs another reduction. Neither source proves the attached general-SLP obstruction. [Cheng, Theorem 1](https://qcheng2023.github.io/paper/factorial.pdf), [Dutta et al., §§5–6](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2021/072/download)

The defensible novelty claim is therefore narrow: exact structural bookkeeping and some potentially useful interval-fibre formulations. There is no new general factoring lower bound.

## 4. Best repair cycle: two-layer compression without hidden order information

I would spend one further cycle on **two-layer interval folding**, specifically separating genuine compression from a hidden-character exponent. It has a finite, exact search space and produces certificates. Unrestricted E-1 circuit searches lack that advantage; small failures would say little about unrestricted circuits.

A larger certificate already survives:
\[
p=1031,\quad L=33,\quad
\phi(x)=\bigl((x+16)^{516}-7\bigr)^2.
\]
For
\[
Q(Y)=\prod_{r\in R}(Y-r),\qquad
R=\{1,4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81,196,441\},
\]
the zero set of \(Q(\phi(x))\) is exactly \(Z_{33}\). I verified all 1,031 inputs. Its outer degree is 11, versus the optimal one-layer degree 17.

The catch is explicit:
\[
516=(p+1)/2,\qquad x^{516}=\chi_p(x)x.
\]
This exponent supplies the hidden quadratic character.

### Exact experiment

For \(T=[-m,m]\), an exact interval fibre union forces the first power-map gcd to be at most two. Gcd one cannot beat \(L/2\) through a gcd-two second layer. Thus every improvement of the tested kind has
\[
\gcd(E,p-1)=2,\qquad A=\{j^E:0\le j\le m\}.
\]
The full first-layer image is \(H=\{0\}\cup(\mathbb F_p^\*)^2\).

Write \(c=-2v\). Exactness is equivalent to
\[
a\in A,\ c-a\in H\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c-a\in A.
\]
For an exact fold,
\[
d=|A|-\#\{\{a,b\}\subset A:a\ne b,\ a+b=c\}.
\]
Any improvement must contain such a pair, so enumerating pair sums covers **every relevant translation**.

Use the first four primes at or above \(2^j\) and \(3\cdot2^{j-1}\), for \(j=8,10,12,14\). Set \(m=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor//2\). Measure the minimum \(d\) in three arms:

1. public exponents \(2\le E\le64\);
2. all exponents;
3. all exponents excluding \(2E-2\equiv0\pmod{p-1}\).

The per-instance success threshold is \(d\le3L/8\), a 25% improvement over the asymptotic one-layer degree. Timeouts are inconclusive.

Executable standard-library code:

```python
from concurrent.futures import ProcessPoolExecutor
from itertools import combinations
from math import gcd, isqrt
from time import process_time
import resource

def prime(n):
    return n >= 2 and all(n % d for d in range(2, isqrt(n) + 1))

def four_primes(a):
    out = []
    while len(out) < 4:
        if prime(a):
            out.append(a)
        a += 1
    return out

def search(p):
    start = process_time()
    m = isqrt(p) // 2
    L = 2*m + 1
    H = {j*j % p for j in range(p)}
    best = [m+1, m+1, m+1]
    witness = [None, None, None]
    checks = powers = 0

    for E in range(2, p-1, 2):
        if process_time() - start > 175:
            return {"p": p, "status": "inconclusive: CPU cap"}
        if gcd(E, p-1) != 2:
            continue

        A = {pow(j, E, p) for j in range(m+1)}
        powers += m+1
        arms = [E <= 64, True, (2*E-2) % (p-1) != 0]

        for c in {(a+b) % p for a, b in combinations(A, 2)}:
            pairs = 0
            for a in A:
                checks += 1
                b = (c-a) % p
                if b in H and b not in A:
                    break
                pairs += b in A and a < b
            else:
                d = len(A) - pairs
                for arm, enabled in enumerate(arms):
                    if enabled and d < best[arm]:
                        best[arm] = d
                        witness[arm] = (E, -c*pow(2, -1, p) % p)

    return {
        "p": p, "L": L, "best": best, "witness": witness,
        "passes": [8*d <= 3*L for d in best],
        "membership_checks": checks, "modular_powers": powers,
        "cpu_seconds": process_time() - start,
        "peak_kib": resource.getrusage(resource.RUSAGE_SELF).ru_maxrss,
    }

if __name__ == "__main__":
    primes = [
        p for multiplier in (1, 3)
        for j in (8, 10, 12, 14)
        for p in four_primes(
            (1 << j) if multiplier == 1 else 3*(1 << (j-1))
        )
    ]
    with ProcessPoolExecutor(max_workers=32) as pool:
        for row in pool.map(search, primes):
            print(row, flush=True)
```

I ran the underlying searches during this audit. The \(3L/8\) threshold was fixed in the first search code; excluding the character exponent was an **exploratory follow-up**, not a pre-registered discovery.

| Arm | Instances meeting threshold |
|---|---:|
| Public exponents through 64 | 0/32 |
| All exponents | 14/32 |
| All non-character exponents | 0/32 |

On all 16 largest instances, the non-character search found **no improvement at all** over the one-layer degree. The two exhaustive passes reported about 104 worker CPU-seconds combined, comfortably within two CPU-hours.

This refutes the finite tested version exactly. It does not prove an asymptotic impossibility.

### Mathematical attack

Try to prove or refute the following classification:

> For all sufficiently large primes \(p\), with \(m=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor//2\), an exact two-layer fold with both power-map gcds equal to two and \(d\le3L/8\) must have  
> \[
> p\equiv3\pmod4,\qquad E\equiv(p+1)/2\pmod{p-1}.
> \]

The reflection criterion reduces this to an explicit problem about additive pairs in the short power image \(A\). The missing bound is: **outside the character-fold exponent, exact reflection closure cannot contain a linear number of internal pairs.** Neither O2′ nor the cited sparse-root bounds establishes that.

The payoff of that classification is concrete. If a constructor supplies such a character-fold exponent \(E>1\), then
\[
p-1\mid 2E-2.
\]
A known polynomial-bit-length multiple of one hidden prime’s \(p-1\) already gives randomized polynomial-time factoring: use gcds of \(a^{2E-2}-1\), or the usual square-root chain if both prime orders divide the exponent.

Thus the next cycle has a precise decision point: find substantial compression outside this order-revealing class, or prove that the observed compression has merely encoded the missing factor information in its exponent.
