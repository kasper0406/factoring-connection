## Verdict (plain language)

**No. We did not obtain a deterministic subexponential factoring algorithm under GRH, for all N or for almost all
N.** The orchestrator's idea does not survive as stated, but analysing it pins down exactly where determinism
fails. **Under GRH, fruitfulness is not the obstruction at all. The whole problem is deterministic relation
supply.**

1. **The multiplicativity idea [proved].**
   - The implication is valid: if the algorithm's square root y(R) were multiplicative on all squares mod N,
     GRH would give a fruitful seed r < 2 log² N.
   - Its hypothesis cannot hold, for two reasons.
     - **Lemma MR.** A multiplicative square-root function on (Z/N)^{×2} exists, even modulo ±1, only when every
       p | N is ≡ 3 (mod 4). It is then unique: the principal root. Computing it already factors N.
     - **Proposition CD / Theorem M.** The square roots that index calculus or NFS produce are multiplicative on
       *certificates*, not on residues. Two certificates of the same residue give roots that differ by exactly
       the full-lattice functional Φ_full.
   - Consequence: any algorithm that certifies small squares honestly (y(r²) = r for r < 2 log² N) has
     non-multiplicative y under GRH. Some product pair (R, R′) is then itself a factor (Theorem M(G)), but nothing
     locates such a pair in a short list.
2. **Structural versus sampling fruitlessness [proved].**
   - Structural fruitlessness, meaning every relation set over the factor base fails, happens iff
     G_B ∩ μ₂(Z/N) = {±1}, where G_B is the subgroup generated by the factor base.
   - lemmaM's barrier B1 is exactly this case.
   - Under GRH it is impossible once the factor base contains the primes below 2 log² N (Bach).
   - All remaining fruitlessness is sampling: the found lattice is incomplete modulo 2. Proposition FL gives the
     exact criterion: fruitful whenever the 2-rank of Λ/Λ_c is < ω − 1.
3. **The Z route [proved, folklore-level combination of Miller 1976 and Bach 1990].**
   - Do the linear algebra over Z instead of F₂. Kernel relations (∏ q^{e_q} ≡ 1 mod N) of full Q-rank, over a
     factor base containing the primes < 2 log² N, give a multiple of λ(N). Then Miller's ERH test factors N
     deterministically.
   - No square roots, no Φ, no quadratic characters and no seeds are needed.
   - The orchestrator's "multiplicative χ plus small seed" is realised correctly by the *power map* a ↦ a^{M/2^j},
     not by an F₂ square root.
   - Conversely, the factorisation gives such relations trivially (q^{λ(N)} ≡ 1). So **under ERH, deterministic
     factoring ⟺ deterministic production of a full-rank kernel-relation set (Hypothesis DS; Proposition EQ).**
4. **Derandomizing Theorem A [assessment, with proved items; §5].**
   - Every random choice except two can be derandomized, unconditionally or under GRH:
     - kernel sampling, by deterministic elimination (exponent ω or 3, which fits the cost slack);
     - character primes, under GRH or by avoiding them through the Z route;
     - the Hensel prime and root finding;
     - the shape test, by deterministic recursion;
     - the seed's *fruitfulness* role, by the Z route with factor-base-product seeds and exact dependencies.
   - The two **irreducible** sources are the field law (g₀, g₁, w, c) and the seed law. Both are irreducible only
     in their **relation-supply** role.
5. **Field derandomization [observation; §6].**
   - FM, LD* and Paley–Zygmund certify a density L^{−o(1)} of good fields only inside families of size N^{Ω(1)}. The
     smooth-numbers-in-progressions input needs progressions with at least modulus^{1+ε} terms.
   - The natural Fourier certificate that an explicit list of seeds hits the descendable set needs a list of
     N^{1+o(1)} seeds, even with GRH's square-root bound for prime character sums. Without GRH it needs N^{3/2}.
   - Checkability does not help without a pointwise smooth-value theorem, which no known GRH-conditional result
     provides.
6. **Literature [§8].**
   - We found no deterministic subexponential factoring algorithm, even under GRH. The deterministic GRH record we
     found is Schoof 1982 (N^{1/5+ε}), now unconditional by Harvey 2021 / Harvey–Hittmeir 2022.
   - We found no deterministic subexponential class-group or prime-field discrete-log algorithm under GRH, and no
     impossibility result.
   - The Z route's ingredients are classical (Miller; Bach; index calculus mod N, cf. Bach 1984). We found no
     explicit statement of Proposition EQ, which is folklore-level.

**The single most promising open statement** is Hypothesis DS-NFS (§5.3): for every N, some field in an explicit

## 1. Setting and notation

- N is odd and not a prime power, ω = ω(N) ≥ 2, and N has no prime factor ≤ B.
- **Factor base.** FB = (−1, p₁, …, p_{n−1}), with p_i the primes ≤ B.
- **Groups.** Γ := Z^n, with −1 treated as a free generator. ev: Γ → (Z/N)^×, ev(e) = ∏ q^{e_q} mod N.
  - Λ := ker ev is the full relation lattice. It contains 2e_{−1}.
  - G_B := ev(Γ) ≅ Γ/Λ.
  - μ₂ := μ₂(Z/N) ≅ {±1}^ω.
  - Λ_c ⊆ Λ is the lattice spanned by the relations the algorithm found. We always include 2e_{−1} in it.
- **Generality.**
  - An NFS certificate group (Q^×_S × K^×_S) reduces to this setting after exact elimination of the algebraic side
    (dlog Prop XK: rational and ideal valuations cancel over Z, units are removed by archimedean logarithms and LLL).
  - The F₂-side statements below hold verbatim for any finitely generated certificate group with a homomorphism ev.
    One replaces {±1} by ev(Γ_tors).
- **Square dependencies and Φ.**
  - A *square dependency* of Λ_c is v ∈ Λ_c ∩ 2Γ.
  - Φ(v) := ev(v/2). Since Φ(v)² = ev(v) = 1, Φ(v) ∈ μ₂.
  - v is *fruitful* iff Φ(v) ∉ {±1}, iff gcd(Φ(v) − 1, N) is proper.
  - Φ is a homomorphism Λ_c ∩ 2Γ → μ₂ that is trivial on 2Λ_c. This is nfsfruit's Lemma H in lattice language: the
    F₂ dependencies S of the relation rows are exactly the v = Σ_S v_i ∈ 2Γ.
- **Bach's theorem (ERH).** Every proper subgroup of (Z/m)^× misses some prime < 2 log² m (Bach, Math. Comp. 55
  (1990) 355–380, Theorem 1 and its corollaries). So if FB ⊇ {primes < 2 log² N}, then G_B = (Z/N)^× and
  G_B ∩ μ₂ = μ₂.

## 2. Multiplicative square roots and the orchestrator's χ

**Lemma MR [proved].** Let N be odd and Q := (Z/N)^{×2}.
- (a) A group homomorphism y: Q → (Z/N)^× with y(s)² = s for all s ∈ Q exists iff every prime p | N is
  ≡ 3 (mod 4). It is then unique, and it is the principal root: the unique root of s that lies in Q.
- (b) The same holds modulo ±1, i.e. for homomorphisms y: Q → (Z/N)^×/{±1} with y(s)² = s, when ω ≥ 2.
  - For ω = 1, every p works trivially, since μ₂ = {±1}.
  - With all p ≡ 3 (mod 4), the class of the principal root is the unique such map.

*Proof.*
- **Structure.** By CRT, (Z/N)^× = ∏_i C_i with C_i = ⟨g_i⟩ cyclic of order n_i = φ(p_i^{e_i}). Each n_i is even.
  - Q = ∏_i ⟨g_i²⟩, a direct product, with |⟨g_i²⟩| = n_i/2.
  - μ₂ = ∏_i ⟨g_i^{n_i/2}⟩.
- **(a), restriction to one factor.** Restrict y to ⟨g_i²⟩ and project to the factor C_j.
  - y(g_i²) is a root of g_i², so y(g_i²) = g_i·ε with ε ∈ μ₂. Its i-component is ±g_i, and its j-component (j ≠ i)
    is ε_j ∈ {±1}.
  - Well-definedness on ⟨g_i²⟩ requires y(g_i²)^{n_i/2} = 1.
  - At the i-th factor this reads (±g_i)^{n_i/2} = (±1)^{n_i/2}·g_i^{n_i/2} = (±1)^{n_i/2}·(−1).
    - If n_i/2 is even, this is −1 for both signs, so no y exists.
    - If n_i/2 is odd, it forces the sign −, i.e. y(g_i²)_i = −g_i = g_i^{1+n_i/2}, which is a square since 1 + n_i/2
      is even.
  - At j ≠ i it reads ε_j^{n_i/2} = 1, which forces ε_j = 1 when n_i/2 is odd.
  - Note n_i/2 is odd iff p_i ≡ 3 (mod 4), because n_i = p_i^{e_i−1}(p_i − 1) with p_i odd.
- **(a), existence and uniqueness.** If all n_i/2 are odd, y is forced on every generator, so it is unique. The forced
  value is the square root that is itself a square, so y is the principal root, and it is a homomorphism.
- **(b).** Put χ(r) := y(r²)/r ∈ μ₂/{±1}, for r ∈ (Z/N)^×.
  - χ is a homomorphism, since y is multiplicative modulo ±1.
  - For ε ∈ μ₂, χ(εr) = ε·χ(r) (as ε = ε^{−1}). So χ(ε) = [ε], the class of ε.
  - The element ε^{(i)}, with −1 in factor i and 1 elsewhere, is not ±1 when ω ≥ 2. So χ(ε^{(i)}) ≠ 0 in
    μ₂/{±1} ≅ F₂^{ω−1}.
  - But ε^{(i)} = g_i^{n_i/2}, so χ(ε^{(i)}) = (n_i/2)·χ(g_i) = 0 in the F₂-space whenever n_i/2 is even.
    Contradiction unless every p_i ≡ 3 (mod 4).
  - Uniqueness: two such maps differ by a homomorphism Q → μ₂/{±1}. Q has odd order ∏ n_i/2, so that homomorphism
    is trivial. ∎

**Corollary MR [proved].** Assume GRH, ω ≥ 2, and that the algorithm's y is multiplicative modulo ±1 on all of
(Z/N)^{×2}. Then:
- every p | N is ≡ 3 (mod 4);
- y is the principal root modulo ±1;
- χ(r) = ((r/p₁), …, (r/p_ω)) modulo the diagonal;
- some prime r < 2 log² N has χ(r) ∉ {±1}, i.e. gcd(y(r²) − r, N) is proper.

*Proof.*
- The first three items are Lemma MR(b).
- χ: (Z/N)^× → μ₂/{±1} is a nonzero homomorphism, since it is the identity on μ₂ and ω ≥ 2. Its kernel is a proper
  subgroup, which by Bach misses a prime r < 2 log² N. ∎

So the orchestrator's implication is **valid**. But its hypothesis amounts to "y computes principal square roots of
arbitrary squares", which factors N directly. Reading off Legendre-vector parities is Rabin–Williams. The actual
question is whether index-calculus y's are multiplicative. They are not (§3).

## 3. Certificate-level multiplicativity; the full-lattice functional

**Certificates.** Put C := 2Γ + Λ_c. A certificate of a residue R is c ∈ C with ev(c) = R. Write c = 2g + λ with
λ ∈ Λ_c, and put y(c) := ev(g). Then y(c)² = ev(c) = R.

**Proposition FL (full lattice) [proved].**
- (i) Φ_full: (Λ ∩ 2Γ)/2Λ → G_B ∩ μ₂, v ↦ ev(v/2), is an isomorphism. The class of 2e_{−1} maps to −1.
  - Put W′ := (Λ ∩ 2Γ)/2Λ. It has dimension rk₂(G_B ∩ μ₂), which is ω if G_B ⊇ μ₂.
  - The fruitful classes correspond to (G_B ∩ μ₂) ∖ {±1}.
- (ii) Let V_c be the image of Λ_c in Λ/2Λ. Then Φ(Λ_c ∩ 2Γ) = Φ_full(V_c ∩ W′). In particular, Λ_c has a fruitful
  dependency iff dim(V_c ∩ W′) ≥ 2.
- (iii) Suppose Λ_c has full rank, and put c := dim_F₂ Λ/(Λ_c + 2Λ), the 2-rank of the finite group Λ/Λ_c. If
  c ≤ dim W′ − 2, then Λ_c has a fruitful dependency. For G_B ⊇ μ₂ the condition reads c < ω − 1; for semiprimes it
  means odd index [Λ : Λ_c].

*Proof.*
- (i) Well-defined: if v ∈ 2Λ then v/2 ∈ Λ, so ev(v/2) = 1. Injective: ev(v/2) = 1 means v/2 ∈ Λ, so v ∈ 2Λ.
  Surjective: for ε = ev(g) ∈ G_B ∩ μ₂ we have 2g ∈ Λ ∩ 2Γ and Φ_full(2g) = ε.
- (ii), image contained in Φ_full(V_c ∩ W′): v ∈ Λ_c ∩ 2Γ has a class in V_c ∩ W′, with the same Φ-value.
- (ii), the reverse: a class in V_c ∩ W′ has representatives v ∈ Λ_c and w ∈ Λ ∩ 2Γ with v − w ∈ 2Λ ⊆ 2Γ. So
  v ∈ Λ_c ∩ 2Γ, and Φ(v) = Φ_full(w)·ev((v − w)/2) = Φ_full(w), because (v − w)/2 ∈ Λ.
- (ii), the criterion: [2e_{−1}] lies in V_c ∩ W′, is nonzero (e_{−1} ∉ Λ) and maps to −1. So a fruitful value
  exists iff V_c ∩ W′ ⊄ ⟨[2e_{−1}]⟩, iff dim(V_c ∩ W′) ≥ 2.
- (iii) dim V_c = n − c, dim W′ = w′ and dim Λ/2Λ = n give dim(V_c ∩ W′) ≥ (n − c) + w′ − n = w′ − c ≥ 2. ∎

**Proposition CD (certificate dependence) [proved].**
- (a) For a fixed c, the value y(c) depends on the decomposition c = 2g + λ only through Φ(Λ_c ∩ 2Γ). If Λ_c is
  fruitless, y is a homomorphism C → (Z/N)^×/{±1}.
  - Fixing a linear complement makes y a homomorphism of F₂-type data. Our code uses the canonical completion: the
    unique combination of a fixed reduced-echelon basis of Λ_c mod 2.
  - "Different dependencies give different y" is exactly this ambiguity. It is canonical in the sense that its
    image is Φ(Λ_c ∩ 2Γ).
- (b) For two certificates c, c′ of the same residue R, y(c)/y(c′) = Φ_full(2(g − g′)).
  - As c′ ranges over all certificates of R, this ranges over all of G_B ∩ μ₂.
  - Hence y descends to a function of R (modulo ±1) independent of certificates iff G_B ∩ μ₂ ⊆ {±1}.

*Proof.*
- (a) Two decompositions differ by 2(g − g′) = λ′ − λ ∈ Λ_c ∩ 2Γ, so the values differ by Φ(λ′ − λ). If Φ only takes
  the values ±1 on Λ_c ∩ 2Γ, y is additive modulo ±1.
- (b) c − c′ ∈ Λ and λ − λ′ ∈ Λ_c ⊆ Λ, so 2(g − g′) ∈ Λ ∩ 2Γ, and ev(g − g′) = Φ_full(·). For any h with
  ev(h) ∈ G_B ∩ μ₂, c′ := c + 2h is another certificate of R, and y(c′) = y(c)·ev(h). ∎

**Theorem M (dichotomy) [proved].** Exactly one of the following holds.
- **(S) Structural.** G_B ∩ μ₂ = {±1}. Then:
  - every square dependency of every Λ_c ⊆ Λ is fruitless;
  - every certificate-based y is a well-defined multiplicative function of R on G_B², modulo ±1;
  - χ(r) := y(r²)/r ∈ {±1} for all r ∈ G_B, i.e. χ is diagonal;
  - Miller's test with bases in G_B and any multiple of exp(G_B) never splits N, because every element of a Miller
    sequence lies in G_B ∩ μ₂.
  - **lemmaM's B1 is an instance.** Take N Blum with ω = 2 and every FB prime with (q/N) = +1. Then G_B ⊆ ker J_N,
    and ker J_N ∩ μ₂ = {(1,1), (−1,−1)} = {±1}, because J_N(ε) = ε_p ε_q for p ≡ q ≡ 3 (mod 4).
  - Under ERH, (S) is impossible when FB ⊇ {primes < 2 log² N} and ω ≥ 2.
- **(G) Generic.** G_B ∩ μ₂ ⊋ {±1}. Then fruitfulness of Λ_c is a sampling property, exactly as in Proposition
  FL(ii). Moreover, assume G_B = (Z/N)^× (ERH, FB ⊇ primes < 2 log² N) and let c(·) be any rule assigning
  certificates to the squares of (Z/N)^×.
  - Suppose c is honest on small squares: c(r²) = 2e_r for primes r < 2 log² N, as in Theorem A's descent, where
    r² < √N splits as (r², 1).
  - Then R ↦ y(c(R)) is **not** multiplicative modulo ±1 on (Z/N)^{×2}.
  - Hence there are squares R, R′ with y(c(RR′)) ∉ ±y(c(R))y(c(R′)), and gcd(y(c(RR′)) − y(c(R))y(c(R′)), N) is a
    proper factor.

*Proof of (G).*
- If y∘c were multiplicative modulo ±1, Corollary MR would give a prime r < 2 log² N with χ(r) ∉ {±1}.
- Honesty gives y(c(r²)) = ev(e_r) = r, i.e. χ(r) = 1. Contradiction.
- The defect δ = y(c(RR′))/(y(c(R))y(c(R′))) satisfies δ² = 1. When δ ∉ {±1} it splits N.
- (S) is a direct consequence of Proposition FL(i) and CD(b). ∎

**What (G) does and does not give.**
- It is the rigorous form of the orchestrator's argument: either y is multiplicative and some small seed is
  fruitful, or y is not multiplicative and some product pair is fruitful.
- The pair is not located.
  - **Lemma SC [proved].** If R, R′ and RR′ are all B-smooth integers below N and are certified by their own
    factorisations, then the cocycle e(RR′) − e(R) − e(R′) is 0.
  - So a fruitful pair needs wrap-around modulo N: products of generators of length ≥ log N/log(2 log² N).
  - The cocycles of a section generate C ∩ Λ only when taken over all of G_B², a set of size ≈ φ(N)/2^ω. This is the
    Schreier-generator count; the proof is the computation in Proposition CD.
- So the dichotomy is an existence statement over an N-size set. That set is the set of residues whose descent the
  algorithm must perform. Locating a fruitful pair deterministically is the same derandomization problem as before.
  E1d measures how often the defect is nontrivial on the pairs a toy run can reach.

**Answer to question 2 (structural fruitlessness and Φ).**
- (a) If Φ ≢ 0 on the dependency space, testing a basis finds a fruitful dependency deterministically (Lemma H). No
  seed is needed.
- (b) Structural fruitlessness (Φ_full ≡ 0 on the whole lattice) is exactly case (S). In that case multiplicativity
  does hold and does force failure, both in the F₂ route and in the Z route. GRH removes (S).
- Fruitlessness for "structural reasons of how y is built" (Lemma H type) in case (G) would mean V_c ∩ W′ = ⟨[2e_{−1}]⟩
  for the specific relations sampled. This is a sampling statement about Λ_c, not a property of y.

## 4. The Z route: fruitfulness for free under ERH

**Proposition Z [proved; folklore-level combination].** Assume ERH. Let FB ⊇ {−1} ∪ {primes < 2 log² N} and let
Λ_c ⊆ Λ have full rank n.
- Put D := [Γ : Λ_c], or any nonzero n × n minor of a generating matrix (each is a multiple of D).
- Then λ(N) | D.
- Miller's test with M = D and bases a = 2, …, ⌈2 log² N⌉ returns a proper factor of N.
- It is deterministic, with cost Õ(log² N·log D·log² N) bit operations. The cost of computing D is
  deterministic polynomial in (n, rows, log ‖entries‖) (HNF; Kannan–Bachem 1979, Storjohann).

*Proof.*
- **λ(N) | D.** G_B ≅ Γ/Λ is a quotient of Γ/Λ_c, which has order D. So exp(G_B) | D. By Bach, G_B = (Z/N)^×, so
  λ(N) | D.
- **Miller.** Write D = 2^s t with t odd.
  - Let k be maximal with 0 ≤ k < s such that some b has b^{2^k t} = −1. Such k exists: (−1)^t = −1 gives k ≥ 0.
  - Put H := {a : a^{2^k t} = ±1}, a subgroup.
  - H is proper. By CRT, choose c ≡ b (mod p^e) and c ≡ 1 modulo the rest. Then c^{2^k t} is −1 at p and 1
    elsewhere, so it is not ±1, as ω ≥ 2.
  - Every a ∉ H gives a factor. Let x be the last element different from 1 in the sequence a^{2^j t}, j ≥ k. The
    sequence ends at 1 because λ | D, and the first term a^{2^k t} ≠ 1. If x = −1, its index j′ would satisfy
    j′ ≥ k, and either j′ > k, contradicting the maximality of k, or j′ = k, giving a ∈ H. So x ∉ {±1}, x² = 1, and
    gcd(x − 1, N) is proper.
  - By Bach, the least a ∉ H is < 2 log² N. ∎ (Miller, JCSS 13 (1976); Bach 1990 for the constant.)

**Proposition EQ [proved; folklore-level].** Assume ERH. The following two algorithmic problems, each required
for *all* odd N that are not prime powers and have no prime factor ≤ 2 log² N, are deterministically equivalent
with polynomial overhead (in log N and the size of the relation set; T(N) ↦ O(log N)·T(N) + poly).
- (1) Find a proper factor of N.
- (2) Output kernel relations, i.e. integer vectors e with ∏_{q∈FB} q^{e_q} ≡ 1 (mod N), of full Q-rank, over
  FB = {−1} ∪ {primes < 2 log² N}.

*Proof.*
- (2) ⇒ (1) is Proposition Z.
- (1) ⇒ (2): apply (1) recursively to N and its factors (≤ log₂ N calls; AKS for primality; trial division for
  factors ≤ 2 log² N; integer roots for prime powers) to factor N completely, compute λ(N), and output
  λ(N)·e_q for each q ∈ FB, together with 2e_{−1}. ∎
- Iterating (2) ⇒ (1) on the factors gives complete factorisation with ≤ log₂ N calls.

**Consequences.**
- **(Z1)** Under ERH, a deterministic algorithm needs no F₂ fruitfulness argument, no hidden roots, no quadratic
  characters and no seeds. It needs kernel relations of full Q-rank on a factor base that contains the small primes.
- **(Z2)** The orchestrator's "homomorphism χ plus small-seed lemma" is exactly Miller's proof.
  - The homomorphism is the power map a ↦ a^{2^k t}, computable once a multiple of λ(N) is known.
  - The small seed is Miller's base a < 2 log² N.
  - Z-linear algebra captures every 2-adic level at once: the full lattice's Bockstein tower, which nfsfruit
    identified as the "second-order" information F₂ lacks.
  - The doubled control 2Λ_c shows the separation (E1b P-Z2). Its F₂ route is dead by construction; its Z route
    works.
- **(Z3)** Q-rank completeness has no structural obstruction of Bockstein type.
  - If w ∈ Q^n vanishes on all of Λ, then w = 0, because Γ/Λ is finite.
  - So a deficient Λ_c misses directions only through sampling.
  - The obvious degeneracy is a factor-base prime that never occurs (E1: a prime 971 was missing at a small cap). It
    is detectable, and handled by enlarging the supply or the factor base.
  - Proving completeness for an explicit relation set is nonetheless open: it is part of DS.
- **(Z4)** The relation source must produce **kernel** relations, with both sides smooth.
  - QS relations x² ≡ Q(x) are not kernel relations, because x is not in the factor base. Adding x as a generator
    adds one column per relation, so no pure factor-base relation survives.
  - So the Z route does not rescue detqs's QS (its H2 stays as it was).
  - NFS relations are kernel relations after exact elimination of the algebraic side (dlog Prop XK), so for NFS-type
    designs the Z route costs no extra smoothness.
- **(Z5)** Randomized aside, not needed for this task [conditional/open]. Under GRH the same route would replace
  fruitfulness hypotheses such as LV's Conjecture 7.1 by Q-rank completeness of the exact-dependency lattice of
  randomized NFS relations. We did not check the cost of exact dependencies at LV's growing degree. Flagged for the
  orchestrator as a possible route, UNVERIFIED.

**Hypothesis DS-NFS(c₀) [open].** There is a deterministic procedure that, given N, enumerates in time
L_N[1/2, c₀] an explicit list of (field, seed) pairs. Its successful descents yield rows whose exact rational
dependencies have full Q-rank on FB ⊇ {primes < 2 log² N}.

**Corollary DZ [conditional; the deduction is proved].** ERH + DS-NFS(c₀) ⇒ deterministic complete factorisation
of every N in time L_N[1/2, max(c₀, C)]. Here C covers the exact linear algebra: L^{3a} by dlog Prop XK, or

- *Proof:* Propositions XK and Z, with ≤ log₂ N recursive calls on factors.

## 6. Derandomizing the field (question 3) and the seeds

**Checkability alone gives nothing [proved, trivial].** For any explicit list E of m fields and any γ < 1 − m/|𝒜|,
some subset of the family 𝒜 of density ≥ γ avoids E. So any proof that E contains a good field must use the
arithmetic of the good set: which fields have many smooth values. Moment bounds over 𝒜 do not supply it.

**Observation FS (family-size floor for the moment method) [observation].**
- **How FM sees a field.** For fixed P, as c varies, F_A(P) runs through the progression F̂(P) + G(P)·j with
  |j| ≤ dHV^{d−1}. The modulus is t = |G(P)| ≍ MV = N^{μ+ν+o(1)}.
- **What FM's analytic input needs.** The input is LV Lemmas 5.13–5.20 together with Lemma 8.5′: smooth numbers in
  progressions, averaged over the moduli t, at level t ≤ X_F^{1/2} (the level floor). It applies to progressions of
  full length, i.e. X_F/t ≥ t^{1−o(1)} terms. Lemma 8.5′ is BV-type, and BV is meaningless for progressions shorter
  than their modulus.
- **Consequence.** A c-subfamily realising only J ≪ t terms would need smooth-number equidistribution in short
  progressions (J terms, modulus t). That is the short-interval problem at scale X_F, with interval length
  tJ ≪ X_F^{1/2+o(1)}. It is not known even for intervals, and certainly not with B = L^{O(1)}.
- **Size of the family.** So a family certified by FM has ≥ N^{μ+ν−o(1)} values of ⟨c, v(P)⟩, hence ≥ N^{Ω(1)}
  fields. The w-average (Lemma W) and the (g₀, g₁)-average (Lemma DS) are polynomial-size in the same way.
- **GRH does not change this.** GRH-conditional results for smooth numbers in an individual progression (see §8)
  still need full-length progressions, and here y = L^{O(1)} ≪ t^{ε}.

**Observation CS (Fourier certification of explicit seeds) [observation; proved computation].**
- **Setup.** Let E be an explicit family of residues x_e (for example products of small primes with exponent
  vectors from a box). Let S ⊂ [1, √N] be the target set of descendable halves, |S| = N^{1/2}L^{−o(1)}. A seed is
  useful if x_e ≡ u/v with u, v ∈ S.
- **The count.** Z := #{(e, u, v) : x_e ≡ u/v} = φ(N)^{−1} Σ_χ Ê(χ)|Ŝ(χ)|². The main term (χ₀) is |E||S|²/φ(N).
- **The inputs.**
  - GRH gives |Σ_{ℓ∈J} χ(ℓ)| ≪ √Y log²(NY) for χ ≠ χ₀, hence max_{χ≠χ₀}|Ŝ(χ)| ≤ |S|N^{−1/4+o(1)}.
  - Parseval gives Σ_χ|Ê(χ)|² ≥ φ(N)|E|.
- **Hölder.** Take exponents (p, q) with p ≤ 2 and use Σ|Ê|^p ≤ φ(N)|E|^{p/2}. Then
  error/main ≤ |E|^{−1/2}N^{1/2+o(1)} for every q ≥ 2: the q-dependence of the GRH saving N^{−(q−1)/(2q)} cancels
  exactly against |S|^{−1/q} = N^{−1/(2q)+o(1)}. Without the GRH bound, using only |Ŝ| ≤ |S|, the best choice
  q = 2 gives |E|^{−1/2}N^{3/4}.
- **Exponents p > 2** are worse: the ratio is |E|^{−1/p}N^{1/2}.
- **Conclusion.** This certificate needs |E| ≥ N^{1+o(1)} under GRH, and |E| ≥ N^{3/2+o(1)} without it.
- **Scope.** This is a statement about one natural proof method. A proof would have to exploit correlation between
  the large spectra of E and S, i.e. "pretentious" small-prime structure, which no known theorem controls. The
  expander property of (Z/N)^× with small-prime generators under GRH (Jao–Miller–Venkatesan type) gives mixing of
  *random* walks of length ≍ log N. A deterministic algorithm would have to enumerate s^t ≥ N walks.

**Q3 verdict [assessment].**
- No known method, under GRH or not, proves that an explicit L^{O(1)} list of fields or seeds contains a good one.
- The short-box FM/LD* variant fails for the method-intrinsic reason of Observation FS.
- A second moment over an explicit initial segment of an enumeration is the same thing as a short box.
- The deterministic analogue of Theorem DR exists only under the Impagliazzo–Wigderson circuit hypothesis
  (polyimpl), which GRH does not imply.
