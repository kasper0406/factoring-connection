## CF hypothesis and test

Select the one with the greatest gcd of Q1 with any denominator already in the bank, ties by smaller k. Include the first 16 denominators of each selected multiplier. A signed denominator (-1)^j Qj has a public square root modulo N. Pool relations by exact rational square-class linear algebra on all positive Qj. An odd total sign in a relation certifies split-prime T0. Hypothesis: adaptive shared factors and cross-multiplier dependencies expose more certificates than testing each multiplier individually. A fallback fair coin gives advantage equal to half the balanced probability of a certificate, or a quarter of the split-prime sensitivity when all certificates are sound. Use that expected advantage, without coin noise. Test the joint bank directly. Report pooled, union of individual certificates, sum of individual frequencies, multiple-hit count and first-six vs full bank. ## T2 hypothesis and test

Compare root-existence truth using factors, and try the public Frobenius substitute: gcd(N, resultant(H, X^N-X)) and the Jacobi sign of that resultant. A proper gcd is a split. On clean rows a zero resultant modulo N votes inert, a unit with Jacobi +1 votes inert in the separate signed diagnostic. Record class-conditional rates for both scores and the oracle square rates. ## Carry construction

For m=floor(N/d), check whether each s|N divides binom(2m,m) exactly when its partner's residue mod d lies in the upper half. Count the consequent unit, proper gcd, and zero strata; distinguish predicted oracle splits from observed factor-free splits. Directly verify binomial integers on small balanced pairs. ## Decision and scaling

For each of three public endpoints (CF certificates, T2 resultant zero, T2 Jacobi sign) form simultaneous conservative 99% intervals via Bonferroni exact binomial bounds on the two class-conditional rates at every registered size (all 20 rates), conditional on the clean counts. For the proved one-sided certificate use the exact zero false-positive rate. Use balanced accuracy minus one half. A finite-range pass requires positive lower bounds at every size, and a lower bound at the largest size at least the smallest-size upper bound (non-decrease at this resolution). No finite experiment establishes asymptotic non-decay. If an endpoint passes, stop widening the search and harden on fresh larger inputs and by proof. For positive endpoints report effective base-two decay exponent from endpoints, with bounds from rate intervals, handling zero counts without pseudocounts. If a signed endpoint crosses zero, report why its exponent is unidentifiable. No training or selection of the best sign on evaluation rows. Source-code checks and proof-only routes are outside the discovery test.
