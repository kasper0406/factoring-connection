## 0. Verdict

**No connection in these ten fields survives as a route to faster factoring.** We examined 79 concrete connections
(70 ours, 9 rows added from the GPT-6.1-sol blind round; another 22 of its 31 items folded into our rows). Every one fails at a step we can name. None is OPEN-WORTH-A-LOOK
as an *algorithmic* route: nothing here plausibly beats MNFS L[1/3, 1.902], the rigorous L[2/5], or the deterministic
N^{1/5}. Two questions survive as *barrier-side* problems (one of them now mostly answered by a sketch), and one as a
constant-level engineering question (§13).
This is a survey verdict about the constructions we could formulate. It is not a lower bound.

**Why these fields fail: five recurring mechanisms.** All are elementary, and most are implicit in earlier project notes.
Stating them once explains about 80% of the tables.

- **(P1) Monte Carlo precision floor.** Physical and probabilistic quantities (mixing times, return probabilities,
  densities of solutions mod N, Weyl-law terms, free energies per site, eigenvalue statistics) usually depend on the
  factors only through *relative corrections of size 1/p*. Examples: ψ(N) = N(1 + 1/p)(1 + 1/q);
  |SL₂(Z/N)| = N³(1 − p⁻²)(1 − q⁻²); the primitive-character count. Estimating a mean from s samples has error s^{−1/2},
  so seeing the correction needs s ≳ p² ≈ N samples. That is worse than trial division. The only statistics with O(1)-size
  factor dependence are events that already split N, or Q-type residuosity events (factorbits PT and PT-m, leakmap).
  *(Elementary: Chernoff/CLT plus the standard coupling lower bound for distinguishing two distributions at TV distance
  δ with ≪ δ⁻² samples.)*
- **(P2) Smoothing hides the jump.** Analytic methods (Voronoi, Poisson, theta and heat kernels, circle method) compute
  *smoothed* divisor counts quickly. The divisor information at N is the jump d(N) or σ(N), and it lives in the
  de-smoothing window. The sharpest current example is Li's N^{9/28+o(1)} algorithm for the divisor summatory
  function D(N) (arXiv:2609.29949, Sept 2026). Its smoothing correction needs d(n) for every |n − N| ≤ H = N^{9/28},
  *including n = N itself*, which it obtains by factoring those integers with Lenstra–Pomerance. For factoring this is
  circular. The project already records this (`analytic` row "Voronoi (Li 9/28)"; Prop F / barrier F in `floorsum`).
- **(P3) CRT tensor structure.** Every operator built from N with additive or multiplicative Z/N symmetry lies under CRT in the
  algebra generated by A_p ⊗ I and I ⊗ A_q, so it is diagonalised by a tensor-product basis (e.g. the cycle Laplacian
  S_p⊗S_q + S_p⁻¹⊗S_q⁻¹; it is not itself a single A_p ⊗ A_q). Examples: quantum cat maps, Weil representations, translation-invariant transfer matrices, the
  DFT, Kloosterman and Salié sums. The factorisation *is* that decomposition, and finding it from the compressed
  description means finding the CRT isomorphism. The spectra split over Q along the Galois orbits of Z/N, which are
  indexed by divisors (x^N − 1 = ∏_{d|N} Φ_d), so splitting them is equivalent to factoring. Eigenvector entries need
  characters of hidden-order groups (periodfind Theorem T, family M). Single traces are Gauss sums (AGGREGATE,
  newfamily rows 19, 20, 32).
- **(P4) Dynamics dichotomy (Ritt–Milnor).** A rational map of P¹ has structured periodic-point counts at every prime
  iff it is *exceptional*: a power map, a Chebyshev map, or a Lattès map. Exactly these give p − 1, Williams' p + 1 and
  ECM (Montgomery's x-only arithmetic is Lattès arithmetic). The closest theorems are Schur's conjecture (Fried 1970:
  integer polynomials permuting Z/p for infinitely many p are compositions of linear, power and Dickson maps) and
  Guralnick–Müller–Saxl (Mem. AMS 2003) for rational functions, whose list adds Rédei and Lattès maps. Every other map behaves like a random map, and the only
  known use is a birthday collision (Pollard rho, √p). Markoff-surface dynamics, with Chen's 2024 divisibility theorem,
  also lands in M (D3). *(The ingredients are KNOWN: Ritt 1923; Milnor, "On Lattès maps", 2006; Fried 1970; Guralnick–Müller–Saxl 2003. The
  reading "only the exceptional maps give group-order methods" is a heuristic organising remark. Maps with structure only at some primes
  are not excluded.)*
- **(P5) Smooth dependence on N is blind; arithmetic dependence comes through e(a/N).** Physical models whose
  N-dependence is analytic (matrix size N, 't Hooft/topological-recursion expansions in 1/N, chains of length N in the
  thermodynamic limit) cannot see divisibility except through boundary terms. Arithmetic enters only through the roots
  of unity e(a/N), and then the Galois group (Z/N)^* ≅ (Z/p)^* × (Z/q)^* acts. Each concrete evaluation is either a
  Gauss-sum aggregate, a class-E continued-fraction cocycle (quantum modular forms; newfamily rows 33–35), or needs all
  φ(N) conjugates (FACT/EXP).

**Surprising or new to the project** (none changes the verdict):
- Chen's theorem that every connected component of the Markoff graph mod p has size divisible by p (Ann. Math. 199
  (2024)) is a striking divisibility statement about a non-group dynamical system. Over Z/N, however, the point count
  is a Prop MF′-type FACT quantity, and single Markoff rotations have orders dividing p − 1, p + 1, p or 2p (family M) (D3).
- The quantum cat map's Hecke eigenfunctions on L²(Z/N) can be chosen as CRT tensor products ψ_p ⊗ ψ_q (Kurlberg–Rudnick). Any zero
  or level-set coincidence of one would give a gcd split. Building one needs characters of the centraliser torus, whose
  order is ≈ (p ± 1)(q ± 1) (M), plus length-N sums (EXP) (S2).
- Good–Thomas FFT: DFT_N is permutation-equivalent to DFT_p ⊗ DFT_q, and the permutation *is* the CRT map. The DFT's
  own eigenvalue multiplicities depend only on N mod 4 (E5).
- Knauf's number-theoretic spin chain (partition function ζ(s − 1)/ζ(s); φ(n) is the degeneracy of energy log n) is a genuine
  stat-mech encoding of φ. Isolating the n = N weight needs ≳ N configurations (M5).
- Li 2026 (P2): the first analytic algorithm beating N^{1/3} for D(N). It is circular for factoring, as the project
  already noted.
- Prop XC (SKETCH, from the Opus critic): the bit-multiplication polytope has extension complexity 2^{Ω(√n)}, because
  a face projects onto a cut polytope. It is the project's first LP-formulation statement. It says nothing about
  algorithms.

**Ranked shortlist (§13).** There is no algorithmic OPEN-WORTH-A-LOOK item. The barrier-side and constant-level items are:
1. *(barrier)* a Walsh-degree-Θ(n) floor for the hidden sign ε (H9): extending leakmap Theorem AC0-ε from degree
   ≍ n^{1/4} to degree cn. Under GRH this needs a uniform power saving X^{−c′} with c′ > H(c); the bilinear structure is
   already in Bourgain (J. Anal. Math. 2013, §2), so the open part is the size of the saving;
2. *(barrier, now mostly answered)* extension complexity of the bit-multiplication polytope (C2). The critic's sketch
   (Prop XC, §7) gives 2^{Ω(√n)} via a face/projection onto a cut polytope. Open: 2^{Ω(n)}, and the integer-coordinate
   version;
3. *(constant only)* cx's "exact sparse parity solver informed by percolation / peeling" for the NFS matrix step
   (Pr10). Even ω < 2 moves only MNFS 1.902 → 1.8595 (suffmap C5).

Subjective probability that any item in this note leads to an asymptotic improvement of a project benchmark: < 1%.

**Prop XC (SKETCH; from the Opus critic; not independently checked in full by us; no prior statement found).**
xc(P_n) ≥ 2^{Ω(√n)}.
*Sketch.* Take m ≈ √n. Setting every x-bit outside positions {0, …, m − 1} and every y-bit outside {0, m, 2m, …,
(m − 1)m} to 0 is a face, because 0 ≤ bit ≤ 1 are valid inequalities. On this face xy = Σ b_i c_j 2^{i+jm}, and the
positions i + jm are distinct, so there are no carries and bit_{i+jm}(xy) = b_i c_j. Projecting onto (b, c, (b_i c_j))
gives the Boolean quadric polytope BQP(K_{m,m}), which is linearly isomorphic to CUT(K_{1,m,m}) (suspension).
Contracting a perfect matching of K_{m,m} leaves a K_m minor. By Avis–Tiwary, extension complexity of cut polytopes is
monotone under minors (UNVERIFIED by us), and Fiorini et al. give xc(CUT(K_m)) = 2^{Ω(m)}. Faces and projections cannot
increase xc. ∎
*Open:* 2^{Ω(n)}; and the integer-coordinate polytope conv{(x, y, xy)}-type encodings, where the x-bit face trick is not
available. Neither bears on algorithms.
