# comp: the compensation lemma for LP_exc^rel (2026-11-20)

**Target** (from notes/dd_results.md). Take reduced linked modules with H = O(x/T) whose conditioning loss is
λ ≍ m log m at u ≍ D embeddings. Show that one of the two failure modes is forced:
- either Q ≥ c_k x/T (every capped candidate is confined to ≤ D subspaces);
- or capped candidates have negligible independent parity rank.

## Verdict

**Partial, and real progress.** The lemma is proved for two large classes, and for their union via a merged lemma.
No construction gives independent small-norm yield (55/55 adversarial boxes have N_ind = 0). One precisely stated
regime remains.

## Proved (after cross-check)

1. **Split-pole compensation theorem (C; verified by G).**
   - Setting: k = Q(√2), poles R_j splitting over Z with integer remainders c_j = P mod R_j, at the stated balance.
   - Bound: Q ≥ (m−6)x/n − 2nm log m − pH/(n−d_*) − O(Dm).
   - Consequence: for degree constant κ < √2 (n = κ√(x/log x)) this gives Q ≥ (2(1−κ²/2) − o(1))·x/T. That holds even
     with Θ(D) loss embeddings, and the proof removes the H = O(x/T) assumption.
   - Ingredients:
     - Exact pole norms, |N w_{j+2}| = |c_j² − 2|^{n−d_j}, so at most one pole is near any root.
     - A cross-side product of root distances: distinct integer poles give a factorial gain, which captures the linked
       resultant |N Res| ≥ N.
   - For κ ≥ √2 it is vacuous, because 1-D integer packing recovers only half of the e = 2 Cramer loss.
   - Extends to collinear imaginary poles T² + r_j² (G). Gaussian poles need κ < √(2/3).
   - Explains both dd constructions: C's quadratic-pole family, where every capped row is scalar, and G's annular
     family, where Q is inflated.
2. **Submodule-KH compensation (G; repaired by C).** For any invertible ternary B, apply KH to the monomials in Bw
   inside the ORIGINAL ideal lattice. That gives Q ≥ (x − D log C_kD)/T − Σ_i log(M_{B,i}/t_i). So one basis within
   e^{O(m)} of threshold on D − o(D) embeddings suffices, with Cramer charging the exceptions o(x/T).
3. **Merged partial-rank lemma (C + G).** Suppose a reduced rank-r subspace with r ≥ δm is generated by ternary rows
   near threshold (Σ log^+ excess = o(x/T)) on D − o(D) embeddings. Then Q ≥ (δ/2 − o(1))·x/T, for all κ.
   - C's combined form: if Bw contains a scalar multiple of a rank-r split-pole tuple with r/m → θ, and B is near
     threshold except on fD tags, then Q ≥ (3θ/2 − f − o(1))·mx/n.
4. **Two-place covolume and support width (G; correct within scope).**
   - Covolume: τ₁τ₂ ≥ (C_k m)^{−2} for the same m independent vectors at both base places. Thresholds attained
     independently are not covered.
   - Support width: a loss λ ≥ γ m log m needs ≥ (γ/e − o(1))m large coordinates.
5. **General-pole divided-difference identity (G/C).** w_{2+j}(α_i) = f_σ[α_i, β_{j,1..d_j}] holds exactly. G's claimed
   dominance ("ρ-inflation for all poles") is **false**: C's counterexample is R = T(T−1), P = T^n + KT.

## Refuted or corrected

- **The lemma's "non-scalar" wording.** Exact non-scalar units of norm 1 exist: in the 112-bit M2 box, 35 non-scalar
  capped orbits, three of norm one. The second branch must read "outside proper k-subspaces and lower-norm products".
- **G's asymptotic loss claims for its twisted families M3 and M4 are unproved.** The exceptional count is
  O(m + log b), and log b ≍ n log x is not o(D). The M4 amplification is only (1+√2)^{2m−1}. The threshold branch for
  admissible M3/M4 twists nevertheless follows from the merged lemma.

## The single remaining statement

Merging both reviewers' formulations: at the L[1/4] balance, take a reduced linked family with:
- H = O(x/T);
- loss Ω(m log m) on Ω(D) embeddings;
- **no** reduced subspace of rank ≥ δm generated by ternary rows near threshold on D − o(D) embeddings, for any fixed
  δ;
- at least one eligible non-scalar row with 0 < |F|/J ≤ Y, log Y ≍ √(x log x).

Prove Q ≥ c_k x/T, or prove the parity rank of such rows is B^{o(1)}.

For divided-difference modules with p = O(m), only these remain open: non-collinear complex poles, nonconstant
remainders, and κ ≥ √2, each in the unshared-basis regime. Intermediate configurations, such as two large groups of
embeddings each using a different near-threshold basis, are explicitly included (C).
