# Wheel comparison, recorded before implementation or trials

**Design.** Use odd wheel primes (no supplementary mod-8 sign) through 37, 67, 97 and 131. These are 42, 82, 120 and 168 bits and have k = 11, 18, 24, 31 signs. Each size has 32 independent paired trials, Python Random seed 20261126. One fresh uniform k-bit target per trial is shared between the consecutive wheel P and its control R. Draw R by starting at P and making 100 proposed single-prime replacements, uniformly selecting a member and a prime among the first 3k odd primes, accepting exact-bit-length distinct-prime sets. Redraw a walk ending at P. This defines the distribution and deliberately preserves rank. Also test a bit-length-only control S with ceil(k/2) distinct odd primes drawn uniformly without replacement in the interval [0.5 g,2 g], g = 2^((bits-0.5)/ceil(k/2)), rejecting until the product has the same bit length. Use the corresponding low bits of the target. Record overlaps.

**Algorithms and budget.** Single process, fresh per-query data structures, no cross-target tables. Charge wheel-dependent setup. Shared generic prime generation, imports, compilation, target generation, output checks and logging excluded. Record process CPU time. For exhaustive integer search, enumerate in ascending order, with optional wheel filtering, to first hit or 2^22 inclusive. For exponent decoding use the first 2k+8 primes outside the wheel, a particular binary solution and the full-rank integer parity kernel scaled by rounded 32 log2(q) weights. Use fpylll LLL (delta .99) and Babai with up to 12 coordinate-order/basis variations, retaining the smallest verified support product. Reduce exponents mod 2. For a third method scan both numerator and denominator signatures (XOR table), stopping at the first rational height admitting a match, or 2^19 inclusive. Return both the fraction and squarefree kernel of its product. Record the exhaustive result as h/1; for LLL additionally split its best support between numerator and denominator by descending-prime greedy balancing. No cross-method seeding. Use a common CPU ceiling of 0.25 s per trial-method (poll in blocks or at completed reductions, retaining completed outputs). Record bound reached and censoring. Use integer and rational heights separately. All reported outputs must independently pass modular exponentiation.

**Analysis.** Report conditional and/or restricted log2(height)/log2(W) by size and method, CPU time, hit counts and censoring explicitly. A capped exact search with no hit gives a lower bound using its completed prefix. For uncensored heights use paired trial bootstrap of means with 5000 replicates and percentile 95% intervals, including paired P-minus-R differences. For censored integer minima report means at a common achieved truncation (if necessary) and label as such. Compare LLL and XOR integer outputs also as best verified integer by the deadline. Primary gap test is mean exact rational minimum exponent for P minus R at 168 bits, clipped at a common completed height if needed. Call a material P advantage only if the entire paired interval is below -0.02. Secondary cross-size and algorithm comparisons are descriptive. Hypotheses: no material P advantage at equal rank; the fewer-sign control has shorter representatives; LLL finds valid products but loses on integer height at large k relative to signature pairing. These thresholds concern this design only. Report implementation deviations and avoid extrapolating fitted exponents to factoring.
