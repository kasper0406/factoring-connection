# leanA: a conditional Lean 4 formalisation of Theorem A / RA / P

Agent `leanA`, 2026-10-20.
- Lake project: `leanA/`, on Lean v4.35.0 and Mathlib v4.35.0. Build and check instructions are in `leanA/README.md`.
- Trusted statement layer: `leanA/Challenge.lean`, with the definition modules `leanA/LeanA/{Defs,DefsNFS,Hypotheses}.lean`.
- Source of truth: `paper2/main.tex`, cited by `\label` name.

## Verdict

**Proved conditionally:** given four published theorems, the project's two moment theorems (FM and LD\*), and an explicit list of the paper's algorithmic claims, Theorem A's cost bound follows in Lean, and so do the cost bounds of Theorem RA and Corollary P.
- **Theorem A:** L_N[1/2, (1+θ)/√2 + o(1)] for every admissible tuple. For every c > 1/√2 there is a tuple with a bound of O(L[1/2, c]).
- **Theorem RA:** L[1/2, √((1+√2)θ) + o(1)].
- **Corollary P:** L[1/2, o(1)].
- **Exponent and constant arithmetic:** proved with no hypotheses. This covers "every c > 1/√2", the necessary and sufficient threshold k ≥ 13 at d = 9, √((1+√2)θ), c_DL and the 2/5 floor arithmetic.

**Proved outright from the published inputs:** the probabilistic and combinatorial cores, namely Theorem DD, Theorems S, S′ and K, Paley–Zygmund / Theorem D_k, reverse Markov, Lemma Kern-U, Lemma Kern, Lemma W, Lemma GB, the forced residue, Lemma F/F_α, and Lemmas E1/E2.
- **The relation supply is derived, not assumed.** From FM, LD\*, CEP and Rosser–Schoenfeld, Lean proves γ ≥ L^{−o(1)} for the concrete filtered count N_ℓ(A) under the concrete field law. It also proves that a non-rejected raw field followed by a uniform root gives a complete descent with probability L^{−o(1)}.

**The checks.**
- Everything was checked in "paranoid mode". The proof layer has no `sorry` and no `axiom`. `#print axioms` gives only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` for all 64 MAIN theorems.
- `lake comparator --paranoid` matched all 64 statements against `Challenge.lean`. The run was sandboxed with bubblewrap inside a Linux container.
- Six kernels accept the solution, each replaying 58,515 declarations: Lean's default kernel, Lean's paranoid kernel, lean4lean, Nanoda, con-leche and con-ron.

**What the formalisation does not establish.**
- The algorithmic layer is a *numerical cost-accounting model*. Its interface inequalities are the paper's own claims about its algorithm (Lemma reject, Prop. select(ii), Prop. BS, Prop. lv6, the factor-tree accounting, and Theorem RA's recursion). Nothing ties them to a program, and correctness and the Las Vegas property are not modelled.
- FM and LD\* themselves are not proved. They contain the deep analytic content: one-point smoothing, Lemma 8.5′ via Drappeau's Lemme 5, divisor switching and L2_fam.
- Theorems U and DL are formalised only as assembly steps.
- So the formalisation raises confidence in the glue, the constants, the combinatorial and probabilistic lemmas, and the claim that γ ≥ L^{−o(1)} follows from the two moment bounds. **It does not certify Theorem A.**

One serious bug was caught by review. The first transcription of the published input `CEP` was refutable in Lean, which made every theorem that uses it vacuous. It is fixed, and both refutation families are excluded.

## 1. Design

**Pattern.** The design follows openai/PrimeGaps186 and openai/math ComparatorChallenges.
- `Challenge.lean` holds the statements with `sorry`. It is generated by `leanA/scripts/gen_challenge.py`.
- The solution module is `LeanA`. Comparator compares the two.
- All inputs are `Prop`s passed as theorem hypotheses, so there are **no `axiom` declarations**.

**Copying versus importing the definitions.** PrimeGaps186 copies its definitions into the challenge file. We could not: Lean abstracts proofs inside `def` bodies into auxiliary lemmas. For example, the `Nat.AtLeastTwo` proof in a literal `(2 : ℝ)` becomes a lemma whose name depends on the module layout (`Params.cA._proof_1` versus `Params.Lv._proof_1`). Comparator then reported "Const does not match" on an otherwise identical definition. So `Challenge.lean` *imports* the three trusted definition modules.

**Toolchain.** The project uses Lean 4.35.0, the version of Comparator HEAD, where Comparator is merged into core as `lake comparator`. Its toolchain bundles nanoda_bin, lean4lean, con-leche, con-ron and leanchecker-paranoid. openai/math uses 4.34.1 and PrimeGaps186 uses 4.34.0-rc2.

**Trusted base.** It consists of `Challenge.lean`, `Defs.lean` (212 lines), `DefsNFS.lean` (140 lines), `Hypotheses.lean` (about 260 lines) and Mathlib. There are 13 proof files, about 6,600 lines in all.

Sandbox notes.
- Docker Desktop was started headless through its `com.docker.backend` binary, because the GUI launch failed over SSH.
- bubblewrap needs `--security-opt seccomp=unconfined`, `apparmor=unconfined` and `systempaths=unconfined` on the container, plus a pre-created `/run/user`. These options relax only Docker's own confinement; the judged code runs inside bubblewrap with an empty network namespace.

| reviewer | scope | verdict |

**Fixed.**
1. **CEP (FATAL; three independent reviewers, two with Lean proofs of ¬CEP).** The original `∀ x u` with no lower bound on x was refutable. For negative x, `Real.log` is defined and Ψ = 0. For x ∈ (0, 1) or x just above e, log log x ≈ 0. So every theorem that used CEP was vacuous. It now requires `16 ≤ x`, and both second-round reviewers' refutation attempts failed.
2. **Silent ℕ-typing.** In the Prop. lv6 interface field, the ε in `(B : ℝ) ^ (1 + ε)` had defaulted to ℕ. It is now real. The second-round elaboration audits found no further case.
3. **DL hypothesis.** It omitted the batch-smoothness term a. Fixed: `max a (max (1/√2) (θ/(2a)))` and a K (log N)^K slack.
4. **`corollaryA`** assumed the hypotheses for every tuple. It now assumes them for one tuple, and `corollaryA_forall` was added.
5. **Strengthened statements.** `cRA_vs_cA` is now an iff; `restart_series` now includes summability. `muOpt_mem` and `d9_k_ge_13` were added.
6. **Labels.** The cost interfaces are now labelled INTERNAL HYPOTHESIS, with the paper claims each one carries. U and DL are labelled assembly only; the E1/E2 lemmas are labelled standalone. The Lenstra–Pomerance citation was corrected (the unnumbered Theorem of §1), and RS is now cited by equations (3.6) and (3.8). Yj's small-N convention is documented.

**Accepted as scope, documented, not fixed** (codex leanA_1 findings #2–#6, critic 3 (g)).
- The cost models are records of real functions. They admit trivial models: all costs zero, or all equal to 1. They identify no program, and they do not express correctness, termination or Las Vegas behaviour.
- Prop. select(ii) (including Theorem DD's per-round success), Prop. lv6, Prop. BS, the factor-tree accounting, Lemmas OS, Wald and T_rec, and the restart schedule (`hport`) enter as interface hypotheses.
- Hence `theoremA`, `theoremRA` and `corollaryP` are **conditional cost-assembly theorems**. The nontrivial certified content is this: given FM and LD\*, the trial-success probability is ≥ L^{−o(1)} for the *concrete* descent event (via `descent_supply`), and all the exponent and uniformity bookkeeping is correct.
- Formalising the algorithm semantics (NFS post-processing, Lenstra–Pomerance as a program) is far outside this scope.

**Not changed** (MINOR, judged harmless).
- Some hypotheses go unused:
  - `hμ` in `theta_ge_thetaMin`;
  - the dyadic Z and the window lower bound in `split_counting_kprod`;
  - `ha`, `ha'` in `kern_prob`;
  - `hℓ` in `pairs`;
  - `hσ` in `theoremU_assembly`;
  - `hC0` in `theoremDL_assembly`.
- These are natural parameter ranges. Their being unused means the Lean statements are slightly more general than the paper's, not less faithful.

## 5. Prior-art setup notes

- openai/PrimeGaps186 copies definitions into `Challenge.lean` and uses three `axiom`s. We pass hypotheses as `Prop`s instead.
- For multi-module solutions on Lean 4.35, definitions must be imported, not copied (§1). This is worth knowing for any future Comparator setup in this project.
- openai/math's `AlgorithmicThinTrees` uses an explicit stack-machine model for a polynomial-time claim. A comparable machine model for the NFS-type algorithm here would be the way to remove the cost-model scope limitation. It is a much larger project.
