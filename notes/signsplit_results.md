# signsplit: CRT sign-splitting in Harvey–Hittmeir search

Agent `signsplit`, 2026-10-18. The pre-registration is `notes/signsplit_prereg.md`, committed
in `a31a84f` before any experiment. Code is in `src/signsplit/`. Tests are in
`tests/test_signsplit.py` (12 pass).

## 0. Verdict

**The identity is true, but the speedup does not exist.** CRT sign-splitting gives no gain over
Harvey–Hittmeir 2022 (HH): not N^{1/5}·exp(−Ω(log N/log log N)), and not even a constant factor.

- **The identity holds** (proved, and checked exhaustively with 0 failures). For fixed HH weights
  (a,b), the residue offsets of the 2^t branches sharing one square class σ² form a signed subset
  sum mod M², exactly as the user said.
- **The meet-in-the-middle fights HH's main trick.** HH reach N^{1/5} by checking all branches
  against one shared list of "baby steps", so each branch costs about one list element. The MITM
  over the signs needs a second list that depends on the weight pair (a,b). That list cannot be
  shared, so the search becomes one small BSGS per group, and that is Hittmeir's older N^{1/4}
  regime. Measured: the exponent rises to 0.251 against HH's 0.196, and the algorithm is 10×
  slower at 64 bits.
- **The only way to keep a shared baby list and still let one giant step cover both signs** is to
  force the weights to make the offset sign-independent. That shrinks the weight lattice by m² per
  prime, which inflates the coefficients by m. Per prime this loses to HH's own lock by a factor
  m^{2/5}/2^{3/5} (1.02 at m = 3, 1.26 at m = 5). Measured: 1–2% worse than HH at best,
  129× worse when applied to all primes.
- **Even if the obstruction were free, the gain would be 2^{t/5}, not 2^{t/2}.** HH's balance
  converts a giant-step saving s into s^{1/5}. And the obstruction is not free.
- **What does survive is minor.** HH's lattice step already depends only on σ², so the 2^t sign
  branches can share one lattice reduction and be generated by a Gray code.
  - It cuts lattice reductions by 2^t, and ring multiplications by 1.3–2.6× against a *fair*
    fixed-base baseline (3–21× against naive square-and-multiply).
  - It leaves the list sizes and the number of Stage-3 leaves unchanged, and collision detection
    is HH's stated bottleneck (Remark 4.5).
  - It is a Stage-2, polylog-constant tweak, not a new bound (§6).
- **The prime 2 is the one exception.** The user's mod-64 identity holds for odd a: the extra
  2-adic root r ↦ r + 4 shifts the offset by a universal ±16. That can be shared, but is worth
  only about 3% after re-balancing, with a cap of 2^{1/5} (§6.2).
- **Prior art covers the ingredients.** The ±-symmetry, the CRT product structure and
  meet-in-the-middle over CRT components of a residue sieve are all in Hittmeir (2018, 2023).
  Neither HH nor any later paper we read contains sign-sharing inside the 1/5 search, and the
  accounting says why: it does not pay.

**Answer to the orchestrator (guess-neutrality / several moduli).**
- Sign-splitting does **not** break guess-neutrality in the calibrated HH model. Its per-prime
  merit is strictly worse than HH's ((m−1)/m)^{3/5} for every odd prime m (§3.4).
- It is **not** a "several moduli" construction. It uses one modulus M and only regroups the
  φ(M) classes by σ².
- The cycloharvey lower bound does not decide it. That bound charges ≥ 1 per (class, vector)
  target, and the sign-split tries to undercut exactly that. Its slack O(1)^{ω(M)} is where a
  2^t-type gain would live. So the refutation here comes from our own finer model (§3.3–3.5) plus
  the experiment, not from that lower bound.

## 1. The identity (all proved; exhaustively verified)

Notation follows HH Prop. 3.3: weights act on aq + bp, and p ≡ σ (mod M). The user writes the same
thing as α^{ap+bq} with a r² ≡ bN, which is (a,b) swapped. Let M be squarefree with gcd(M, N) = 1.
The **lock** is bσ² ≡ aN (mod M), equivalently b ≡ γa (mod M) with γ = N/σ².

**Lemma 2 (value lemma, proved).** Assume the lock. Let x, y be any integers with xy ≡ N (mod M²)
and x ≡ σ (mod M). Then
ay + bx ≡ τ(σ) := aNσ^{−1} + bσ (mod M²).

*Proof.*
- Write x = σ + Mt. Then y ≡ Nx^{−1} ≡ Nσ^{−1}(1 − Mtσ^{−1}) (mod M²).
- Hence ay + bx ≡ aNσ^{−1} + bσ + Mtσ^{−2}(bσ² − aN) (mod M²).
- The last term is 0 mod M², because M | bσ² − aN. ∎

**Lemma 1 (representative independence, proved).** τ(σ) depends only on σ mod M. This is Lemma 2
with x = σ′ for any other representative σ′. In particular, aq + bp ≡ τ(p mod M) for every
factorisation N = pq.

**Lemma 3 (CRT locality, proved).** For every prime m_i | M, τ(σ) mod m_i² equals τ_i(σ mod m_i),
computed with modulus m_i. This is Lemma 2 at modulus m_i, where the lock holds because m_i | M.

**Lemma 4 (sign flip, proved).** τ_i(−σ) ≡ −τ_i(σ) (mod m_i²). The lock depends only on σ², and
τ is odd in σ.

**Theorem 5 (signed subset sum, proved).**
- Fix a square class. For each odd prime m_i | M, let r_i be one root of σ² ≡ N/γ (mod m_i). The
  class consists of the 2^t units σ(ε) = CRT(ε_i r_i), plus the single residue at m = 2.
- Let E_i be the CRT idempotents mod M², and set Δ_i = E_i τ_i(r_i) ∈ [0, M²) and
  Δ_0 = E_2 τ_2.
- Then τ(σ(ε)) ≡ Δ_0 + Σ_i ε_i Δ_i (mod M²) for all ε. As integers,
  τ(σ(ε)) = Δ_0 + Σ ε_iΔ_i − M²c(ε), where the **carry** c(ε) lies in
  [⌊(Δ_0 − ΣΔ_i)/M²⌋, ⌊(Δ_0 + ΣΔ_i)/M²⌋] ⊆ [−t, t].
- The carry is not separable over a split ε = (ε_L, ε_R).
- *Proof:* CRT plus Lemmas 3 and 4. ∎

**One weight pair per square class (proved).** HH's lattice L = ⟨(1, γ), (0, M)⟩ depends on σ only
through γ = N/σ². So all 2^t classes σ(ε) get the same Prop. 3.3 vector (a,b) and the same
Archimedean window. Only the offset τ(σ(ε)) differs.

**Exhaustive verification** (`src/signsplit/verify_identity.py`, record in
`experiments/raw/signsplit_identity.jsonl`).
- Range: all odd N < 2001; M ∈ {3, 5, 7, 15, 21, 35, 105, 6, 10, 30, 42, 210, 143, 1155} coprime
  to N; every unit σ; 14 lock-satisfying weight pairs per σ.
- Lemma 2 over every unit x in the class mod M² (factor-free, so it covers every factorisation):
  4.26·10⁸ checks.
- Lemma 1: 2.1·10⁷ checks. Lemma 3: 1.6·10⁷. Lemma 4: 5.2·10⁶.
- Theorem 5 and the carry range: 2.4·10⁶ checks.
- Real semiprimes (aq + bp ≡ τ): 43 628 checks.
- **0 failures.**
- Negative control: with the lock violated, aq + bp mod M² varies within the class in 39 074 of
  39 074 cases.

**Status:**
- No prior formulation of sign-sharing *inside the shared-baby-list 1/5 search* was found by us.
  The ingredients (±-symmetry, CRT product sieve, MITM over CRT blocks) are in Hittmeir
  2018/2023.
- **UNVERIFIED:** Lehman 1974, McKee–Pinch 1996 and Costa–Harvey 2014 were not read. Hittmeir 2021
  was text-searched only.

**The crux: why Hittmeir 2018's saving does not transfer to 1/5.**
- *Hittmeir 2018 (1/4).* There is one target, one BSGS, and the baby list *is* the sieved residue
  set. A 2^{−t} sieve becomes a 2^{−t/2} saving by √-balancing. Nothing is shared across targets.
- *Harvey/HH (1/5).* The point is to share **one** baby list across T ≈ N^{1/5} targets, each paying
  about one giant. Residue information is already used maximally per target: given σ, aq + bp is
  known exactly mod M² (one residue, not 2^t). It is paid for by enumerating σ, and that is cheap
  precisely because the baby list is shared.
- *Regrouping the σ by σ²* gives a group whose candidate set has 2^t residues mod M². This is a
  Hittmeir-type sieved set, but **its residues depend on the group's (a,b)**: Δ_i = E_i(a Nr_i^{−1}
  + b r_i).
- *The consequence.* Exploiting the sieve by √-balancing needs a per-group baby list, which is
  Hittmeir's per-target BSGS at exponent 1/4. Keeping the shared list instead needs the groups'
  residue sets to coincide, which costs lattice index (§3.4).
- In short: Hittmeir's saving lives in the per-target √ of BSGS. HH's 1/5 deliberately gives up the
  per-target √ in exchange for sharing, so there is no per-target √ left to improve.

## 3. Cost accounting (calibrated HH model)

### 3.1 Calibration

- The baseline V0 is HH Alg. 4.3 restricted to the balanced range p ∈ [√(N/2), √N]. Its exact
  giant and baby counts agree with cycloharvey's calibrated `cost_hh` in **240/240** (N, M, m_0)
  cases (test `test_v0_counts_match_cycloharvey_calibrated_hh`).
- cycloharvey's gate reproduces the exponents 1/4, 1/3, 2/9 and 1/5, with an HH fitted slope of
  0.1997.
- On our instances the fitted V0 slope is 0.196 (upper half, 48–64 bits; best over M ∈ {6, 30,
  210, 2310}).
- Cost model: work = giants + babies. HH's bound weights giants by lg⁴ and babies by lg², and both
  are linear in the counts.

### 3.2 Component-by-component (proved unless marked)

- **Coverage of residues by (a,b) pairs.** Each Archimedean window (σ_0 bucket) needs exactly one
  pair per square class: φ(M)/2^t pairs (t = number of odd primes of M), where HH use φ(M) calls.
  The pair automatically has N/γ a QR mod every m_i, since γ = N/σ². The 2^t classes in the group
  share the pair and the window.
- **√M coefficient inflation.** Prop. 3.3 with lattice index I gives |aN/σ_0|, |−aN/σ_0 + bσ_0| ≤
  2N^{1/2}I^{1/2}m_0^{±1/2}, so the window is E = 4N^{1/2}I^{1/2}m_0^{−3/2}.
  - HH lock: I = M, so λ = ⌈E/M²⌉ ∝ M^{−3/2}.
  - Sign-collapse (V3) on a set A: I = M·M_A², so λ × M_A. The measured extra coefficient bits on
    the true-branch vector are 2, 4, 7, 10, against log₂ M_A = 1.6, 3.9, 6.7, 10.2 (experimentally
    supported).
- **Integer carries.** τ(σ(ε)) = Δ_0 + Σε_iΔ_i − M²c(ε), with c in a range of width ≤ 2t + 1, and c
  not separable over (ε_L, ε_R). A split search must absorb the carry into the j-window, which
  widens it from 2λ+1 to 2λ+1+(c_hi − c_lo) ≤ 2λ+2t+1. This is negligible (t ≪ λ) and is implemented
  exactly in V1/V2. So carries break the pure sumset only additively: **not** the obstruction.
- **Separability / cross-difference.**
  - Within a group, the split exponent E = [aN+b − Δ_0 − S_L(ε_L) − M²(base + W_R h)] − [M² i +
    S_R(ε_R)] is separable (left minus right), so a product tree plus multipoint evaluation applies.
  - **Across groups** the right part S_R depends on (a_G, b_G), so the right lists cannot be one
    shared list. This is the obstruction (§3.3).
  - It is the user's cross-difference law in this setting.
- **One-sidedness.** No cost. β^{aN+b} ≡ β^{aq+bp} holds mod p only. Two-sided matches (for example
  a = b, which is symmetric) are caught by sort-and-match plus Lemma 2.4, as in HH Step 12. This is
  observed: some V2 hits are exact matches with a = b.
- **Cost of Δ_i, roots r_i, idempotents.**
  - Square roots mod m_i ≤ B = O(log N): O(B) each.
  - Idempotents E_i mod M²: t inverses, once.
  - Per group, t values τ_i mod m_i² and a Gray-code walk with ≤ 2 ring multiplications per giant
    plus 2t exponentiations of O(log M) bits.
  - Total: polylog per group, negligible.
- **Can one (a,b) serve many windows?**
  - Across the 2^t signs: yes, for free (V1).
  - Across buckets σ_0: this is the m_0 parameter.
  - Across different square classes γ: this needs b ≡ γa and b ≡ γ′a (mod m_i), forcing
    m_i | a, b. That is index m_i², which carries no residue information at that prime, and is
    strictly worse.

### 3.3 Theorem G (per-target BSGS; proved in the model)

**Statement.** Suppose the right lists are group-specific, as in V2. Then the total cost is
≥ Σ_G 2√(|S_G|) = G·2^{t/2+1}√(2λ+1). With G = (φ(M)/2^t)·#buckets and λ ∝ N^{1/2}/(Mm_0)^{3/2},
this is ≍ N^{1/4}·φ(M)M^{−3/4}2^{−t/2}·m_0^{1/4}, minimised at m_0 = 1.

**Consequence: exponent 1/4 for every M.** The constant ∏(m−1)m^{−3/4}2^{−1/2} is minimised at
M = 2·3·5·7 (about 0.31).

**Proof.** A group's candidate set S_G has 2^t(2λ+1) distinct integers. Any pair of lists L, R with
S_G ⊆ L + R has |L||R| ≥ |S_G|. ∎

**Measured:** V2's best-over-menu slope is 0.250 (all sizes) and 0.251 (upper half).

### 3.4 Theorem S (shared baby list; proved in the HH lattice model)

**Model.** A baby set D (union of residue classes mod M², each needing a full window of length
L = 2λ+1) is shared by all groups. Each giant g_G·β^{−c} tests c + D against S_G.

**Statement.**
- (i) A giant covers ≤ L·s_G(c) candidates, with s_G(c) = #{ε : τ_G(ε) − c ∈ R_D}, where R_D is the
  residue set of D. So Σ|C_G| ≥ G·2^t/max s, and |D| ≥ |R_D|·L ≥ max s·L.
- (ii) Re-optimising m_0 (giants ∝ m_0, babies ∝ m_0^{−3/2}, optimum ∝ giants^{3/5}·babies^{2/5}),
  a saving factor s on giants with K = |R_D| baby classes changes the optimum by s^{−3/5}K^{2/5}
  ≥ s^{−1/5}.
  - **Even the idealised, free version gains only 2^{t/5}**, not 2^{t/2}.
  - With t ≍ log N/log log N this idealised gain would be exp(Ω(log N/log log N)), i.e. the
    user's hoped-for form. So the issue is whether the idealisation is achievable.
- (iii) s ≥ 2 for a group requires two of its offsets to differ by an element of R_D − R_D. The
  offsets are τ_G(ε) − τ_G(ε′) = 2Σ_{i∈I} ε_iΔ_i^G, with Δ_i^G = E_i(a_G N r_i^{−1} + b_G r_i).
  - To guarantee this for all groups, the HH vector must satisfy a congruence on (a_G, b_G) mod
    m_i² for the shared primes.
  - Given the lock, V_i = aNr_i^{−1} + br_i takes all m_i² values equally often. Restricting it to
    K_i values multiplies the lattice index by m_i²/K_i, hence λ by (m_i²/K_i)^{1/2} under the
    Minkowski/Lemma 3.1 certificate that HH's proof uses.
- Per-prime merits (factor on the optimal cost, continuous model; `accounting.merit_table`):

| prime m | HH lock (giant ×(m−1), baby ×m^{−3/2}) | sign-collapse V3 (K=1: giant ×(m−1)/2, baby ×m^{−1/2}) | V_i ∈ {±δ} (K=2) | idealised free sign (impossible) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.784 | 0.803 | 0.922 | 0.683 |
| 5 | 0.875 | 1.099 | 1.262 | 0.762 |
| 7 | 0.912 | 1.310 | 1.505 | 0.794 |
| 11 | 0.944 | 1.626 | 1.868 | 0.822 |

- **V3/HH = m^{2/5}/2^{3/5} > 1 for every m ≥ 3.** It is 1.024 at m = 3 and grows like m^{2/5}.
  So the HH lock dominates every sign-sharing option prime by prime, and the best achievable bound
  in this family is HH's own: no gain.

**Status of the parts.**
- (i), (ii) and the per-prime table: **proved** within the model.
- (iii) uses "the cheapest way to force s ≥ 2 for every group is a congruence on (a,b)". This is
  **proved for algorithms that certify windows by Minkowski/Gauss (as HH do)**. A typical-case
  short vector of a sparser coset could be shorter for some σ_0, but correctness needs every σ_0.
  - Typical-case version: **conjectured**, supported by the measured inflation and by V2d below.

**Structure of V3, for intuition.** V3 means m | a, m | b with (a/m)N + (b/m)σ² ≡ 0 (mod m): an
"anti-lock". Then aq + bp ≡ 0 (mod m²) for both signs. In effect the residue at m is known only mod
m (value 0) instead of mod m².

### 3.5 Best achievable bound

**In this family** (HH lattice weights, one-sided β-collisions, separable BSGS / multipoint
detection, single modulus M, any per-prime choice of lock / anti-lock / offset constraint / sign
MITM):
- the optimum is HH's choice, the lock at every prime;
- the cost is O(N^{1/5}lg^{16/5}N/(lg lg N)^{3/5}).

**Statement:** CRT sign-splitting gives **no gain at all** on the exponent, the subexponential
factor, or the leading constant of the list counts. It breaks at the step "MITM over ε in 2^{t/2}":
the right half of the MITM is per-(a,b) and cannot join the shared baby list.

**What remains:** a polylog-constant saving from V1 (shared lattice reduction plus Gray-code
giants).
- Lattice reductions are cut by exactly 2^t, and ring multiplications by 0.23–0.64× (measured).
- List sizes and Alg. 4.1 are unchanged, and Alg. 4.1 is HH's stated bottleneck.
- So this halves at most the constant of HH's lg⁴N term.

Status: proved (counting), experimentally supported. We claim no improvement over HH.

      c_{q−1}/(q−1))), with c_n = Σ_{d\|n}φ(d)/d. It predicts 0.33 / 0.56 / 0.997 for V0 / V3 / V2;
      the observed first-hit accidental rates are 0.19 / 0.27 / 0.67. That is below the null, as it
      must be, since a structural hit also exists in every run.
    - These are toy-size effects: pairs/p ∝ N^{2/5}/N^{1/2} → 0.
  - Also, V2's right lists from different groups coincide as integers: S_R takes few residues
    mod M². Pooled detection then reports another group's label. V2 hits are therefore classified by
    value against all groups.

**Post-hoc V2d (labelled post-hoc).** Deduplicating V2's pooled right lists, which share values
across groups, is the fairest form of the literal claim.
- It improves V2 (64 bits: 5.1× V0 instead of 10×), and its slope drifts toward 1/5.
- Its asymptotic constant is still worse than HH by m^{4/5}/2^{3/5} per R-prime.
- It is the "union of offset patterns" scheme of §3.4.

## 5. Candidate card

- **Mechanism.** In HH Alg. 4.3, group the φ(M) branches by σ² and replace 2^t giants per group by
  a meet-in-the-middle over the sign vector ε of the signed subset sum
  τ(σ(ε)) = Δ_0 + Σε_iΔ_i − M²c(ε).
- **Representation.** (Z/N)^*, HH lattice weights, base β, baby list β^{M²i}.
- **Factor feature.** One-sided collision β^{aN+b−j} ≡ β^{M²i} (mod p).
- **Extraction.** Sort-and-match plus product tree / Bluestein / multipoint and gcd.
- **Outcome:**
  - identity proved;
  - speedup **refuted for this model and implementation**, by the pre-registered rule
    (R(56) = 1.021, R(64) = 1.009);
  - the literal MITM has exponent 1/4 (Thm G);
  - every shared-list sign-sharing loses to the HH lock prime by prime (Thm S);
  - the idealised cap would be 2^{t/5}.
- **Scoped obstruction.** In HH-type search with a shared baby list, a giant can serve s sign
  branches only if the groups' offset cubes share s residues mod M². That forces congruences of
  index ≥ m²/K on the weights. With Minkowski-certified windows this inflates λ by (m²/K)^{1/2}, and
  each such prime then costs ≥ m^{2/5}/2^{3/5} relative to the plain lock.
- **What would escape.**
  - A collision detector that is not a sumset, i.e. can test structured candidate sets in less than
    |L|+|R| for unequal right lists. Not available for equality mod an unknown prime.
  - Non-Minkowski certificates showing that sparse cosets of the weight lattice have short vectors
    for every σ_0.
  - Several moduli: not covered here, and not what sign-splitting is.
- **Positive by-product (minor).** Share Prop. 3.3 across the 2^t classes of a square class, and
  build giants by Gray code. This is a constant factor in the lg⁴ term.

### 6.1 Fair baseline (experimentally supported)

**What the optimised versions do.**
- V0-fixed computes each giant by fixed-base windowed exponentiation. Tables β^{±d·2^{wk}} are
  shared, w is chosen to minimise total cost, and precomputation is counted. A giant then costs
  about ⌈bits/w⌉ multiplications instead of about 1.5·bits.
- An HH branch has a *single* giant, so there is no arithmetic progression to walk with one
  multiplication per point. Fixed-base tables are the fair "shared exponent ladder".
- V1-fixed uses one fixed-base exponentiation per square-class group, then a Gray-code walk.
  - Each step costs 1 multiplication, plus 1 when the carry changes.
  - The steps come from **global** tables β^{±2(M/m_i)²k}, k < m_i². This uses
    Δ_i = (M/m_i)²·k_i, which we confirm: the code asserts it on every group, as in the user's
    "CRT fiber power tables".

Median ring multiplications (`rn_mul`; this includes the baby list and Bluestein pre/post
multiplications, but not product-tree coefficient arithmetic), on the same 100 instances at E1

### 6.2 The user's mod-64 identity (proved; exhaustively verified, with a correction)

**Claim checked.** Let 8 | M and k ≥ 3. For odd r, r² ≡ 1 (mod 8), so the four roots
{±r, ±r + 2^{k−1}} of one 2-adic square class share the HH lattice. With the lock
bσ² ≡ aN (mod 2^k),

τ(r + 2^{k−1}) − τ(r) ≡ 2^{2k−2}(aNr^{−3} + 2u) (mod 2^{2k}), where u = (b − aNr^{−2})/2^k.

- *Proof.* Expand (r + h)^{−1} = r^{−1} − hr^{−2} + h²r^{−3} − …, with h = 2^{k−1}. The h³ term
  vanishes mod 2^{2k} for k ≥ 3. ∎
- **Corrected statement.**
  - If a is odd, the shift is ±2^{2k−2}: **±16 mod 64** at k = 3, as the user said.
  - **If a is even, the shift is 0 or 2^{2k−1}** (0 or 32 mod 64), not ±16.
- Exhaustive check (`verify_two_adic`): k = 3..6, odd N < 400, |a| ≤ 9, three lifts of b, all odd
  r. Odd-a cases: 360 000, 0 failures. Even-a cases: 312 000, 0 failures.

**Why 2 is different from odd primes.**
- At 2^k the shift is **universal**: it depends on (a,b) only through a sign, or vanishes. So a
  *shared* baby offset set {0, 2^{2k−2}E_2} covers the pair {r, r + 2^{k−1}} for every group, with
  no lattice constraint. For a shift of −16, take the lower element of the pair as the giant.
- At odd m the sign pair's offset difference 2V_i = 2(aNr^{−1} + br) mod m² takes all values as
  (a,b) varies. Sharing it costs lattice index (Thm S).
- So **only the 2-adic involution r ↦ r + 2^{k−1} is free.**

**What it is worth (count-only, exact, V4 vs HH).**
- One giant per pair for odd a or a zero shift; two giants for a shift of 32. The baby list
  doubles.
- Measured fractions: odd a ≈ 0.67, even with zero shift ≈ 0.16.
- Result: **V4/V0 = 0.96–0.97 at the same M_odd** (56–64 bits).
- Best over the modulus menu: **0.997 (64 bits), 0.999 (56)**.
- Predicted merit: 0.583^{3/5}·2^{2/5} ≈ 0.95. The cap with all a odd is 2^{−1/5} ≈ 0.87.
- So the "constant 2–4×" holds for *Stage-3 giant leaves at frozen parameters*. Halving leaves
  doubles the Bluestein side, and after re-balancing the net is **≈ 3%**, at most 13%.

### 6.3 Does sign-splitting reduce the number of distinct Stage-3 leaves? (proved in the model)

**Answer: no, except at the prime 2.**
- **V1 (Gray-code sign sharing):** leaves are identical to HH, φ(M)·#buckets, exactly. It changes
  Stage 2 only.
- **V2 (literal MITM):** more leaves in total. Right lists of about G·2^{t_R}·2λ elements are added,
  and the exponent becomes 1/4.
- **V3 / offset constraints at odd m:** giant leaves drop by 2 per prime, but Bluestein babies grow
  by m (V3) or m·√2 (±δ), and that is a net loss (§3.4).
- **2-adic pairing:** giant leaves drop by up to 2, babies double, net ≤ 2^{1/5}: a constant.

**Consequence.**
- Stage 3 is Θ(T lg³N) for the product tree on T leaves (HH Lemma 2.1), plus Θ(λ lg²N) for
  Bluestein.
- Stage 2 after V1 is Θ(T/2^t·lg N + T), and the fair baseline is already Θ(T·lg N/lg lg N).
- So the sign-splitting gain is confined to Stage 2: **at most a log-type factor overall**, plus
  the ≤ 2^{1/5} 2-adic constant. Never exp(Ω(log N/log log N)).
- An asymptotic gain would need fewer distinct leaves at equal coverage. In the single-modulus HH
  family this is excluded prime by prime (Thm S). Whether several moduli escape it is the multimod
  question, not addressed here.
