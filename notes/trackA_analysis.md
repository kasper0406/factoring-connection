# Track A: binary coefficient sections. Analysis (agent infraA)

Status labels follow AGENT_RULES §5. Notation: `F_N = (1+X)^N - 1 - X^N = Σ_{0<i<N} a_i X^i`
over `R_N = Z/NZ`. Section `S_{r,M}(Y) = Σ_j a_{r+jM} Y^j`, with `M = 2^k` at level `k`.
Balanced family: `N = pq`, `p < q < 2p` (same bit length). Lucas support facts are in
`notes/seed_lemmas.md` (L7): `supp F_N = S_p ⊔ S_q`, `S_q = {qj : 1 ≤ j ≤ p-1}`,
`S_p = p·([1, q-p] ∪ [p, q-1])`, `|supp| = 2q - p - 1`.

Prior-art notes from the orchestrator (codex round 1, `notes/codex_r1_mechanisms.md`):
(i) randomized formal PIT over `R_N` via random monic quotients is known (Agrawal–Biswas;
Arvind–Mukhopadhyay–Srinivasan, arXiv 0801.0514 §6). (ii) The generating function
`Σ_t [X^r]((1+X)^t mod X^M - c) z^t = z^r(1-z)^{M-1-r}/((1-z)^M - c z^M)` gives a
dimension ≥ M bound for constant-coefficient linear realizations *in the exponent `t`*.
I checked (ii) numerically (A2 below) and do not claim it. Theorem A1 is a different
statement: it works in the coefficient-index direction for the single fixed exponent `N`,
and it covers nonlinear evaluation (repeated squaring), arbitrary finite algebras, towers,
products, support-aware access and multilinear read-outs. §3.4 states exactly what
goes beyond (ii).

**Bottom line.** Within the access model that every cheap quotient evaluation lives in,
selecting a nonzero child at level `k` costs algebra rank `≥ 2^k` (A1). In the balanced
family the first `≈ log2 p` levels need no selection (C1), and every later level needs
rank `> p`. Zero testing is *not* the obstacle in this family: by C2 and C4 the sections that need testing are
≤3-sparse and never scalar-false-zero, and general PIT over `R_N` is known anyway. What remains open
is a **non-uniform, binomial-specific** construction of section circuits (escape route E-1 below).
Our measurements show that the generic gate-local transform does not provide it.

## 1. Setup and the roots-of-unity closed form (task b)

* `S_{r,M}(Y)` is the `X^r`-coordinate of `(1+X)^N` in `R_N[Y][X]/(X^M - Y) ≅ R_N[X]` (minus
  endpoint terms). Scalar evaluation `S_{r,M}(c)` is coefficient `r` of `(1+X)^N mod (N, X^M - c)`.
  It is computed by repeated squaring in a rank-`M` algebra: `≈1.5 log2 N` products of
  degree-`<M` polynomials (`src/trackA/sections.py: section_values`). **proved** (definition),
  tested against explicit binomials.
* Roots-of-unity filter: over any ring containing `1/M` and a primitive `M`-th root `ω`,
  `S_{r,M}(t^M) = M^{-1} t^{-r} Σ_{i<M} ω^{-ir} (1+ω^i t)^N`. Over `R_N` the `ω` live in
  `R_N[Z]/(Φ_M)` (rank `M/2`) and `t` lives in `R_N[T]/(T^M - c)` (rank `M`), so the filter is never
  cheaper than the direct quotient. Taking `c = t_0^M` with `t_0 ∈ R_N` gives the identity
  `t_0^r S_{r,M}(t_0^M) = [Z^r]((1+t_0 Z)^N mod Z^M - 1)`, which is again rank `M`.
* Descent: `X^{2M} - s^2 = (X^M - s)(X^M + s)`, and by CRT (when `2s` is a unit)
  `R_N[X]/(X^{2M}-s^2) ≅ R_N[X]/(X^M-s) × R_N[X]/(X^M+s)`. The children of class `r` at `c = s^2` are
  `S_{r,2M}(s^2) = (P(s)+P(-s))/2` and `S_{r+M,2M}(s^2) = (P(s)-P(-s))/(2s)` with `P = S_{r,M}`
  (`descent_split`, gcd-checked: a nonunit `2s` is returned as `NonUnit`). The tower form
  `R_N[s]/(s^2-c)[X]/(X^M - s)` is isomorphic to `R_N[X]/(X^{2M}-c)` via `s ↦ X^M`. In every form the
  rank is `2M`, so "conservation" holds with equality in the canonical construction. **proved**, tested.
* Equivalently: child 0 vanishes iff `P` is odd (`P(-Y) = -P(Y)`), and child 1 vanishes iff `P` is even.
  So the child test is a parity test on the parent. It requires `P(-c)`, which lives in a *different*
  rank-`M` algebra `R_N[X]/(X^M + c)`. Relating the two algebras needs a primitive `2M`-th root of unity
  (rank `M` extension).

## 2. Circuit section transforms (task a)

Implementation: `src/circuit_sections.py`. Circuits are hash-consed DAGs over `R_N`,
with constant folding (`0+a`, `0·a`, `1·a`, const∘const). One level maps each gate `v` to
`(e(v), o(v))` by the BRIEF gate rules. A mul gate costs 4 products (naive) or 3 (Karatsuba:
`O(ab) = (e_a+o_a)(e_b+o_b) - e_a e_b - o_a o_b`, sharing `e_a e_b`, `o_a o_b` with `E`).
Only the chosen section is kept, and it is pruned to reachable nodes before the next level.
Correctness is checked against explicit coefficients (tests).

Paths: `diag_q` (bits of support index `q`), `diag_pp` (bits of `p^2`); these are diagnostic and use factors.
`op_zero`, `op_random`, and `op_exact` (bits chosen by the operational exact selector).
Balanced 16/24/32-bit, seeds 0,1, levels 0..9, both gate variants.
(Instance note: `balanced_semiprime(16, 0)` and `(16, 1)` coincide, since only 11 primes in [192, 255] qualify.)

| bits | variant | unique nodes at k = 0, 3, 6, 9 (op_zero) | mean ratio k=5..9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | naive | 57, 1080, 39 984, 1 458 016 | 3.34 |
| 24 | naive | 79, 1827, 80 197, 3 973 018 | 3.63 |
| 32 | naive | 97, 2563, 119 819, 6 411 324 | 3.72 |
| 32 | Karatsuba | 97, 3370, 107 274, 2 848 691 | 3.04 |

All five paths agree to within 3% at every level (full table in `experiments/analysis/trackA_summary.md`).
**Experimentally supported:** growth is path-independent and is `≈ 4^k` (naive) or `≈ 3^k`
(Karatsuba) per squaring gate, approached from below as `log N` grows relative to `k`.

*Mechanism (proved for this transform).* Evaluating the level-`k` section circuit at `Y = c`
computes, gate by gate, the coordinates of each gate's polynomial in `R_N[X]/(X^M - c)` that the
output depends on. For a product, coordinate `t` is `Σ_s f_s g_{t-s}` (with a `c` twist), so it depends
on all coordinates of both factors. Hence once `deg ≥ M`, every gate of the squaring chain carries all
`M` coordinates. A level then multiplies the product count of each gate by 4, or by 3 with Karatsuba sharing.
The generic transform is therefore *exactly* the rank-`M` quotient evaluation in disguise, with
schoolbook/Karatsuba polynomial multiplication. Theorem A1 applies to its evaluations. The node count
cannot fall below the `M` coordinates per gate unless the transform stops being gate-local.
At level 9 for 16-bit `N` (`M = 512 > p = 193`) the section has ≤ 3 terms (C2), yet its circuit
has 1.46·10^6 nodes. The representation is exponentially larger than the object.

Construction cost, representation size and evaluation cost are therefore all `Θ(M^{log2 3})`–`Θ(M^2)` per
squaring gate for this transform. The formal identity test is not the bottleneck (§4).

## 3. Dimension conservation (task b)

### 3.1 Model

An **algebra observable** of a polynomial `f ∈ R[X]` (`R` any commutative ring, here `R_N`) is
`L(f) = λ(f(x))`, where `B` is a commutative `R`-algebra generated *as an `R`-module* by `D`
elements, `x ∈ B`, and `λ : B → R` (or into any `R`-module) is `R`-linear. `D` is the *rank*. How
`f(x)` is computed is irrelevant: repeated squaring, towers, CRT splittings and nonlinear
intermediate arithmetic in `B` are all allowed. For `f = (1+X)^N` this is the cost model of every
"cheap quotient evaluation": cost `≈ log N` multiplications in `B`, `≥ D` ring elements each.

Examples covered: `R_N[X]/(g)` with `g` monic of degree `D`; `R_N[X,Y]/J` finite over `R_N`;
towers `A[X]/(g)` with `A = R_N[t]/(h)`, which have rank `deg g · deg h`; finite products of such
(rank = sum); a scalar `c` (rank 1); `R_N[Z]/(Z^s - a)` substitutions. A family of observables whose
`R_N`-linear combination is used counts as one observable on the product algebra.

`L` **isolates class `r mod M` on `T ⊆ Z_{≥0}`** if `L(X^i) = 0` for all `i ∈ T` with `i ≢ r (mod M)`.
For `T = [0, N]`, this says exactly that `L(f)` depends only on the section `S_{r,M}(f)` for every
`f` of degree `≤ N`. This is the "access to the section through a quotient evaluation" in the task.

### 3.2 Gap lemma

**Lemma G (proved; standard LFSR fact in this language).** Let `y ∈ B` (e.g. `y = x^b`) and
`v_t = λ(z y^t)` for a fixed `z ∈ B`. Then `y` satisfies a monic `χ ∈ R[T]` of degree `D`
(determinant trick: if `B = Σ R e_i`, then `y e_i = Σ m_ij e_j` and `det(T·I - m)` annihilates `y`). Hence
`v_{t+D} = -Σ_{s<D} χ_s v_{t+s}`. If `v` vanishes at `D` consecutive indices `t_0..t_0+D-1`,
it vanishes for all `t ≥ t_0`.

*Fitting form (field coefficients).* Over a field `F`, write `B = B_nil ⊕ B_inv` for `y`
(nilpotent part of dimension `e`, invertible part of dimension `D-e`). If `v` has `D`
consecutive zeros anywhere, then `v_t = 0` for all `t ≥ e` (the invertible part satisfies an
invertible recurrence of order `D-e`, which is forced to zero in both directions). Over `R_N` with
`N` squarefree, apply this modulo each prime (`B/pB` is generated by `D` elements over `F_p`).

### 3.3 Theorem A1 (dimension conservation): **proved**

Let `M ≥ 2`, `0 ≤ r < M`, and let `L = λ(·(x))` be an algebra observable of rank `D`.

**(i) Uniform access.** If `L` isolates class `r` on `[0, N]` and `L(X^i) ≠ 0` for some
`i ≡ r`, `M ≤ i ≤ N`, then **`D ≥ M`**.
*Proof.* The `M-1` indices `i-M+1, …, i-1` are `≢ r`, so `L` vanishes there. If `D ≤ M-1`, Lemma G
(`y = x`, `z = x^{i-D}`) gives `L(X^i) = 0`. ∎

**(ii) Support-aware access.** Suppose only `L(X^{a+bt}) = 0` is required for `0 ≤ t ≤ t_1`,
`a+bt ≢ r`, with `gcd(b, M) = 1`. If `L(X^{a+bt_1}) ≠ 0`, `a + b t_1 ≡ r`, and `t_1 ≥ M-1`, then
`D ≥ M`. *Proof.* `t ↦ a+bt` hits class `r` exactly on one class of `t` mod `M`, so the `M-1`
preceding `t` are zeros. Apply Lemma G with `y = x^b`, `z = x^a`. ∎
*Application.* For `N = pq`, the coefficient vector of `F_N` satisfies `a_i ≡ 0 (mod p)` unless `p | i`
(`k a_k = 0`). An observable that "knows" this need only isolate mod `p` along `i = p t`, `0 ≤ t ≤ q`.
By (ii) over `F_p` (`b = p` odd, `M = 2^k`), it still needs rank `≥ M` as soon as it sees a
`p`-multiple `p t_1` in the class with `t_1 ≥ M-1`. The same holds mod `q` along `i = q t`, `t < p`, when `M ≤ p`.
**Exploiting the known vanishing on indices coprime to `N` does not lower the rank.**

**(iii) Multilinear read-outs.** Let `Φ(f_1,…,f_m) = λ(f_1(x_1)···f_m(x_m))`, `x_s ∈ B`. If `Φ`
vanishes whenever some `f_s` is supported off the class (on `[0,N]`), and `Φ(X^{i_1},…,X^{i_m}) ≠ 0`
with some `i_s ≥ M`, then `D ≥ M`. *Proof.* Fix the other monomials. Then `i ↦ Φ(…, X^i, …)` is
`λ'(x_s^i)` with `λ' = λ(·∏_{s'≠s} x_{s'}^{i_{s'}})`. Apply (i). ∎ (Example: `P(c)·P(-c)`,
"norms", and products of evaluations of `f` in one algebra do not help.)

**Corollaries.**
1. *Towers / extensions:* a section accessed through `A[X]/(g)` with `[A : R_N] = e` and
   `deg g = d` needs `d·e ≥ M = 2^k`. That is the "(quotient dimension) × (extension degree) ≥ 2^k"
   statement asked for.
2. *Families of evaluations:* if the selection rule uses an `R_N`-linear combination of evaluations
   in algebras of ranks `D_1, …, D_s`, then `Σ D_s ≥ M`. Evaluating the parent at `±c` (rank `M` + `M`) for
   the child at level `k+1` is tight.
3. *Selection:* a child-selection rule that reads isolating observables for the two children at level `k+1`
   uses rank `≥ 2^{k+1}`. Combined with C1, any path to a support index in the balanced family pays
   rank `> p` at every level after `≈ log2 p`. The model cost is `Ω(p)` ring operations, the same order as trial division.

### 3.4 Scope: what A1 does and does not cover

*Beyond codex r1 §4.2 (exponent-direction linear states).* A1 bounds the rank for the single
exponent `N`, whatever nonlinear arithmetic computes `f(x)`. It applies to every finite algebra
(general monic quotients, `R_N[X,Y]/J`, towers, products), not only `X^M - Y`. It also includes the
support-aware (ii) and multilinear (iii) variants. Codex §4.2 constrains realizations of the whole
sequence `t ↦ S^{(t)}_{r,M}` by a constant matrix power. A1 says nothing about that sequence.

*Not covered (escape routes, i.e. what a mechanism must do):*
* **E-1 Non-uniform, binomial-specific section circuits.** A1 is about observables that isolate the class
  for *every* polynomial (or every polynomial with the `k a_k = 0` pattern). A straight-line program over
  `R_N[Y]` computing `S_{r,M}((1+X)^N)` for the specific `N`, built from identities particular to
  binomial powers, is not an algebra observable and is not bounded. This is exactly the BRIEF's
  conditional target. Together with the known PIT, poly-size *constructible* such circuits for
  all `S_{r,2^k}`, `k ≤ n`, would give randomized polynomial-time factoring. The gate-local E/O transform
  does not provide them (§2). We found no binomial-specific identity that does (§6).
* **E-2 Hidden-support isolation.** (ii) needs an arithmetic progression of length `≥ M` with step
  coprime to `M` inside the region where isolation is required. An observable that is correct only
  thanks to the *digit structure* of the hidden support (the two short blocks of `S_p` when `q-p` is
  small) escapes A1. C3 is the instance: gcds appear at rank `≈ q-p`. That is a Fermat-type regime, so no gain.
* **E-3 Non-isolating, nonlinear post-processing.** gcd/NonUnit events, branching, and comparisons of
  values from different algebras are outside A1. The only such signal we observed is C3, again E-2.
* **E-4 Non-finite representations.** Power series, `R_N[[X]]`, `p`-adic lifts `R_{N^t}`: A1 holds
  verbatim over `R_{N^t}` (any commutative ring), so lifting does not escape. Infinite-rank algebras escape
  formally but have no cheap arithmetic.

### 3.5 A2 (exponent recurrence): checked, credited to codex r1 §4.2

Berlekamp–Massey over `F_p` (`p ∈ {101, 257, 1009}`, `M ≤ 32`, random `r, c`) on
`t ↦ [X^r]((1+X)^t mod X^M - c)`: order `M` in 45/45 cases (`theory_checks.check_A2_order`).

## 4. Zero testing and the exact nonzero pattern (task c)

**Characterization (proved, L7 + CRT).** `S_{r,M}(F_N) ≠ 0` iff `r ≡ i (mod M)` for some
`i ∈ supp F_N`. For squarefree `N` that means `r ∈ ⋃_l l·{j ≼_l N/l} mod M`. Decided without
enumeration in `src/trackA/diagnostic.py: section_support` (harness).

**C1 (proved).** Balanced `N`. If `gcd(M, q) = 1` and `M ≤ p - 1`, then `{q j mod M : 1 ≤ j ≤ p-1}`
contains a full residue system, because `q` is invertible mod `M` and `j` runs over `≥ M` consecutive
integers. So every class is hit. For `M = 2^k` this covers `k ≤ ⌊log2(p-1)⌋`, about the first half of the
`n` levels. Your expectation is confirmed. Checked for 21 instances 16–40 bits (`check_C_props`) and all 92
balanced `N ≤ 5000`.

**C2 (proved).** Balanced `N`, `M > p`. The `S_q` indices in a class have `j` in one residue class mod `M`
within `[1, p-1]`, so there is at most one. The `S_p` indices are `p j` with `j` in two blocks of length
`q - p < p < M`, so at most one per block. Hence **≤ 3 nonzero terms**. (For `p < M` the section is a
"monomial + binomial" over CRT components: `S mod q` is 0 or one term, `S mod p` has 0–2 terms.) Checked as above.

**C4 (proved): no scalar false zeros for balanced semiprimes at levels `M ≥ 2`.** Mod `q`, the
terms of a section come from `j ≡ j_0 (mod M)`, `j < p`. Their `Y`-exponents `(qj - r)/M` form an
arithmetic progression with step `q ≡ 1 (mod q-1)`, with fewer than `q-1` terms, so the exponents are
distinct mod `q-1`. The monomials `c^e` with distinct `e mod (q-1)` are linearly independent
functions on `F_q^*`, and `c = 0` handles `e = 0`. So a nonzero `q`-component is a nonzero function. Mod `p`,
the exponents along `j = j_0 + tM` step by `p ≡ 1 (mod p-1)`, with `t` ranging over `< (q-1)/M + 1 ≤ p-1`
values when `M ≥ 2` (`q < 2p`). The same argument applies. At `M = 1` the section is `F_N` itself,
which vanishes as a function iff `N` is Carmichael (L5), never for a semiprime. ∎
**Census** (`src/trackA/zero_tests.py`, all 92 balanced `N ≤ 5000` plus 5 Carmichael and 3
three-prime `N`; every level; every nonzero class):

| family | nonzero sections | scalar false zeros (exhaustive c) | `Z^s - a` false zeros (s≤4, a≤3) | random monic quadratic (4 trials) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| balanced semiprime | 44 110 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Carmichael | — | 1 per N (only M = 1, i.e. F_N) | 0 | 0 |
| three-prime (105, 1001, 2431) | — | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The only "561 traps" in the whole catalogue are `F_N` itself for Carmichael `N` (`M = 1`),
and they are removed by any `Z^s - a` or monic-quadratic quotient. In the descent experiment the exact selector
certified a nonzero child with its **first** random scalar at every level of every run (mean evals 1.00).

**Correction to the task's expectation.** The task anticipated "zero testing is the whole difficulty". For the
balanced family it is the opposite. Levels with `M < p` need no test (C1). Levels with `M > p` involve ≤ 3-term
sections that a single scalar evaluation tests (C2, C4), and general PIT over `R_N` is known (Agrawal–Biswas/AMS).
**The difficulty is entirely the cost of evaluating, i.e. of isolating the class: rank `≥ M` (A1).**

## 5. Weaker certificates: selectors (task d)

Operational selectors (`src/trackA/selectors.py`), harness scoring with Lucas truth
(`src/trackA/run_descent.py`). Balanced 16, 20, 24, 28, 32 bits × seeds 0–4. Rank cap `2^20`
(full depth for 16 and 20 bits; 19 levels beyond).

* `random`: free coin.
* `exact_scalar`: one evaluation of `(1+X)^N mod (X^{2M} - c)`, random `c` (both children at once).
  A nonzero value *certifies* that child. It retries up to 3 scalars, then `Z^2 - a`, then falls back to a coin.
* `budget_1024`: exact while `2M ≤ 1024`, coin afterwards. By A1(i), no rank-1024 observable can
  distinguish the two children's classes once `2M > 1024`.

| k − log2 p | both children nonzero | random | exact | budget_1024 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ≤ −1 | 100% (all) | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| 0 | 24/75 | 22/25 | 25/25 | 22/25 |
| 1 | 1/69 | 8/22 | 25/25 | 14/22 |
| 2 | 0/47 | 4/8 | 25/25 | 8/14 |
| 3 | 0/37 | 1/4 | 25/25 | 3/8 |
| 4 | 0/29 | 0/1 | 25/25 | 1/3 |
| 5..10 | 0 | — | 100% | — |

(success = P(chosen child nonzero | parent nonzero); the denominators shrink as paths die.)
Random paths die 1–3 levels after `bitlen(p)` (first dead level `bitlen(p)+1 … +3` in all 25 runs), which matches the support density `≈ 2/p`.
**Cost per level of the exact selector doubles exactly** (ratio 2.00 at every level, all sizes;
e.g. 32-bit: level 9 → 23 040, level 15 → 1 474 560, level 20 → 47 185 920 rank-weighted products).
The wall time also doubles (32-bit: 4.8 s for 20 levels). **This selector is exponential.**
Reaching a support index costs `Σ_k 2^{k+1}·1.5 log2 N = Θ(N log N)` rank-weighted products, worse
than trial division. Stopping early and scanning `gcd(r + tM, N)` is uninformative for `M < q`. Every
progression `r + tM`, `t ≤ N/M`, contains `≈ q/M ≥ 1` multiples of `p` whatever `r` is. The scan
becomes informative only once `M > q`, and then the descent has already cost `> q`.

**Factor discoveries along the way (AGENT_RULES §3).** The exact selector gcd-checks the two
evaluated values per level. Across 25 runs: **338 STRUCTURAL, 3 ACCIDENTAL** (null expectation for
accidental hits `Σ 2/p ≈ 2.05`). STRUCTURAL means the evaluated class is *pure*: hit only by `S_q`, so
the value is `≡ 0 (mod p)` and `≢ 0 (mod q)`, or vice versa (C3). The first structural hit occurs at
evaluation modulus `2M ∈ (q-p, 4(q-p)]` in all 25 runs (e.g. 32-bit seed 4: `q-p = 12 286`, hit at
`2M = 16 384`). **Proved necessity:** a pure class needs `M > min(p-1, q-p)`, because if `M ≤ q-p` each
`S_p` block covers all classes, and if `M ≤ p-1` `S_q` covers all classes. The cost is therefore
`Θ((q-p) log N)` products of size `≈ q-p`. That is useless for random balanced primes (`q-p = Θ(p)`) and far
worse than Fermat's method when `q-p` is small. This is the Track-B "sketch coefficient gcd" phenomenon,
explained here by Lucas; reported to the B/D owner as a cross-reference, not developed further.

## 6. Search for binomial-specific identities (escape E-1)

Tried and rejected (all reduce to rank-`M` evaluation or to circuits measured in §2):
* Lucas/Chebyshev doubling (`U_{2m} = U_m^2 + Y V_m^2`, `V_{2m} = 2U_mV_m`): this *is* the level-1 E/O transform.
  Iterating needs all `2^k` components.
* Roots-of-unity filter with `c = t_0^M`: equals `[Z^r](1+t_0 Z)^N mod (Z^M - 1)`, rank `M`.
* Hypergeometric/holonomic view: `j ↦ C(N, r+jM)` has a term ratio that is rational of degree `M` in `j`.
  The section satisfies an order-`M` linear ODE in `Y` (dimension `M` again). Generating its coefficients by
  recurrence over `R_N` divides by `r + jM + t` and hits a nonunit exactly at multiples of `p`. That is
  trial division in disguise (Track C territory).
* Frobenius: over `F_l`, sections in base `l` (`M = l^k`) are trivial products by Lucas. The obstruction is
  the mismatch between binary sectioning and the hidden base-`p` Frobenius. Guessing the base is trial division.
No identity found. **Status of E-1: open.** It is the single most decisive question (§9).

## 7. Masks via Hadamard product (task e)

**Corollary E1 (proved from Lemma G).** An algebra observable isolating mask `m ⊆ [0, N]` (vanishing on
`[0,N] \ m`) that is nonzero at an index preceded by a run of `g` zeros of `m` has rank `≥ g+1` (any
`R`). Over a field, i.e. for squarefree `N` componentwise (Fitting form), it has rank `≥ i* + 1`, where `i*` is
the largest nontrivial index followed by a zero-run of length `≥` rank. Concretely:

| mask | short description | lower bound (rank) | construction attaining it |
|---|---|---|---|
| class `r mod M` | `X^M - c` | `M` | `R_N[X]/(X^M - c)`, rank `M` |
| bit `j` of index = 1 | `2^{j+1}`-periodic | `2^j + 1` | `X^{2^{j+1}} - c`, sum of `2^j` classes, rank `2^{j+1}` |
| interval `[a, b)` | two endpoints | `a + 1` (any ring); `b' + 1` over fields (`b'` last used index) | `R_N[X]/(X^b)`, rank `b` |
| prefix `[0, K)` nontrivial at `K-1` | `X^K` | `K` (field form) | truncation, rank `K` |

Exhaustive check over `F_2`, `F_3`, length 20 (`check_A1_exhaustive`): the minimum linear
complexity equals the predicted bound in 38/38 cases, so the bounds are tight. The prefix mask is the BRIEF §2.4 prefix test.
By L3 it is informative only for `K ≈ p`, so it costs rank `≈ p` in this model, which links Track A to Track C's
factorial-unit predicate. Measured: `prefix` nonzero ⇔ `K > p` in 36/36 runs (24–48 bits, `K = 2^4..2^20`). The cost
scales linearly in rank (48-bit, rank `2^20`: 7.9·10^7 rank-weighted products, 9.6 s).
**Status:** the Hadamard product with any mask that has a long zero-run is as expensive as a section in the
algebra model. Short description does not imply cheap application.

## 9. Most decisive open question

**Do the sections `S_{r,2^k}((1+X)^N)` (`k ≤ n`) admit straight-line programs over `R_N[Y]` of size
`poly(n)` that can be constructed from `N` in `poly(n)` time?** Equivalently, is there a non-gate-local,
binomial-specific identity (escape E-1)? By A1 such programs cannot work by evaluating `(1+X)^N` in any
finite algebra and reading off linearly. By §2 the generic transform grows like `3^k`. A concrete
attack is to search, over `Z` (characteristic 0, then reduce mod `N`), for small circuits for
`S_{r,M}` at `M = 4, 8, 16`, as functions of a *symbolic* `N` (e.g. the bits of `N` as inputs) via
exhaustive/SAT-guided straight-line program search. Then test whether any discovered program size is
sub-linear in `M`. A negative answer for small `M` and unrestricted programs would be the first evidence
beyond the gate-local model.
