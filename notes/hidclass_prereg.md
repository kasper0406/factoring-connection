# Hidden prime class experiment preregistration

Freeze this design before collecting data. Use Python's deterministic pseudorandom generator with seed 20261126. Generate independent balanced semiprimes of exact bit lengths 40, 48, 56, 64, 72 and 80 using distinct primes of half that bit length. At each size collect 1,024 examples of each label for each target. Generate primes by uniform rejection within the required residue classes. For T1 fix N = 3 modulo 16, so the two (r,q) classes are (1,3) and (9,11). For T2 balance the possible ordered prime-class pairs within each label as evenly as possible, with product 1 modulo 7. Randomize the order of the examples. Use the factors only to generate and label the examples.

Prediction for all three tests: no persistent advantage detectable at this sample size. For the cubic sign this also follows from multiplying the sample by a scalar unit of Jacobi sign -1, since the norm has odd degree. Zero-symbol frequencies need not obey this symmetry.

Three binary features are specified. For T1 take (1) the parity of the sum of the first 64 partial quotients after the integer part of sqrt(7N), and (2) the parity of the denominator of its 64th convergent, indexing the integer part by zero. These are bounded-cost probes of continued-fraction behavior. Also consult the existing entropy notes for the distinction between the original statistic and this finite prefix. For T2 sample four independent uniform elements a of the cubic algebra, calculate the Jacobi symbol of the norm of a - sigma(a) modulo N, and take (3) the product of the first two signs times the product of the last two signs, encoded as a bit for sign +1. Record proper gcds from symbol arguments and any zero symbols separately and omit such examples from that feature's rate. Also flag a proper gcd from any tested continued-fraction complete denominator or convergent denominator with N. Retain CF examples in the primary analysis.

Report signed advantage as half the difference of positive-feature rates (T1 label r=9 minus r=1, T2 inert minus split). Give two-sided 95% difference-of-proportions intervals, halved, by size and pooled with equal size weights. Success requires a pooled interval excluding zero at Bonferroni-adjusted two-sided 0.05/3 and the same direction in both the smallest three and largest three sizes. Estimate a decay exponent by fitting log2 absolute advantage against bit length only if size effects are distinguishable from sampling noise (at least four individual 95% intervals exclude zero with consistent sign). Otherwise report the exponent as unresolved. Compare the noise floor with the previously reported exponent. No post-hoc selection of feature direction or CF prefix.
