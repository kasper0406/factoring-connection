# Blind research round: classical non-black-box period finding for a^x mod N

You are a research mathematician/theoretical computer scientist. Work independently (blind); be rigorous, cite literature precisely (author, year, title, theorem number where possible), and label every claim PROVED / FOLKLORE-KNOWN (with citation) / CONJECTURED / UNVERIFIED. You may use web search. You have read-only access to the repo in the parent directories; you need not read it.

## Setting
N = pq is an RSA-type semiprime (balanced, unknown factors). For a random a in (Z/N)^*, let r = ord_N(a). Any of the following gives randomized poly-time factoring: r; any nonzero multiple of r; a multiple of λ(N); a single sample j/r (j uniform) — since it reveals r/gcd(j,r). Shor's algorithm samples j/r from the Fourier spectrum of x ↦ a^x mod N. The QFT alone is classically simulable on product states; the power is in the entangled state. Classical lower bounds for period finding (~√r queries; Cleve 2004 "The query complexity of order-finding") are black-box only, but a^x mod N is NOT a black box.

We want classical (deterministic or randomized) methods asymptotically faster than L_N[1/3], ideally polynomial. Do not propose quantum algorithms. Known facts to take as given: p−1, p+1, ECM, algebraic tori all need a smooth group order or a coincidence ("order-annihilation barrier"); generic-group lower bounds (Shoup/Nechaev style) apply to anything treating (Z/N)^* generically; Weyl differencing leaves Shor's phase h·a^x/N invariant (so analytic exponential-sum methods don't see r).

## Questions (answer each with a mechanism or a precise barrier)

1. Non-black-box structure. What structure of x ↦ a^x mod N beyond black-box access could a classical algorithm exploit to learn about r? (a^{x+y}=a^x a^y; bit-structure of exponents; additive/multiplicative ring interplay; known values like a^N ≡ a^{p+q−1} when r | φ(N), with N − φ(N) = p+q−1.) Map exactly what partial information about r is cheaply computable classically: r mod small primes ℓ, the ℓ-adic valuations v_ℓ(r), the 2-adic valuation, the smoothness profile, gcd(r, known numbers), Jacobi-symbol information. Note: (a/N) Jacobi symbol is computable; (a/p),(a/q) individually are the QR problem. Does any computable partial info give a factor, or reduce the search for r (e.g., to a BSGS of size √(r/m) where m is a known divisor)? Is "v_2(r)" or "r mod 3" as hard as factoring? (Relate to quadratic residuosity / ℓ-th power residuosity.) Give exact reductions in both directions where possible.

2. Fourier-side classical sampling. Kushilevitz–Mansour / Goldreich–Levin, Akavia's SFT over Z_N (Akavia 2009-2010, "Solving hidden number problem with one bit oracle and advice", "Learning noisy characters, MPC and cryptographic hardcore predicates"), Gilbert–Muthukrishnan–Strauss sparse FFT, Hassanieh–Indyk–Katabi–Price sFFT. Why don't they find the peaks of the indicator f_c(x) = [a^x ≡ c mod N] on Z_Q, with poly(log Q) queries? Quantify exactly: Fourier mass per peak, the threshold τ at which SFT costs poly(log Q, 1/τ, ‖f‖_∞), and how this compares to √r. KEY QUESTION: can one construct, from N and a (using ring operations mod N, and anything else classically computable), a function g: Z_Q → C (Q chosen by us) that (i) is efficiently evaluable classically, (ii) has its Fourier mass concentrated (≥ 1/poly(log N) of ‖g‖_2^2) on frequencies that reveal r (multiples of Q/r, or more generally some j/r info), so that Akavia-style SFT finds a heavy coefficient in poly(log N) queries? Natural candidates: g(x) = χ(a^x mod N) for a multiplicative or additive character χ of Z/N computable classically (Jacobi symbol (·/N)! additive e(h·y/N)); g(x) = ψ(a^x mod N) for ψ a "smooth" function on Z/N (e.g. ψ(y) = [y < N/2], low-frequency additive characters); compositions g(x) = h(a^x, a^{x+1}, ...). For each, compute the Fourier spectrum in terms of the period r and the additive/multiplicative character sums over the subgroup <a> ⊂ (Z/N)^*. Is there a fundamental reason (e.g., an equivalence: if g is heavy on period-revealing frequencies and classically computable then one could already compute something factoring-equivalent) ? Consider the Jacobi symbol in particular: x ↦ (a^x / N) = (a/N)^x is trivially periodic with period ≤ 2 — what does that tell you? More generally, any classically computable function of a^x mod N that is invariant under a "large" subgroup gives a period dividing a small number — characterize which subgroup-invariant functions of Z/N are classically computable without factoring.

3. Special a. a = 2, small a, a in a known subgroup, a constructed from N (Lucas sequences, u+√N in quadratic extensions, elements of norm-1 tori, elements of order dividing N±1, etc.). Is ord(a) easier for any special a? Relate to p±1 and to cyclotomic methods (Bach–Shallit). E.g., ord of (1+√d) in (Z/N[√d])^* of norm-twisted type has order | p+1 or p−1 depending on (d/p). What about a^N computed in Z/N[x]/(Φ_k(x)) for various k?

4. Dequantization literature. Tang 2018 and successors, Gharibian–Le Gall 2022 (QSVT dequantization limits), Chia–Gilyén–Li–Lin–Tang–Wang; Aharonov–Landau–Makowsky (QFT classical simulation); Schwarz–Van den Nest (sparse/compressible states + QFT simulable); Yoran–Short ("Efficient classical simulation of the approximate quantum Fourier transform"); Van den Nest's "Simulating quantum computers with probabilistic methods"; Bravyi–Gosset. Precisely why do they not apply to Shor? Is there any "partial dequantisation" of Shor-type problems (e.g., hidden subgroup problems over abelian groups classically solvable for special classes; Simon's problem classical lower bound; Abelian HSP with known structure; "period finding with structured oracles")?

5. Barrier statement. Give the sharpest precise classical barrier you can PROVE: e.g. "any classical algorithm that learns r via sparse-Fourier (membership) queries to a function g(x) = Ψ(a^x mod N), where Ψ ranges over [some class computable in the ring Z/N without factoring], must make ≥ ... queries unless ...". Try to make it a real theorem (in a well-defined model) rather than a slogan. Generic-ring models (Aggarwal–Maurer 2009: factoring is equivalent to breaking RSA generically; Jager–Schwenk) may be relevant.
