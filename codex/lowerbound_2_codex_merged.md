# Cross-review and merged theorem

## 1. The cutoff and the constants

**G’s Step 2 fails under SM2 as written.** Put $U=T^2x^C$, $x=\log N$. When earlier incidences are revealed, SM2 covers the analyst’s threshold, and its incidence factors are polynomial in $x$, two unforced hits of the same prime above $U$ have total probability $O(T^2\operatorname{poly}(x)\sum_{p>U}p^{-2})=o(1)$ for sufficiently large $C$. That estimate misses a forced first occurrence followed by an unforced hit. SM2 exempts every $p\mid Q_t$. Consider two dependency rows with large-prime parts $q$ and $q^3$, the latter forced only modulo $q$. Their product has even valuation. The last row’s residual contains $q^2$, which SM2 leaves unrestricted. Even powers can also occur outside the set of odd-valuation columns.

A repair needs conditional bounds on **excess valuations even at forced primes**. If there are at most $T\operatorname{poly}(x)$ previously exposed labels, a union bound also controls later residual hits on labels first exposed by forced moduli, even outside the chosen dependency. Use ideal-level bounds for ideal columns. After excluding known large residual labels and fresh large squares, the last row touching a large label has smooth residual because a fresh residual prime of exponent one would have no partner. Alternatively, record all earlier labels and strip their full valuations, including current forced primes. This changes the residual whose size must be bounded. A global cutoff based on total batch work cannot be substituted for a per-target cutoff.

**The pointwise size bound fails independently.** Take $f=X-Y$, $g=X^2+(N-1-q)XY+qY^2$, with prime $U<q<N/4$ coprime to $N$. Their resultant is $N$. For large $N$ the quadratic discriminant lies strictly between $(N-2-q)^2$ and $(N-1-q)^2$, so $g$ is irreducible. On the index-$q$ lattice $q\mid X$, the primitive point $(0,1)$ gives $\lvert fg\rvert/q=1$. Choose box radius $E\ge\sqrt q$ so the large-modulus clause of $H_{\rm sel}$ does not apply. FT’s additive $O(D\log E)$ can permit one exception per selected lattice. A dependency needing one terminal row cannot discard that allowance in each lattice. The example refutes the size inference without requiring this particular row to split anything. The constant $(1/18)^{1/3}$ is correct arithmetic given the extra bottleneck below.

**C’s bound survives.** The block must open predictably, the exceptional addresses must be fixed then, and the size envelope must hold on every path. One revelation gives at most one credit per block. Relabelling cached successes, choosing a base after seeing valuations, or borrowing separate exception budgets for pooled columns breaks the proof. GNFS’s $A=2$ uses a typical two-sided profile. GAE bounds coefficient heights. C2/FT alone give $A=\sqrt g$ for a binary product with $\lvert\operatorname{disc}\rvert\ge N^g$ at growing ranks after budgeted exceptions. Thus 1.2114 and 1.5263 are scoped single-block floors for $g=1,2$ respectively. SNFS attains the latter heuristically. A private MNFS field needs a separately verified discriminant or a private size hypothesis. The pair resultant alone gives neither. G’s special-input constants therefore do not extend automatically to adaptive DAGs. The 1.2114 ramified floor also supplies no attaining construction.

**Free linear algebra.** Both quoted numbers check in the classical programs with priced partner tests. Let $e,\beta,a,v$ denote box, rational-base, private-base and fan log-coefficients, with degree coefficient $\delta$, rational penalty $R=1/(3\beta\delta)$ and private penalty $P=(\delta e+1/\delta)/(3a)$. Put $p=P/c,\ z=1/(3\delta c^2),\ u=2e/c,\ b=\beta/c$. MNFS feasibility gives $u\le1,\ b\le1-p$ and $p(u-z/b-p)\ge z+u/(18zc^3)$. Divide by $u$ and relax to $u=1,b=1-p$. Maximizing $z[p(1-p)-z/(1-p)]$ gives $p=2/5,\ z=p(1-p)^2/2$, with equality attainable. Hence $c^3=3125/486$, or $c=1.859536$. In the one-field factory the optimum takes equal bases $bc$ and $2e-R=c$. Increasing the smaller base improves feasibility, and the yield inequality divided by $2e-R$ improves as that budget increases. Minimizing over degree gives $c^{3/2}\ge[3\sqrt2\,b(\sqrt{2-b}-1)]^{-1}$. Equality occurs at $\sqrt{2-b}=(1+\sqrt7)/3$, giving $1.636264$. Even a classical factory fan with unlimited preprocessing reduces to one field. Write $H=2e-R\le c$. Feasibility gives $H\ge a+P,\ \beta+P\le c$. Set $H'=c,\ a'=a+c-H,\ v'=0$ at fixed $\beta,\delta$. Then $P'=[aP+\delta(c-H)/6]/a'\le P$ since $2e\ge a$ implies $P\ge\delta/6$. The optima are conditional on the prescribed survivor-testing schedule. ## 2. A1 and the batch cap

A1 budgets enough pair lookups. It does not prove that $u-v$ hits sufficiently many individual targets, or that their dependencies split them. Even granting fast enumeration and the stated cardinalities, both lists could consist of even smooth numbers, missing every odd target. An unrestricted dense batch really does admit polylogarithmic amortized factoring by sieving least prime factors up to $2N$ for $K=N/\operatorname{polylog}N$. That establishes the obstruction for general computation. A1’s relation-only construction still needs a distribution estimate for smooth differences and fruitful row supply.

For an **independent** nonzero $W$ of bit length $N^{o(1)}$, there are $N^{o(1)}$ semiprime divisors, by counting pairs of its $O(\log|W|)$ prime divisors. G’s bound on the entire divisor function does not follow. With target point mass $N^{-1+o(1)}$, the expected number of hits is at most $TK N^{-1+o(1)}$ under the requisite leave-one-target-out independence. At $T=K N^{o(1)}$, $K\le N^{1/2-\epsilon}$ suffices for zero hits with high probability. For $o(K)$ hits, $K\le N^{1-\epsilon}$ suffices. Even $W=N_1N_2$ defeats the independence calculation for adaptive candidates. Divisibility alone also says nothing about arbitrary shared links. Neither exponent is a necessary batch cap.

## 3. One merged theorem

**Model.** Use a uniform, charged computation replayable from public inputs, coins and paid answers. Check the declared link maps, denominators and applicable idempotent/index gcds. Count only splits certified by relations. G’s finite gcd checklist alone cannot detect advice hidden in a coefficient, such as $p+1$ for a hidden factor $p$. Count each construction and bound every auxiliary input channel. An optional batch-wide $O(\log x)$-bit advice string costs only polynomially many guesses if the bounds below hold for every fixed guess in the unconditioned experiment. Integer-route extraction needs its own row-demand hypothesis. PIN asserts membership in a rational span.

Put $F=x^{1/3}(\log x)^{2/3}$. Fix $A,\kappa>0$. Register the target, block, threshold and address of each credit before revelation, and let $Q_i$ count registered probes for target $i$, with $\sum_i Q_i\le e^{o(F)}W$ for total work $W$. Shared filters may be reused freely. For every trial budget $t=\lfloor e^{cF}\rfloor$, each extraction by $t$ target incidences requires one of the following witnesses.

1. **Quota witness (R).** Some block needs $h$ distinct successes at $B=e^\mu$, $\log h\ge(1-o(1))\mu-O(1)$. Grant $\lfloor h/4\rfloor$ exceptions. Ordinary sorted residual log-sizes satisfy $\lambda(k)\ge(A-o(1))\sqrt{x\log(k+1)}$ through rank $t$ (only $k\ge h/8$ when $h>x$).
2. **Optional single witness.** A registered probe has residual log-size $s\ge(1-o(1))\sqrt{\kappa x\log U}$ and is entirely $U$-smooth, $U=t^2x^C$. This is an additional certificate requirement for DAGs using this alternative.

**H** bounds conditional fresh success by $\rho(s/\log B)^{1-o(1)}$ at the relevant threshold, uniformly after all earlier tests. **Conclusion, proved under these assumptions.** For any $K$ targets satisfying $\log N_i=(1+o(1))x$ uniformly and expected solved fraction bounded away from zero, $\liminf\log(\mathbb EW/K)/F\ge\min((4A/3)^{2/3},(\kappa/18)^{1/3})$, omitting any unused alternative.

**Proof.** In a quota block let $M=\sum_{k\le t}\rho(\lambda(k)/\mu)^{1-o(1)}$, $d=\lceil3h/4\rceil$. The Bernoulli exponential supermartingale stopped at $d$ hits bounds success by $(eM/d)^d$ when $M<d$. Here the predictable sum is bounded by sorting distinct addresses on each path. For a general profile, dyadic ranks give $M\le(1+\log_2t)\max_{k=2^j\le t}k\rho(\lambda(k)/\mu)^{1-o(1)}$, recovering the SS inequality up to $O(\log\log t)$ in log-count. Sum over at most $t$ openings. For $h\le x$, each ordinary probability is $e^{-\Omega(\sqrt{x})}$. Otherwise minimizing $\mu-\log\rho(s/\mu)$ gives $(1+o(1))\sqrt{2s\log s}$. At $\log k=cF$ its ratio to $\log k$ at the profile floor tends to $\sqrt{4A/(3c^{3/2})}$ and is larger at smaller eligible ranks. Below threshold $M\le h/8+h\sum_{k\ge h/8}k^{-1-\delta}$ for fixed $\delta>0$. The single-witness union bound is $t\exp[-(\sqrt{\kappa/(2c)}/3+o(1))F]$. The probabilities of either certificate within budget vanish below their thresholds even after polynomially many guesses. Summing target incidence bills proves the conclusion. ## 4. Three referee objections

1. **Smoothness after selection.** H is an oracle heuristic. $H_{\rm sel}$ is an additional large-lattice occupancy assumption when used to establish a profile. For ordinary line sieves fixed lattice counting is arithmetic. Adaptive large-modulus selection and public beyond-Johnson decoding need separate control. SM2 is needed only for the proposed incidence repair.
2. **Arithmetic profile.** Binary counts with pooled errors, scoped general-position/frame bounds, and C’s coefficient-restricted KH subclass give proved inputs. The latter gives $A=\theta/\sqrt7$ for its stated norm-to-height ratio $\theta$. KH alone bounds generator height. QS/MPQS and the studied towers enter under their respective typical residual profiles. The conventional GNFS/MNFS programs attain exponent $1/3$ with constants 1.9230/1.9019 at quadratic linear algebra. General sparse high-rank selection needs a residual sublevel estimate such as a strengthened $\mathrm{LP}_{\rm exc}^{\rm rel}$ with exceptions within the exception budget. G’s additive $B L^{o(1)}$ can exceed the entire quota.
3. **Witness supply and reuse.** R and the incidence bill are model assumptions. Tree incidence gives distinct leaves, still requiring residual profiles. General DAGs need a separately proved quota or single witness. Preassignment fixes the amortized proof inside this model. A numerical cap cannot replace it.
