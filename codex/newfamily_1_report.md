## Scope, conventions, and what the verdicts mean

This is an independent blind list. I read only §§2–3 of `notes/factorbits_results.md` for the permitted internal context; I did not read `notes/newfamily_*`. The repository context remained read-only. A validation script was initially placed in `/private/tmp`, contrary to the scratch-only instruction; I removed it and its generated bytecode. This document is the sole retained artifact, in the requested current directory.

Let n = ⌈log₂N⌉, let e_N(t)=exp(2πit/N), and assume distinct odd balanced primes. **P means polynomial in n**, including output bit length. An algorithm taking N or √N steps is exponential in n. “No known P” below is a literature-status statement, not an impossibility theorem.

**The requested taxonomy needs one repair.** The label **NOT COMPUTABLE*** means “no known qualifying efficient algorithm,” not uncomputable. Sometimes factoring supplies the missing algorithm (04, 19); sometimes even knowing p,q leaves a hard evaluation problem (15), and enumeration never requires their factorization. Calling all these objects “best computed only with the factors” would be false. Generic factoring followed by evaluation also does not qualify as an independent fourth mechanism. All complexity claims are classical unless explicitly stated otherwise.

**AGGREGATE** is used literally for the invariant defined in that entry. For example, the Gauss *phase* is an aggregate; its unnormalized value √N times that phase is not a function of a fixed residue class of N. The same distinction matters much more for Dedekind sums. Explicitly removing a public height term identifies a small residual, but does not prove that the original unnormalized function has zero statistical correlation with a factor bit.

**GENUINELY OPEN** has the question’s operational meaning: a proved polynomial-time algorithm, and no demonstrated fixed-conductor aggregate formula for the proposed growing-input statistic. It does **not** mean demonstrated leakage, a new hardness assumption, or established novelty in the literature. Entries 08–10 use lattice-point algorithms and therefore have an obvious connection to family (A); they are computationally eligible probes, with weaker claims to being a fourth mechanism.

Every external factual claim below is **KNOWN**, meaning proved in the cited literature, when supported by the linked reference. Short algebraic deductions marked **PROVED HERE** are supplied explicitly. All negative literature claims (“no known algorithm/reduction/floor formula”) are **UNVERIFIED for completeness**: this search is not an exhaustive review of every publication. Proposed correlations and their absence are **UNPROVED**. Numerical checks are **EXPERIMENTAL**.

Parameters must be fixed before looking at factor labels. For the growing numerators below, use public probes

\[
 a_j(N)=2+\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{SHAKE256}(
 \texttt{newfamily-blind-v1:j:N}),32\text{ bytes})\bmod(N-3),
 \quad j=0,\ldots,7.
\]

The string uses decimal j,N with colon separators. This is a deterministic polynomial-time choice on the requested size range, not a theorem that the probes are random. For unbounded input lengths use an output length 2n+128 bits. Compute gcd(a_j,N) first: a nontrivial gcd is an explicit factor discovery, recorded separately. Never silently discard it. For experiments, increasing the number of probes after inspecting results requires a new holdout set.

## Candidate details

### 01. Jacobi/Kronecker symbol and permutation sign

**Definition/algorithm.** Fix a nonzero integer a and evaluate J_a(N)=(a/N). Binary gcd, the supplementary laws, and quadratic reciprocity give a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm. The sign of multiplication by a on the residue classes modulo odd N is the same value, by Zolotarev’s lemma. See [Handbook of Applied Cryptography, Chapter 2](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/hac/about/chap2.pdf) and the treatment of Zolotarev in [Hittmeir](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.07791).

**AGGREGATE — KNOWN.** Write a=a₀t² with a₀ signed squarefree. Away from aN=0, J_a is a Dirichlet character of conductor dividing 8|a₀|, and
\[
 J_a(N)=J_a(p)J_a(q)=\chi_a(N).
\]
Permutation terminology does not change the invariant. If a grows with N, this remains a joint local-symbol product, but there is no longer a *fixed* conductor bound; that distinction must not be suppressed.

### 02. Abelian Artin symbols

**Definition/algorithm.** Fix an abelian extension L/ℚ with conductor m. Evaluate Art_L((N)) for N prime to m. Identify Gal(L/ℚ) with the appropriate quotient of (ℤ/mℤ)× and reduce N modulo m. All precomputation is independent of N. The relevant reciprocity statement is in [Milne, Class Field Theory](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf).

**AGGREGATE — KNOWN.**
\[
 \operatorname{Art}_L((N))
 =\operatorname{Frob}_p\operatorname{Frob}_q
 =\rho(N\bmod m).
\]
This gives the product of Frobenius elements, not the unordered pair of their conjugacy data. Recovering a separate local component is precisely the missing operation.

### 03. Number-field power residue symbols: what de Boer–Pagano actually computes

**Definition/algorithm.** Fix K containing μ_r and fix α∈O_K. Consider the power residue symbol (α/(N))_r, where **(N) is the full rational principal ideal**, not a selected prime ideal above an unknown factor. A fixed Kummer character has a fixed ray modulus 𝔣. Its value can be obtained by reducing N modulo a fixed integer m with mO_K contained in the finite part of 𝔣, with any sign conditions included. This is polynomial-time for fixed data by fixed-table evaluation.

For varying number-field inputs, [de Boer–Pagano, ISSAC 2017](https://koendeboer.com/publication/prs/PRS16.pdf) use Hilbert symbols, LLL and near-prime sampling. Their expected-polynomial complexity claim is **HEURISTIC**, explicitly so in the paper. It is not an unconditional polynomial theorem for arbitrary r,K,α.

**AGGREGATE — PROVED by ray-class reciprocity.** For rational integers N,N′ with N≡N′ mod m, the principal ideal ratio is ray-trivial, so
\[
 (\alpha/(N))_r=\psi_\alpha(N\bmod m),\qquad
 \psi_\alpha(pq)=\psi_\alpha(p)\psi_\alpha(q).
\]
Choosing one prime ideal over p rather than the whole ideal (N) requires extra structure. Changing α with N also changes the conductor and invalidates this fixed-floor conclusion. Neither variation computes 04 automatically.

### 04. Rational quartic residuosity is a different object

**Definition/algorithm.** Put ρ₄(a;p)=+1 if a is a fourth power modulo p and −1 otherwise, and define ρ₄(a;N)=ρ₄(a;p)ρ₄(a;q). In the standard promised problem the numerator is already a square at every prime divisor. No general polynomial-time algorithm without factoring is known; given p,q, exponentiation tests fourth-power membership. See [Hittmeir, Problem 1.2 and Theorems 1.3–1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.07791).

**NOT COMPUTABLE* — KNOWN status; leakage reduction PROVED HERE.** For N≡1 mod 4 choose t with (t/N)=−1, by random sampling and Jacobi evaluation. Then
\[
 \rho_4(t^2;N)=
 \begin{cases}-1,&p,q\equiv1\pmod4,\\+1,&p,q\equiv3\pmod4.\end{cases}
\]
At primes 3 mod 4 every square is a fourth power; at primes 1 mod 4 the quartic test of t² equals (t/p). Thus an oracle reveals ε. **No factoring equivalence is asserted**: deciding ε is not known equivalent to factoring. A product of locally defined quantities is not automatically a product of fixed-conductor characters.

### 05. Corrected fixed-numerator Dedekind sum

**Definition/algorithm.** Fix a>0, gcd(a,N)=1, and let s(a,N)=Σ_{k=1}^{N−1}((k/N))((ak/N)), where ((x)) is zero at integers and {x}−1/2 otherwise. Define
\[
 H_a(N)=12s(a,N)-a/N-N/a-1/(aN)+3.
\]
Reciprocity and Euclid compute the exact rational number in polynomial time; the same algorithm underlies [Beck–Robins, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0112076).

**AGGREGATE — PROVED.** Reciprocity gives exactly
\[
 H_a(N)=-12s(N,a)=-12s(N\bmod a,a).
\]
The invariant H_a therefore factors through N mod a. The **uncorrected** s(a,N) has an explicit N-dependent height term and is not literally a fixed-floor function. This normalization distinction is essential, not cosmetic.

### 06. Full Dedekind sum and the unwrapped Rademacher integer

**Definition/algorithm.** Take a=a_j(N), let u=a⁻¹ mod N with 1≤u<N, and b=(au−1)/N. Define
\[
 \gamma=\begin{pmatrix}u&b\\N&a\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \Phi(\gamma)=\frac{a+u}{N}-12s(a,N)\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Euclidean reciprocity computes s and Φ exactly in polynomial bit time. Rademacher’s integer and its signature cocycle are treated in [Kirby–Melvin](https://math.berkeley.edu/~kirby/papers/Kirby%20and%20Melvin%20-%20Dedekind%20sums,%20mu-invariants%20and%20the%20signature%20cocycle%20-%20MR1275766.pdf).

**GENUINELY OPEN — algorithm KNOWN; leakage UNPROVED.** The exact identity
\[
 D=12Ns(a,N)=a+u-N\Phi(\gamma),\qquad D\equiv a+u\pmod N
\]
explains the inverse residue, which Euclid already supplies. Eta explains Φ modulo 24 through a Jacobi symbol and public matrix entries (11 below). Neither identity determines the full integer Φ. For growing a, the fixed-a proof in 05 has a growing modulus and is no floor theorem. Full permutation inversion count is the same candidate in another guise: I(a,N)=−3Ns(a,N)+(N−1)(N−2)/4; its parity alone is 01.

**Concrete test.** Use Φ mod 5,7,31, asinh Φ, and their joint categorical distribution across the eight fixed probes. Compare with the N mod 256 baseline by the protocol below. Treat these as a single candidate family, not as separate discoveries for each presentation.

### 07. Shifted Dedekind–Rademacher sums

**Definition/algorithm.** With a=a_j(N), set
\[
 R_j(N)=\sum_{k\bmod N}
 \left(\left(\frac{a(k+1/5)}N+\frac13\right)\right)
 \left(\left(\frac{k+1/5}N\right)\right).
\]
Use the shifted reciprocity formula with periodized B₂. When a=qN+r, reduce a to r and replace the first shift x by x+qy; swapping by reciprocity then decreases the modulus. The rational shifts remain of denominator dividing 15 and their bit sizes remain polynomial. See [Hall–Wilson–Zagier, Dedekind–Rademacher sums](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/acta/73-4/fulltext.pdf).

**GENUINELY OPEN — algorithm KNOWN; leakage UNPROVED.** This returns the complete rational sum, rather than a finite-order exponential of it. The shifts add boundary terms to reciprocity; no multiplicative CRT identity reducing the full sum to two fixed characters is supplied. Fixed small a is much less interesting, since reciprocity immediately moves the long modulus to that small a. Nonmultiplicativity by itself is not evidence of useful leakage.

**Concrete test.** The integer U_j=900N²R_j(N) is exact. Test U_j modulo 7,31,127 and asinh(R_j−s(a_j,N)), conditioning additionally on N mod 15 in robustness checks. Residue choices and shifts must be frozen before the holdout labels are opened.

### 08. Fixed-weight Bernoulli Dedekind sums

**Definition/algorithm.** Let
\[
 D_{1,3}(a,N)=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\bar B_1(k/N)\bar B_3(ak/N),
 \quad B_3(x)=x^3-\tfrac32x^2+\tfrac12x.
\]
Use a=a_j(N). Introduce t=⌊ak/N⌋. The summand is a fixed-degree polynomial in k,t on the two-dimensional integer region 1≤k<N, Nt≤ak<N(t+1). Apply short rational generating functions and fixed-order differentiation to obtain its weighted count. This is a constructive polynomial-time consequence of [Barvinok’s fixed-dimensional algorithm](https://library.slmath.org/books/Book38/files/barvinok.pdf).

**GENUINELY OPEN — computability PROVED by that construction; leakage UNPROVED.** The scalar weighted count has no evident common-character product law. Constant weights and dimension matter: the statement does not grant polynomial time when the weight or dimension grows. Because the computation is already a lattice enumeration problem, a positive result would still need an argument that its leakage mechanism is outside (A).

**Concrete test.** U_j=4N⁴D_{1,3}(a_j,N) is an integer. Test its residues modulo 7,31,127 and asinh(D_{1,3}/N), including regression against candidate 06 so that a rediscovery of its features is not counted as independent evidence.

### 09. Fourfold cotangent/Zagier sum

**Definition/algorithm.** For four independently labelled public unit slopes a₁,…,a₄, evaluate
\[
 C_4(N;\mathbf a)=\frac1N\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}
 \prod_{j=1}^{4}\cot(\pi a_jr/N).
\]
The result is rational. Compute the constant/Laurent coefficient of its fixed-dimensional cone generating function by a short cone decomposition. Polynomial time in fixed dimension is [Beck–Robins, Theorem 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0112076); a unified treatment is [Beck, Dedekind cotangent sums](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa109-2-1).

**GENUINELY OPEN — algorithm KNOWN; leakage UNPROVED.** CRT does not turn a product of cotangents into a product of two scalar quadratic characters. Growing generic slopes avoid the elementary fixed-slope polynomial/quasipolynomial special cases. This is a broader probe than the ordinary Dedekind sum, but still belongs to the lattice-point computational ecosystem. There is no proved correlation with ε here.

**Concrete test.** Compute exact rational C₄ for slope quadruples (a₀,a₁,a₂,a₃) and (a₄,a₅,a₆,a₇). Test asinh(C₄/N) and reductions in ℤ/ℓ for ℓ=7,31,127 wherever the reduced rational denominator is invertible. Record denominator valuations otherwise; do not drop those samples.

### 10. Fourier–Dedekind sums

**Definition/algorithm.** With t,a,b,c fixed public probe functions of N and a,b,c units, put
\[
 F_t(a,b,c;N)=\frac1N\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}
 \frac{e_N(tr)}{(1-e_N(ar))(1-e_N(br))(1-e_N(cr))}.
\]
This is rational by Galois invariance. Use the constant-term/cone algorithm in [Xin–Xu, A polynomial time algorithm for calculating Fourier–Dedekind sums](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01185), published in [Experimental Mathematics](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2024.2364264). Fix the number of denominator factors at three; no dimension-uniform polynomial bound is claimed.

**GENUINELY OPEN — algorithm KNOWN; leakage UNPROVED.** These are constituents of restricted-partition and Ehrhart formulas, rather than multiplicative characters of N. A constant-dimensional sum can be evaluated without listing N roots of unity. A hidden reduction to simpler Euclidean statistics remains possible; a signal would require comparison with 06–09 and with their lattice interpretation.

**Concrete test.** Use (a,b,c,t)=(a₀,a₁,a₂,a₃) and a second disjoint quadruple. Test signed-log magnitude and rational reductions as in 09. Conditioning on the simpler Dedekind features tests whether the extra denominator supplies anything new.

### 11. Eta multipliers: finite phase versus its logarithm

**Definition/algorithm.** For c>0 and γ=(A B; c d)∈SL₂(ℤ), use η(γτ)=ν_η(γ)(cτ+d)^{1/2}η(τ), with the standard branch. For odd c, Petersson’s formula is
\[
 \nu_\eta(\gamma)=\left(\frac dc\right)
 e_{24}\big((A+d)c-Bd(c^2-1)-3c\big).
\]
This is a gcd/Jacobi computation plus small-modulus arithmetic. The formula, including conventions, appears as Theorem 2.1 of [Williams et al., Analogues of Ramanujan’s 24 squares formula](https://people.math.carleton.ca/~williams/papers/pdf/350.pdf).

**AGGREGATE — KNOWN formula; fixed-floor deduction PROVED HERE.** Take c=N and d=5, A=5⁻¹ mod N, B=(5A−1)/N. Here B∈{1,2,3,4}, and all matrix entries modulo 24 are determined by N modulo 120. Consequently ν_η is a function of N mod 120. For a growing d it is still the displayed Jacobi product times an explicit phase, but it is **not** thereby a fixed-conductor function. For the matrix in 06,
\[
 \nu_\eta(\gamma)=e_{24}(\Phi(\gamma)-3).
\]
Thus this formula constrains Φ modulo 24, not its integer lift.

### 12. Theta transformations

**Definition/algorithm.** For θ(τ)=Σ_{k∈ℤ}exp(2πik²τ) on Γ₀(4), the scalar multiplier for γ=(A B;c d), d odd and c divisible by 4, is (c/d)ε_d⁻¹, where ε_d=1 or i according as d is 1 or 3 mod 4. This is the standard quadratic theta transformation, also obtained from the Gauss evaluations in 13. Choose c=4N,d=5 and solve 5A−4NB=1.

**AGGREGATE — KNOWN transformation; deduction PROVED.** In this case
\[
 \nu_\theta(\gamma)=(4N/5)=(N/5).
\]
It depends only on N mod 5. General theta transformations also include explicit powers, square roots and elliptic-variable phases. Keeping those public analytic terms must not be mistaken for learning a local sign at p.

### 13. Quadratic Gauss sums, including Landsberg–Schaar

**Definition/algorithm.** Let G(a;N)=Σ_{x mod N}e_N(ax²). For odd N and gcd(a,N)=1,
\[
 G(a;N)=\epsilon_N\left(\frac aN\right)\sqrt N,
 \qquad \epsilon_N=\begin{cases}1&N\equiv1\pmod4,\\i&N\equiv3\pmod4.\end{cases}
\]
Compute the symbol by Euclid and √N to the requested precision. This is polynomial in n+B for B output bits, and also gives an exact radical representation. See [Murty–Pathak, Evaluation of the quadratic Gauss sum](https://www.cmi.ac.in/~siddhi/Quadratic_Gauss_Sum.pdf).

**AGGREGATE — KNOWN.** For coprime u,v,
\[
 G(a;uv)=G(av;u)G(au;v).
\]
Quadratic reciprocity combines the twisting signs into ε_{uv}; the normalized answer is exactly the displayed character. Landsberg–Schaar provides another reciprocity evaluation, not a finer factor-dependent sign. A linear term is also easy:
\[
 \sum_x e_N(ax^2+bx)=e_N(-\overline{4a}b^2)G(a;N).
\]
The additional phase is an explicitly computable modular inverse. These conclusions concern **quadratic** Gauss sums, not arbitrary multiplicative-character Gauss sums or higher powers.

### 14. Multivariate Gaussian and abelian topological partition functions

**Definition/algorithm.** Fix a nonsingular integral symmetric d×d matrix A, with d fixed, and gcd(det A,N)=1. Evaluate the normalized quadratic partition function
\[
 Z_A(N)=N^{-d/2}\sum_{x\in(\mathbb Z/N)^d}e_N(x^TAx).
\]
Diagonalize by congruence over ℤ/N, using unit pivots and gcd checks, and apply 13. If a pivot yields a nontrivial gcd, it has already factored N. The same Gauss calculations occur in fixed-rank abelian Chern–Simons/linking-form formulas; compare the lens-space Gaussian formulas of [Li–Li](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/generalized-gaussian-sums-chern-simons-witten-jones-invariants-of/).

**AGGREGATE — PROVED from 13.**
\[
 Z_A(N)=\epsilon_N^d\left(\frac{\det A}{N}\right).
\]
The determinant changes under basis changes only by a square. Thus the normalized phase has fixed conductor. This covers the specified Gaussian topological sector, not all nonabelian WRT invariants. Changing a topological presentation or calling the sum a partition function does not create separate local information.

### 15. Kloosterman sums: CRT is not an efficient evaluation

**Definition/algorithm.** For unit a,b define
\[
 K(a,b;N)=\sum_{x\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}e_N(ax+bx^{-1}).
\]
No general classical algorithm polynomial in n+B is known for K/√N to error 2⁻ᴮ, nor a general algorithm at inverse-polynomial-in-n accuracy in that normalization. Direct summation takes O(N) summands. [Bruin, introduction and Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.03600) discusses the evaluation problem; a polynomial quantum state-preparation algorithm there is **not** polynomial-time extraction of an individual classical value.

**NOT COMPUTABLE* — literature status, not a hardness theorem.** With \bar q=q⁻¹ mod p and \bar p=p⁻¹ mod q,
\[
 K(a,b;pq)=K(a\bar q,b\bar q;p)K(a\bar p,b\bar p;q).
\]
Those local sums are not fixed-conductor characters. Factoring only changes a length-N computation into two growing-prime computations; it does not supply a known polynomial-time evaluation. There is no factoring-equivalence claim for ordinary scalar Kloosterman approximation here.

**Precision trap — PROVED.** For squarefree N with two prime factors, |K|≤4√N. Consequently returning zero already approximates **K/N** to 1/poly(n) for sufficiently large N. Random sampling also approximates the average K/N. Neither result approximates the meaningful √N-normalized value.

### 16. Salié sums: a closed form that exposes the obstacle

**Definition/algorithm.** Put χ_N(x)=(x/N) and
\[
 S(a,b;N)=\sum_{x\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\chi_N(x)e_N(ax+bx^{-1}),
 \qquad \tau_N=G(1;N).
\]
For odd squarefree N and unit b,
\[
 S(a,b;N)=\tau_N\chi_N(b)
 \sum_{v^2\equiv4ab\ (N)}e_N(v). \tag{S}
\]
The prime-modulus closed form is classical; see [Kowalski’s derivation](https://blogs.ethz.ch/kowalski/2010/03/07/the-computation-of-salie-sums/). The extension (S) follows directly by additive Fourier transform, as proved below. Given p,q, the root set and the value are computable in randomized polynomial time. Without them there is no known general polynomial algorithm for the meaningful scalar value.

**FACTORING-EQUIVALENT — PROVED HERE for scalable precision, even at a=b=1.** Define H=S(1,1;N)/τ_N. The four roots of 4 are ±2 and ±v with v≢±2. Hence
\[
 H=2\cos(4\pi/N)+2\cos(2\pi v/N).
\]
From H to error N⁻⁴, subtract the first cosine and invert cosine. Round N·arccos((H−2cos(4π/N))/2)/(2π) to recover min(v,N−v). Then gcd(v−2,N) is nontrivial. The endpoint bound |arccos x−arccos y|=O(√|x−y|) makes rounding valid even without a derivative lower bound. Only B=4n+O(1) bits are required.

Conversely, factoring N gives the four roots by CRT and evaluates H to B bits in polynomial time. Thus this is a deterministic factoring reduction for this semiprime promise, not merely an appeal to generic square-root hardness. The standard root-to-factor connection is [Rabin, 1979](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/15016449-ca26-4d45-82ea-7c200d82d777); the cosine reduction above is supplied here, without a claim of historical originality.

**What this does not prove.** A machine offering only error 1/poly(n) in H need not recover v: its positional uncertainty can be of order N/poly(n). No factoring equivalence for that coarse task is claimed. Likewise, the fact that a formula contains square roots is not by itself a reduction from a scalar approximation oracle.

**Proof of (S).** Let R(a)=Σ_{v²=4ab}e_N(v). Its Fourier transform at t is
\[
 \sum_aR(a)e_N(-at)=\sum_v e_N(v-t v^2\overline{4b}).
\]
For unit t, completing the square gives τ_Nχ_N(−tb)e_N(b/t). For nonunit t the sum vanishes, since at a prime dividing t its linear coefficient remains a unit. Multiplication by τ_Nχ_N(b), using τ_N²=χ_N(−1)N, gives exactly the transform Nχ_N(t)e_N(b/t) of S(a,b;N). Fourier inversion proves the formula.

### 17. Cubic exponential sums

**Definition/algorithm.** T₃(a;N)=Σ_{x mod N}e_N(ax³), with a=1 as the simplest probe. No general classical polynomial-time evaluation theorem is asserted. Direct enumeration is exponential in n; CRT reduces it to growing-prime cubic sums, not to a Jacobi algorithm. General-character Gauss-sum algorithms such as [van Dam–Seroussi](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0207131) are quantum, and do not supply the desired classical algorithm.

**NOT COMPUTABLE* — algorithmic status; informative zero test PROVED HERE.** For p≠3, T₃(a;p)=0 when p≡2 mod 3 because cubing is a permutation. When p≡1 mod 3 and a is a unit, it is nonzero: a rational polynomial of degree <p vanishing at ζ_p must have all coefficients equal, whereas cube multiplicities are 1 at zero and 0 or 3 elsewhere. CRT therefore gives, for N≡1 mod 3,
\[
 T_3(1;N)\ne0\iff p,q\equiv1\pmod3.
\]
This is potentially useful information, but does not manufacture an efficient zero test. Finite-precision near-zero decisions require a separation bound; none adequate for a polynomial-time algorithm is supplied here.

### 18. Fixed-level cuspidal modular symbols

**Definition/algorithm.** Fix Γ₀(11), not Γ₀(N). Compute the rational relative-homology symbol {∞,a_j(N)/N}, subtract {∞,0}, and express the result in a fixed basis. For 11∤N this difference lies in absolute/cuspidal homology, of dimension two. Continued fractions decompose the path into O(n) adjacent-cusp edges; each edge uses one entry in a fixed table indexed by ℙ¹(𝔽₁₁). Manin’s relations and the construction are given in [Stein, General Modular Symbols](https://wstein.org/books/modform/modform/modular_symbols.html).

**GENUINELY OPEN — algorithm KNOWN; leakage UNPROVED.** A fixed finite table is accumulated **additively** along a variable Euclidean path, giving unbounded rational coordinates. Fixed level therefore does not mean that the full accumulated symbol depends only on N mod 11. The fixed-numerator cusp 1/N would have a short decomposition and is a poor probe; growing a is intentional. Growing the level to N instead would invalidate the bit-polynomial preprocessing claim.

**Concrete test.** Use the two exact coordinates, their residues modulo 5,7,31 after clearing the fixed basis denominator, and signed-log coordinates. Add N mod 11 to the conditioning floor. Test increments over candidate 06 as well: a scalar continued-fraction statistic and a cuspidal homology statistic should not be assumed independent.

### 19. Two-square theta coefficients

**Definition/algorithm.** r₂(N)=[zᴺ](Σ_{k∈ℤ}z^{k²})² counts ordered signed representations. The classical divisor identity gives
\[
 r_2(N)=4\sum_{d\mid N}\chi_{-4}(d)
       =4(1+\chi_{-4}(p))(1+\chi_{-4}(q)).
\]
Given the factors this is immediate. No general polynomial-time extraction of this coefficient from binary N is known. Fast evaluation of a theta function at one point is not fast extraction of its Nth coefficient. Classical theta representation identities are discussed in [Milne’s paper on sums of squares formulas](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC26345/).

**NOT COMPUTABLE* — identity PROVED; complexity OPEN.** On N≡1 mod 4,
\[
 r_2(N)=16\,[\varepsilon=1].
\]
The *sum* of the two local signs survives; their product alone is +1. For a concrete fiber collision, 101·137=13837 and 103·107=11021 are both 13 mod 256, while r₂ is respectively 16 and 0. This establishes nonaggregation on that floor, not efficient computability or factoring equivalence.

### 20. Half-integral weight: three-square coefficients

**Definition/algorithm.** r₃(N)=[zᴺ]θ(z)³ is a weight-3/2 coefficient. For odd squarefree N>3, Gauss’s formulas give 12h(−4N) for N≡1,5 mod 8, 24h(−N) for N≡3 mod 8, and zero for N≡7 mod 8. See the class-number and Hurwitz-class-number formulas in [A limiting case of a higher-dimensional Kronecker-type identity, Theorems 1.3–1.4](https://archive.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/1851/1/preprint_2017_17.pdf). Enumeration or class-number algorithms compute it, but no general polynomial-time method in n is supplied by modularity.

**NOT COMPUTABLE* — KNOWN obstruction, not a fourth family.** The vanishing condition is the small local floor; the nonzero amplitude is class-number data. General class-group relation methods return to (M)/(Q), often with conditional or heuristic subexponential bounds. Merely naming a half-integral-weight coefficient does not bypass those computations, and knowing p,q does not trivially evaluate these class numbers.

### 21. Four-square coefficients

**Definition/algorithm.** Jacobi’s four-square theorem gives r₄(N)=8Σ_{d|N,4∤d}d. For odd semiprimes this is 8σ₁(N); see the explicit formulas in [Kac–Cheung, Quantum Calculus, Chapter 16](https://lib.ysu.am/open_books/418595.pdf). There is no known general polynomial-time coefficient algorithm here independent of factoring.

**FACTORING-EQUIVALENT — PROVED.**
\[
 r_4(N)/8=N+1+p+q.
\]
Recover s=p+q, compute the integer square root of s²−4N, and return (s±√(s²−4N))/2. Conversely p,q give r₄ immediately. The concise divisor formula conceals exactly the missing factor-dependent sum; it is not an evaluation algorithm from N.

### 22. Hyperbolic quantum invariants at Nth roots of unity

**Definition/algorithm.** For the figure-eight knot take the Kashaev value
\[
 \mathcal K_N=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|1-e_N(j)|^2.
\]
It can be computed by its finite sum, or by colored-Jones recurrences. [Garoufalidis–Zagier, Proposition 10.1](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/preprints/KnotsAndQuantumModularity.pdf) gives O(N) numerical arithmetic steps for a fixed knot. That is not polynomial in n and does not by itself bound numerical bit cost. Raw fixed-point output can also have Θ(N) bits; logarithmic/scaled output must be specified.

**NOT COMPUTABLE* — no qualifying general evaluation theorem verified.** Quantum modularity provides powerful exact structures and asymptotic transformations, but an asymptotic expansion is not automatically a uniformly accurate Euclidean recursion. A finite number of perturbative terms may approximate a coarse scaled value using only N’s magnitude; that does not resolve exponentially small, factor-sensitive corrections. I do not promote this object to a polynomial-time open candidate on conjectural complexity grounds.

### 23. Nonlinear dynamical periods

**Definition/algorithm.** Start x₀=2 and x_{t+1}=x_t²+1 mod N, and ask for the eventual cycle length L_N. Floyd/Brent cycle finding computes it without factoring, but can require exponentially many steps in n. Knowing p,q permits separate local cycle searches and CRT combination, not a general polynomial-time solution.

**NOT COMPUTABLE* — no efficient general algorithm known.** CRT gives
\[
 L_N=\operatorname{lcm}(L_p,L_q),\qquad
 \mu_N=\max(\mu_p,\mu_q)
\]
for the eventual periods and preperiods. These are not fixed-character products, but extracting them is the computational obstacle. Instrumenting collisions with gcd yields the familiar Pollard-rho splitting mechanism. Short, N-independent polynomial-iterate transcripts fall under the permitted Theorem PT until they split; no extra non-splitting inference is established here.

### 24. Linear dynamical periods

**Definition/algorithm.** Let I(a,N)=ord_N(a), with a selectable unit. No known general classical polynomial-time algorithm computes exact orders. The order/factoring connection is the classical mechanism behind [Miller’s work](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/Mi76.pdf), and later quantum order finding.

**FACTORING-EQUIVALENT — randomized Turing reductions, PROVED.** Query r=ord_N(a) for a random unit. If r is even and a^{r/2}≠−1 mod N, gcd(a^{r/2}−1,N) splits; for two distinct odd primes the favorable local 2-adic order mismatch has probability at least 1/2. Conversely a **general integer-factoring oracle** factors N, p−1 and q−1, after which order testing is polynomial. Knowing only p,q is not known sufficient for a polynomial-time exact-order algorithm; that qualification is necessary. This is explicitly family (M), not a fourth candidate mechanism.

### 25. Transfer matrices and finite-state statistical mechanics

**Definition/algorithm.** Fix d, a prime ℓ and A∈GL_d(𝔽_ℓ). Evaluate Z(N)=tr(Aᴺ)∈𝔽_ℓ by binary matrix powering in O(log N) fixed-size matrix multiplications. This includes fixed-width finite-state partition functions reduced modulo a fixed prime. The algorithm is elementary and requires no group-order computation.

**AGGREGATE — PROVED.** Let t=ord(A), a constant independent of N. Then
\[
 Z(N)=\operatorname{tr}(A^{N\bmod t}).
\]
No smoothness assumption is used: finite fixed data alone gives the floor. Allowing the matrix size, field modulus, or a nonreduced integer output to grow changes the claim; in particular tr(Aᴺ) over ℤ can have Θ(N) output bits.

## Numerical tests and an audit already run

### A confirmation protocol on 100,000 fresh semiprimes

Use 20,000 samples for each prime bit length b∈{16,20,24,28,32}. Sample distinct primes uniformly from [3·2^{b−2},2ᵇ), rejecting the **pair** unless their mod-4 classes agree. This gives balanced, exactly 32-,40-,48-,56-,64-bit semiprimes with N≡1 mod 4. The target ε is orientation-free. For a separate p mod 8 experiment, restrict to N≡1 mod 8, where both factors have the same mod-8 class; alternatively define p=min(p,q) explicitly and use a four-class target.

Freeze the probes, features listed in 06–10 and 18, and a 50,000/50,000 training/holdout split before looking at the fresh labels. Deduplicate N, or keep repeated copies together in one split. The classifier receives only the specified features and public baseline covariates; factor labels are used only as targets, and cross-instance gcd computations are excluded. Use the training half to fit the floor predictor P(ε=1 | b,N mod 256), with smoothing. Fit an augmented model adding one candidate’s features; simple categorical/ridge-logistic models suffice initially. For 18 also include N mod 11 in both models. Compare holdout log loss and accuracy, and report stratified linear and categorical association, since linear correlation alone can miss signals.

Permute labels within (b,N mod 256) strata, or within (b,N mod 256,N mod 11) for the stricter modular-symbol comparison. Refit the complete training pipeline for each permutation and use a maximum statistic across the six candidate families for familywise calibration. Use at least 999 permutations. Small additional conductors from explicit formulas should enter robustness baselines as public covariates; do not claim a new mechanism from rediscovering N mod 3 or 5. A second baseline should add N’s scaled magnitude and the Euclidean controls from 06. This checks finite-size distribution effects beyond the narrow residue floor.

At 50,000 holdout observations, the unstratified binomial standard error near accuracy 1/2 is about 0.00224. Thus an advantage of 0.001 is below the resolution of this design, especially after model selection and multiple testing. A hit should replicate on fresh 64-bit samples, new fixed probe seeds, and a different balanced interval. None of these tests proves asymptotic non-negligible advantage.

**Feasibility qualification.** The polynomial algorithms for 08–10 are genuine existence/constructive results, but no optimized implementation was benchmarked here. A 100,000-instance run may require substantial engineering; the asymptotic theorem alone does not promise workstation-scale throughput. Use exact cone algorithms, not N-term trigonometric enumeration. Test correctness against enumeration only on small moduli.

### Reproducible core algorithms for the fast probes

The following dependency-free Python code computes the exact Φ and fixed-level Manin coordinates. The latter uses rational row reduction only once, on a fixed 12-generator presentation. It also specifies the public hash encoding used above. Combine it with the sampling and statistical protocol just stated; no factor is an input to these feature functions.

```python
from fractions import Fraction as Q
from hashlib import shake_256

def probe(N, j=0):
    s = f"newfamily-blind-v1:{j}:{N}".encode()
    return 2 + int.from_bytes(shake_256(s).digest(32), "big") % (N-3)

def rademacher_phi(a, N):
    # Barkan–Hickerson–Knuth formula, regular finite CF with last digit >= 2.
    x, y, alternating, sign, length = N, a, 0, 1, 0
    while y:
        digit, r = divmod(x, y)
        alternating += sign * digit
        sign, length, x, y = -sign, length+1, y, r
    return (3 if length % 2 else 1) - alternating

def index(c, d):
    c, d = c % 11, d % 11
    return c * pow(d, -1, 11) % 11 if d else 11

def manin_table():
    rows = []
    for i in range(12):
        c, d = (i, 1) if i < 11 else (1, 0)
        row = [Q(0) for _ in range(12)]
        row[i] += 1
        row[index(d, -c)] += 1                 # g + gS
        rows.append(row)
        row = [Q(0) for _ in range(12)]
        for x, y in [(c,d), (d,-c-d), (-c-d,c)]:
            row[index(x,y)] += 1               # g + gU + gU^2
        rows.append(row)
    pivots, r = [], 0
    for c in range(12):
        j = next((j for j in range(r,len(rows)) if rows[j][c]), None)
        if j is None:
            continue
        rows[r], rows[j] = rows[j], rows[r]
        z = rows[r][c]
        rows[r] = [v/z for v in rows[r]]
        for j in range(len(rows)):
            if j != r and rows[j][c]:
                z = rows[j][c]
                rows[j] = [v-z*w for v,w in zip(rows[j],rows[r])]
        pivots.append(c)
        r += 1
    free = [c for c in range(12) if c not in pivots]
    return [tuple(Q(int(c == f)) for f in free) if c in free else
            tuple(-rows[pivots.index(c)][f] for f in free)
            for c in range(12)]

TABLE = manin_table()

def relative_symbol(a, N):
    # {infinity,a/N}; each edge is {g0,g(infinity)} with det(g)=1.
    x, y = a, N
    pm2, pm1, qm2, qm1 = 0, 1, 1, 0
    z = [Q(0) for _ in TABLE[0]]
    while y:
        digit, remainder = divmod(x,y)
        x, y = y, remainder
        p, q = digit*pm1+pm2, digit*qm1+qm2
        det = p*qm1-pm1*q
        edge = TABLE[index(q if det == 1 else -q, qm1)]
        z = [u+v for u,v in zip(z,edge)]
        pm2, pm1, qm2, qm1 = pm1, p, qm1, q
    return tuple(z)

def cuspidal_symbol(a, N):
    # For 11 not dividing N, subtract {infinity,0}.
    return tuple(x-y for x,y in zip(relative_symbol(a,N), TABLE[11]))
```

The continued-fraction identity used in `rademacher_phi` is the standard Barkan–Hickerson–Knuth formula; see, for example, [Theorem 2.4 in Equality of Dedekind sums modulo 8ℤ](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/83370). The audit used relative coordinates; subtracting the constant base symbol does not change their correlations. The basis is fixed by the displayed pivot order, so coordinate labels are reproducible.

## Theorem attempt: where a reciprocity floor is actually provable

### A finite-conductor multiplicative reciprocity class R

Fix a finite abelian group A, an integer M, and an involutive automorphism ι of A. Suppose F(a,b) is defined for coprime positive integers in an admissible domain and has the following properties, with supplementary rules at primes dividing M:

1. **Numerator congruence:** F(x,b) depends only on x mod Mb.
2. **Finite reciprocity defect:**
   \[
   F(a,b)=\rho(a\bmod M,b\bmod M)\,\iota(F(b,a)),
   \]
   where ρ is a fixed table on admissible residue pairs.
3. **Denominator multiplicativity:** F(a,bc)=F(a,b)F(a,c) whenever the terms are admissible; supplementary factors are completely multiplicative too.

Assume the admissibility conditions and supplementary rules for a fixed numerator a can be decided by residues modulo a fixed multiple of Ma. Euclidean evaluation using these laws is assumed to have polynomial bit complexity. This is a restricted **multiplicative, finite-conductor reciprocity class**, not a claim about every cocycle algorithm.

**Theorem R — PROVED HERE.** For every fixed admissible a there is a fixed modulus m_a such that
\[
 F(a,N)=\chi_a(N\bmod m_a),\qquad
 F(a,pq)=F(a,p)F(a,q).
\]
On the unit classes, χ_a is an A-valued group character. After applying any character of A, it is an ordinary finite-conductor Dirichlet character. Consequently every fixed finite vector of these values lies at the fiber floor for the lcm of its conductors.

**Proof.** Apply reciprocity once:
\[
 F(a,N)=\rho(a\bmod M,N\bmod M)\,
 \iota(F(N,a)).
\]
The second factor is determined by N mod Ma by property 1; the first by N mod M. Include the fixed supplementary moduli in m_a. This proves periodicity. Property 3 makes the induced function multiplicative. Since its values are invertible, F(a,1)=1. Every unit residue has a positive representative coprime to the fixed admissibility modulus, so periodicity and multiplicativity descend to a homomorphism on the unit group. The semiprime formula and the floor conclusion follow. ∎

The finite abelian group assumption can be replaced by an explicitly fixed abelian ray-class target; 02–03 are the number-field version. Supplementary quadratic laws account for powers of two in 01. No smooth group order is needed in either the hypothesis or proof.

**Cocycle/gauge extension — PROVED, but weaker than a blanket no-go.** If a recursion computes f(a,N)=g(a,N)F(a,N), with F in R and g a separately specified public analytic or coboundary factor, then the normalized value f/g has the floor property. The theorem says nothing comparable about the full g, about growing a, or about an additive integer lift of the cocycle. A finite phase can forget precisely the integer that remains in 06.

### Why the broad Euclid/cocycle theorem is false

**Counterexample — PROVED.** Dedekind reciprocity is an exact Euclidean recursion with a rational defect, but
\[
 s(1,N)=\frac{(N-1)(N-2)}{12N}
       =\frac{N}{12}-\frac14+\frac1{6N}.
\]
It is strictly increasing for N≥2. Thus it has infinitely many distinct values on balanced semiprimes. A fixed residue class plus finitely many fixed-conductor local-symbol products takes only finitely many values, so it cannot determine s(1,N). Even s(1,N)/N, a bounded statistic, has infinitely many values. This disproves a blanket claim that reciprocity-computable quantities are finite local aggregates.

This counterexample is intentionally elementary: it demonstrates failure of the proposed **representation theorem**, not useful factor leakage. Removing the explicit height term gives 05. The interesting unresolved case is a growing a after accounting for the publicly computable inverse and finite multiplier.

**The cocycle explanation — KNOWN identity.** In the normalization of 06, Rademacher’s Φ satisfies
\[
 \Phi(\gamma\delta)=\Phi(\gamma)+\Phi(\delta)
 -3\operatorname{sgn}(c_\gamma c_\delta c_{\gamma\delta}).
\]
This is an additive integer-valued primitive of the signature cocycle, not a multiplicative character in N. The eta multiplier retains e₂₄(Φ−3); it does not recover Φ from that root of unity. The congruence 12Ns≡a+a⁻¹ mod N similarly discards an integer multiple of N. Neither fact proves that this discarded integer is an aggregate, independent of ε, or factoring-hard.

Fixed-level modular symbols supply a second obstruction to an overly broad theorem: a finite table of Euclidean transitions can accumulate nontrivial periods in homology. Fixed weight and level make computation cheap, while the path length and accumulated integer vector remain variable. No theorem turning that additive accumulation into a product of local characters was found or proved here.

### What would strengthen the result

A useful stronger theorem would control **factor-bit correlations of integer cocycle lifts**, after explicit height and finite-character terms are removed, for a prescribed distribution of semiprimes and public numerators. Mere equidistribution over all denominators or all numerators is not enough: conditioning on the prime-factor residue classes changes the problem. Proving such a statement for Φ or for cuspidal modular symbols would go beyond Theorem R and beyond the permitted pure-tree theorem.

## Top three open candidates, ranked

1. **The unwrapped Dedekind/Rademacher integer Φ at growing public numerators (06).** Exact, extremely cheap, and directly adjacent to the successful Jacobi reciprocity mechanism. The sharp question concerns what survives beyond the inverse residue and eta phase. The tested linear features did not produce a reproducible signal; no general floor theorem is proved.

2. **Fixed-level cuspidal modular symbols at growing cusps (18).** Exact Euclidean computation with a genuinely additive homology target offers the clearest structural variation on 06. Fixed level avoids expensive preprocessing. Its relation to continued-fraction statistics and known asymptotic distribution results should be exploited before treating any signal as new.

3. **Shifted Dedekind–Rademacher sums with small rational shifts (07).** Exact reciprocity remains practical, and the shifts expose boundary information discarded by the usual eta multiplier. Compare them directly with 06 before moving to the more expensive, explicitly lattice-based candidates 08–10. A positive factor-bit correlation remains unproved.
